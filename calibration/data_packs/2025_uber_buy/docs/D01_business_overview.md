Title: A global marketplace, not a vehicle manufacturer
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1543151/000154315124000033/uberq324earningspressrelea.htm
Date: 2024-10-31

Uber matches consumers with drivers, couriers and merchants through software, payments, dispatch, safety and support systems. Its three reporting segments are Mobility, Delivery and Freight. The distinction between gross bookings and revenue matters: bookings measure the value flowing through the marketplace; revenue is Uber's recognized share, subject to differing principal-versus-agent accounting across businesses and countries. A bookings dollar is not a revenue dollar.

Third-quarter 2024 bookings were $40,973 million. Mobility supplied $21,002 million, Delivery $18,663 million and Freight $1,308 million. Recognized revenue was respectively $6,409 million, $3,470 million and $1,309 million. Freight's gross revenue presentation makes its sales scale misleading alongside the marketplace segments.

Mobility segment adjusted EBITDA was $1,682 million, an 8.0% margin on bookings. Delivery generated $628 million, or 3.4% of bookings; Freight lost $19 million. Corporate general administration and platform research consumed $601 million. Segment earnings must therefore not be added without deducting shared costs. These adjusted earnings also exclude stock compensation and other GAAP expenses.

The marketplace had 161 million monthly active platform consumers, up 13%; quarterly trips were 2,868 million, up 17%. Monthly trips per active consumer reached 5.9, up 4%. Uber One exceeded 25 million members. These observations support, but do not prove, a density advantage: more local supply can reduce waits, while greater demand improves earning opportunities. Membership can connect rides and delivery without requiring ownership of the underlying vehicles.

Dara Khosrowshahi leads the company. Management's current strategy combines profitable growth, new lower-cost products, advertising and autonomous-vehicle partnerships. The quarter's diluted weighted-average share count was 2,154.466 million; basic weighted-average shares were 2,101.660 million. Neither is an exact January 6 point-in-time share count. Dilution remains relevant despite repurchases.

The central underwriting distinction is between owning a customer marketplace and owning the technology that drives a car. Uber has announced partnerships rather than a commitment to manufacture autonomous fleets. Whether that distribution role remains valuable is an open question, not an established moat against every future competitor.
