Title: Robotaxi distribution risk is the central bear case
Source: compiled from: https://www.investors.com/news/technology/uber-stock-lyft-stock-waymo-miami-robotaxi-fears/; https://www.cnbc.com/2024/12/26/waymo-dominated-us-robotaxi-market-in-2024-but-tesla-zoox-loom.html; https://www.cnbc.com/2024/12/10/gm-halts-funding-of-robotaxi-development-by-cruise.html
Date: 2025-01-02

Investor's Business Daily on December 5, 2024 described Uber and Lyft falling after Waymo announced Miami expansion. It quoted an analyst concern that Google was “laying the ground work to cut out Uber.” The bear proposition is not that rides stop growing immediately. It is that an autonomous operator owning vehicles, driving software and customer distribution can retain the economics now shared with a marketplace.

Waymo's own-app expansion matters because not every market is being entered through Uber. Uber's Austin/Atlanta agreement cannot be generalized into universal exclusivity. A technologically superior supplier could use initial marketplace demand while building its own brand and then renegotiate fees.

CNBC's December 26 industry review, updated January 2, portrays 2024 as a year of emerging mainstream adoption for Waymo. Tesla and Zoox are additional competitive possibilities. Tesla's October 10 “We, Robot” presentation increased investor attention to a driverless future; a product presentation is not evidence of broad commercial deployment or regulatory permission.

The opposing evidence is the industry's difficulty. CNBC reported on December 10 that General Motors would stop funding Cruise robotaxi development. The financial burden of developing, deploying and maintaining autonomous fleets is therefore material even for an established automaker. This does not demonstrate that Waymo must fail; it does challenge the assumption that unlimited low-cost autonomous supply is imminent everywhere.

Other risks already visible in Uber's October earnings include rising insurance costs, reliance on independent earners and legal or regulatory changes to worker status. Mobility's healthy adjusted earnings can be pressured by insurance inflation without any AV displacement. Delivery competes for consumers, merchants and couriers, while incentives can reduce margins.

There is no single verified pre-cutoff sell-side consensus quantifying ultimate AV market share or the date of autonomous profitability. An analyst must instead separate near-term bookings and cash flow from terminal-value risk. The stock can look inexpensive on current growth if the marketplace is durable, and expensive if an outside supplier captures its customer relationship. Both propositions are scenarios as of January 6.
