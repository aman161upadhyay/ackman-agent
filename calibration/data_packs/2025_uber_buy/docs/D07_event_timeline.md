Title: The dated path from cash generation to autonomy anxiety
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1543151/000154315124000033/uberq324earningspressrelea.htm; https://www.cnbc.com/2024/12/10/gm-halts-funding-of-robotaxi-development-by-cruise.html; https://www.investors.com/news/technology/uber-stock-lyft-stock-waymo-miami-robotaxi-fears/; https://www.cnbc.com/2024/12/26/waymo-dominated-us-robotaxi-market-in-2024-but-tesla-zoox-loom.html
Date: 2025-01-06

February 2024: Uber authorized a $7 billion share-repurchase program, subsequently referenced in its October release. The authorization indicates capacity and intent to return capital; it does not mean the entire authorization has been executed.

Third quarter 2024: Uber issued $4 billion of senior notes, its first investment-grade debt issuance, comprising maturities in 2030, 2034 and 2054. It also entered a $5 billion credit agreement. This extends financing flexibility while leaving an obligation to service debt; it is not operating cash generation.

September 2024: Uber expanded its Waymo relationship to bring autonomous service to Austin and Atlanta only through Uber. The agreement is documented in the October 31 earnings release. Its significance is commercial distribution: an AV technology developer chose to access Uber demand rather than rely exclusively on direct customer acquisition in those cities.

October 10, 2024: Tesla's “We, Robot” event focused market attention on robotaxis. The issue for Uber is whether cheap autonomous transport expands demand available to marketplaces or creates an integrated competitor that bypasses them. No later launch outcome is assumed.

October 31, 2024: Q3 results showed $1.061 billion GAAP operating income, $2.109 billion FCF and 20% constant-currency bookings growth. Management guided Q4 adjusted EBITDA growth of 39%–47%. This is the latest available company earnings outlook.

December 5, 2024: Waymo's Miami expansion announcement revived fears about direct-app competition, according to Investor's Business Daily. Partnerships and bypass risk can coexist in different geographies.

December 10, 2024: GM announced it would stop funding Cruise robotaxi development, as reported by CNBC. This underlines the capital and execution burden of the technology rather than guaranteeing a particular surviving platform.

December 31, 2024–January 6, 2025: historical closes moved from $60.32 to $66.31. This short recovery does not resolve the terminal-value debate. The timeline ends January 6; no subsequent earnings or investment announcements are included.
