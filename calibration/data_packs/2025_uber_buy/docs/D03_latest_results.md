Title: Latest published results and the operating outlook
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1543151/000154315124000033/uberq324earningspressrelea.htm
Date: 2024-10-31

Uber's latest earnings release covers Q3 2024. Bookings increased 16% reported and 20% in constant currency to $41.0 billion. Trips increased 17%; consumer growth and greater frequency both contributed. Revenue grew faster than bookings, increasing 20% reported and 22% in constant currency.

CEO Dara Khosrowshahi said: “We delivered yet another record quarter of profitable growth at a global scale, reflecting the strength of our platform, which now has over 25 million Uber One members.” He also described advancing the autonomous strategy as “demonstrating how Uber can help unlock this exciting technology for the world.”

CFO Prashanth Mahendra-Rajah highlighted “delivering over $1 billion in GAAP operating income for the first time in our company's history.” His statement that the business was on track for 20% full-year constant-currency bookings growth is guidance, not a reported full-year outcome.

For Q4, management forecast bookings of $42.75 billion–$44.25 billion, representing 16%–20% constant-currency growth. It expected trip growth similar to Q3 and approximately two percentage points of currency headwind to reported bookings growth. Adjusted EBITDA guidance was $1.78 billion–$1.88 billion, up 39%–47% year over year.

Mobility bookings grew 17% reported and 24% constant currency; its 8.0% bookings-based adjusted EBITDA margin increased from 7.2%. Delivery bookings grew 16% reported, while adjusted EBITDA grew 52%. Advertising and fixed-cost leverage helped delivery profitability. Freight remained loss-making amid industry headwinds.

Uber reported insurance expense as a source of pressure: cost of revenue excluding depreciation rose to 16.5% of bookings from 16.0%. Corporate and platform spending grew little year over year, providing operating leverage. Investors therefore need to distinguish improvements from genuine scale efficiency from changes in take rates or accounting.

The release announced an expanded Waymo partnership for Austin and Atlanta exclusively through Uber, plus other autonomous partnerships. Those announcements demonstrate operator interest in distribution, but do not establish the economics of a mature autonomous fleet. A future fleet operator can still choose its own app in another city. No Q4 earnings release is used in this pack.
