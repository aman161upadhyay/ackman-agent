Title: Marketplace peers and the economics of local density
Source: compiled from: https://investor.lyft.com/financials/sec-filings/content/0001759509-24-000121/lyft-20240930xpressrelease.htm; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1792789/000162828024044309/dashq32024ex991-pressrelea.htm; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1543151/000154315124000033/uberq324earningspressrelea.htm
Date: 2024-11-06

The most direct listed benchmarks are Lyft for North American rides and DoorDash for delivery. Neither replicates Uber's geographic and product mix. Their adjusted EBITDA definitions also differ, so bookings-based margins are more informative than revenue margins alone, but still not perfectly comparable.

| Q3 2024 | Uber | Lyft | DoorDash |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | $11.188bn | $1.5227bn | approximately $2.7bn |
| Revenue growth | 20% | 32% | 25% |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $1.690bn | $107.3m | $533m |
| GAAP net income/(loss) | $2.612bn | $(12.4)m | $162m |

Lyft's November 6 release reports $4.1 billion gross bookings, up 16%, with 217 million rides and 24.4 million active riders. Adjusted EBITDA was 2.6% of bookings. Quarterly FCF was $242.8 million and trailing FCF $641.2 million. Its Q4 bookings guide of $4.28 billion–$4.35 billion implies 15%–17% growth.

DoorDash's October 30 release reports 25% revenue growth, a 13.5% net revenue margin and a move to GAAP profitability. Adjusted EBITDA increased from $344 million to $533 million. This is evidence that improving delivery economics are not unique to Uber; it is also evidence of a well-funded competitor capable of reinvesting in merchant selection and consumer demand.

Uber's Mobility adjusted EBITDA margin of 8.0% of bookings exceeds its Delivery margin of 3.4%. The lower corporate margin reflects shared platform costs, Freight and adjustments. Comparing Uber's consolidated revenue multiple with a pure delivery marketplace can therefore obscure different monetization and maturity.

Matching pre-cutoff peer forward P/Es, EV/EBITDA multiples and consensus EPS growth estimates are not recovered. No current valuation screen is substituted.

Industry economics are local rather than purely global: a dense supply network in one city cannot instantly supply another. Consumers and earners can use multiple apps, limiting switching costs. Uber's case rests on demand aggregation, liquidity and operating efficiency overcoming that multi-homing, not on contractual monopoly. AV operators introduce a new supplier class whose bargaining power remains uncertain.
