Title: Portfolio opportunity cost and restaurant concentration
Source: compiled from: https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2019/08/20165951/PSH-2019-Interim-Financial-Statements1.pdf; https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2022/10/07150943/2018-Annual-Report-Letter-Only.pdf
Date: 2019-08-15

The 2019 interim letter discusses a concentrated portfolio including Chipotle, Restaurant Brands, Hilton, Lowe's, ADP, Berkshire and the mortgage enterprises, as well as Starbucks. The names span different business models but include significant consumer/restaurant exposure.

The original annual letter reports PSH invested $495 million in Starbucks at an average $51.30. That is the listed vehicle's historical investment, not the all-funds approximately $900 million stake reported at initial disclosure.

The investor praised new-store returns, growth runway and the pricing opportunity. By the interim period, Starbucks was a substantial positive contributor to performance. Performance contribution, however, is not current position weight and should not be used to calculate precise remaining capacity.

The manager's Berkshire discussion in the same interim report provides an alternative style of exposure: a diversified insurer/industrial business with a large cash reserve and management succession in progress. That illustrates opportunity cost within an actual portfolio rather than an abstract comparison with the S&P.

Chipotle offers a more active restaurant-repair thesis; Restaurant Brands has franchise-fee economics. Starbucks combines owned stores, licensed operations and consumer habit. Similar industry labels do not imply identical margins, capex needs or risk, yet common consumer and valuation factors can affect them together.

Precise current manager-wide AUM, vehicle cash, January weights and transaction dates were not recovered from a qualifying pre-cutoff source. No post-cutoff monthly report revealing a sale is used.

An existing owner has different choices from a new buyer: maintaining, trimming, reallocating or fully exiting. Taxes, position liquidity, prospective return and the availability of new opportunities can all matter. The documented cost basis is relevant context, not a reason to anchor intrinsic value to past gains.

No evidence in this pack establishes a planned replacement security or requires an immediate sale simply because Starbucks appreciated. The business and valuation need to be evaluated against the portfolio's hurdle independently.

The portfolio context therefore helps separate three possible interpretations: core thesis impairment, fair-value realization and superior opportunity cost. It does not disclose which interpretation the manager ultimately adopted in January.
