Title: Original owner thesis — consumer habit, store returns and temporary operating disappointment
Source: https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2022/10/07150943/2018-Annual-Report-Letter-Only.pdf
Date: 2019-03-25

This is an archival copy of the 2018 annual manager letter, published in March 2019. The hosting path does not change the historical information date. It explains an investment established in 2018, not the decision at the current cutoff.

The manager calls Starbucks the dominant global specialty-coffee brand, with roughly 30,000 owned/licensed stores and $35 billion annual system sales. It emphasizes a loyal customer base with a frequent consumption habit and trade-up potential, protected by advantages over low-cost and boutique competitors.

The letter describes Starbucks as a rare large business with a long runway to reinvest at high returns. It estimates new-store pretax returns of 55% in the US and 75% in China. These are the manager's store-level estimates, not consolidated GAAP ROIC.

The manager expects high-single-digit revenue growth from underpenetrated markets, especially China, and same-store product innovation. Chinese per-capita coffee consumption is described as less than 1% of US levels; that indicates possible runway but does not guarantee Starbucks captures it profitably.

The entry valuation argument is explicit: shares had been roughly flat over three years while earnings rose 50%, allowing purchase at a 25% discount to the company's historical average of 26 times forward earnings. The letter reports an average $51.30 purchase price for the PSH investment.

The perceived discount reflected slowing US comparable sales, questions about long-term targets and management turnover. The thesis was operating improvement and continued franchise growth, not a hostile proxy contest.

By the letter date, the manager cites US same-store sales growth of 4% in each of the previous two quarters, following four quarters of 1%-2%. Initiatives include Nitro, marketing to less frequent customers, rewards changes, delivery and overhead simplification.

The letter also welcomes a $20 billion repurchase program expected to reduce shares roughly 20% over 2018-2020. It says prospective returns remained compelling despite appreciation already achieved. That was the historical owner's judgment in March 2019, not a statement of the appropriate action in January 2020.

The present evaluation must distinguish a continuing attractive business from a continuing attractive price. Successful execution can validate the original operating thesis while simultaneously reducing the remaining valuation discount.
