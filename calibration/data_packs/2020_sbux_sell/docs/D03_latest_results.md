Title: Q4 results show improved traffic, rewards engagement and China growth
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/829224/000082922419000047/sbux-9292019xexhibit991.htm
Date: 2019-10-30

Starbucks' latest available earnings release is the October 30 report for the fiscal quarter/year ended September 29. The January 28, 2020 release is outside the cutoff even though its fiscal quarter ended in December.

Q4 comparable-store sales grew 5% globally, led by 6% in the US and 5% in China. Global net store growth was 7%, including 17% net growth in China. The release presents both existing-store performance and continued system expansion.

Quarterly GAAP EPS was $0.67. Non-GAAP EPS was $0.70, up 13% year over year. The distinction reflects items excluded in the company's reconciliation and should not be blurred when computing a forward multiple.

US active rewards membership increased 15% to 17.6 million. The company has been using beverage innovation, digital convenience and loyalty engagement to improve operating performance. Membership growth is supportive evidence, while transaction and profitability data remain necessary to establish incremental economics.

FY2019 revenue reached $26.509 billion. Non-GAAP EPS was $2.83 compared with $2.42 in FY2018, despite lower GAAP EPS caused by the prior-year transaction effects.

For FY2020, management guides consolidated GAAP revenue growth of 6%-8% and operating income growth of 8%-10%. Non-GAAP EPS guidance is $3.00-$3.05; GAAP EPS guidance is $2.84-$2.89.

Capital expenditures are projected at approximately $1.8 billion. This is required investment in a growing owned-store network as well as the broader platform, not a negligible franchise-only cost structure.

The forward adjusted EPS midpoint of $3.025 implies growth of roughly 6.9% from $2.83. That is below the historical adjusted EPS growth just delivered and should be distinguished from the longer-term model or a bull-case acceleration assumption.

The evidence supports improvement in the original operating concerns: US comps and Chinese growth are stronger than at entry. It does not itself identify the correct valuation. A current owner must ask how much of that recovery has become reflected in price, what growth remains and whether the expected return still exceeds the hurdle. No later China closures, revised guidance or pandemic losses inform that assessment.
