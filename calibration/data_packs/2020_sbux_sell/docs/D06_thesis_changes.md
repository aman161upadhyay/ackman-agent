Title: What has changed since the original owner thesis
Source: compiled from: https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2022/10/07150943/2018-Annual-Report-Letter-Only.pdf; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/829224/000082922419000047/sbux-9292019xexhibit991.htm; https://fiscal.ai/company/NASDAQ_SBUX/
Date: 2020-01-15

The original thesis combined a durable franchise, fixable operating disappointment, high-return expansion and a discounted valuation. These elements should be reassessed separately rather than treating the stock's appreciation as either automatic success or automatic overvaluation.

**US operations:** The owner letter described a period of only 1%-2% same-store growth, then early improvement to 4%. Q4 FY2019 US comparable sales grew 6%, with contemporary press reporting traffic growth across all dayparts. The operating repair has progressed in the available data.

**China:** The original growth runway remains supported by reported expansion: Q4 China comps grew 5% and net store count 17% year over year. Competition and macro risk persist. A continuing runway does not mean every future unit earns the manager's historical estimated pretax return.

**Digital/loyalty:** US active rewards membership reached 17.6 million, up 15%. That supports execution on one of the original initiatives. It does not independently quantify future incremental profit or remove normal customer-acquisition and reward costs.

**Capital allocation:** FY2019 repurchases exceeded $10 billion and diluted weighted-average shares declined materially. That validates substantial delivery of capital return, while also changing debt, cash and the future EPS denominator.

**Valuation:** The manager's entry argument was an approximately 25% discount to a 26x historical forward multiple. The January $91.54 close implies about 30.3x the company's FY2020 adjusted EPS midpoint. The original valuation gap has plainly changed, even allowing for differences in forward conventions.

**Forward growth:** FY2020 revenue guidance is 6%-8% and operating-income guidance 8%-10%, while adjusted EPS guidance implies roughly 6%-8%. The analyst must decide whether a higher long-term growth assumption is warranted, rather than carry forward past buyback-enhanced growth mechanically.

There is no dated evidence here that the core coffee moat has disappeared or that management is fundamentally untrustworthy. The changed facts mainly concern execution, price and capital structure.

That creates a genuine ownership decision: retain a high-quality compounding business at its current expected return, or release capital if the return no longer clears the hurdle. The pack supplies the comparison, not the manager's eventual answer.
