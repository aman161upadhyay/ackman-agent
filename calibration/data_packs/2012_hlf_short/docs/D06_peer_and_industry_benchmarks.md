Nu Skin reports particularly strong greater-China and South-Asia/Pacific growth and raises annual revenue guidance to US$2.11 billion. Product launches and increased distributor activity contribute. A rapidly expanding Asian direct-selling network is not unique to Herbalife.

USANA attributes its EPS growth to sales, lower relative associate incentives, a lower tax rate and fewer diluted shares. Quarterly net margin is approximately 10.6%, calculated from US$17.5 million earnings/US$165.2 million sales. Tax and buybacks explain why EPS growth exceeds sales growth; similar distinctions matter at Herbalife.

Avon reports active representatives down 1%, unit growth 1% and flat price/mix. Adjusted operating margin is 5.9%, versus GAAP 4.2%. Its disappointing performance shows that the direct-sales distribution channel does not guarantee growth or high margins. Cosmetics, nutrition and weight management also have different customer habits and economics.

These peers compete for customers, distributors, attention and discretionary spending. Their existence does not resolve Herbalife's specific legality, just as an allegation against one network would not prove every direct seller unlawful. Product demand and a compensation plan must be analyzed at the relevant issuer and jurisdiction.

No date-matched peer share-price/forward-multiple set is independently extracted; unadjusted EPS cannot be compared across firms as if it measured relative cheapness. Herbalife's apparent discounted earnings multiple in D04 must instead be tested against durable consumption, distributor incentives and regulatory downside.

A business can generate real products, positive cash flow and attractive gross margins yet still face material distribution-law risk. Equally, a network model with genuine retail consumption can remain valuable even when distributor turnover is high. The contemporaneous industry evidence is mixed, not a conclusive sector-wide verdict.
