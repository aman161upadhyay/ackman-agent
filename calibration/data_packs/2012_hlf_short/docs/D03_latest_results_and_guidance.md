Title: All regions grow; 2013 outlook assumes continued distributor expansion
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1180262/000115752312005483/a50458256ex99_1.htm
Date: 2012-10-29

Herbalife's October 29 release calls Q3 2012 a record quarter. Net sales increased 14% to approximately US$1.017 billion. Volume points increased 17%, net income rose approximately 9% and EPS rose 20% to US$1.04 from US$0.87. Repurchases explain part of the stronger per-share growth.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| North America | 287.4 | 14% | 67,826 |
| Asia Pacific | 305.6 | 17% | 66,433 |
| EMEA | 145.5 | 10% | 44,861 |
| Mexico | 211.2 | 17% | 60,123 |
| South/Central America | 186.0 | 24% | 46,466 |
| China | 57.1 | 42% | 12,692 |

Worldwide volume points were 1,192.8 million and average active sales leaders 288,397, up 22%. Regional leaders do not sum exactly to the worldwide count because some operate in more than one region and the calculation uses averages.

Volume points are company-defined measures tied to product sales; active sales leaders indicate network participation. Neither is a direct count of outside retail buyers or a net-profit distribution across all entrants. A growing network can be compatible with real demand, recruiting dependence or a mixture; further evidence is needed.

CEO Michael Johnson attributes momentum to daily consumption and city-by-city penetration of existing markets. Annual 2012 guidance is EPS US$3.99–US$4.03, net-sales growth 16–18%, volume growth 18–20% and capex US$110–US$120 million. Q4 EPS guidance is US$0.97–US$1.01.

Initial 2013 guidance is EPS US$4.40–US$4.55, volume growth 8.5–10.5%, net-sales growth 10–12% and capex US$165–US$185 million. It assumes a Venezuelan currency rate of 10:1 rather than the prior 5.3:1 for ongoing operations, while excluding potential one-time devaluation or cash-repatriation effects.

The board approves a US$0.30 quarterly dividend. Reported growth, modest net debt and shareholder returns are important opposing evidence against a thesis based simply on imminent financial distress. No independently authenticated consensus-beat or miss figure is available for this release; the document does not fabricate one. The short-case burden would be to establish unsustainable demand or an adverse enforceable legal outcome despite currently strong statements.
