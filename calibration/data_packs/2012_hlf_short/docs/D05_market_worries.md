Title: Einhorn's questions and the company's contemporaneous defense
Source: compiled from: https://www.marketfolly.com/2012/05/transcript-of-david-einhorns-questions.html; https://www.latimes.com/business/la-xpm-2012-may-02-la-fi-herbalife-einhorn-20120502-story.html; https://ir.herbalife.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/473/herbalife-responds-to-stock-drop-today
Date: 2012-05-02

On May 1, David Einhorn asked how much final product consumption was outside Herbalife's distributor network. Management's answer relied on the 70% rule and the growth of nutrition clubs. When pressed for the percentage sold to consumers who are not distributors, the contemporary transcription records: “we don't have an exact percentage David because we don't have visibility to that level of detail.”

This is a disclosure limitation, not an admission of illegal conduct. Independent distributors may legitimately join principally for discounts and consume products personally. But if rewards are economically tied to recruiting purchasers seeking an income opportunity, company sales can overstate sustainable end-consumer demand. The distinction requires evidence about incentives, realized retail sales and participant outcomes.

Einhorn also asked why a supervisor would enroll a customer as a discounted distributor rather than retain the retail markup. Management emphasized retention and preserving the recruit's position within the supervisor's lineage if that customer later builds a business. That explanation identifies both a commercial customer-retention rationale and a recruitment-linked benefit.

A further question concerned discontinued disclosure of distributor categories. The company had previously identified personal consumers, small retailers and potential sales leaders. The call response attributes the disclosure change to a previous CFO's judgment about usefulness, rather than offering a current independently measured classification.

The Los Angeles Times reports the stock closed May 1 down approximately 20% at US$56.30 after an intraday decline of approximately 26%, despite a better-than-expected profit increase. Analyst Timothy Ramey called the inquiries “basic.” A famous short seller asking questions can move a stock without disclosing any position or proving a thesis.

Herbalife's May 1 statement says the questions raise no new subjects, describes them as elementary questions often asked by investors new to the industry and calls the price drop a buying opportunity. It affirms confidence in financials, disclosures and network marketing.

The robust defense and reported profitability deserve equal visibility with the doubts. A prospective short needs a legal and factual explanation of why this particular compensation structure violates applicable rules or destroys value, not a generic claim that multilevel marketing is necessarily fraudulent. No adverse adjudication, planned regulator action or future activist allegation is established by this record.
