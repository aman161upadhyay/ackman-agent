FY2021 revenue grew 19%; operating income grew 35%. The full-year operating margin was 21%, versus 18% in 2020. Q1 2022 revenue grew 9.8% and operating margin was 25.1%. A strong quarterly margin does not imply an acceleration in underlying demand: management attributes the favorable operating-profit variance partly to content expense arriving below forecast.

FY2021 operating cash flow was $392.610 million. Property/equipment purchases were $524.585 million and changes in other assets used $26.919 million, producing company-defined FCF of negative $158.894 million. Content additions were $17,702.202 million, while content amortization was $12,230.367 million. Consequently, conventional physical capex is a poor measure of the economic reinvestment burden.

At March 31, cash was $6,008.946 million and carrying-value debt was $14,534.561 million. The company's net-debt measure, adding debt issuance costs, was $8,614.244 million. Net debt/LTM adjusted EBITDA was 1.3x. This EBITDA measure retains content amortization; an EBITDA measure adding it back would be economically misleading.

Q1 operating cash flow of $922.839 million less $121.158 million property/equipment purchases yielded FCF of $801.681 million. Acquisitions used another $124.521 million, outside that FCF definition.

Netflix repaid $700 million of notes in Q1 and made no share repurchases. FY2021 cash repurchases were $600.022 million; the treasury-stock balance also reflects equity-award settlement. No dividend is announced in these releases. A consistently defined ROIC series is not supplied. Management still expects positive full-year 2022 FCF, but that expectation is not a realized annual result.
