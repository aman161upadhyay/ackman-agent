Title: Streaming competitors and the limits of subscriber comparisons
Source: compiled from: https://s206.q4cdn.com/979796730/files/doc_events/2021/11/q4-fy21-earnings.pdf; https://www.reuters.com/markets/europe/hbo-max-shows-growth-despite-industry-slowdown-2022-01-05; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528022000035/ex991_q421.htm; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528022000144/ex991_q122.htm
Date: 2022-04-19

The available dated comparisons describe two major competitors. The Disney figures below are deliberately the older November release, not a claim that they represent Disney's latest April subscriber count. They provide a verified pre-cutoff economic benchmark. A fully synchronized peer-valuation table is not available in the retrieved sources.

| Company/service | Observation date | Scale and economics |
|---|---|---|
| Netflix | January 20, FY2021 results | 221.84m year-end members; 21% operating margin; $29.698bn revenue |
| Disney+ | November 10, FY2021 release | 118.1m members; 60% year-on-year growth |
| Disney direct-to-consumer | November 10, fiscal Q4 release | $4.6bn quarterly revenue; $630m operating loss |
| HBO/HBO Max | January 5 public update | 73.8m combined year-end subscribers, versus 69.4m in September |
| Netflix | April 19, Q1 results | 221.64m members; 25.1% quarterly operating margin |

Disney's DTC segment includes Hulu and ESPN+ as well as Disney+. Its loss cannot be assigned entirely to Disney+, and a Disney+ subscription is not necessarily equivalent to a full-priced Netflix subscription. Likewise, HBO/HBO Max's combined count includes the traditional premium network as well as streaming. These definitions matter when using scale to infer programming economics.

The industry offers relatively low distribution costs but high and continuing content investment. A program can be watched globally without reproducing physical inventory, supporting operating leverage. Yet subscriptions can be cancelled readily, and competing libraries reduce the exclusivity of any one service's entertainment offering.

Netflix's advantage is demonstrated current profitability and a large global base over which to spread content expense. The peer evidence also shows that rivals can sustain losses while investing in subscriber acquisition, especially inside diversified parent companies. A profitable incumbent need not win every incremental household to create value; equally, industry growth does not guarantee rising margins for every provider.

Peer P/E and EV/EBITDA figures are omitted: parent-company valuation mixes parks, linear networks, telecom or other businesses with streaming, and timestamped segment multiples were not recovered. Comparisons should therefore focus on disclosed operating economics and definitions rather than imply false precision from incomparable headline subscriber totals.
