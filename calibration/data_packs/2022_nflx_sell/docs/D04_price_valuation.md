Title: April 20 price shock and valuation limits
Source: compiled from: https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/business/story/2022-04-20/lat-et-ct-netflix-stock-plunges; https://www.cnbc.com/2022/01/24/netflix-shares-fall-to-lowest-since-april-2020-on-subscriber-concern.html; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528022000144/ex991_q122.htm
Date: 2022-04-20

The Los Angeles Times reports an April 20 close of $226.19, down 35.1% in one session following the first-quarter subscriber loss. This replay uses the closing price, so it is an end-of-day analytical snapshot rather than an intraday execution simulation. It excludes any manager decision announcement.

CNBC's January 24 article recorded a $387.15 close on that date and said Netflix traded above $700 in November. Comparing $226.19 with $700 indicates a decline exceeding 67%; that is a reference-level comparison, not a fully verified 52-week high/low series.

Using reported FY2021 EPS of $11.24 gives a trailing P/E of 20.1x, calculated as $226.19/$11.24. Replacing Q1 2021 EPS of $3.75 with Q1 2022 EPS of $3.53 gives a simple rolling-EPS proxy of $11.02 and a 20.5x multiple. This proxy does not recompute changing diluted-share denominators and is not a vendor-certified trailing P/E.

Using Q1 basic weighted-average shares of 444.146 million gives illustrative equity capitalization of $100.46 billion. Adding the company's $8.614 billion net-debt measure gives an illustrative enterprise value of $109.08 billion. Dividing by reported $6.869 billion LTM adjusted EBITDA yields about 15.9x. These are explicit calculations using a quarterly average share count, not exact closing market capitalization or a consensus enterprise multiple.

The FY2021 free-cash-flow yield is negative, since reported FCF was negative. Annualizing the seasonally strong Q1 cash result is not an appropriate normalized yield. No reliable timestamped forward EPS consensus, forecast EPS growth, five-to-ten-year forward-multiple range, S&P 500 forward multiple, complete 52-week range or April 20 volume was recovered from the cited sources. Those figures are not supplied as substitutes from memory.

The share is substantially cheaper on trailing earnings than before the price shock. Whether it is cheap on prospective owner earnings depends on subscriber retention, content spending and the economics of the proposed business-model changes. A falling multiple does not by itself establish a wider margin of safety when the earnings distribution also changes.
