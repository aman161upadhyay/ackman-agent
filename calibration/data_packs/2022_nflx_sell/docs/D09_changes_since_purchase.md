Title: Original assumptions compared with information now available
Source: compiled from: https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2022/01/26170421/Pershing-Square-Capital-Management-L.P.-Releases-Letter-to-Investors-01-26-2022.pdf; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528022000144/ex991_q122.htm; https://www.cnbc.com/2022/04/19/netflix-is-exploring-lower-priced-ad-supported-plans-after-years-of-resisting.html
Date: 2022-04-19

The January purchase case assumed a high-quality subscription business facing disappointing short-term growth. The current information must be separated into evidence confirming that case, evidence weakening it, and variables not yet measurable. The purpose is a fresh underwriting decision, not a conclusion dictated by the investment's purchase price.

**Evidence that remains favorable:** Netflix is profitable, has global production capabilities and substantial viewing engagement. Q1 operating profit exceeded January guidance. Reported price changes remain revenue-positive, and management still forecasts positive 2022 FCF. The company has reduced gross debt and reports only 1.3x net leverage under its stated adjusted EBITDA definition.

**Evidence that has deteriorated:** Q1 memberships fell rather than rising 2.5 million. Russia explains 0.7 million removals, but the ex-Russia result still falls materially short. The company forecasts a further two million subscriber losses. January's explanation emphasized pandemic pull-forward and slow acquisition; April's explanation explicitly adds household penetration, account sharing and competition as significant barriers.

**A changed margin pathway:** The original letter emphasized substantial margin expansion from scale. Management now plans to hold margin roughly around current levels while attempting to restore growth. Lower subscriber growth spreads programming costs over fewer incremental payers. Current profitability therefore does not settle the trajectory of future profitability.

**A changed monetization model:** Advertising and paid sharing may create value, but their unit economics are not yet disclosed. An ad tier introduces advertiser demand, sales costs, viewing mix and possible subscription cannibalization. Account-sharing enforcement introduces conversion and churn uncertainty. Neither opportunity can be valued simply by treating all non-paying households as future full-price members.

**What is not established:** There is no disclosed, tested ad-tier margin; no robust conversion curve for paid sharing; and no demonstrated timetable for restoring the prior subscriber-growth rate. The price shock alone does not fill these gaps.

The analytical alternatives are to underwrite a conservative growth path using established subscription economics, explicitly model new business lines with wide ranges, or conclude that the range is too broad for the portfolio's concentration. A credible management team can still face an outcome distribution that differs substantially from the one assumed at purchase. This comparison deliberately supplies no manager decision.
