Title: Original investment thesis — January 26 investor letter
Source: https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2022/01/26170421/Pershing-Square-Capital-Management-L.P.-Releases-Letter-to-Investors-01-26-2022.pdf
Date: 2022-01-26

The following short extracts preserve the original underwriting assumptions; intervening explanation is paraphrase. The position was initiated following the January earnings-related price decline, not before it. The letter reports more than 3.1 million shares purchased beginning the preceding Friday. It distinguishes an attractive long-term franchise from disappointing short-term subscriber guidance.

“Netflix is a primary beneficiary of the growth in streaming and the decline in linear TV”

The supporting explanation emphasizes customer experience, continually refreshed content, broadband availability and increasingly convenient viewing devices. This is an industry-transition thesis: streaming gains entertainment share, and Netflix's scale helps it capture the growth. It does not require exclusive ownership of every successful show.

The letter identifies “subscription-based, highly recurring revenues” and “a truly best-in-class management team”. It cites the company's transitions from mailed DVDs to streaming and original production as evidence of adaptability and a high-performance culture. Management credibility is therefore an explicit part of the purchase case, not just an incidental characteristic.

Its moat argument combines content quality and economies of scale. A large subscriber base spreads the cost of programming, while desirable programming attracts and retains subscribers. The pricing argument is “pricing power derived from the enormous value it delivers to consumers compared with other alternatives”.

Two further underwriting pillars are margin expansion as the global subscriber base grows, and improving free cash flow that permits both reinvestment and shareholder distributions. These claims connect revenue growth to per-share intrinsic value; recurring billing alone does not establish recurring cash profit.

The purchase was funded by monetizing most of an interest-rate hedge. The letter reports $1.25 billion of hedge proceeds and says the Netflix investment offered a more compelling risk/reward. That financing history is already public; it does not establish the correct decision now.

The original thesis can be tested against the April results along four independent dimensions: member acquisition and retention; pricing net of churn; content-cost leverage; and conversion of accounting earnings to cash. A revised business model may be sensible but still require a fresh forecast rather than extrapolation of the original subscriber-growth curve.
