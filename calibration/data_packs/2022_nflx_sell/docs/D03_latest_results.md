Title: April 19 results — subscriber shortfall and a changed growth diagnosis
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528022000144/ex991_q122.htm; https://www.cnbc.com/2022/04/19/netflix-is-exploring-lower-priced-ad-supported-plans-after-years-of-resisting.html
Date: 2022-04-19

Q1 revenue was $7,867.767 million, operating income $1,971.626 million and diluted EPS $3.53. January's forecast had called for $7,903 million revenue, $1,765 million operating income and $2.86 EPS. Profit therefore exceeded forecast while the subscriber measure central to the growth model missed materially.

Paid net additions were negative 0.2 million, versus management's positive 2.5 million forecast. Russia's service suspension removed 0.7 million memberships; excluding Russia, additions were positive 0.5 million. Even on that adjusted basis, acquisition was considerably below the original company forecast.

The letter says: “Our revenue growth has slowed considerably”. It no longer attributes the slowdown principally to pandemic pull-forward. Management lists four interacting constraints: broadband/connected-TV adoption and other addressable-market limits; widespread account sharing; streaming competition; and macroeconomic weakness, inflation and geopolitical disruption.

US/Canada lost 0.6 million memberships. Management says the price change is tracking expectations and is “significantly revenue positive”. Latin America lost 0.4 million; Europe/Middle East/Africa lost 0.3 million including Russia. Asia-Pacific remains a source of member growth. Revenue-positive pricing can coexist with falling memberships; neither measure alone resolves long-term customer economics.

For Q2, Netflix forecasts another 2.0 million net subscriber losses. Revenue guidance is $8,053 million, 9.7% growth. The company retains its 19%-20% full-year operating-margin target, assuming no material currency shifts from January levels. It intends to protect profitability near current levels while rebuilding revenue growth, rather than continue automatic annual margin expansion.

The earnings interview also marks a strategic change. CNBC reports Hastings is open to lower-priced, advertising-supported plans, with development over the next year or two. This is exploration, not an operating product with disclosed advertiser demand or measured margins.

The company is also testing paid account-sharing features in three Latin American markets. Management views the non-paying audience as an opportunity, but acknowledges it cannot monetize all sharing immediately. Neither advertising economics nor conversion/churn from account enforcement is quantified. A forecast now needs explicit assumptions for both initiatives, not simply a new subscriber count.
