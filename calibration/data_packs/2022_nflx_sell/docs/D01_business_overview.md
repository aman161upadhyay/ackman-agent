Title: Business, regional economics and governance
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528022000035/ex991_q421.htm; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528022000144/ex991_q122.htm
Date: 2022-04-19

Netflix sells access to an on-demand library of films and television, with a small legacy DVD operation. The subscription is cancellable, so billed recurring revenue is not a long-term customer contract. Producing and licensing programming requires substantial cash before viewing occurs. Content assets are amortized through expenses over their expected useful lives; spending and reported content expense therefore differ.

Reed Hastings and Ted Sarandos are co-CEOs. Sarandos also leads content; Greg Peters is COO and chief product officer, and Spencer Neumann is CFO. Management's January letter proposes declassifying the board, removing supermajority provisions and allowing shareholders to call special meetings. April's letter reiterates that these changes will be presented at the June meeting. They are proposals, not completed changes.

At December 31, 2021, the regional subscription economics were:

| Region | Paid memberships, millions | Q4 revenue, $millions | Monthly revenue per membership |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| US/Canada | 75.22 | 3,309 | $14.78 |
| Europe/Middle East/Africa | 74.04 | 2,523 | $11.64 |
| Latin America | 39.96 | 964 | $8.14 |
| Asia-Pacific | 32.63 | 871 | $9.26 |

Regional revenue is not regional profit. The company does not provide comparable regional operating margins in these earnings letters. Lower local pricing and currency movements make a subscriber in one region economically different from a subscriber elsewhere.

The April letter identifies global content production, personalized presentation and viewing engagement as competitive advantages. Netflix produces in more than 50 countries. Management describes Netflix as the largest subscription streaming service by memberships, engagement, revenue and profit. These are company assertions, not proof that growth remains predictable.

The quarter ended March 31 with 221.64 million paid memberships, down from 221.84 million in December. Management estimates more than 100 million additional households share paying members' accounts, including over 30 million in US/Canada. These are potential monetization targets, not paid subscribers or signed contracts.

Q1 weighted-average diluted shares were 452.984 million, versus 455.795 million in Q4. The share denominator includes dilution and is not an exact period-end outstanding share count. The firm's principal operating leverage runs through content: serving additional viewers costs relatively little at the delivery layer, but keeping the library competitive is not free.
