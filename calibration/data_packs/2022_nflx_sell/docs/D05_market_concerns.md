Title: Contemporary bear case — saturation, competition and monetization risk
Source: compiled from: https://www.reuters.com/technology/netflix-subscribers-fall-first-time-decade-forecasts-more-losses-2022-04-19; https://www.cnbc.com/2022/04/19/netflix-nflx-earnings-q1-2022.html; https://www.cnbc.com/2022/04/19/netflix-is-exploring-lower-priced-ad-supported-plans-after-years-of-resisting.html
Date: 2022-04-20

Reuters' April 19 report, updated April 20, describes inflation, the Ukraine war and fierce competition as contributors to Netflix's subscriber loss. CNBC's April 19 earnings report contrasts negative 200,000 net additions with StreetAccount expectations for positive 2.73 million. These are separately dated market expectations, not the company's January guidance of positive 2.5 million.

The principal concern is that the highly penetrated core market no longer supports the acquisition curve investors used to justify large content commitments. Russia explains part of the reported decline, but excluding it does not restore the expected growth. The Q2 forecast for another two million losses challenges a one-quarter explanation.

The company's own April letter says the pandemic obscured the growth problem. That is more consequential than a single miss because it changes how prior data should be interpreted: pandemic subscriber additions may have pulled future demand forward, while competition and account sharing independently constrain conversion.

Competition acts at several levels. Disney+, HBO Max and other services compete for subscriptions and programming. YouTube and other entertainment compete for time. Households may maintain multiple services, but a discretionary budget limits pricing freedom and increases churn risk. Netflix's content scale is an advantage, not immunity from an escalating programming bill.

CNBC reports the reversal of Hastings' resistance to advertising. A lower-priced ad tier could expand the audience, but it could also draw existing full-price subscribers into a cheaper plan. Advertising sales, measurement, technology and revenue sharing create economic variables absent from a pure-subscription extrapolation. No operating history for that tier is available in this snapshot.

Paid-sharing enforcement similarly offers possible revenue without new content, yet the conversion rate is unknown. A non-paying household can subscribe, stop watching or remain difficult to identify. The 100-million-household estimate is not a receivable.

These concerns do not establish that Netflix is a poor company. The immediate underwriting question is whether its long-run revenue, margins and cash needs can still be estimated tightly enough for a concentrated position. The distinction between business quality and forecast confidence is essential: an apparently lower price can accompany both a compelling recovery opportunity and a much wider range of plausible intrinsic values.
