The fact sheet describes a concentrated portfolio, usually8–12 core liquid large-cap businesses, and limited annual new-name turnover. An ordinary US13F table omits foreign, OTC and some derivative assets, so its percentages cannot be assumed to be full-fund NAV weights.

The public investment framework emphasizes predictable FCF, durable entry barriers, capable management, strong financial profiles and limited dependence on external funding. SPGI should be tested through trusted benchmarks, data rights, customer retention and cash conversion rather than a generalized label such as “high-quality information.”

High accounting margins are not sufficient. The investor must determine whether current products remain indispensable, whether AI changes only delivery or also pricing power, and whether the forecast survives weaker issuance and market levels. Minority distributions and debt must remain part of owner-cash analysis.

The portfolio already owns technology-enabled franchises exposed to AI narratives. An information provider might add a different revenue model while increasing sensitivity to similar valuation and disruption debates. Correlation should be checked economically rather than only through sector classifications.

No authenticated current SPGI-specific Ackman thesis statement dated by March31 is included. Earlier familiarity with a company does not establish today's decision, cost basis or forecast. This pack contains current public evidence, not a later investment explanation.

The appropriate hurdle is prospective return from the present price under a defensible distribution of outcomes. A large multiple decline can create opportunity, but it cannot substitute for confidence in data/benchmark durability. Permanent capital supports patience only when the underlying long-term economics remain underwritable.
