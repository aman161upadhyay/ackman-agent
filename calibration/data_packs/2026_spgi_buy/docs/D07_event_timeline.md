Date: 2026-03-31

During 2025: SPGI's businesses grew, with full-year revenue ultimately reaching $15.336 billion. Ratings, indices and commodity information remained distinct profit pools alongside Market Intelligence and Mobility. A planned Mobility separation would change the reporting perimeter, but had not completed by this cutoff.

January2026: Competitor MSCI reported growth in both subscriptions and asset-based fees. That public evidence offers an industry benchmark before the sharp February sentiment shift.

February4: Reuters described a broad software/services rout following concern about Anthropic's rapidly advancing AI capabilities. Financial-information providers were included in the debate about tasks, interfaces and customer spending.

February10: SPGI reported FY2025 revenue growth8%, adjusted EPS$17.83 and 2026 guidance$19.40–19.65. The range fell below the reported$20.02 consensus. Management expected6–8% organic constant-currency revenue growth and modest operating-margin expansion.

The same day: Shares fell9.7% to$401.08 according to the Journal, the lowest close since November2023. Moody's fell6.8%, showing that the reaction extended beyond SPGI's particular guidance. The price event does not identify how much each causal factor contributed.

February18–26: Moody's released and documented strong2025 annual results, including$7.7billion revenue and51.1% adjusted operating margin. The continued industry profitability is relevant counterevidence, not proof that future disruption is impossible.

March18: SPGI's annual report provided full2025 financial statements and historical cash-flow reconciliation. The FCF definition deducts minority distributions in addition to capex, a material detail for valuation.

March31: Shares closed$402.40. This is the conservative pre-Q2 review date. Exact investor execution dates remain outside the public company evidence, and no later quarter or completed spin is included.

The question now is whether a de-rated valuation overstates disruption of trusted benchmark/data economics or instead anticipates a durable change in how customers purchase and use information. The answer requires mapping the threatened products to revenue, profit and cash, rather than projecting a broad sector narrative onto every division.

The planned separation also requires keeping comparable reporting perimeters explicit.
