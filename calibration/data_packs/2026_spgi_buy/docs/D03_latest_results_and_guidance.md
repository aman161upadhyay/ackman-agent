Title: February10 — growth remains, but guidance disappoints expectations
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/64040/000006404026000007/a4q2025earningsrelease.htm; https://www.wsj.com/livecoverage/stock-market-today-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-02-10-2026/card/s-p-global-stock-price-sinks-dUg62l87LpnluTzWGDWM
Date: 2026-02-10

S&P Global reported Q4 revenue of $3.916 billion, up 9%, and FY2025 revenue $15.336 billion, up 8%. Full-year reported EPS was $14.66 and adjusted EPS $17.83. Adjusted operating margin was 50.4%. Strong recent profit does not resolve the market's question about the next earnings trajectory.

Management's 2026 adjusted outlook calls for organic constant-currency revenue growth of 6–8% and EPS $19.40–19.65. It projects $215–225 million capital expenditure and 10–35 basis points operating-margin expansion. The pack does not substitute a later revised outlook.

The Wall Street Journal's February10 account gives analyst consensus of $20.02 EPS before the announcement. Guidance midpoint $19.525 is approximately 2.5% below that figure, calculated. The miss is against expectations, not a forecast of an absolute EPS decline from 2025.

Management expects Mobility separation in mid-2026 and says GAAP 2026 guidance will be provided on completion. The adjusted guidance and its separation assumptions should not be confused with a fully comparable post-spin GAAP forecast. No completed separation belongs in this March information set.

The Q4 segment table demonstrates differing economics. Ratings generated $723 million operating profit on $1.187 billion revenue; Indices $330 million on $498 million; Energy $220 million on $576 million. Market Intelligence generated $235 million on $1.264 billion, while Mobility generated $71 million on $444 million.

Those reported figures show that revenue share and operating-profit share are different. The largest revenue division is not necessarily the dominant source of profit. They also do not establish the precise profit exposed to AI replacement within any division.

2025 capital return was $6.2 billion, including $5.0 billion repurchases. Buybacks can support EPS growth, but should be evaluated against debt, valuation and operating reinvestment. They are not a substitute for maintaining customer value.

A complete call-level AI-product discussion and precise subproduct recurring-revenue schedule were not independently extracted. Current analysis should therefore use verified segment evidence and explicit scenarios, with conservative sensitivity ranges for vulnerable products rather than an unsupported exact exposure percentage.

The forward test is whether benchmark and data economics can continue compounding while workflow interfaces change and debt issuance remains cyclical.
