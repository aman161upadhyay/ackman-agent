Title: The operating assets, ownership structure and governance problem
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/895648/000095012308014370/c47435exv99w1.htm; https://wwd.com/business-news/financial/general-growth-shares-fall-on-missed-3rd-qtr-estimates-1851570/
Date: 2008-11-06

General Growth is a US real-estate investment trust with ownership interests or management responsibility for more than 200 regional shopping malls in 44 states. Its reported portfolio contains approximately 200 million square feet and more than 24,000 stores. These gross portfolio descriptions include properties managed or jointly owned; they are not all wholly owned assets available to corporate creditors.

The November 5 supplemental report separates Retail and Other from Master Planned Communities. The retail business collects minimum rents, tenant expense recoveries, overage rents and other property income. Master-planned communities sell land and development inventory, making cash realization more dependent on homebuilding and credit. Office and management activities add complexity. A stable mall cannot automatically offset a development obligation at a different legal subsidiary.

At September 30, retail occupancy was 92.7%, versus 93.2% a year earlier. Trailing tenant sales were US$455 per square foot. Nine-month Retail and Other NOI, including the company's share of unconsolidated properties, was US$1,885.970 million. Master Planned Communities NOI was negative US$24.961 million after impairment. The mall franchise and the residential development exposure should therefore be valued separately.

Longer leases provide a buffer between a bad shopping season and rental receipts. Nevertheless, tenant bankruptcies, non-renewals, vacancies, concessions and capital required to re-lease stores can erode cash flows. NOI excludes interest, corporate costs and much capital expenditure. It is evidence about property operations, not common-equity free cash flow.

Governance has changed abruptly. WWD on November 6 reports Adam Metz as interim CEO and Thomas Nolan Jr. as interim president after John Bucksbaum and Robert Michaels left those posts. Bucksbaum remains chairman and Michaels retains the COO title. The report links the changes to unsecured Bucksbaum family-trust loans to executives used to meet margin calls. A founding family's economic exposure does not eliminate financing or conflict-of-interest risks.

The supplemental report lists approximately 268.2 million common shares and 51.3 million exchangeable operating-partnership units at September 30, plus US$121.399 million preferred securities. The distinction matters: residual asset value is shared with OP units and preferred claims, not divided solely among the publicly quoted common shares. Asset quality, corporate governance and the debt stack must be assessed together.
