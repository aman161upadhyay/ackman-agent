Title: Why default is not automatically zero, and why positive NOI is not enough

Date: 2008-11-13

Chapter 11 should be analyzed as a legal restructuring process, not a synonym for extinguishing every common share. The cited 2007 edition of 11 USC §1129 is the law in this information set. It is not a prediction of any particular GGP court ruling.

Section 1129(a)(7) requires specified protection relative to a Chapter 7 liquidation for impaired dissenting holders. Section 1129(a)(11) requires feasibility: confirmation should not ordinarily be followed by liquidation or another reorganization. Section 1129(b) permits confirmation over a dissenting class when statutory requirements are satisfied and treatment is fair and equitable. For secured claims, one route is retention of liens plus deferred payments meeting required value; for unsecured claims, priority rules protect the value of their allowed claims before junior interests retain value over their objection.

Consequently, a company whose going-concern asset value genuinely exceeds all senior claims may have residual common value even when it cannot pay a balloon maturity today. But “solvent” must mean adequate value after valid creditor claims, interest where applicable, fees, preferred interests and reorganization costs—not merely positive historical book equity. A maturity extension is not automatic and a proposed rate must satisfy legal valuation standards.

GGP's September 30 maturity schedule shows US$1.207364 billion due in the remainder of 2008 and US$3.318788 billion in 2009 on a portfolio basis including JV share. Consolidated-only amounts are US$1.130131 billion and US$3.066477 billion. By November 5 some 2008 debt has been addressed, leaving approximately US$958 million by December 1. The changing dates and scope explain different headlines; they should not be combined into an invented single figure.

The November 13 Arizona Daily Star quotes a Tucson bankruptcy attorney saying the property-level tenant business need not visibly change even if the owner files. Operational continuity supports using ongoing rental cash flows rather than immediate liquidation as one valuation scenario. It does not determine who receives the cash.

The D04 sensitivity demonstrates the key uncertainty: a 9% cap rate nearly exhausts retail value above portfolio debt before other claims. Equity requires defensible asset coverage, adequate process liquidity and legal advocacy; sustained mall income alone is insufficient. No seven-year extension, cramdown result, sponsor or equity recovery is presumed agreed.
