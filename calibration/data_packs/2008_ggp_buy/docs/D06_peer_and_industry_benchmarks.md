The comparison is evidence that mall rental economics are not universally collapsing. Occupancy definitions and portfolio quality differ; core mall-store occupancy is not exactly the same as whole-portfolio occupancy. Share-based FFO is useful for each company's year-over-year trend, not as a cross-company margin comparison.

Macerich says it has closed or obtained commitments on more than US$1.5 billion financing since May. CEO Arthur Coppola emphasizes lender relationships and capital access. It raises annual FFO guidance by US$0.35 to US$5.35–US$5.50 per share, but guidance includes transaction-specific effects. A peer's financing success does not establish that GGP can refinance its particular collateral and maturities.

Simon is the larger strategic competitor. The Indianapolis Business Journal report gives quarterly FFO of US$463.9 million, up 10.8%, and a US$67.03 October 31 close. Dividing that old close by four times quarterly FFO yields 10.4x, mechanically calculated, not a November 14 forward estimate. Equivalent date-matched multiples for the full peer group are unavailable.

A potential buyer can exploit distress rather than pay full intrinsic value. The October 27 Las Vegas Review-Journal report identifies Simon as a possible whole-company acquirer in RBC analyst Rich Moore's view: https://www.reviewjournal.com/news/mall-owner-to-seek-buyers-for-three-lv-malls/. GlobeSt's November 4 headline, “Simon Not Buying General Growth, CEO Says,” supplies an important counterweight: https://www.globest.com/2008/11/04/simon-not-buying-general-growth-ceo-says/. Neither an agreed offer nor a shareholder recovery should be assumed. Industry scale may support operating value while financing scarcity transfers that value away from current common holders.
