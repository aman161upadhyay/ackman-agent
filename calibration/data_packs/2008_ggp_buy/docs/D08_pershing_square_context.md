The November 3 investor letter, available as a third-party transcription, says cash and near-cash positions, including Longs and the Wachovia/Wells Fargo long/short, equal approximately 39% of capital. It says the funds operate unleveraged. These statements describe fund financing, not the leverage of investee companies. Exact firmwide AUM and an independently reconciled November 14 cash balance are unavailable.

The letter states: “We believe risk should be determined based on the probability that an investor will incur a permanent loss of capital.” It also emphasizes businesses whose future cash flows can be predicted within a reasonable band, resistance to extrinsic factors and capitalization sufficient for difficult times. A very leveraged distressed stock must therefore justify an exception, not merely look statistically cheap.

Public earlier holdings in Target, Sears, Borders and Longs provide experience with asset-rich retailers and real-estate-related value. The August 5 Longs 13D reporting discusses advisers and possible discussions of assets, capital structure and governance. This is relevant experience, not evidence that a mall bankruptcy can be successfully executed.
