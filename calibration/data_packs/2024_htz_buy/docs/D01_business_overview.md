Title: Rental operations layered on a financed vehicle portfolio
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1657853/000165785324000138/q32024earningsrelease.htm
Date: 2024-11-12

Hertz operates the Hertz, Dollar and Thrifty rental brands across North America and international markets, with additional international brands and used-car sales. Customers rent vehicles for leisure, business and other transportation needs. The business combines airport/local distribution, reservations, fleet procurement, maintenance and eventual vehicle disposal.

Its economics are not simply a rental-rate spread over employee costs. Revenue depends on the number of rentable cars, utilization and revenue per day. Profit also depends on depreciation between purchase and sale, financing costs, repairs, insurance and the channels used to dispose of cars.

In Q3, average vehicles were 583,516, of which 550,074 were average rentable vehicles. Utilization was 82%; transaction days were 41.298 million. Total revenue per day was $62.63 and monthly revenue per unit $1,567. These are company-defined constant-currency operating measures. A car awaiting repair or sale can consume capital without generating a rental day.

Americas revenue was $2.062 billion and adjusted segment EBITDA $(169) million, while International revenue was $514 million and adjusted EBITDA $63 million. The international business remains profitable, but the larger Americas exposure dominates group performance.

Monthly depreciation per unit was $537, up from $284 a year earlier. Total vehicle depreciation was $937 million. The scale of this cost means improvements in purchase price, holding period and sale proceeds can materially change earnings even without higher rental prices.

Gil West became CEO effective April 1 following his March appointment. His previous operating roles include Delta and Cruise. The current plan emphasizes back-to-basics execution and fleet rotation. The latest quarter uses approximately 307 million diluted shares in a loss period; that denominator does not include every potential dilutive instrument in a profitable recovery.

Brand recognition and airport access create meaningful operating assets, but vehicles require continuous capital and are not a perpetual intangible franchise. Debt is partly secured against fleet collateral, with separate corporate obligations. Equity underwriting must evaluate the fleet and the operating company together, without assuming that collateralized debt disappears from economic risk.
