Title: Q3 impairment, fleet rotation and the stated endpoint
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1657853/000165785324000138/q32024earningsrelease.htm
Date: 2024-11-12

The November 12 release reports $2.576 billion Q3 revenue, down 5%, and $(157) million adjusted Corporate EBITDA, versus $359 million a year earlier. The company identifies the $436 million increase in vehicle depreciation as the main reason for the earnings decline.

CEO Gil West said Hertz was “focusing on our back-to-basics strategy to deliver sustainable, long-term returns for shareholders.” He added: “There is still work to be done, but I am confident that the enhancements achieved over the course of this quarter demonstrate that we are on the right track.”

The $1 billion noncash asset impairment reflects declining fleet residual values and the acceleration of vehicle rotation. It is not the same charge as the earlier EV write-down. Writing down vehicles recognizes past economic loss; it does not produce cash to buy replacement cars or eliminate future depreciation.

Revenue per day was nearly flat year over year, while transaction days fell 4%. Management says it favored higher-value rentals and reduced lower-yielding channels while remaining disciplined on capacity. This can improve mix but also reduces the number of revenue-generating days.

Monthly depreciation per unit was $537. Management expects to substantially complete fleet rotation by the end of 2025, when DPU “could normalize to under $300.” That is a forward operating objective and an important sensitivity, not guidance that all other costs remain unchanged or a guarantee of restored profitability.

Direct vehicle and operating expense per transaction day increased 2% year over year, but declined 2% sequentially. Insurance, licensing and taxes remain headwinds. Structural operating efficiencies are expected to improve unit costs as the transformation progresses.

Corporate liquidity was $1.6 billion at September 30. The release does not justify treating all reported GAAP operating cash flow as available corporate cash; adjusted operating and free cash measures are negative.

The release describes the overall transformation as ongoing and expected substantially complete by end-2025. The near-term investment question is therefore whether liquidity and financing access bridge a lengthy operational repair, with vehicle sale proceeds and replacement purchase economics both uncertain. A low nominal share price does not remove that financing dependence.
