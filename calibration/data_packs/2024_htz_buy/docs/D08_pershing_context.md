The September 30 portfolio update reports firm AUM of $18.4988 billion, including PSVII, and PSH AUM of $15.1353 billion. These figures include bond proceeds and are not equivalent to equity NAV or cash available for a new position. The update was public October 1 despite its current rehosted URL.

The latest 13F available by November 12 is the June 30 quarter filed August 14, not the September-quarter filing due later in November. Reported holdings total $10.411824 billion. The table includes Alphabet, Hilton, Chipotle, Restaurant Brands, Canadian Pacific Kansas City, Howard Hughes, Nike and Brookfield.

The newly disclosed June holdings were approximately $226 million Nike and $285 million Brookfield, approximately 2.2% and 2.7% of the 13F total, respectively. Those are filing weights, not PSH NAV weights. Foreign Universal Music, OTC Fannie/Freddie, derivatives and cash require separate sources. Complete current portfolio weights and November cash were not recovered.

The March annual report states a concentrated strategy oriented toward durable businesses, capable management and long-term value. It identifies Alphabet as the 2023 addition and Lowe's as the exit. Its December 2023 cash balance of $1.929 billion is historical, not a November liquidity estimate.

No verified Hertz-specific Ackman recommendation or operating scenario publicly dated before the cutoff is supplied. An analyst must not infer an endorsement from the manager's transportation holdings or from private future plans.

Hertz differs from asset-light hotel and restaurant franchises: it owns rapidly depreciating vehicles and relies heavily on financing. The investment framework therefore needs an explicit exception analysis. A branded oligopoly and experienced CEO may support repair, but predictability, balance-sheet risk and normalized cash flow are materially weaker than in a mature fee business.

For portfolio construction, the relevant question is whether a small, asymmetric operational turnaround can be justified without treating it as a full-sized predictable compounder. That requires independently stress-testing liquidity and dilution rather than relying on a price far below the recent high.
