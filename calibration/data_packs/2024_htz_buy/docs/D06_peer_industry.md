Title: A concentrated rental industry with large differences in execution
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/723612/000072361224000049/a03-pressreleasexq3f24.htm; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1657853/000165785324000138/q32024earningsrelease.htm; https://www.coxautoinc.com/insights-hub/october-2024-muvvi
Date: 2024-11-12

Enterprise, Avis Budget and Hertz are the major US rental groups. Enterprise is privately held, so a directly comparable listed equity multiple is unavailable. Concentration can support fleet discipline and pricing, but does not remove competition for airport concessions, corporate accounts, leisure travelers or replacement demand.

Avis Budget's October 31 results provide a useful operating benchmark before Hertz's release:

| Q3 2024 | Avis Budget | Hertz |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue | nearly $3.5bn | $2.576bn |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $503m | $(157)m |
| Approximate adjusted margin | 14% | (6)% |
| GAAP net income/(loss) | $238m | $(1,332)m |

Avis reported pricing down 2% and rental days approximately in line with Q3 2023. Hertz's revenue per day was down approximately 1%, transaction days down 4% and depreciation much higher. This suggests Hertz's weakness cannot be explained solely by an industry-wide disappearance of rental demand.

The comparison is not perfect: geography, fleet age, accounting adjustments and purchasing contracts differ. Avis's positive EBITDA does not prove Hertz can earn the same margin without additional investment. It does show that substantial operating profitability remains possible for a competitor in the current environment.

Used-car supply and new-car purchasing terms influence every fleet operator. Cox's October index decline is an important external benchmark for residual-value risk. A rental company can manage vehicle mix and holding period, but cannot independently set the market price at disposal.

A complete pre-cutoff table of Enterprise margins, listed peer forward multiples and consensus EPS growth was not recovered. Those figures remain unavailable rather than replaced with current data.

The industry thesis is therefore narrower than “oligopoly equals pricing power.” It requires rational fleet capacity, satisfactory utilization and purchasing discipline. Excess fleet can depress rental rates, while underinvestment can raise repairs and reduce customer satisfaction. Hertz's opportunity, if any, is the combination of an intact industry structure and company-specific repair, with leverage magnifying both success and failure.
