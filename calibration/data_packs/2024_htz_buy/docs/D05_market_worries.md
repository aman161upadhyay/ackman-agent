Title: Depreciation, execution and financing risk
Source: compiled from: https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/hertz-posts-weaker-than-expected-quarterly-results-shares-fall-2024-11-12/; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1657853/000165785324000010/htz-20240111.htm; https://www.coxautoinc.com/insights-hub/october-2024-muvvi
Date: 2024-11-12

Reuters on November 12 reports a wider-than-expected adjusted loss and attributes the pressure to high depreciation costs. Its syndicated early report describes shares falling more than 8% in premarket trading. That is a dated market response, not a full-session closing observation.

Hertz's January 11 filing said it planned to sell approximately 20,000 EVs and recognize approximately $245 million additional depreciation in Q4 2023. The need to reduce electric exposure raised questions about demand matching, repairs and residual values. The latest $1 billion impairment demonstrates that the depreciation problem is broader than a single quarterly charge.

Cox Automotive's November 7 report puts October's Manheim Used Vehicle Value Index at 202.8, down 3.2% year over year and 0.1% month over month on a seasonally adjusted basis. Unadjusted prices fell 1.9% sequentially and 3.7% year over year. Those are market-level used-car observations, not an exact mark for Hertz's mix.

For a financed fleet, weaker resale values can affect both earnings and collateral. The relationship is not symmetrical with a normal inventory business because many cars are already committed to rental use and financed against expected asset values. Accelerating replacement can improve future economics while crystallizing losses and requiring capital today.

The bear scenario is that repair costs, fleet inefficiency and residual losses consume liquidity faster than operating improvements arrive. A younger fleet can reduce repairs, but new vehicles can also cost more and depreciate. Management's targeted sub-$300 monthly depreciation depends partly on an external used-car environment it cannot control.

The constructive case is that the current losses include a transition burden, that fleet rotation can reset carrying values and that a more experienced operating leader can improve utilization and cost discipline. None of those possibilities establishes a guaranteed EBITDA recovery.

No pre-cutoff autonomous fleet-service contract or tariff-driven used-car windfall is included. Those are not present earnings or committed catalysts in this information set. Financing sufficiency and execution deserve greater attention than speculative optionality.
