Title: Comparing recurring fee earnings without ignoring the balance sheet

Date: 2024-02-08

Blackstone, KKR and listed BAM are relevant alternative-manager benchmarks, but none should be used as a direct consolidated accounting comparison for BN. The investable unit and capital intensity differ. BN includes significant invested capital and property exposure beyond its stake in the fee manager.

Blackstone's January 25 release reported 2023 fee-related earnings of $4.3 billion, or $3.58 per share, and approximately $5.1 billion DE. Its earnings discussion says fee-earning AUM increased 6%, while base management fees rose 7% to a record $6.5 billion. Performance revenues were lower in a difficult realization environment.

KKR's February 6 release reported $446 billion fee-paying AUM, up 8%, with $71 billion organic capital raised during 2023 and $32 billion in Q4. KKR also combines management economics with insurance exposure through Global Atlantic, making it useful for understanding both scale benefits and the additional balance-sheet risks.

Brookfield's latest release reports $457 billion fee-bearing capital, up 9%, and 6% growth in fee-related earnings. Its $2.554 billion asset-management DE is BN's reported economic stream, not the same as 100% of the asset manager's total fee revenue.

| Relevant comparison | What it tests |
|---|---|
| Blackstone fee growth versus Brookfield | Durability of recurring institutional fees |
| KKR fundraising versus Brookfield | Ability to attract new capital in a difficult market |
| BAM versus BN | Pure manager exposure versus holding-company capital allocation |
| Insurance earnings versus fees | Spread/capital risk versus capital-light recurring economics |

Pre-cutoff comparable forward multiples and consistently defined peer FRE margins are not independently recovered. Applying a premium peer fee multiple to every dollar of BN DE would overvalue property and investment income if those deserve lower multiples.

The alternative industry benefits from long-duration institutional commitments and specialized investment/operating capabilities. However, fundraising, realizations and carry remain cyclical. Scale can improve access to deals and capital, while private-market complexity can conceal risk. A defensible BN valuation must separate recurring management income from capital-dependent returns before borrowing a peer multiple.
