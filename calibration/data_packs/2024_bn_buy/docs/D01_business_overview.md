Title: What BN owns, and why it is not BAM
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1001085/000117184324000675/exh_991.htm; https://bam.brookfield.com/sites/brookfield-bam/files/2023-02/2023-q1-bam-investor-presentation.pdf; https://bn.brookfield.com/sites/brookfield-bn-v2/files/brookfield-bn/events-news/investor-presentations/2023-bn-investor-day.pdf
Date: 2024-02-08

Brookfield Corporation is a diversified owner and allocator of capital, not simply the publicly listed fee manager. Its economic streams include asset management, insurance, listed operating affiliates, directly held real estate, fund investments and carried interest. Consolidated accounts include assets, debt and earnings partly belonging to outside investors.

In December 2022, Brookfield distributed 25% of its asset-management business through listed Brookfield Asset Management Ltd, ticker BAM. Contemporary 2023 investor materials show BN retaining 75% of the underlying manager. The residual ownership is not described as 73% here: that is not the structure established by the cited pre-cutoff evidence. Buying BN and buying BAM are different exposures.

Asset management earns recurring fees on third-party capital and performance-related carried interest. Fee-bearing capital was $457 billion at December 2023, up 9%. Realizations determine when some performance economics become cash; accumulated unrealized carry is not immediately distributable cash.

BN's annual asset-management DE was $2,554 million, insurance operating earnings $740 million and operating-business distributions $1,462 million. Corporate costs and other items were $(533) million, leaving $4,223 million DE before realizations. Realized carry and disposition gains increased total DE to $4,806 million.

Insurance invests policyholder-funded capital and earns a spread after liabilities, expenses and underwriting effects. Assets were approximately $60 billion; management reported a 5.5% average investment yield, about two percentage points above average cost of capital. This spread is not risk-free and can change with credit losses, asset/liability mismatch or policyholder behavior.

Bruce Flatt is CEO; Nick Goodman is president. Management's credibility rests on a long operating and capital-allocation record, but complicated affiliated transactions and valuation judgments require scrutiny. Annual diluted average shares and equivalents were approximately 1,588.2 million.

The moat argument combines access to institutional capital, operating expertise in real assets and the ability to execute large transactions. The difficulty is determining which earnings are recurring, how much capital each stream requires and what portion actually belongs to BN shareholders.
