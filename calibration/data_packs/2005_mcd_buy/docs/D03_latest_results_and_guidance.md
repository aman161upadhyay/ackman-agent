Title: Strong US execution, weaker overseas markets and continued sales momentum
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63908/000119312505145941/dex991.htm; http://www.marketwatch.com/story/mcdonalds-earns-decline-revenue-rises; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63908/000006390805000058/exhibit99.htm
Date: 2005-09-09

Q2 revenue increased8%, approximately5% in constant currencies, toUS$5.1 billion. Global comparable sales rose2.8% and operating income rose5%, approximately3% in constant currencies.

Net income wasUS$530.4 million, US$0.42 diluted EPS, versusUS$590.7 million andUS$0.47 a year earlier. The headline profit decline includesUS$0.09 per share incremental tax expense from repatriating approximatelyUS$3.2 billion foreign earnings under the Homeland Investment Act.

MarketWatch's July21 report gives analyst expectations ofUS$0.42 EPS andUS$5.03 billion revenue. The result is therefore not an ordinary operating earnings miss. First-half figures also include a separate tax-audit settlement benefit, so an adjusted earnings series must remove favorable as well as unfavorable items consistently.

Management credits US menu variety, everyday value, extended convenience and execution. Europe remains uneven, with challenges in several major markets. Australia is strong, while China and Japan were weak in the quarter. A global recovery should not be assumed from the US result alone.

The September9 release reports August global comparable sales up3.4%, US3.2%, Europe3.6% and Asia/Pacific/Middle East/Africa4.0%. US results benefit from Premium Chicken Sandwiches, breakfast and extended hours while facing a tough comparison with the prior-year Chicken Selects launch.

Europe's improvement reflects France and Germany, partly offset by continued UK weakness. The differing markets reinforce the need for local customer execution rather than a single global financial-engineering solution.

The company says US comparable sales have been positive for29 consecutive months. That record is evidence of an operating turnaround already underway, not proof that the existing management must be replaced.

The period largely predates Hurricane Katrina's full effect. Subsequent closures, reconstruction, labor/fuel pressures and customer disruption remain uncertain. The pack contains no future September sales release or Q3 earnings.

The publicly stated longer-term objectives are3–5% annual sales growth and6–7% operating-income growth, with high-teens incremental returns. These are strategic goals, not a precise current-year EPS guarantee. A sound investment case separates those organic goals, cash distributions and any possible restructuring upside.
