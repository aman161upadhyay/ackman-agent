Title: A global restaurant system with two very different earnings streams
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63908/000119312505042609/d10k.htm; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63908/000119312505158749/d10q.htm
Date: 2005-08-04

McDonald's operates a global quick-service restaurant system through company-operated, franchised and affiliated restaurants. At year-end2004 it reports31,561 systemwide restaurants. The business combines a familiar consumer brand, consistent service/value, purchasing scale, menu development and a large physical network.

The company earns restaurant sales from directly operated locations and rent/royalty-type revenue from franchise relationships. Franchisees bear much of the restaurant labor, food and operating investment. McDonald's may own or lease the underlying property and collect rent linked to contractual terms and sales.

Reported2004 company-operated sales wereUS$14.224 billion; franchised/affiliated revenue wasUS$4.841 billion. Franchisees' and affiliates' total restaurant sales are not consolidated revenue. Their system sales nevertheless influence royalty/rent receipts and brand economics.

Company-operated restaurant margin was15.3% in2004 versus79.3% franchise margin. Q2 2005 figures are14.8% and80.2%. These measures have different denominators and cost responsibilities. It would be wrong to infer corporate stores could instantly earn80% margins or that all franchise revenue is costless.

Company and affiliates own approximately37% of restaurant land and59% of buildings at year-end2004. Historical-cost property values may understate some market values, but no independently authenticated current appraisal is supplied. Leased property, franchise agreements and operating continuity restrict a simple liquidation approach.

Management's Plan to Win emphasizes people, products, place, price and promotion. Menu variety, extended hours, convenience, restaurant modernization and better execution have helped rebuild demand. CEO Jim Skinner is overseeing an ongoing recovery rather than a business whose operations have universally failed.

The potential investment appeal is therefore not necessarily replacing a poor operator. It may involve durable brand/franchise cash flows, better capital allocation and clearer separation of restaurant versus property/franchise economics. Those opportunities must respect the integrated system and franchisees' incentives.

McDonald's also owns developing restaurant concepts including Chipotle. A minority IPO has been publicly announced as a way to highlight that business and support focus. No eventual IPO price, complete divestiture or future restructuring plan is an established fact. The key question is what existing shareholders own after all costs, taxes and operating obligations, not just the gross value of attractive components.
