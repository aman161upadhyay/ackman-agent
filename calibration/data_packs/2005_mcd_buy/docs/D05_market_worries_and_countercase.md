Title: Management resists property restructuring as the operating recovery continues
Source: compiled from: https://www.foxnews.com/story/mcdonalds-limited-impact-from-gas-prices; https://www.foxnews.com/story/mcdonalds-august-sales-up-3-4-percent; https://www.fool.com/investing/general/2005/09/23/digesting-mcdonalds-presentations.aspx
Date: 2005-09-23

The September analyst meeting produces an immediate test of the property-value narrative. Reuters's September22 account says shares had fallen3.7% the prior day after CFO Matthew Paull said the company would not pursue a new structure for its vast real-estate assets. Options activity had increased on transaction hopes.

Management's concern is the relationship with franchisees and the integrated operating system. A transaction that creates value for one security while damaging franchisee economics, credit quality or restaurant investment may fail to create durable value for the system.

The counterargument is that existing ownership may obscure the economic contribution of high-margin rent/royalty receipts versus lower-margin corporate restaurants. Clearer reporting and capital allocation could reveal value without a wholesale breakup. The existence of a possible valuation gap does not establish the best legal structure.

Operating risks remain meaningful. US comparable-sales growth has slowed against strong prior-year comparisons. Europe is mixed and the UK faces a poor perception of the food; China and Japan have been weak. The global brand is durable, not immune to shifts in customer preferences or execution errors.

Fuel prices and hurricane disruption threaten discretionary budgets and distribution costs. North American head Ralph Alvarez tells investors higher gasoline prices have had limited sales impact, emphasizing convenience and value. This is management's current assessment, not proof that persistent inflation cannot hurt margins or demand.

Reuters's September9 report says August sales were near the low end of analyst expectations for3–6% growth. Banc of America analyst Andrew Barish nevertheless called the result impressive against the difficult comparison. A slowing growth rate can coexist with a strong two-year sales stack.

The September23 Motley Fool commentary favors the cash-generative business and announced Chipotle IPO, while noting disappointment over real estate. It is a disclosed shareholder's opinion, not a completed transaction valuation.

A constructive case should credit existing management's recovery and identify incremental capital/organizational improvements. A hostile “broken operator” narrative would contradict much of the public evidence. The principal uncertainty is whether additional value can be unlocked on acceptable terms without undermining the franchise network that generates the earnings.
