Title: Wendy's and Tim Hortons show the opportunity and limits of separation
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/105668/000129993305003828/exhibit1.htm; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/105668/000095015205006300/l15276aexv99.htm; https://www.fool.com/investing/general/2005/09/23/digesting-mcdonalds-presentations.aspx; https://foodservice411.com/fesmag/flashnews/2005-09-12/news-7.asp
Date: 2005-09-23

Wendy's July29 strategic announcement proposes selling15–18% of Tim Hortons through an IPO, authorizes anotherUS$1 billion stock repurchase, increases its annual dividend25% toUS$0.68 and plansUS$100 million debt repayment. It expects to retain82–85% of Tim Hortons initially.

This is an announced plan, not a completed spin-off at this cutoff. Wendy's says its board began a strategic review in2004. Public commentary credits activist pressure, but exclusive causal attribution to one investor would exceed the company's own account.

Its July28 results show why segment separation can matter. Q2 revenue isUS$951 million and EPSUS$0.61. Wendy's US company comparable sales decline4.6%, while Tim Hortons Canada rises5.6% and US9.1%. A strong growth business can be obscured by a weaker consolidated brand.

McDonald's differs: its flagship US business is improving, not suffering the same contemporaneous sales decline. Separating an emerging concept like Chipotle is more directly analogous to the announced Tim Hortons minority IPO than restructuring the property economics of an integrated global franchise.

The September23 McDonald's commentary cites trailing P/Es of24.9x Sonic,25.9x Red Robin and30.6x California Pizza Kitchen versus17.1x McDonald's and19.8x S&P500. These are dated third-party comparisons, not fully standardized franchise-mix or growth-adjusted valuations.

Higher multiples for smaller restaurant concepts do not establish that every McDonald's restaurant or property asset deserves the same valuation. Growth runway, company ownership, rent obligations, capital intensity and risk differ.

Industry monthly sales also show dispersion. A September12 roundup reports Yum domestic comparable sales up2% for the period endedSeptember3, with Taco Bell up6%, KFC up4% and Pizza Hut down4%. Brand relevance and local execution matter even within one parent.

The useful benchmark is an analytical method: disaggregate different economic streams, value each after the appropriate costs, then deduct shared obligations and transaction friction. It is not “another chain announced a spin, therefore this stock must rise.”

No fully reconciled peer franchise/real-estate sum-of-parts model is available. The pack preserves observed market valuations and structural proposals without assigning an invented appraisal to McDonald's property or assuming management must accept a specific plan.
