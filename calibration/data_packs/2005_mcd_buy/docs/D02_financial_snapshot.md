The operating-income trajectory shows a substantial existing recovery. Capital spending has declined from the aggressive expansion period while cash generation improves. The simple cash-flow measure is not pretax unlevered FCF and must not be confused with an analyst's independently adjusted sum-of-parts cash flow.

Reported2004 results include US$130 million asset/goodwill impairment andUS$160 million lease-accounting correction, partly offset by aUS$49 million nonoperating real-estate-partnership gain. The combined disclosed after-tax effect wasUS$172 million, US$0.13 per share. Not every accounting adjustment is a sustainable expense saving.

At December2004 debt was approximatelyUS$9.220 billion and cashUS$1.380 billion. At June2005 cash wasUS$1.5606 billion, current debt maturitiesUS$793.2 million and long-term debtUS$7.7637 billion. Total debt isUS$8.5569 billion and net debtUS$6.9963 billion, calculated.

June30 common shares outstanding are1,250,048,706. First-half2005 CFO isUS$1.7455 billion and capexUS$602.5 million. These figures demonstrate cash generation but should not be doubled mechanically without considering seasonality and annual investment plans.

The September analyst meeting reiterates high-teens return on incremental invested capital as an objective, not an audited standardized historical ROIC. A matched current cost-of-capital/maintenance-capex analysis remains missing.

The financial question is how much stable cash can be returned while supporting restaurant modernization, brand investment, property obligations and franchisee health. A transaction that increases distributions through leverage must be assessed separately from an operational improvement that raises sustainable cash flow.
