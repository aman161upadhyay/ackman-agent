A capital-structure proposal would need to value company-operated restaurants separately from rent/royalty economics. Corporate stores' accounting margins do not include precisely the same contractual rent/franchise burden borne by independent franchisees. A fair comparison must normalize those charges before claiming underperformance.

Likewise, owned property cannot simply be added to the full value of the earnings it supports. A sale/leaseback produces proceeds but introduces rent, taxes and transaction costs; a property transfer can change franchisee and creditor risk.

No independent property appraisal, segment market-rent study, current forward-consensus EPS or authenticated pre-cutoff activist target is available. The case for upside is plausibly stronger cash distributions and clearer economics, but its magnitude cannot be verified by substituting a future presentation.

At this price, underlying operational momentum already has value. The investor must distinguish a reasonably priced compounding franchise from a transaction-dependent thesis with leveraged timing risk.
