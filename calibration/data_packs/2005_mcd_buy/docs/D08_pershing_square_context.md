Title: Young activist manager, public rumors and limits of available exposure data
Source: compiled from: https://www.theledger.com/story/news/2005/09/17/briefing/26173805007/; https://www.fool.com/investing/general/2005/09/19/is-mcdonalds-restructuring.aspx; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/105668/000129993305003828/exhibit1.htm
Date: 2005-09-19

Pershing Square is a young investment manager with a visible activist role in Wendy's. September19 public commentary says it held9.9% of Wendy's and connects its pressure to the company's announced capital and structural initiatives. This is issuer ownership, not a verified percentage of Pershing NAV.

Wendy's own July29 announcement describes board/management review beginning in2004 and a package including the Tim Hortons minority IPO, repurchases, dividend and debt repayment. This supplies a real public precedent for constructive asset-value advocacy while limiting claims that one shareholder alone created every action.

September reports circulate a possible4.9% McDonald's stake. The Ledger says neither McDonald's nor Pershing confirms or denies the rumor. ItsUS$2 billion hypothetical purchase cost assumes common shares at market prices; it does not authenticate execution, derivatives, cash committed or a NAV allocation.

The rumor is already public information at the snapshot and is presented only as rumor. It is not converted into the answer or supplemented with a later detailed proposal. A model should still judge whether the operating and valuation evidence supports a new investment independently.

No complete authenticated pre-cutoff Pershing13F, exact current firm AUM, NAV, unrestricted cash or full derivatives exposure is recovered in this pack. Later filings cannot be silently substituted. Consequently there is no defensible numerical claim about how much of the firm is available for a new McDonald's position.

The potential toolkit includes private engagement, public advocacy, capital-allocation proposals and negotiation with management/shareholders. Ordinary majority control is not established. The company's sensitivity to franchisee relationships is a visible constraint on what an activist can accomplish.

Any use of options would introduce expiry, financing and path risk distinct from ownership of a durable franchise. No particular instrument or notional stake is assumed from the public rumor. The business may compound while a short-dated instrument fails.

The best contemporaneous lesson from the peer situation is to separate businesses with different economics and seek value-enhancing changes that stakeholders can accept. It does not justify assuming a hostile CEO campaign or a specific property transaction before an actual public plan exists.
