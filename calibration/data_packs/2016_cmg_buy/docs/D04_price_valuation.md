Title: Valuing a depressed restaurant base without assuming recovery

Date: 2016-09-06

Historical September 6 daily data give split-adjusted open $8.16, close $8.28 and volume 54,380,000. The database applies a 50-for-one adjustment. Multiplying the rounded close by 50 gives approximately $414 in contemporaneous pre-split units; dividing volume by 50 gives approximately 1,087,600 original shares.

The converted price is approximate because the database rounds its adjusted price to cents. It must not be represented as an exact $414.00 original close, an investor cost basis or a price target. A complete cutoff 52-week range was not recovered.

At approximately $414, FY2015 EPS of $15.10 implies 27.4x earnings; FY2014 EPS of $14.13 implies 29.3x. Those are old earnings benchmarks, not verified forward consensus. FY2015 includes late-year disruption, while FY2014 reflects a lower restaurant count; neither is a perfect normalized forecast.

Using June's approximately 29.150 million outstanding shares, the approximate $414 reference implies $12.068 billion equity value. Subtracting reported cash/investments of $646.508 million gives $11.422 billion before leases and other enterprise adjustments. These are approximate calculations using a stale share count, not exact cutoff capitalization.

The current earnings run rate is dramatically lower. Annualizing Q2 EPS of $0.87 would give $3.48, but seasonality, promotional activity and evolving customer behavior make that an unreliable forward forecast. The contrast explains why this situation cannot be evaluated with one unqualified P/E.

A normalized model should state the mature-store sales recovery assumption, restaurant margin, new-unit productivity, corporate expenses, safety costs and buyback funding. Restoring old sales with permanently higher supplier/safety costs may yield less profit than the old margin. Partial volume recovery can still materially improve contribution because of fixed operating costs.

A company-specific historical multiple range, S&P forward P/E, consensus EPS growth and forward peer valuations were not independently recovered. They are not represented by the hypothetical sensitivity.

The valuation challenge is whether recoverable earning power provides enough return at the observed executable price, once obtained. Historical excellence supports a scenario worth testing; it does not justify treating the recovery as certain or the old peak multiple as intrinsic value.
