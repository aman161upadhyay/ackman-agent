Title: July results — recovery initiatives but deeply negative comparable sales
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1058090/000105809016000078/cmg-20160630xex99_1.htm
Date: 2016-07-21

Q2 revenue fell 16.6% to $998.4 million. Comparable restaurant sales fell 23.6%, with comparable transactions down 19.3%. New units partly offset the comparable-store decline. The company opened 58 restaurants in the quarter and reported 2,124 at quarter end.

Restaurant-level operating margin was 15.5%, versus 28.0% in Q2 2015. Food costs were 34.2% of revenue, up 110 basis points, reflecting new supplier safety procedures and food waste, partly offset by prior menu-price increases. Thus not all margin pressure is simply fixed-cost deleverage; some new costs may persist.

Labor was 27.7% of revenue versus 22.6%; occupancy 7.2% versus 5.4%; other restaurant costs 15.2% versus 10.9%. General/administrative expense was $70.756 million versus $70.212 million, essentially flat in dollars but materially higher as a percentage of sales. This distinguishes an impaired revenue denominator from uncontrolled absolute overhead.

Ells says: “We returned to profitability, and saw a modest improvement in comp sales trends”. The statement is relative to a very weak first quarter. First-half comparable sales were still down 26.5% and first-half restaurant margin only 11.6%.

Management highlights Chiptopia, the frequency program launched in July. Ells says nearly 30% of transactions are engaged in the program and early July comparable-sales trends improved 200-300 basis points. These are early observations, not proof that promotional visitors will return at full price after rewards end.

Moran emphasizes restoring trust through safe food, service and strong restaurant teams. Those objectives connect safety execution and the ordinary customer experience; marketing cannot independently cure a renewed operational failure.

The release forecasts 220-235 new restaurant openings for 2016 and an approximately 38.6% effective tax rate. It supplies no reliable full-year EPS or comparable-sales recovery schedule. A valuation based on normalized profit must therefore be an analyst scenario, not management guidance.

The evidence supports a distinction between initial stabilization and full recovery. The company is no longer reporting the same quarterly loss as the first quarter, yet traffic and margins remain far below pre-crisis levels. The next questions are repeat visits, safety credibility, promotional cost and whether incremental transactions can restore contribution without rebuilding the concept.
