Title: Disinflation, continued growth and restrictive policy
Source: compiled from: https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_07122023.pdf; https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/files/docs/publications/bea/newsreleases/bea_newsreleases_23-34_20230728.pdf; https://www.bea.gov/sites/default/files/2023-07/gdp2q23_adv.pdf; https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20230726a.htm
Date: 2023-07-28

The July 12 CPI release reports June headline inflation of 3.0% year over year and core inflation of 4.8%. Monthly headline and core increases were both 0.2%. Inflation had moderated, but the core rate remained above the Federal Reserve's objective.

The July 28 personal-income/outlays release reports June PCE inflation of 3.0% year over year and core PCE inflation of 4.1%. CPI and PCE use different coverage and weights; they are not interchangeable series.

These observations support disinflation and continued underlying price pressure at the same time. Neither a permanent return to 2% nor a permanently higher inflation regime follows mechanically from one month's data.

The July 27 advance GDP release reports real growth of 2.4% annualized in Q2, following 2.0% in Q1 on that release's vintage. These are the estimates then available, not later revised figures.

On July 26, the FOMC raised the federal-funds target range to 5.25%-5.50%. It said inflation remained elevated and that it would assess additional information, cumulative tightening and policy lags in determining the appropriate stance.

The short policy rate and the 30-year yield answer different questions. The latter reflects expected future short rates, inflation, real rates and term/risk compensation over a much longer horizon. A high current fed-funds rate does not prove that long yields must rise.

The long-rate risk case is that inflation settles above the market's implicit path, fiscal demand remains strong or term premium rises. The bond-supportive case is that disinflation persists and restrictive policy eventually slows growth.

No July CPI release published in August, later payroll revisions, October yield peak or geopolitical event is included. The information set ends with August 2 market/supply information in other documents.

The hedge question is therefore probabilistic and price-dependent. The economy has not shown the immediate contraction feared by some investors, yet monetary policy is restrictive and lagged effects remain uncertain. A defensible position must relate this range of outcomes to long-duration portfolio risk and actual option terms rather than simply extrapolate the latest inflation print.
