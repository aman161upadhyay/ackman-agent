Title: Borrowing requirements, refunding and a sovereign downgrade
Source: compiled from: https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy1662; https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy1671; https://www.reuters.com/markets/us/fitch-cuts-us-governments-aaa-credit-rating-by-one-notch-2023-08-01
Date: 2023-08-02

On July 31, Treasury projected $1.007 trillion privately held net marketable borrowing in July-September, assuming a $650 billion end-September cash balance. The estimate was $274 billion above the May projection.

Treasury attributed the increase primarily to a lower beginning cash balance, a higher assumed ending cash balance and fiscal-flow changes. A cash-balance rebuild is not the same as a permanent increase in annual deficits, and those components should not be conflated.

The same announcement projected $852 billion borrowing for October-December with a $750 billion ending cash balance. These are financing estimates, not completed issuance or a forecast of which maturity investors will absorb at a given yield.

The August 2 refunding statement offers $103 billion securities to refinance approximately $84 billion maturing August 15, raising about $19 billion new cash. The securities are $42 billion three-year notes, $38 billion ten-year notes and $23 billion thirty-year bonds.

The distinction between gross issuance, refunding and net new cash is important. Calling all $103 billion incremental deficit financing would overstate the immediate new funding requirement.

Greater duration supply can require higher yields if demand does not rise comparably. However, auction composition, cash-management choices, private demand, regulatory demand and broader macro conditions affect the outcome. Supply alone does not determine a fair 30-year yield.

Fitch downgraded the United States from AAA to AA+ on August 1. Reuters reports concerns about fiscal deterioration and repeated debt-ceiling confrontations; Treasury criticized the decision as arbitrary.

The downgrade provides a fiscal/governance risk signal. It does not establish imminent default, a particular change in institutional mandates or a quantified forced-sale flow. No such mechanism is asserted without evidence.

For a duration hedge, the supply/downgrade backdrop can strengthen a term-premium risk scenario. The countercase is that weaker growth, disinflation or safe-haven demand more than offsets new supply.

All facts in this document were public by August 2. Later auction outcomes, yield peaks and geopolitical shocks are excluded, leaving the genuine underwriting question of whether these financing pressures are inadequately reflected in current long-rate pricing.
