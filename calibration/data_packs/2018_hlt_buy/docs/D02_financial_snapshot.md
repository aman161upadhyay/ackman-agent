Amounts are millions except EPS. Hilton's February release gives effect to January 2017 separations; it also includes pro-forma presentations for 2016. The July release retrospectively applies new revenue and pension accounting to its comparative periods. These are not interchangeable historical bases.

FY2017 adjusted EBITDA grew 11% on the February pro-forma comparison. FY2017 EPS includes the provisional impact of tax reform and is not a clean forward earnings base. A comparable 2015 post-spin series was not recovered; mixing old consolidated Hilton into this table would create false precision.

On the July accounting basis, H1 2017 revenue was $3,972 million, operating income $541 million, net income $199 million and adjusted EBITDA $914 million. H1 2018 figures were $4,365 million, $685 million, $380 million and $1,000 million respectively.

Q2 2018 total revenue was $2,291 million and operating income $406 million. Total revenue includes $1,330 million of other revenues from managed/franchised properties, with $1,327 million related expenses. Their near-pass-through nature makes consolidated revenue margin a poor measure of the underlying fee business.

June long-term debt was $7.7 billion before financing costs/discount. Excluding capital leases and consolidated variable-interest-entity debt, the figure was $7.4 billion. Cash including restricted balances was $505 million, of which $82 million was restricted.

Management guides 2018 adjusted EBITDA to $2.070-$2.100 billion, adjusted EPS $2.64-$2.71, capital expenditures/contract acquisition costs excluding owner reimbursements $175-$200 million, and cash available for capital return $1.8-$1.9 billion.

That last measure is a company-defined capital-return projection, not a verified conventional CFO-minus-capex figure. A complete historical CFO/capex/FCF/ROIC series was not recovered. The available data support capital-light economics but do not justify labeling all fee revenue as free cash flow or ignoring debt-funded repurchases.
