Title: Portfolio context before any public Hilton announcement
Source: compiled from: https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/media/2014/09/02164905/Performance-Report-June-2018-PSH.pdf; https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2022/10/07155009/2018-Interim-Financial-Statement-Letter-Only.pdf
Date: 2018-08-09

The July 2 performance report gives PSH June 30 AUM of $4,114.9 million. This is the listed vehicle, not total manager-wide AUM and not wholly undeployed cash. Reported net portfolio exposure was 113%, with no short exposure in the table.

The August 9 interim letter describes a portfolio of long-term stakes in “large capitalization, simple, predictable, free-cash-flow-generative, high-quality, dominant companies with catalysts for shareholder value creation.” It says the manager often acts as a catalyst through influential ownership and sometimes board representation.

The letter discusses newly disclosed investments in United Technologies and Lowe's. United Technologies offers separable aerospace, elevator and climate/control businesses with materially different capital needs and investor constituencies. Lowe's is presented as an operating-gap opportunity relative to Home Depot, with incoming CEO Marvin Ellison.

Other public discussions include ADP, Chipotle and the government-sponsored mortgage enterprises. First-half gross performance contributions included Chipotle +5.6 percentage points and ADP +5.1; Herbalife detracted 3.3 and Fannie/Freddie 3.3 combined. These are performance attributions, not position weights.

PSH returned 6.7% net through June and 12.7% through August 7. Its May $300 million own-share tender and subsequent insider purchases reflect capital allocation at the fund level. Those amounts should not be confused with cash available to buy a new operating-company stake.

The same interim letter describes Chipotle's early operational and digital improvement under Brian Niccol, illustrating that some portfolio companies require active business repair. A hotel fee platform with an established operating team could present a different kind of opportunity: continued execution and capital returns rather than replacing a broken core model.

Name-by-name current NAV weights, precise October fund cash and manager-wide AUM were not recovered in this pack. The analyst should avoid using stale attribution as a substitute for portfolio capacity.

No October 25 public announcement, later annual letter or later investor cost/return chart is supplied in the model-facing documents. Even though the assigned cutoff falls after a retrospectively identified first purchase, the pack is framed as an independent new-investment evaluation. The question is whether the platform, runway and price justify concentrated ownership on information already public.
