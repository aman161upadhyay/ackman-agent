Title: Hilton and Marriott operating benchmarks

Date: 2018-10-11

Marriott's August 6 second-quarter release provides a contemporaneous fee-platform comparison, rather than a hotel-property REIT. Its reported net income was $610 million, up 25% from $489 million, and adjusted net income $619 million, up 46%. Adjusted EBITDA was $939 million, up 15%.

Hilton's July 25 Q2 release reports $555 million adjusted EBITDA, up 10%, and $217 million net income, up 44%. Adjusted diluted EPS grew 37% to $0.70; system-wide comparable RevPAR grew 4.0%.

The EBITDA figures are company-defined non-GAAP measures. Different brand mix, geography, accounting, integration costs and capital structures can materially affect comparability. Higher absolute EBITDA does not by itself establish superior unit economics or a cheaper valuation.

Hilton's fee lines grew 11% in the quarter and it guided approximately 6.5% annual net-unit growth. This illustrates the separate contribution of expanding the affiliated room base alongside same-property performance. The available peer extracts do not provide a sufficiently complete identical-basis fee/margin table to rank competitive moats numerically.

The October weekly STR data are a demand indicator, not directly comparable quarterly corporate results: US RevPAR rose 1.5% while occupancy fell 0.9%. They show that a rate-led revenue increase can coexist with weaker occupancy, and hurricane comparisons can distort regional year-over-year statistics.

Timestamped valuation ratios and complete financial tables for Hyatt and Wyndham were not recovered. No two-to-four-peer forward-multiple ranking is fabricated. The concrete comparison is to Marriott and to observed industry demand; full relative valuation remains a known gap.

When comparing these businesses, isolate management/franchise fees, pass-through revenue, owned/leased hotel exposure, owner-funded capex and debt. A property company and a branded distribution/loyalty system may share a travel cycle while having very different reinvestment needs and incremental returns on additional rooms.
