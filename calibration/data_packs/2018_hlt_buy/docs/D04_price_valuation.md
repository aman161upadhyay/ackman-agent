Title: Price evidence and the correct earnings denominator
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1585689/000158568918000120/a2018-q2earningsrelease.htm; https://www.hotelnewsresource.com/article102020.html; https://fiscal.ai/company/NYSE_HLT/
Date: 2018-10-15

Historical daily data show an October 15 open of $73.34, close of $72.99 and volume of 2,994,000 shares. Only the historical row is used from the modern database. A verified cutoff 52-week range was not recovered.

The primary July release provides an authentic transaction benchmark: Hilton bought 18.5 million shares during Q2 at an average $72.01 per share. This includes large blocks connected with HNA's and Blackstone's exits and is not necessarily representative of a freely executable October price.

Using the cutoff close against the July adjusted FY2018 EPS guidance midpoint of $2.675 gives 27.3x. This uses the company's fiscal-year guidance, not next-year consensus. GAAP EPS guidance midpoint of $2.645 would imply 27.6x. The earlier repurchase benchmark would imply 26.9x on adjusted guidance.

The result shows why a broad claim of “about 20x forward earnings” requires a specified future earnings year and a documented forecast. No independently verified 2019 consensus EPS or forward multiple was recovered by the cutoff. The pack does not equate a later entry presentation's forward P/E with the company's contemporaneous 2018 guidance.

June cash including restricted funds was $505 million versus long-term debt of $7.7 billion. A simple debt-minus-cash measure is approximately $7.2 billion before distinguishing restricted cash, leases and variable-interest entities. These obligations matter even though the franchise business requires relatively little property capital.

A standard enterprise-value calculation also needs current outstanding shares. Q2 weighted diluted shares of 303 million are an earnings denominator, not a verified October spot count. Exact market capitalization and EV are therefore unavailable, not guessed, even with the historical closing quote now established.

The October 9 Baird/STR report says the hotel stock index rose 1.5% in September and was up 1.1% year to date; the brand sub-index rose 3.4% in September. This supports a mixed sector-sentiment account rather than a blanket claim that all hotel stocks had collapsed.

A valuation should bridge fee growth, unit expansion, capital requirements, leverage and cash returns. Historical GAAP EPS distorted by tax reform, pass-through revenue and property-REIT multiples are all weak shortcuts. The economic attraction can be real without establishing that any particular price meets the investor's return hurdle.
