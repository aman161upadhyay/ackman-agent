Title: Q4 results, Q1 guidance and the $100 billion investment plan
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872425000002/amzn-20241231xex991.htm; https://www.cnbc.com/2025/02/06/amazon-expects-to-spend-100-billion-on-capital-expenditures-in-2025.html; https://www.straitstimes.com/business/amazon-echoing-microsoft-says-it-cant-keep-up-with-ai-demand
Date: 2025-02-07

The latest results were released February 6, covering Q4 and full-year 2024. Quarterly revenue increased 10% to $187.8 billion. AWS revenue increased 19% to $28.8 billion, with operating income $10.6 billion. North America operating income increased to $9.3 billion and International generated $1.3 billion.

For Q1 2025, Amazon forecast net sales of $151.0 billion–$155.5 billion and operating income of $14.0 billion–$18.0 billion. These are the published ranges available on April 7, not forecasts incorporating every tariff announced since February. Investors must therefore consider whether the original guidance remains a useful baseline under a new trade regime.

The earnings discussion placed capital spending at the center of valuation. CNBC reported February 6 that Amazon intended approximately $100 billion of 2025 capex, with most directed toward AWS and AI. Jassy stated: “We spent $26.3 billion in capex in Q4, and I think that is reasonably representative of what you expect an annualized capex rate to be in 2025.”

Bloomberg's report, reproduced by The Straits Times February 7, quotes Jassy: “It is true we could be growing faster were it not for some of the constraints on capacity.” Constraints included chips and power availability. That explanation differs from an assertion that customers lack demand, but it does not by itself establish that all new capacity will earn attractive returns.

The bear interpretation is that near-term cash generation will be consumed by an expensive capacity race, while AWS growth is not accelerating enough to justify it. The constructive interpretation is that supply bottlenecks are restricting a valuable recurring business and that investment is needed to fulfill demand.

Retail productivity and advertising provide a second earnings engine independent of the exact timing of AI monetization. Nevertheless, retail remains exposed to consumer purchasing power and imported merchandise costs. The release supplies no quantified forecast for the April 2 tariff regime. No subsequent earnings call or tariff-related management revision is imported into this information set.
