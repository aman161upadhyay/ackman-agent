FCF improved much less than operating earnings in 2024 because investment accelerated. Gross cash purchases of property and equipment were approximately $83.0 billion; the company's FCF reconciliation subtracts purchases net of proceeds from sales and incentives, approximately $77.7 billion. The two capex measures should not be mixed. Lease-related investment creates additional differences from a simple cash-purchases measure.

Q4 revenue was $187.8 billion, up 10%; operating income was $21.2 billion compared with $13.2 billion. Quarterly diluted EPS was $1.86. The full-year improvement reflects retail operating efficiency as well as AWS earnings. It is not solely an investment-mark gain, though nonoperating items can affect net income.

At December 31, cash, equivalents and marketable securities totaled approximately $101.2 billion. Long-term debt was approximately $52.6 billion, excluding lease liabilities and current maturities. Calling the resulting difference “net cash” without discussing leases understates the infrastructure and fulfillment obligations.

No dividend or material completed 2024 common-share buyback is used in this snapshot. A consistent reported ROIC series is not recovered.

The central analytical issue is how much investment maintains existing earnings versus expands future capacity. The filings do not disclose a precise maintenance-capex amount. Neither assigning all capex to growth nor subtracting an invented maintenance figure is justified by the published accounts.
