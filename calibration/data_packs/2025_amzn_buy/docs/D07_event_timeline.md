Date: 2025-04-07

2024: annual revenue increased 11% and operating income rose from $36.852 billion to $68.593 billion, as disclosed February 6, 2025. Retail margin improvement and cloud profit both contributed. The spending debate therefore concerns reinvestment of an already profitable operation, not a company without operating earnings.

January 29, 2025: Microsoft's December-quarter results reported Azure growth of 31%. This supplies a contemporaneous competing cloud-growth benchmark and reinforces investor focus on AI-related infrastructure investment across the sector.

February 6, 2025: Amazon released Q4 results: $187.8 billion revenue, $21.2 billion operating income and 19% AWS growth. Q1 sales guidance was $151.0 billion–$155.5 billion. Management discussed approximately $100 billion of 2025 capex, primarily associated with AWS and AI. The combination creates a valuation tension between current cash outlay and future earnings capacity.

February 7, 2025: press coverage of the earnings call emphasized capacity constraints involving chips and power. Jassy attributed some unmet growth opportunity to those constraints. That is an explanation from management, not independent verification of every proposed project's economic return.

April 2, 2025: the US tariff announcement changed the macroeconomic setting for imported merchandise and global trade. The February earnings guidance predates this event. The exact net impact on Amazon is not quantified in the available company release.

April 3–4, 2025: historical closes fell to $178.41 and $171.00, with heavy trading volume. Price volatility reflects an abrupt change in risk appetite, but does not isolate the portion attributable to Amazon-specific earnings.

April 6, 2025: Ackman publicly urged a negotiating pause, warning of damage to confidence from broad, rapid tariff increases. His post addresses the economy generally rather than announcing a company investment.

April 7, 2025: Amazon opened at $162.00 and closed at $175.26 on 109,327,115 shares. This is the decision cutoff. No later tariff pause, seller interview, earnings report or share-price observation is included.
