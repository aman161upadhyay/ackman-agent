Title: Retail logistics, third-party services and cloud infrastructure
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872425000002/amzn-20241231xex991.htm
Date: 2025-02-06

Amazon combines a global retail marketplace with a large cloud-computing business. It reports North America, International and Amazon Web Services, rather than separating advertising and seller services into independent operating segments. Retail revenue includes owned inventory, while marketplace economics include fees and services supplied to third-party sellers. Those business models have different inventory and tariff exposures.

In Q4 2024, North America sales were $115.6 billion and operating income $9.3 billion. International sales were $43.4 billion and operating income $1.3 billion. AWS generated $28.8 billion sales and $10.6 billion operating income. Calculated margins are approximately 8.0%, 3.0% and 36.8%, respectively. AWS therefore contributes roughly half of quarterly operating profit despite a much smaller share of sales.

Retail advantages include broad selection, Prime membership, delivery speed and a fulfillment network whose geographic density can reduce cost per package. Third-party sellers expand selection without Amazon financing every unit of inventory, but Amazon still supplies services and bears reputational risk for the shopping experience. Advertising monetizes purchasing intent within that same consumer traffic.

AWS supplies computing, storage, databases and other technology services. Its customer economics depend on workload availability, switching effort, security and cost, not simply on the number of servers installed. Large initial infrastructure investments can support recurring usage revenue, but require confidence that utilization and pricing will justify depreciation and power expense.

Andy Jassy is CEO. He emphasizes innovation and long-term capacity investment alongside retail productivity. Founder Jeff Bezos remains associated with governance as executive chair. Q4 diluted weighted-average shares were 10,771 million; this is a reporting-period average, not the precise April 7 share count.

The business should not be valued as either a traditional low-margin retailer alone or a pure software subscription company. A sum-of-parts approach can separate AWS, advertising and retail economics, but must avoid counting advertising profit twice within reported segment earnings. The tariff question is most direct for merchandise, while cloud demand and infrastructure returns require separate analysis.
