The latest recovered firm-wide AUM figure is September 30, 2024: $18,498.8 million, including PSVII and bond proceeds. This is not an April 2025 estimate. PSH's March 14 annual report reports December 31, 2024 net assets of $13.01 billion. Year-end cash and equivalents were approximately $436.5 million, versus $1.929 billion a year earlier; other liquid instruments and financing mean this is not the entire firm's buying capacity.

The December-quarter 13F, publicly discussed February 14, shows Brookfield at 15.89% of reported holdings, Restaurant Brands 11.88% and Chipotle 11.78%. These are 13F weights, not PSH NAV allocations. The portfolio also includes Hilton, Alphabet, Nike, Howard Hughes, Canadian Pacific Kansas City and Seaport Entertainment. Universal Music and OTC Fannie/Freddie are omitted from ordinary US listed-share tables.

The annual report explains trimming Hilton after strong share-price performance and a higher multiple, while retaining the business. That illustrates opportunity-cost discipline: liking a company need not imply accepting every price. A complete current cash balance, April portfolio weights and all recent exits are not recovered.

Publicly available materials by March identify Uber as another core investment. That context demonstrates willingness to own a large technology-enabled platform; it supplies no Amazon-specific transaction instruction. No verified Amazon-specific Ackman recommendation dated before April 7 is included.

On April 6, Ackman wrote that business confidence depends on trust and warned of an “economic nuclear winter” if trade conflict escalated without a pause. He favored negotiation rather than a conclusion that every tariff-affected company is impaired permanently.

The manager's stated framework favors concentrated ownership of high-quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, capable management and attractive prices. For this candidate, the unresolved issues are whether tariffs impair long-term franchise value, how much AWS spending is economically productive growth investment, and whether retail productivity can support earnings compounding. The public macro warning makes those questions more urgent; it does not answer them.
