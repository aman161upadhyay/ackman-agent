Title: What could make portfolio protection lose money or fail to protect
Source: compiled from: https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/coronaviruse/situation-reports/20200224-sitrep-35-covid-19.pdf; https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt1806b.htm; https://www.nytimes.com/2020/02/24/business/stock-market-coronavirus.html
Date: 2020-02-24

A protection buyer can be broadly correct about a serious disease and still make a poor investment. Timing, cost, tenor and payoff basis determine whether the instrument helps the portfolio.

Containment is a genuine alternative. WHO's high global risk assessment is not a deterministic forecast of a worldwide shutdown. Public-health intervention, changed behavior or limited onward transmission could reduce the economic tail without generating a large credit loss.

Credit spreads can tighten or remain stable while premiums accrue. Mark-to-market losses and coupon drag may occur before a severe scenario arrives, or the protection may expire before the relevant shock. An affordable monthly budget matters more than an impressive gross notional.

The equity/credit basis can also move unfavorably. Portfolio companies may face lower earnings and valuation multiples even if investment-grade borrowers remain solvent and credit markets stay liquid. Conversely, a credit-index spike may not match the size or duration of the portfolio drawdown.

Government or central-bank support could change credit pricing without immediately repairing underlying operating profits. A policy response can therefore reduce the hedge's prospective payoff while the manager still has weak long-equity marks.

Liquidity is another risk. A quoted theoretical value is not necessarily a bid at which a large position can be closed. Bid/ask spreads, collateral, counterparty arrangements and operational capacity affect monetization.

Falsifiers and decision checkpoints should be defined in advance: evidence of contained international transmission; restored mobility and activity; spreads already compensating for the severe scenario; excessive cumulative carry; or a portfolio whose own valuation has become sufficiently attractive to redeploy protection gains.

The exact thresholds cannot be calculated from the retrieved data because execution terms and a portfolio stress model are absent. They should be stated as required analysis rather than filled with invented numbers.

Nothing in this document uses later lockdowns, actual hedge gains or a later interview as evidence of foresight. The proposition is a bounded, probabilistic risk-management decision as of February 24. Conviction in the possibility of a shock is different from conviction that an unspecified derivative at an unspecified price is automatically attractive.
