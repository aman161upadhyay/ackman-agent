Title: Why a concentrated quality-equity portfolio can still need liquidity insurance
Source: compiled from: https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2020/02/03182429/Performance-Report-January-2020-PSH.pdf; https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2019/08/20165951/PSH-2019-Interim-Financial-Statements1.pdf
Date: 2020-02-03

The January 31 portfolio update, published February 3, reports PSH assets/NAV of $5,582.6 million and total strategy assets/NAV of $7,019.2 million. The accompanying financial reporting identifies these as shareholder-capital measures. They are not a derivative notional or an estimate of cash ready to deploy.

The update reports total CDS exposure of zero at January month end. This does not establish the hedge book on February 24; it only identifies the latest recovered public observation.

The 2019 interim manager discussion covers concentrated positions in consumer, business-service and financial/industrial franchises, including Chipotle, Hilton, Lowe's, Restaurant Brands, ADP and Berkshire. This is a historical portfolio context, not a guarantee that all weights or holdings remain unchanged at the cutoff.

Those businesses have different revenue models, but a broad economic interruption can affect several simultaneously. Restaurants and hotels face mobility/consumer shocks, home improvement responds to household behavior, and a diversified insurer/industrial company still has market and operating exposures.

Owning strong long-run businesses does not remove short-run mark-to-market risk or create cash when other opportunities appear. A hedge may aim to protect purchasing power and avoid selling favored holdings into a disorderly market rather than express a permanent negative view on their intrinsic value.

The protection must nevertheless fit the actual risk. A diversified corporate credit index is not a precise hedge of a handful of equities. Credit issuers, geographies, duration and default exposure may differ substantially from the portfolio's consumer/operating sensitivities.

Exact current weights, cash, borrowing constraints, position-level stress losses and the manager-wide February NAV were not recovered. Consequently no optimal dollar notional or protection percentage is derived from the January NAV.

The relevant portfolio question is asymmetric liquidity: can a limited, affordable carrying cost produce useful cash in a severe common shock without forcing sales of high-quality holdings? That objective needs an explicit budget and exit discipline. It does not mean any amount of index protection is sensible, nor that a hedge should be retained indefinitely after risk is repriced.
