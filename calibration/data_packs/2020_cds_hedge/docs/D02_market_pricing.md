Title: Equity repricing has started; exact credit execution levels remain unavailable
Source: compiled from: https://www.cnbc.com/2020/02/24/us-futures-coronavirus-outbreak.html; https://www.nytimes.com/2020/02/24/business/stock-market-coronavirus.html; https://www.cnbc.com/2020/02/24/global-stocks-roiled-as-italy-and-south-korea-sound-coronavirus-alarm.html
Date: 2020-02-24

US markets sold off sharply on February 24 as cases outside China rose. CNBC reports the S&P 500 down 3.35% to 3,225.89, Nasdaq down 3.71% to 9,221.28 and the Dow down 1,031.61 points, or 3.56%, to 27,960.80.

The same report describes VIX increasing by more than seven points, roughly 46%, to about 25. This is the article's rounded contemporaneous observation, not a claim that a VIX option could be bought at that number.

The New York Times notes that the S&P had reached a record as recently as the preceding Wednesday and calls the decline the worst single-day performance since February 2018. It describes an abrupt change from earlier investor willingness to look past China's outbreak.

International evidence also matters. CNBC reports South Korea's Kospi down 3.9% and substantial Italian market weakness as new disease clusters raised fears. The repricing therefore spans countries rather than one company-specific earnings disappointment.

The exact February 24 CDX investment-grade, high-yield and iTraxx spreads, their bid/ask ranges, series and historical percentiles were not recovered from a dated source. No precise claim of record-tight spreads or a particular basis-point entry is made in the model-facing pack.

This is an important limitation, not a minor missing field. The attractiveness of credit protection depends on the executable spread, remaining tenor, upfront payment and coupon. A macro narrative alone cannot establish that the derivative offers favorable asymmetry.

Equity volatility has already risen, so put protection may be more expensive than before the day's decline. However, no option premium surface or direct cost comparison was recovered. Credit spreads and equity implied volatility are related risk prices, not substitutes with a fixed conversion.

The available evidence supports a live risk-management question: substantial uncertainty has become public, while equities remain near recently reached highs relative to a deep crisis scenario. It does not establish the precise hedge notional, fair spread or optimal execution time. Those require dealer terms and a portfolio stress model, neither of which is invented here.
