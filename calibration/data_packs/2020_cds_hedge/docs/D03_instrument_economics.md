Title: Credit-index protection mechanics and what can actually pay off
Source: https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt1806b.htm
Date: 2018-06-05

The BIS June 2018 review describes major changes in the credit-default-swap market after the financial crisis, including smaller outstanding notionals and greater use of central clearing. It reports outstanding CDS notional declining from $61.2 trillion at end-2007 to $9.4 trillion ten years later.

A CDS protection buyer takes exposure to deterioration in reference credit risk while paying contractual consideration. An index references a defined basket of issuers. The trade can gain mark-to-market value as credit spreads widen before any constituent actually defaults.

That distinction matters for a portfolio hedge. The intended monetization need not wait for bankruptcies or full credit losses. If protection becomes more valuable during stress and can be closed at an attractive bid, it can create liquidity while long equity positions are impaired.

Economic premium, fixed coupon, upfront settlement, accrued interest and collateral are different quantities. Standardized contracts can combine coupons and upfront amounts; a quoted spread is not automatically equal to a simple annual cash premium. Exact documentation and dealer terms determine cash flows.

Notional is the reference amount, not the capital initially spent or the maximum plausible profit. Nor is it a directly comparable percentage of an equity portfolio. Credit duration, recovery assumptions, defaults, index composition and spread changes affect the payoff.

Spread sensitivity can be approximated locally using risky duration, but large shocks and defaults require a fuller valuation. Convexity is not free: protection can lose value through spread tightening and time/coupon payments if the adverse scenario does not arrive.

Central clearing changes counterparty exposure; it does not eliminate liquidity requirements, basis risk or all operational risks. Bilateral terms can create additional counterparty and collateral considerations. The pack does not assert that any specific proposed transaction is centrally cleared.

No dealer-confirmed index series, maturity, notional, upfront price, coupon or initial margin is supplied. The document is a mechanics reference available before 2020, not a term sheet. A decision should require explicit carry budgets, stress scenarios and close-out liquidity instead of treating the phrase “cheap insurance” as a complete instrument analysis.
