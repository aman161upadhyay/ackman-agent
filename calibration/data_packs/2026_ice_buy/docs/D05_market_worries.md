Title: Perpetual futures — a dated competitive scare with a defined mechanism
Source: compiled from: https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-06-02/us-exchanges-extend-selloff-perpetual-futures-approval-unnerves; https://ir.theice.com/press/news-details/2026/Intercontinental-Exchange-Reports-Record-First-Quarter-2026/default.aspx
Date: 2026-06-02

Reuters' Arasu Kannagi Basil, June2, reports US exchange shares extending a selloff after the CFTC enabled perpetual crypto futures through domestic regulated exchanges. Cboe fell9%, while CME and ICE each fell roughly4%. The concern was potential extension into other asset classes and more competition for incumbent derivatives venues.

Perpetual contracts lack a traditional expiration date. They have been popular with retail traders, particularly for leveraged crypto speculation. Their approval creates an identifiable market-structure question rather than a generic claim that “technology changes everything.”

TD Cowen's Bill Katz asked how quickly perps might be approved across equities and commodities. That is a future regulatory/competitive uncertainty, not evidence those products had already displaced ICE's institutional energy contracts by June.

The same Reuters report supplies counterarguments. Raymond James's Patrick O'Shaughnessy says the contracts are designed more for retail speculation than hedging and that it is difficult to envision them displacing existing CME/ICE liquidity. RBC's Ashish Sabadra describes competitive risk as manageable given product differences and structural advantages.

Those defenses should be tested, not simply repeated. Institutions may need particular durations, delivery terms, financing characteristics, clearing protection and margin offsets. A cheaper or continuously rolling product is not necessarily equivalent for each use case. Conversely, retail activity and some speculative flows can matter to exchange economics.

AI is a separate possible threat to data and workflow products. The pack does not have a verified pre-cutoff estimate of the exact ICE revenue replaceable by AI, so affected profit pools require conservative product-level scenarios. Proprietary data, licensing rights and embedded processes may defend some revenue while generic interfaces face pressure.

Mortgage weakness remains another risk. The segment reported a Q1 operating loss despite revenue growth, and substantial acquisition amortization complicates economic assessment. A prolonged origination slump could delay deleveraging or expected returns.

The counterevidence is strong Q1 exchange growth, high margins and cash generation. The serious bear case is lower future fees, transferred liquidity or a slower mortgage recovery, not an already-demonstrated collapse. The review must quantify affected profit pools and distinguish cyclical volume from structural competition.
