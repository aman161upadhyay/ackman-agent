CME's April22 release reports record revenue and adjusted earnings, with reported net income and EPS up20%. Adjusted operating income was about$1.4billion. This demonstrates robust institutional trading/hedging demand before the June selloff.

Cboe's body-dated May1 release reports net revenue up29%, reported EPS up54% and adjusted EPS up48%. A crawler's later page timestamp does not change the release's explicit May1 date. The document also raises its organic net-revenue-growth target to low double digits.

The strong operating data across exchanges are counterevidence against an already-occurring industry earnings collapse. However, trading volumes can benefit from unusual volatility and should not automatically be extrapolated at the same growth rate.

Different product franchises have different competitive exposure. Cboe's options ecosystem, CME's major futures franchises and ICE's energy benchmarks each depend on liquidity, participant needs and market conventions. A perpetual contract may be more relevant to one use case than another.

The June2 press reaction affected all three despite these differences. That creates an analytical reason to map each proposed competitive product to actual revenue/profit rather than value every exchange as an identical software platform.

Cutoff peer forward P/E, EV/EBITDA and harmonized FCF yields were not authenticated. No false precise ranking is supplied. The useful comparison is that institutions still generate substantial demand and profitability, while future market-structure changes require separate underwriting.
