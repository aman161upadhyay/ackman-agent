Title: A residual long-duration holding inside a concentrated portfolio

Date: 2025-09-15

The August performance report gives gross core-fund AUM of $20.7111 billion, firm assets including Howard Hughes $30.4653 billion, and PSH gross assets $18.5445 billion. These are not identical to PSH net assets or uncommitted cash.

The August transparency report gives PSH NAV of $15.3991 billion, cash 6.55% and debt -20.43% as portfolio weights. The June interim report gives June NAV $14.7146 billion and cash $1.4035 billion. These dated observations establish scale and liquidity without pretending to provide November balances.

The public June portfolio includes Alphabet, Amazon, Brookfield, Chipotle, Fannie/Freddie, Hertz, Hilton, Howard Hughes, Nike exposure, Restaurant Brands, Uber and Universal Music. Exact current weights were not independently reconstructed. A US 13F table would omit some foreign, OTC and derivative positions.

December 2024 PSH owned 21,996,590 Chipotle shares, worth $1.3264 billion or 10.19% of NAV, per the March annual report. That is a historical market allocation, not original cost and not November weight. The position has been monetized in part over its long life; a precise current residual cost basis is unavailable.

The June interim report's discussion preserves the unit-growth and operational case, but ownership itself does not establish the right decision after October's new evidence. A long holding period creates knowledge and a record of successful execution; it can also make anchoring to past performance a risk.

Portfolio fit requires considering restaurant and consumer exposure alongside Hilton, Restaurant Brands and other franchises, while recognizing differences in business models. Company-operated Chipotle has more direct labor/food-cost and site-investment exposure than an asset-light franchisor.

Pershing's stated framework favors predictable cash generation, durable moats, strong management and attractive prospective returns. That framework requires re-underwriting when traffic trends or leadership execution change, even if a long-run restaurant-development runway remains.

No November transaction or later investor explanation is included. The decision should compare current future-return uncertainty with alternative uses of capital, rather than treat original purchase cost or previously realized gains as evidence that the remaining shares must be held.
