Title: Company-operated restaurants and the unit-economics flywheel
Source: compiled from: https://ir.chipotle.com/2025-02-04-CHIPOTLE-ANNOUNCES-FOURTH-QUARTER-AND-FULL-YEAR-2024-RESULTS; https://ir.chipotle.com/2025-10-29-CHIPOTLE-ANNOUNCES-THIRD-QUARTER-2025-RESULTS
Date: 2025-10-29

Chipotle sells customizable Mexican-inspired meals, primarily through company-operated restaurants. Unlike a predominantly franchised restaurant system, it recognizes restaurant sales and directly bears food, labor, occupancy and operating costs. Restaurant growth therefore requires real site investment and operating execution rather than only licensing a brand.

At September 30, 2025, it had 3,916 company-owned restaurants, versus 3,726 at year-end 2024 and 3,437 at year-end 2023. More units expand the revenue base, but attractive returns also require new sites to maintain adequate volumes and avoid excessive cannibalization.

The business's prospective moat combines brand, food value, simple menu architecture, customer habit, throughput, supply chain and digital convenience. Chipotlanes improve access for digital orders rather than functioning like traditional order-at-the-speaker drive-throughs. Their value depends on incremental sales and efficient pickup execution.

Q3 digital sales represented 36.7% of food and beverage revenue. That is channel mix, not an independent high-margin software segment. Digital ordering can improve convenience and throughput, but also brings delivery fees, packaging and execution demands. The pack does not assume every digital dollar is incremental.

Q3 total revenue was $3.003 billion, up 7.5%, while comparable sales rose only 0.3%. Average check increased 1.1% and transactions declined 0.8%. New units account for much of the difference between consolidated sales growth and same-restaurant performance.

Restaurant-level operating margin was 24.5% versus 25.5%; consolidated operating margin was 15.9% versus 16.9%. Restaurant-level profit excludes corporate and certain other costs, so it must not be described as shareholders' final operating margin.

Scott Boatwright is CEO. The prior turnaround benefited from Brian Niccol, who departed for Starbucks in 2024. The current company must be evaluated under today's leadership and operating environment rather than assuming identical execution from a past management team.

The restaurant model has substantial growth runway if sites retain attractive returns, but US consumers can substitute other fast-casual chains, conventional fast food, grocery meals or cooking at home. Brand strength and value positioning provide defenses, not immunity from lower visit frequency. The central economic question is whether unit growth, healthy restaurant margins and consistent traffic can coexist as the base expands.
