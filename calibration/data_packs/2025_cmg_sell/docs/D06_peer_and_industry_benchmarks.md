Title: Fast-casual demand is uneven, not uniformly collapsing
Source: compiled from: https://s202.q4cdn.com/514401993/files/doc_financials/2025/q2/CAVA-Group-Q2-2025-Earnings-Release.pdf; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1477815/000162828025038952/q225sweetgreenearningsrele.htm; https://ir.chipotle.com/2025-10-29-CHIPOTLE-ANNOUNCES-THIRD-QUARTER-2025-RESULTS
Date: 2025-10-29

CAVA and Sweetgreen are relevant fast-casual comparisons, with important scale, menu, geography and maturity differences. They offer evidence on consumer demand and restaurant economics, not perfect substitutes for a much larger company with a longer operating history.

| Latest included observation | Chipotle Q3 | CAVA Q2 | Sweetgreen Q2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Release date | October 29 | August 12 | August 7 |
| Revenue growth | 7.5% | 20.3% | 0.5% |
| Comparable sales | +0.3% | +2.1% | -7.6% |
| Transactions/traffic | -0.8% | Approximately flat | Not extracted |
| Restaurant-level margin | 24.5% | 26.3% | Not extracted |
| Unit count | 3,916 | 398 | Not extracted |

CAVA's comp increase came primarily from price and product mix, while traffic was approximately flat. It opened 16 net restaurants and its unit count rose 16.7% year over year. The high revenue growth therefore reflects expansion as well as existing-store sales; it is not proof of accelerating traffic.

Sweetgreen reported $185.6 million revenue against $184.6 million, and average unit volume of $2.8 million versus $2.9 million. Its sharp negative comp illustrates that some health-oriented fast-casual demand is under significant pressure. It does not establish that Chipotle will follow the same trajectory.

Restaurant-level margins are non-GAAP measures whose exclusions vary. A 26.3% CAVA ratio cannot automatically be interpreted as superior full-company profitability to Chipotle's 24.5% ratio. Corporate cost, opening expense, maturity and rent accounting need reconciliation.

The broad industry issue is balancing value, frequency and cost inflation. A company can gain share in a weak market while reporting lower traffic, or grow consolidated revenue through openings while unit productivity deteriorates. These distinctions matter for projecting reinvestment returns.

Chipotle's scale, cash generation and established restaurant economics differentiate it from smaller chains. Its size also means new-unit expansion is a smaller percentage of the base than at a young chain, raising the importance of sustained same-store demand.

Exact November 3 forward peer multiples and harmonized FCF yields were not authenticated. No relative-valuation ranking is fabricated. The public operating data provide a concrete benchmark against which to test macro explanations and company-specific execution.
