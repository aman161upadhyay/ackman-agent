Date: 2025-11-03

2016: Pershing initiated the position during food-safety-related operating trouble. The subsequent annual letter described a 9.9% company stake at an average $405 pre-split price and a thesis of intact moat plus operational recovery.

2018: Brian Niccol was appointed CEO. Pershing's 2017 annual letter, published in 2018, described him as the right leader to reinvigorate the company. That leadership milestone followed the initial investment rather than preceding it.

June 2024: Chipotle implemented a fifty-for-one split. Historical prices and EPS need consistent conversion. The original average cost becomes $8.10 per current share, but later portfolio transactions change the economics of the remaining position.

2024: Niccol departed for Starbucks and Boatwright took leadership. The company nevertheless finished the year with 7.4% comparable-sales growth, 26.7% restaurant-level margin, more than $2.1 billion CFO and 3,726 restaurants. Those observations establish a strong comparison base.

February 4, 2025: Management initially projected low-to-mid-single-digit full-year comparable-sales growth. The company planned continued restaurant expansion and repurchases after another year of earnings and cash growth.

Through 2025: The growth outlook weakened as consumers visited less frequently. By October, Reuters counted three reductions in the sales forecast. The exact magnitude and dates of every intermediate revision are not reconstructed without primary confirmation.

October 29: Q3 revenue rose 7.5%, but transactions fell 0.8% and restaurant-level margin declined to 24.5%. Full-year comparable-sales guidance became a low-single-digit decline. Management announced 350–370 planned 2026 openings, showing that the unit-development pipeline remained active.

October 30: Public reporting described a roughly 19% earnings-related share-price drop, younger-customer frequency pressure and worsening October comps. This is information about the current run rate, not just the completed quarter.

November 3: Shares closed $31.51 on 33.59 million shares. The review ends here. Later investor transactions and disclosures are not part of the company evidence. The current challenge is deciding whether cash generation and unit expansion outweigh a less predictable traffic and management-execution path.
