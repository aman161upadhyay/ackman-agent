Title: October 29 — positive sales, negative transactions and another reset
Source: compiled from: https://ir.chipotle.com/2025-10-29-CHIPOTLE-ANNOUNCES-THIRD-QUARTER-2025-RESULTS; https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/30/chipotle-stock-falls-after-q3-earnings-report.html; https://www.reuters.com/business/chipotle-shares-drop-margins-face-heat-tariffs-inflation-2025-10-30
Date: 2025-10-30

Q3 revenue increased 7.5% to $3.003 billion. Comparable restaurant sales rose 0.3%, composed of 1.1% higher average check and 0.8% fewer transactions. Consolidated operating margin declined one percentage point to 15.9%; restaurant-level margin likewise declined one point to 24.5%.

Food, beverage and packaging cost was 30.0% of sales versus 30.6% a year earlier. Improvement in that line did not prevent overall margin pressure. Unit-opening expense, labor, occupancy and other costs must be evaluated separately rather than attributing every profitability movement to food inflation.

Diluted and adjusted diluted EPS were both $0.29 for the quarter. The nine-month diluted EPS was $0.89. Share repurchases can support per-share earnings even when same-restaurant economics slow, so traffic and margins remain important independent tests.

The release forecasts low-single-digit full-year comparable-sales declines and 315–345 restaurant openings in 2025. Its initial February outlook had called for low-to-mid-single-digit comparable-sales growth. Reuters characterizes October as the third sales-forecast reduction of the year.

For 2026, the company plans 350–370 openings, with more than 80% expected to include a Chipotlane. This is an announced expansion plan, not proof of future site-level returns or achieved 2026 growth. The near-term unit pipeline remains robust even as the existing-store sales picture weakens.

Boatwright says “persistent macroeconomic pressures” remain, while pointing to the brand's value proposition. Management priorities include restaurant execution, marketing, menu innovation and more engaging digital experiences. Those actions are identifiable, but the release does not quantify a guaranteed recovery timetable.

CNBC's October 30 report says the CEO described less-frequent visits particularly among customers aged 25–35, and that comparable sales worsened during October. That post-quarter observation matters more for the live run rate than assuming the slightly positive Q3 comp persisted unchanged.

The evidence supports both an intact growth platform and weakening transaction momentum. An analyst should ask whether the problem is temporary household pressure, fixable execution or diminished brand relevance. The company still generates cash and opens restaurants; the forecast range nevertheless depends increasingly on assumptions about traffic recovery and consumer resilience.
