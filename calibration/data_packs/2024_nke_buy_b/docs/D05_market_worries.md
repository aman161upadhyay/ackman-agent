Title: The market questions product relevance and the direct strategy
Source: compiled from: https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/nikes-downbeat-outlook-drags-jd-sports-puma-briefly-lifts-adidas-2024-06-28; https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/nike-plots-100-and-less-sneakers-lure-price-conscious-shoppers-2024-06-28; https://www.nbcnews.com/business/consumer/nike-expects-sales-declines-attempts-comeback-rcna159412
Date: 2024-06-28

Reuters on June 28 describes a surprise annual sales decline intensifying concern over Nike's ability to arrest market-share losses to newer brands. The critical issue is not simply an economic slowdown: competitors are attracting consumers while Nike deliberately reduces some older franchises and tries to accelerate innovation.

CNBC's June 28 report contrasts a new mid-single-digit annual revenue decline outlook with the market's expectation for modest growth. The size of the forecast revision raises questions about management's visibility and the duration of the repair. Strong Q4 gross margin does not settle those questions, because pricing and freight benefits can coexist with an eroding sales base.

Reuters's separate June 28 report says Nike plans a new range of shoes priced at $100 and below around the world. This acknowledges the importance of value-sensitive consumers. The opportunity is to broaden demand without sacrificing brand positioning. The risk is that lower price points or a greater need for promotions reduce the profitability of regained volume.

The channel dispute is equally important. Selling directly can look attractive in a spreadsheet because it captures the retailer's margin. Yet it can weaken wholesale relationships, reduce shelf presence and expose Nike to the full operating cost of digital fulfillment and stores. Q4 Direct revenue down 8% while wholesale grows 5% is consistent with a need to rebalance; it does not demonstrate that the full fix is already in place.

The bear scenario is that lost share reflects persistent product and cultural relevance problems rather than temporary inventory or distribution mistakes. In that case, rebuilding sales requires more marketing, lower prices and sustained innovation spending, limiting the recovery in earnings.

The constructive scenario is that the brand, athlete relationships and global scale survive a period of execution errors, with wholesale repair and new products restoring demand. Neither scenario assumes a CEO change: John Donahoe remains in charge as of June 28.

No verified dated sell-side target-price table is supplied. The sourced market concern is the revision in revenue expectations and competitive position, not an invented consensus prediction of a completed turnaround.
