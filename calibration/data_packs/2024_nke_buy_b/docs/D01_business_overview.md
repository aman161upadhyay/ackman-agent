Title: Global sporting-goods franchise with a channel problem
Source: https://investors.nike.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000320187-24-000028/q4fy24exhibit991er.htm
Date: 2024-06-27

Nike designs and markets athletic footwear, apparel and equipment through wholesale partners and its own stores and digital channels. Manufacturing is principally outsourced. The company owns the Nike, Jordan and Converse brands. Scale is visible in revenue, athlete marketing and geographic distribution, but a large installed brand does not guarantee that each new product attracts consumers.

Fiscal 2024 revenue was $51,362 million, of which Nike Brand generated $49,322 million and Converse $2,082 million, with a small corporate reconciliation. Nike Brand footwear accounted for $33,427 million, apparel $13,775 million and equipment $2,075 million. Footwear is therefore the principal driver of product relevance and demand.

Nike Brand geographic revenue was $21,396 million North America, $13,607 million Europe/Middle East/Africa, $7,545 million Greater China and $6,729 million Asia Pacific/Latin America. A North American slowdown is important, but the franchise is not a single-country retailer.

Wholesale Nike Brand sales were $27,758 million; Nike Direct sales were $21,519 million. Direct selling offers customer data and the retail gross spread, but requires stores, fulfillment, technology, returns processing and demand acquisition. Wholesale sacrifices part of the retail margin while supplying distribution, shelf presence and consumer discovery. A higher direct mix is not automatically better if digital demand weakens or partner relationships deteriorate.

Annual gross margin was 44.6%. Company-defined EBIT was $6,539 million, or 12.7% of sales, after central brand and corporate expenses. Geographic segment EBIT should not be treated as fully allocated company earnings. Nike spent $4,285 million on demand creation, reflecting the ongoing cost of sustaining brand desirability.

John Donahoe is president and CEO; Matthew Friend is CFO. No change in chief executive is announced in the information supplied. The quarter's basic weighted-average common shares were 1,508.0 million and diluted shares 1,516.7 million.

The underwriting question is whether product innovation, balanced distribution and cost discipline can restore growth without damaging brand equity. Financial scale and liquidity provide room to act, but do not identify the correct product assortment or establish a recovery timetable.
