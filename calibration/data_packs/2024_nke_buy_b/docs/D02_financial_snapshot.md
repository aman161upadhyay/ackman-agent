Nike's fiscal year ends May 31. Figures below are the fiscal years publicly reported by June 28; no July 2024 annual report is used.

| Metric | FY2021 | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue, $m | 44,538 | 46,710 | 51,217 | 51,362 |
| Revenue growth | 19% | 5% | 10% | approximately flat |
| Gross margin | 44.8% | 46.0% | 43.5% | 44.6% |
| Diluted EPS | $3.56 | $3.75 | $3.23 | $3.73 |
| Operating cash flow, $m | 6,657 | 5,188 | 5,841 | Not in June release |

The fiscal 2024 EPS increase is not equivalent to a restoration of revenue growth. Net income was $5,700 million, up 12%, while the effective tax rate declined to 14.9% from 18.2%. EPS also benefits from lower average shares. Restructuring charges reduced annual EPS by $0.22; the release's non-GAAP EPS excluding those charges was $3.95.

Latest-quarter revenue was $12,606 million, down 2%; gross margin was 44.7%, up 110 basis points. Company-defined EBIT margin was 13.3%, compared with 9.5%. Quarterly diluted EPS was $0.99 and net income $1,500 million. Improved freight costs, pricing and lower overhead helped earnings despite sales weakness.

At May 31, cash and equivalents were $9,860 million and short-term investments $1,722 million. Current debt was $1,000 million, long-term debt $7,903 million and notes payable $6 million. Simple financial net cash is therefore approximately $2.673 billion, calculated, before operating leases. Inventories fell 11% to $7,519 million.

Annual dividends were $2.2 billion and buybacks $4.3 billion, retiring 41.4 million shares. Full-year 2024 FCF and cash capex are not supplied in the June release and are not backfilled from the later annual filing. The prior audited history is available for analysis; a matching four-year ROIC series is not recovered.

The company can fund repairs without an obvious immediate liquidity crisis. The harder question is normalized earnings when classic products are reduced and new demand has not yet replaced them.
