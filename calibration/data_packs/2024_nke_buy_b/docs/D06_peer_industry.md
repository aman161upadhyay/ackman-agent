Title: Competition is broader than the traditional sporting-goods duopoly
Source: compiled from: https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/nikes-downbeat-outlook-drags-jd-sports-puma-briefly-lifts-adidas-2024-06-28; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1858985/000185898524000014/ex991pressreleasedatedmarc.htm; https://www.adidas-group.com/en/media/press-releases/adidas-performs-better-than-expected-in-transition-year-2023; https://investors.nike.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000320187-24-000028/q4fy24exhibit991er.htm
Date: 2024-06-28

Nike competes with Adidas and Puma across sportswear, with Hoka and On in performance running, and with a broad assortment of lifestyle brands. Reuters's June 28 report explicitly links the sales outlook to concern about share losses to newer competitors. Investors should therefore avoid assuming that the only alternative to Nike is an incumbent global brand with similar economics.

The immediate numeric benchmark available in the pack is Nike's own channel and segment history:

On's March12 release reports2023 revenue of CHF1.7921billion, up46.6%, gross margin59.6% and adjusted EBITDA margin15.5%. It guided at least30% constant-currency2024 growth. Adidas's March13 release reports2023 revenue EUR21.427billion, down4.8% reported, with47.5% gross margin. These are dated fiscal comparisons, not June28 consensus. Different currencies, scale, calendars and channel mix limit comparability. Matching forward multiples were not recovered; no current screen is substituted.

Industry structure provides both protection and risk. Athlete sponsorships, global marketing, design capability and distribution scale are costly to replicate across categories. However, a focused challenger can enter a narrower running or lifestyle niche without replicating Nike's complete platform. The ability to win a category with a small assortment makes aggregate market share less secure than total revenue size suggests.

Wholesale retailers are both partners and gatekeepers. They can redirect shelf space and recommendations toward products customers want, which means restoring those relationships can matter as much as adding direct digital traffic. A balanced route to market may be more profitable than maximizing direct share at every cost.

The relevant peer comparison is consequently not just P/E. It is growth in newer products, full-price sell-through, promotional intensity, distribution access and marketing efficiency. Those operating indicators would help distinguish a repairable execution gap from structural brand-share loss. The pack does not contain a complete measured series for them, so the decision must retain uncertainty rather than treating scale as proof of immunity.
