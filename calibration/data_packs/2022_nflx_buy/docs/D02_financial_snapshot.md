Title: Three-year financial record and cash-flow inflection

Date: 2022-01-20

Company-reported annual results below are in $millions except per-share figures. The 2019/2020 comparison was public January 19, 2021; FY2021 and Q4 results were released January 20, 2022. No subsequently filed annual report is needed for this table.

| Metric | FY2019 | FY2020 | FY2021 | Q4 2021 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | 20,156 | 24,996 | 29,698 | 7,709 |
| Operating income | 2,604 | 4,585 | 6,195 | 632 |
| Diluted EPS | 4.13 | 6.08 | 11.24 | 1.33 |
| Free cash flow | approximately -3,300 | 1,922 | -159 | -569 |

FY2020 revenue increased 24% and operating profit increased 76%. FY2021 revenue increased 19% and operating profit increased 35%. Operating margin reached 21% in 2021, versus 18% in 2020. The contrast between profit growth and member-growth deceleration is central to the case: pricing and operating leverage have mattered, not only new households.

Cash flow differs materially from net income. In 2021 Netflix recorded $17,702.202 million of content additions and $12,230.367 million of content amortization. Operating cash flow was $392.610 million. Property/equipment purchases of $524.585 million and changes in other assets of $26.919 million brought company-defined FCF to negative $158.894 million. Therefore, the business is approximately cash breakeven, not generating free cash equivalent to its $5,116.228 million net income.

The year-end cash balance was $6,027.804 million. Debt carrying values were $699.823 million current and $14,693.072 million long-term; subtracting cash gives approximately $9.365 billion net debt, a calculation rather than a company-defined leverage ratio. Content obligations totaled $23,161.360 million, including commitments not yet recognized as balance-sheet liabilities.

Netflix spent $600.022 million on cash repurchases in 2021 and made none in Q4, when cash funded the Roald Dahl Story Company acquisition. Management targets $10-$15 billion gross debt, plans a $700 million Q1 maturity repayment, and expects positive FCF in 2022 and beyond. No dividend or consistently defined ROIC series is provided. The FCF forecast is an explicit management expectation, not an established annual cash yield.
