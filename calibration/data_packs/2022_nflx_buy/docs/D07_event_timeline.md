Title: Relevant events before January 24
Source: compiled from: https://s22.q4cdn.com/959853165/files/doc_financials/2020/q4/FINAL-Q420-Shareholder-Letter.pdf; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528022000035/ex991_q421.htm; https://www.cnbc.com/2022/01/24/netflix-shares-fall-to-lowest-since-april-2020-on-subscriber-concern.html
Date: 2022-01-24

**January 19, 2021:** Netflix reported 37 million 2020 paid net additions, approximately $25 billion revenue and $4.6 billion operating income. It forecast approximately breakeven FCF in 2021 and said it believed external financing was no longer required for day-to-day operations. This was an important change from financing an expanding content slate through repeated debt issuance.

**During 2021:** Membership additions slowed sharply from the pandemic year. January 2022's release reports about 18 million additions for the full year. Meanwhile, operating margin and profit grew. This divergence is the central evidence for separating near-term membership volatility from progress toward scale economics.

**Late Q3 2021:** Squid Game became a global hit. By the January earnings release, management reports 1.65 billion first-four-week viewing hours. The example demonstrates that a locally produced series can travel internationally, supporting the broad production model. It does not establish a predictable hit rate for the entire slate.

**November 2021:** Netflix rolled out mobile games globally on Android and iOS. January's release says ten games were available by year-end. Management calls the initiative early-stage; no material standalone economics are disclosed. The company also launched Tudum, a fan-information site intended to deepen engagement around programming.

**Q4 2021:** Netflix funded its Roald Dahl Story Company acquisition from cash and made no quarterly share repurchases. The transaction expands intellectual-property ownership while consuming liquidity.

**December 2021:** Prices were lowered across all plans in India. Management cites unusually low local pay-TV prices and the goal of maximizing long-term revenue. Global pricing must be evaluated by market rather than extrapolated mechanically from US evidence.

**January 20, 2022:** Q4 results and weak Q1 guidance were released. Management also announced proposed governance reforms, including board declassification and removal of supermajority provisions.

**January 21 and 24:** CNBC reports a 21.8% Friday decline followed by a 2.6% Monday decline to $387.15. This timeline contains only operating and public-market information through the cutoff. No subsequent quarter's results, investor purchase announcements or later management strategy changes are included.
