Title: Global subscription entertainment and scale economics
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528022000035/ex991_q421.htm
Date: 2022-01-20

Netflix provides on-demand films and television through subscriptions, plus a small legacy DVD business. Members choose programming without a linear schedule. Global broadband and connected devices expand distribution, while local-language production extends the potential audience beyond English-speaking households. The subscription can be cancelled; repeat monthly billing should not be confused with contracted multi-year revenue.

The company ended 2021 with 221.84 million paid memberships. Its regional mix is economically important:

| Region | Year-end memberships, millions | Q4 revenue, $millions | Monthly revenue per membership |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| US/Canada | 75.22 | 3,309 | $14.78 |
| Europe/Middle East/Africa | 74.04 | 2,523 | $11.64 |
| Latin America | 39.96 | 964 | $8.14 |
| Asia-Pacific | 32.63 | 871 | $9.26 |

More than 90% of 2021 net member additions came from outside US/Canada. Consequently, identical worldwide subscriber growth can produce different revenue growth depending on geographic mix and currency. Regional operating margins are not supplied in the release.

Reed Hastings and Ted Sarandos are co-CEOs; Sarandos also serves as chief content officer. Greg Peters is COO/chief product officer and Spencer Neumann is CFO. The board proposes declassification, removal of supermajority provisions and shareholder special-meeting rights at the next annual meeting. These are prospective governance changes, not already approved amendments.

Content is the principal investment. A large paying base can spread programming costs across more households, generating operating leverage. However, the catalog requires ongoing replenishment and subscriptions have low contractual switching barriers. A scale advantage must be maintained through engagement, retention and economical acquisition of content.

Squid Game reached 1.65 billion viewing hours in its first four weeks, according to the release, demonstrating that a Korean production can reach global audiences. Netflix's Q4 films also include Red Notice and Don't Look Up. Individual hits establish reach but do not substitute for a portfolio-level return on content spending.

Q4 weighted-average basic shares were 443.462 million and diluted shares 455.795 million. These are average earnings denominators, not an exact January 24 outstanding-share count. Netflix's own case is that improving content and discovery can sustain a larger global audience while spreading fixed costs; the key uncertainty is the rate and cost of that expansion.
