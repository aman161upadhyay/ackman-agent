Title: Q4 results and weak Q1 guidance
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528022000035/ex991_q421.htm
Date: 2022-01-20

Q4 revenue was $7,709 million, up 16.0%. Operating income was $632 million, margin 8.2%, and diluted EPS $1.33. The lower quarterly margin reflects a large content slate rather than a full-year collapse: FY2021 operating margin was 21%, above management's 20% target. Q4 EPS also benefited from a $104 million noncash gain on euro-denominated debt remeasurement.

Paid net additions of 8.28 million were below management's 8.5 million forecast. Full-year additions totaled about 18 million, compared with 37 million during 2020. The US/Canada region nevertheless added 1.19 million in Q4, its strongest quarter since the early pandemic. Asia-Pacific added 2.58 million and EMEA 3.54 million.

The issue driving the market reaction is Q1 2022 guidance: only 2.50 million paid net additions, compared with 3.98 million a year earlier. Management forecasts $7,903 million revenue, 10.3% growth; $1,765 million operating income; 22.3% margin; and $2.86 diluted EPS. These are internal forecasts, not guarantees.

The letter says “retention and engagement remain healthy” but that acquisition has not returned to pre-COVID levels. It cites pandemic overhang and macroeconomic hardship, including Latin America. It also says the Q1 content schedule is back-end weighted, with Bridgerton season two and The Adam Project arriving in March.

For FY2022, the target operating margin is 19%-20%. The stronger dollar has reduced expected revenue by roughly $1 billion and creates an estimated two-percentage-point margin headwind because much of the cost base is in dollars. Management does not hedge currency.

The medium-term objective remains an average three-percentage-point annual margin increase over periods of several years. The letter says there is “no change to our goal” but expects short-term fluctuations. Whether the latest pause is timing/FX or a warning about the underlying growth algorithm is the analytical question.

Management also expects positive FCF in 2022 and beyond, with excess cash returned after reinvestment, selective acquisitions, debt targets and minimum liquidity. The release offers a pathway from a debt-funded content buildout to self-funded expansion, but does not quantify a normalized annual FCF level.
