Title: Competitor scale, profitability and industry structure
Source: compiled from: https://s206.q4cdn.com/979796730/files/doc_events/2021/11/q4-fy21-earnings.pdf; https://www.reuters.com/markets/europe/hbo-max-shows-growth-despite-industry-slowdown-2022-01-05; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528022000035/ex991_q421.htm
Date: 2022-01-20

The relevant comparison is not simply who has the largest subscriber count. Streaming competitors differ in price, bundling, geography, accounting and parent-company objectives. Two dated competitor benchmarks illustrate both Netflix's scale and the possibility of sustained competitive investment.

| Service or segment | Public source date | Disclosed benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| Netflix | January 20, 2022 | 221.84m year-end paid memberships; FY2021 margin 21%; revenue $29.698bn |
| Disney+ | November 10, 2021 | 118.1m subscribers; 60% year-on-year growth |
| Disney DTC segment | November 10, 2021 | Fiscal Q4 revenue approximately $4.6bn; operating loss $630m |
| HBO/HBO Max combined | January 5, 2022 | 73.8m year-end subscribers, versus 69.4m in September |

Disney's direct-to-consumer segment includes Hulu and ESPN+ as well as Disney+. Its segment losses are not solely Disney+ losses. HBO's figure combines streaming and the premium network; it should not be labeled a pure HBO Max paid-streaming count. Netflix's year-end base is much larger than either comparator, but different monthly revenue per member makes the ratios imperfect economic comparisons.

Netflix argues the biggest opportunity is migration from linear viewing. Its January letter says it has under 10% of total US television screen time. Even the largest subscription service therefore has room to increase engagement without assuming all entertainment spending migrates to a single provider.

The industry combines high fixed programming costs with inexpensive incremental digital delivery. This favors scale, but does not create exclusive distribution over all desirable content. Disney and WarnerMedia possess their own franchises and may accept losses to build direct customer relationships. Their broader businesses also make parent P/E ratios poor proxies for streaming valuations.

An economically attractive path for Netflix is subscriber and pricing growth faster than necessary content-cost growth. A less attractive path is persistent content inflation and higher churn as households rotate services. The observed current profitability gap favors Netflix, while competing subscriber growth shows that industry expansion need not accrue exclusively to it.

Timestamped comparable forward P/E or segment EV/EBITDA estimates for these peers were not recovered. Rather than blend unrelated telecom, parks or linear-network earnings, this pack retains the dated operating comparisons and identifies their definition limits.
