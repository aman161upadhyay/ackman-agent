Date: 2022-01-24

CNBC reports a January 24 close of $387.15, down 2.6% for the session, following Friday's 21.8% decline. The January 24 article describes the lowest close since April 2020. It reports that the stock passed $700 to a record in November. Relative to the $700 reference, the close is down 44.7%; the price is not literally 50% below that reference. Intraday lows and differing reference points should not be conflated with the closing comparison.

Using FY2021 diluted EPS of $11.24 gives a trailing P/E of 34.4x. This is a transparent calculation, $387.15 divided by $11.24. It is not a claimed forward consensus multiple. A verified timestamped forward EPS estimate was not recovered; consequently a roughly 30x forward valuation sometimes associated with the episode is not asserted as an independently verified pack fact.

Multiplying the close by Q4 basic weighted-average shares of 443.462 million gives illustrative equity capitalization of $171.69 billion. Adding approximately $9.365 billion of carrying-value net debt gives illustrative enterprise value of $181.05 billion. These proxies use a stale quarterly average share denominator, not an exact January 24 capitalization.

Reported FY2021 operating income of $6.195 billion implies roughly 29.2x enterprise value/EBIT on those inputs. A conventional EBITDA measure that adds back all content amortization would exaggerate economic cash generation because the library must be replenished. No comparable forward EV/EBITDA estimate is supplied.

The reported FY2021 FCF yield is slightly negative, since cash generation was approximately breakeven. Management's expectation of positive 2022 FCF offers a possible inflection, but no quantitative normalized FCF forecast is available here.

The exact 52-week low, full historical five-to-ten-year multiple range, daily trading volume, S&P 500 forward P/E and consensus EPS growth are not available in the retrieved dated sources. Those omissions constrain relative valuation precision, not the reported price decline. The investor must decide whether the observed multiple now compensates for slower acquisition, rising content costs and uncertain margin progression, while recognizing that much of the global streaming transition remains in progress.
