CooperCompanies' CooperVision and Bausch+Lomb provide useful operating comparisons. Their product mix, financial reporting and capital structures differ from Alcon's; neither is a perfect pure-play equivalent to the combined surgical and vision-care business.

Cooper's June4 release reports fiscal Q2 revenue$1.082billion, up8% reported and5% organic. CooperVision revenue was$723.5million, up8% reported and4% constant currency/organic. This is a direct contact-lens demand benchmark close to Alcon's4% currency-neutral lens growth.

Cooper reported corporate gross margin68% and non-GAAP operating margin27%, up260basis points. Those figures include other operations and adjustments. They cannot be used as an exact margin target for Alcon without segment and accounting reconciliation.

Bausch+Lomb's April29 release reports Q1 revenue$1.244billion, up9% reported and6% constant currency. Vision Care was$711million, up8%/5%, and Surgical$228million, up7% reported but1% currency-neutral. Pharmaceuticals contributed another$305million.

The categories are not identical, but the data show positive vision-care demand across competitors. Weak Alcon implantables cannot be assumed to reflect an industry-wide collapse, nor should stronger equipment growth be assumed permanent.

The competitive moat depends on clinical outcomes, surgeon workflow, distribution, brand, manufacturing quality and innovation. Broad demographic tailwinds benefit multiple companies and do not guarantee Alcon gains share or expands margins.

Peer capital intensity also matters. Contact-lens manufacturing requires capacity and quality investment, while surgical equipment can create downstream consumable economics. A simple gross-margin ranking does not capture those different reinvestment needs.

Cutoff peer forward P/E, EV/EBITDA, ROIC and harmonized FCF yields were not authenticated. No precise relative-value ranking is invented. The operating evidence provides a grounded benchmark for testing management's growth/margin expectations and distinguishing market demand from company-specific execution.

Fiscal-calendar differences also limit direct quarter-to-quarter comparisons.
