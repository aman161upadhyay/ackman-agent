Title: Surgical equipment, recurring consumables and vision care
Source: compiled from: https://s1.q4cdn.com/963204942/files/doc_earnings/2025/q4/supplemental-info/2025-Annual-Report.pdf; https://s1.q4cdn.com/963204942/files/doc_earnings/2026/q1/earnings-result/Q1-2026-Press-Release.pdf
Date: 2026-05-05

Alcon is an eye-care company with Surgical and Vision Care businesses. Surgical includes equipment, procedure-related consumables and implantables such as intraocular lenses. Vision Care includes contact lenses and ocular-health products. These categories have different growth, margin and regulatory characteristics.

Surgical equipment can create recurring demand for consumables and service as clinicians perform procedures. Installed systems, training, workflow familiarity and product compatibility may support retention. The economic claim requires evidence of continued procedure utilization and consumable capture, not merely counting machines once sold.

In Q1 2026, Surgical revenue was $1.460 billion: $438 million implantables, $769 million consumables and $253 million equipment/other. Reported growth was 4%, 8% and 27%, respectively; constant-currency growth was 1%, 4% and 23%. New equipment strength therefore coexists with weak implantable momentum.

Vision Care revenue was $1.225 billion. Contact lenses were $738 million, up 7% reported and 4% currency-neutral, while ocular health was $487 million, up 13% reported and 10% currency-neutral. Dry-eye products including Systane and Tryptyr supported ocular-health growth.

Demand can benefit from aging populations, prevalence of visual impairment, access to care and willingness to adopt premium correction. Those secular factors do not remove competition, reimbursement, physician preferences or affordability constraints. Contact lenses also require manufacturing quality and efficient capacity utilization.

David Endicott is CEO. Management emphasizes innovation, surgical-platform launches, commercial execution and manufacturing efficiency. Unity and Tryptyr are current operating programs; AURN001 is a separate developmental clinical asset, discussed in D09.

FY2025 sales were $10.319 billion, with 13.2% reported operating margin and 19.8% core margin. The large difference reflects material adjustments and must not be ignored. Q1 core margin was 21.2%, while reported margin was 10.9%.

The moat case combines installed workflows, clinical trust, product breadth, distribution and scale in development/manufacturing. It is not equivalent to a patent-guaranteed perpetual annuity. The central question is whether recurring consumables and vision-care earnings remain predictable enough to underwrite the business without requiring success from an unapproved therapy.
