Reuters' May6 account reports quarterly sales$2.69billion versus$2.71billion consensus, with implantables almost stagnant on an underlying basis. The precise primary figure is1% constant-currency implantable growth. The distinction prevents a modest reported increase from concealing a weak underlying category.

Investing.com's Vahid Karaahmetovic, May6, reports shares down around7% despite a raised full-year earnings outlook. Its framing combines a revenue miss with sharply lower reported operating profit. That is a dated market reaction, not a later investor's retrospective description.

The bear mechanism is that equipment and product launches create growth without delivering the expected durable margin expansion. Selling expense, development cost, manufacturing ramp-up and tariffs may persist longer than assumed. A company can have valuable products and still produce lower returns than its valuation requires.

FY2025 core margin fell from20.6% to19.8%, while reported margin fell from14.4% to13.2%. That history makes the2026 margin-improvement promise something to prove. Q1 core margin21.2% is encouraging, but a single quarter does not establish a steady25%-plus structural margin.

Tariff mitigation is not a free earnings windfall. Management expects$100–150million net expense and plans to reinvest savings relative to the prior outlook. Competitors, currencies, procedure mix and pricing can affect realized profitability independently.

Surgical share and consumable capture are also important. A growing installed base does not guarantee every future procedure uses the expected products at the expected price. Physician preference, reimbursement and competing platforms can change the economics.

AURN001 adds clinical and regulatory uncertainty. Its Phase3 program is underway, but success is not established and no approval or commercial revenue is assumed. If the investment only works with a favorable trial outcome, it becomes a different risk proposition from owning an established recurring-consumables franchise.

Counterevidence includes positive underlying growth in both divisions, improving cash generation, new product uptake and substantial existing FCF. The decision requires separating temporary investment burdens from lasting margin impairment, and separating an optional clinical asset from the core business's forecastability.
