Title: Capacity overbuild, affordability and a balance sheet in transition
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1690820/000169082022000139/ex99_1q12022.htm; https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/carvana-lay-off-about-2500-employees-2022-05-10/; https://investors.carvana.com/news-releases/2022/04-28-2022-052107506
Date: 2022-05-10

The Q1 shareholder letter attributes weakness to both external and internal causes: Omicron, used-car prices, interest rates, and disruptions in reconditioning/logistics. Management had planned capacity for more volume than it fulfilled. Fixed costs and lower unit throughput compressed profitability.

Higher vehicle prices and borrowing costs affect consumer affordability. Rising rates and widening credit spreads can also reduce the economics of selling originated loans. These risks may reinforce each other rather than diversify the business.

Reuters reports May10 layoffs of approximately2,500 employees, about12% of the workforce. This is a cost response, not evidence of a completed return to profitability. The company is simultaneously integrating a major acquisition and planning future site improvements.

The new10.25% notes provide liquidity and extend financing maturity. They also commit substantial annual interest. A long maturity does not eliminate the economic burden of paying that coupon while operations are losing money.

Management's bull case is that the used-car market is large and normally stable, current pressures are transitory, and Carvana is gaining share. It highlights growth among higher-credit-score financing customers as evidence that demand remains attractive when affordability is less constrained.

That argument deserves consideration. A better-utilized network could lower costs per unit, and an improved customer experience may win share over time. The ADESA footprint could accelerate capacity availability relative to building entirely new sites.

The counterargument is that scale has been financed before sustainable profitability is demonstrated. Inventory and finance receivables require capital, and capacity may remain underutilized during weak demand. Equity owners could face further funding needs even if the long-term customer proposition survives.

Neither a bankruptcy prediction nor an assumed inevitable rebound is established at this date. The immediate diligence requirement is a conservative liquidity runway using realistic margins and achievable cost cuts, excluding asset collateral that cannot be converted promptly.

Key observations are unit growth, gross profit after financing effects, selling expense per unit, cash burn and the distinction between funding availability and actual cash. A large drawdown is not evidence that these operational and financing questions have been answered.
