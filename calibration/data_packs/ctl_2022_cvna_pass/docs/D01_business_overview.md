Title: Online used-car retail requires a physical network and financing
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1690820/000169082022000139/ex99_1q12022.htm; https://investors.carvana.com/~/media/Files/C/Carvana-IR/documents/adesa-u-s-acquisition-101-final.pdf
Date: 2022-05-10

Carvana sells used vehicles through an online buying experience supported by inventory acquisition, inspection/reconditioning centers, logistics, delivery and customer financing. Its consumer interface is digital; the underlying operations require physical capacity and working capital.

Management argues customers value convenience and the ability to complete a purchase without a conventional dealership visit. Brand, technology, logistics density and reconditioning scale could improve the model as volume grows. Those potential efficiencies are not yet proof of durable cash returns at the present scale.

Q1 retail units sold are105,185, up14%. Revenue rises56%, much faster than units, reflecting vehicle prices and business mix as well as activity. Gross profit per retail unit isUS$2,833, downUS$823. Revenue growth alone therefore overstates the improvement in unit economics.

The business also originates auto loans and monetizes them through financing arrangements. Interest rates, credit spreads and investor appetite can affect gains and funding. These drivers differ from the recurring economics of a pure subscription or marketplace commission.

Management says it prepares capacity six to twelve months ahead of demand. It staffed and built for substantially more Q1 volume than materialized. Short-run fixed costs then spread over fewer units, increasing reconditioning, transport and selling/general costs per vehicle.

The ADESA US acquisition closes May9 for approximatelyUS$2.2 billion. Its physical auction sites provide potential reconditioning/logistics capacity and real-estate flexibility. Management expects significant long-term scale benefits and plans improvements, but integration and future utilization require further work.

The transaction is financed withUS$3.275 billion senior unsecured notes and approximatelyUS$1.25 billion common equity. A growing physical footprint is therefore accompanied by greater financing obligations, not merely software-like scaling.

The bull case is a large used-car market, a differentiated customer experience and a network that can earn attractive margins at higher utilization. The competing case is that cyclicality, inventory, finance dependence and heavy capacity costs make the path substantially less predictable than customer satisfaction suggests.

The investment question is whether existing liquidity and realistic unit-level improvements can reach sustainable cash profitability before the next large capital need. No later turnaround success or failure is assumed.
