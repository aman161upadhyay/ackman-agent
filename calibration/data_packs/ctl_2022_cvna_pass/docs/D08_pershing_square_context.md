Title: Capital-market independence matters before a scale thesis is proven
Source: compiled from: https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2014/09/08113002/PSH-Annual-Report.pdf; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1690820/000169082022000139/ex99_1q12022.htm; https://investors.carvana.com/~/media/Files/C/Carvana-IR/documents/adesa-u-s-acquisition-101-final.pdf
Date: 2022-06-15

Pershing Square's2014 annual report, published March2015, describes long investments in cash-generative businesses with durable advantages. It specifically favors companies that “do not need access to the capital markets to survive and thrive.” That principle addresses financing resilience as well as the apparent attractiveness of a product.

Carvana's recent financing is a completed source of resources, so an analyst should not pretend liquidity is unchanged from March31. Equally, debt and equity issuance show that internally generated cash has not financed the full investment plan. The new funding must be allocated to the acquisition, improvements and operating needs.

The same report emphasizes “simple, predictable, free-cash-flow generative businesses.” Understanding how online car retail works is different from bounding the future returns on a large physical network and loan-monetization platform.

Carvana's customer proposition and potential scale are meaningful positives. The question is whether they imply a defensible moat and positive cash after all required costs, not whether customers like buying cars online. Gross profit per unit and cash conversion provide better tests than headline revenue.

A concentrated shareholder could advocate cost discipline, better capital allocation or slower expansion. It cannot control used-car prices, consumer credit conditions or the terms available in future financing markets. Those variables must be stressed in a downside case.

The published principles are general preferences with historical exceptions, not a literal rule that every loss-making company is permanently excluded. A special situation needs its own asset values, funding runway, bounded downside and credible mechanism; a90%-plus price decline does not supply them.

No authenticated public Ackman comment specifically endorsing or rejecting Carvana is found in the sources used here. No actual Pershing position, short recommendation or private rejection is inferred. Current firm liquidity is not reconstructed.

The appropriate framework asks whether conservative cash and financing scenarios support a durable owner return before additional capital is required. A successful future business could still be an unsuitable security today if the path to that future transfers value away from present common holders.
