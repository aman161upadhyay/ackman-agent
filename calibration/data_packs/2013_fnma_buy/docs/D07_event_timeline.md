Title: Improving earnings meet a hostile capital-allocation framework
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/310522/000031052213000138/a2013q2pressrelease.htm; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1026214/000119312513323022/d554149dex991.htm; https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/20130910_R43219_fbc0312cf2ec8be7526714c62ad2210d046573ec.html; https://www.refinblog.com/fairholme-or-foul-investor-complaint-over-fannie-and-freddie-preferred/
Date: 2013-09-10

**September2008 — conservatorship.** FHFA assumes control and Treasury provides financial support through senior preferred agreements. The common remains outstanding but ordinary corporate governance and capital distributions are constrained.

**August17 2012 — third amendment.** Treasury/FHFA replace the fixed dividend with the net-worth-based mechanism effective2013. Capital buffers decline over time. Shareholders now face an explicit contractual barrier to retaining profits for ordinary recapitalization.

**April2013 — FY2012 report.** Fannie reports US$17.2 billion net income and expects continued profitability. The result confirms a substantial operating recovery but does not remove senior claims or the amended dividend.

**Q1 2013 results — deferred-tax recognition.** Fannie releases its valuation allowance, producing an approximately US$50.6 billion income-tax benefit. This explains why headline profits greatly exceed a normal recurring earnings run rate.

**June/July — legislative proposals.** Corker-Warner and the PATH Act outline different replacement systems. They create genuine wind-down risk without establishing an enacted final settlement.

**July — shareholder lawsuits.** Perry and Fairholme challenge the amended arrangement through different legal theories. Their actions demonstrate available arguments, not a favorable ruling.

**August6 — presidential housing remarks.** The administration supports winding down the existing enterprises and putting private capital at the center of a reformed mortgage system.

**August7–8 — Q2 results.** Freddie reports US$5.0 billion net income; Fannie reports US$10.1 billion. Both forecast large September Treasury dividends and retain unchanged senior-preferred balances.

**September10 — CRS comparison.** The report clarifies the key differences between House and Senate proposals and the substantial common objective of replacing the current arrangement.

By October31, the core tension remains unresolved: profitable national franchises coexist with highly constrained common claims. The next quarterly results are not yet public. No final court outcome, amended sweep, capital-retention permission, relisting timetable or completed release is an available fact.

A prospective shareholder can monitor profits, credit, legal proceedings and policy proposals. These are observable milestones but not a date-certain control catalyst. The event sequence supports a contingent-value analysis rather than treating the position like a normal operating turnaround.
