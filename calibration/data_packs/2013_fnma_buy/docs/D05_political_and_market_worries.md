Title: The administration and competing bills favor replacing the companies
Source: compiled from: https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/2013/08/06/remarks-president-responsible-homeownership; https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/20130910_R43219_fbc0312cf2ec8be7526714c62ad2210d046573ec.html; https://www.politico.com/story/2013/07/fannie-freddie-mae-unite-ralph-nader-wall-street-093917
Date: 2013-09-10

The September10 Congressional Research Service report describes substantial housing-finance reform activity. The House PATH Act proposes winding down Fannie/Freddie and replacing their role with a utility that facilitates private securitization without a comparable government guarantee. The House Financial Services Committee ordered it reported July24.

The Senate Corker-Warner proposal also contemplates winding down the existing GSEs, but would replace them with a new federal mortgage-insurance structure. Private capital would absorb a meaningful first-loss layer before federal reinsurance responds. The proposals differ on government support while agreeing the current institutional arrangement should change.

President Obama's August6 Phoenix remarks support private capital at the center and winding down the existing enterprises. These are powerful counterweights to an investment case premised on simply restoring the old common-stock franchise unchanged.

Legislative proposals are not enacted law. Implementation would be complex because mortgage-market liquidity, the fixed-rate mortgage, affordable housing, taxpayer risk and financial stability all matter. A replacement system must attract capital and preserve functioning markets. Those obstacles may slow change but do not guarantee preservation of today's shareholders.

Politico's July10 report describes a coalition of hedge-fund investors and Ralph Nader seeking fair treatment for existing shareholders. This is evidence that the shareholder debate is active, not evidence that the coalition controls Congress or the administration.

The bull view is that profitable infrastructure would be expensive and risky to dismantle, and private capital may ultimately be needed in the existing organizations. The bear view is that policymakers can preserve the public function while changing the entities and allocating economics away from current common.

The Treasury warrant can create potential alignment with common appreciation, but Treasury also receives the senior dividend and can prioritize taxpayer recovery or policy. Its incentives are not identical to those of a minority holder who paid cash for common.

No scheduled vote, court date or administrative release guarantees resolution within a conventional activist holding period. The required patience may be long and politically driven. Common investors must separately price the chance, terms and timing of an outcome; “essential business” is not a sufficient catalyst.
