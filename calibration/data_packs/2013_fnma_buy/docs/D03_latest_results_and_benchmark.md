Title: Both enterprises are profitable; neither common stock has normal distributions

The August releases cover Q2, the latest published quarter at the October31 cutoff. No November Q3 earnings or deferred-tax event is included.

Fannie's six consecutive profitable quarters and Freddie's seven show that improved results are not an isolated one-quarter event. Both attribute material benefits to improving credit and housing conditions. They remain exposed to changes in interest rates, house prices, reserve estimates and legacy claims.

Fannie says it expects to remain profitable for the foreseeable future, with stable revenues and strong annual earnings over the next few years. It also warns that quarterly earnings may vary significantly. This is management's outlook, not a commitment to distribute profits to common.

Freddie's deferred-tax valuation allowance was still US$28.6 billion at June30. That balance is a disclosed accounting item, not a completed release or a forecast amount that can be booked into the next quarter.

Freddie reports post-2008 loans at 70% of its single-family credit-guarantee portfolio, including relief refinance loans. The newer book supports improved credit performance but does not remove all housing risk. Its July US$500 million STACR transaction transfers a portion of credit risk to private investors, illustrating a possible role for private capital under conservatorship.

The two companies are useful operating and policy benchmarks for each other, but each has separate shares, preferred claims, lawsuits and economics. Buying both does not diversify the shared Treasury/FHFA policy decision.

No truly matched listed private comparator combines their scale, charter, funding support, mortgage-guarantee role and conservatorship. Commercial-bank P/E and private mortgage-insurer margins may inform pieces of a model but cannot justify a mechanical comparable multiple. The pack does not invent an ordinary listed-peer valuation table.
