Title: Low price, extreme volatility and a nonstandard denominator

Date: 2013-10-31

Historical unadjusted closes on October31 are approximately US$2.34 for FNMA and US$2.24 for FMCC. Session volumes were approximately 29.58 million and 12.76 million respectively. Twenty-session average volumes calculated from the historical series are approximately 30.69 million and 13.82 million.

The series records one-year intraday highs around US$5.44 and US$5.00 respectively. An anomalous FNMA low observation prevents asserting a clean independently verified rolling low. These are highly volatile OTC securities; volume on a favorable day does not establish that a large position can exit without market impact.

Fannie has 1,158,077,970 common shares outstanding at June30. Multiplying by US$2.34 gives approximately US$2.710 billion current common capitalization. That is not the fully diluted valuation of a recapitalized enterprise.

Treasury holds a warrant to acquire 79.9% of the common at a nominal exercise price. Ignoring all other issuance, current holders would retain approximately 20.1% after full exercise. Their US$2.710 billion market value therefore corresponds to approximately US$13.48 billion warrant-diluted common value, calculated. This does not include any new recapitalization shares, senior-preferred conversion, junior-preferred treatment or required fresh capital.

A simple earnings/price division is misleading. The company earns billions while the net-worth dividend directs value to Treasury. Treasury's US$117.1 billion senior preferred does not disappear as dividends are paid. Junior preferreds also rank ahead of common. Positive enterprise earnings do not imply a positive currently distributable common earnings stream.

A proper scenario model must specify policy/legal resolution, normalized earnings, required capital, recapitalization time, financing terms, senior and junior claims, and final share count. Only then can an ordinary equity multiple and discount rate be applied. No authenticated contemporaneous Ackman target or final capital plan is available at this cutoff.

The downside is not merely a lower P/E: wind-down, adverse policy, continued sweep or heavy dilution could leave common worth little even if mortgage finance remains profitable. The upside requires a favorable bridge from franchise value to this particular security, not just national need for the service.
