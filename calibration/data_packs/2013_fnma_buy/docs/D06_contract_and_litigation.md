Title: The net-worth dividend and contested shareholder remedies
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1026214/000119312512359938/d398152d8k.htm; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/310522/000031052213000065/fanniemae201210k.htm; https://www.refinblog.com/fairholme-or-foul-investor-complaint-over-fannie-and-freddie-preferred/
Date: 2013-07-24

The August17 2012 third amendment changed the senior-preferred dividend mechanism. Beginning2013, the dividend generally equals net worth above the applicable capital reserve rather than the old fixed-rate amount. The capital reserve is US$3 billion in2013 and falls by US$600 million annually until zero in2018.

This removes the ability to retain ordinary positive earnings beyond the contractual reserve while the terms remain in force. It does not cancel the existing senior preferred. An investor cannot assume that cumulative dividends automatically repay the liquidation preference or terminate the agreement.

The amendment also accelerates reduction of retained investment portfolios toward US$250 billion by the end of2018. A valuation should distinguish the durable guarantee business from investment assets whose size policy requires to shrink.

Perry Capital and Fairholme initiated challenges in July2013. The publicly reported claims include statutory/administrative objections and constitutional takings arguments. A complaint is an allegation by a plaintiff, not a judicial finding that the sweep is illegal or that common shares must receive a particular recovery.

Cornell-affiliated law professor David Reiss's July24 commentary reviews Fairholme's complaint. It notes the argument that the amendment effectively created a new security after Treasury's purchase authority expired, but treats that as highly contested. He argues the government was already deeply involved through conservatorship, distinguishing the case from a straightforward seizure of an ordinary private company.

The commentary concludes shareholders face substantial legal difficulty even if their settled expectations were disrupted. This is an interpretation, not a forecast of a final judgment. Fairholme's preferred holdings also have different contractual priorities from common.

Legal routes can differ in plaintiffs, standing, governing law, remedies and timelines. Invalidating one action would not necessarily dictate immediate recapitalization, cash damages to all owners or release from conservatorship. A damages award, contract remedy and full common-equity restoration are distinct outcomes.

An investment model should not combine all favorable legal arguments as independent certainties or assume a near-term binary win. There may be years of litigation and appeals while profits continue flowing to Treasury. The key risk is inability to translate a plausible fairness argument into an enforceable, timely remedy benefiting the security actually purchased.
