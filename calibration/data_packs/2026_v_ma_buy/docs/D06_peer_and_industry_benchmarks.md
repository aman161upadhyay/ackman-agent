Title: Distinguishing networks, lenders and digital wallets

Date: 2026-02-11

Visa and Mastercard are each other's closest listed global network comparison. American Express and PayPal are useful adjacent benchmarks but have different business models. Treating all payment companies as one homogeneous sector would confuse credit risk, merchant acquisition, network fees and wallet/checkout economics.

American Express's January 30 release reports 2025 net income of $10.8 billion and EPS $15.38. Worldwide billed business was approximately $1.670 trillion. Its integrated model combines network, merchant relationships and cardholder lending, so an interest-rate cap can affect it differently from a non-lending network.

PayPal's February 3 release reports Q4 transaction-margin dollars of $4.034 billion versus $3.935 billion, up 3%, or $3.741 billion excluding interest on customer balances, up 4%. This is a non-GAAP operating metric, not net revenue or operating income. Customer-balance interest and checkout economics distinguish it from the networks.

The cross-border figures come from different latest quarters and definitions; they are not a perfectly synchronized market-share comparison. Visa's operating margin includes substantial litigation provision, so adjusted economics require reconciliation.

Both networks have high cash conversion and broad acceptance. Their common advantage is a trusted multi-party system rather than simply the visible card logo. Their shared risk is that regulation or alternative rails reduce the fee pool faster than volume and services can expand it.

Digital wallets may use existing card rails rather than replace them. Stablecoin settlement may similarly complement some parts of the stack while competing with others. The key is tracing which participant retains authorization, settlement, identity, credit, fraud and customer-service economics.

Exact cutoff peer forward multiples, harmonized capital returns and comparable FCF yields are unavailable. No unsupported ranking is supplied. The operating evidence instead helps separate genuinely different payment profit pools and identify which bear mechanism applies to each.
