Title: Agents, stablecoins and proposed credit-card regulation
Source: compiled from: https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-07/stablecoin-firms-bet-big-on-ai-agent-payments-that-barely-exist; https://finance.yahoo.com/news/visa-mastercard-arent-real-casualties-133113889.html; https://www.wsj.com/livecoverage/stock-market-cpi-today-inflation-01-13-2026/card/visa-mastercard-shares-drop-after-trump-endorses-credit-card-bill-4TxzQPadSUn1ibPQhOEa; https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/3623/text
Date: 2026-03-07

Emily Mason's March 7 Bloomberg report describes stablecoin firms building for AI-agent payments and notes that a Citrini scenario about agents bypassing card fees shook card-network shares two weeks earlier. This is a dated description of market concern, not proof that mass agentic bypass already occurs.

Kaustubh Bagalkote's February 28 Benzinga account emphasizes that Citrini's “2028 Global Intelligence Crisis” was explicitly “a scenario, not a prediction.” Its mechanism is nevertheless analytically relevant: agents might select cheaper settlement rails, weakening consumer inertia and network pricing.

The bear case must distinguish consumer-facing convenience from the underlying payment stack. If agents can cheaply acquire identity, authorization, risk allocation and merchant acceptance elsewhere, card tolls could compress. If they still need trusted credentials, issuer credit and consumer recourse, the networks may remain important infrastructure.

Regulation is a separate risk. Xavier Martinez's January 13 Wall Street Journal account reports shares falling after Trump endorsed the Credit Card Competition Act; Visa fell 4.5% that session. Congress's primary text shows S.3623 introduced January 13, proposing rules on competition in credit-card transaction networks.

The proposal is not assumed enacted, defeated or permanently stalled at this cutoff. Routing competition could pressure network economics differently from a proposed cap on card interest. A cap primarily affects lenders, with possible indirect effects on credit availability and spending; the networks do not ordinarily earn cardholder interest.

Stablecoins could address real pain points in cross-border transfers and business settlement. Those markets are not identical to consumer credit-card purchases with rewards, dispute resolution and fraud protection. Adoption in one use case does not prove immediate replacement in every other use case.

Counterevidence includes January transaction growth, high cash conversion and announced network investment in AI/tokenization. But management programs are responses to risk, not independent proof the moat is invulnerable.

The serious question is the earnings sensitivity to lower take rates, alternate routing and changing checkout behavior. Recession, travel mix, client incentives, litigation and cybersecurity also remain risks. Current fear should be quantified against the actual profit pool rather than either extrapolated to total collapse or dismissed because current earnings are strong.
