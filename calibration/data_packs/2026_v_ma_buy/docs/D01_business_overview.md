Title: Global payment networks — transaction infrastructure, not card lenders
Source: compiled from: https://s29.q4cdn.com/385744025/files/doc_downloads/2025/Visa-Fiscal-2025-Annual-Report.pdf; https://s25.q4cdn.com/479285134/files/doc_financials/2025/ar/MA-12-31-2025-10-K-as-filed-with-standard-exhibits.pdf
Date: 2026-02-11

Visa and Mastercard connect consumers, issuing banks, merchants, acquiring institutions and processors. Their networks authorize, route, clear and support settlement of payments, while related services include security, authentication, analytics and other capabilities. They are not ordinarily the institutions lending revolving balances to cardholders.

This distinction matters economically. A card interest-rate cap directly affects lenders' revenue and underwriting, while routing mandates or network-fee competition can affect network economics more directly. Interchange, merchant discount, issuer interest and the networks' own fees are not the same revenue stream.

The moat is multi-sided acceptance. Consumers value broad merchant acceptance; merchants value access to paying customers; banks and processors value reliable global connectivity, fraud controls and established standards. Replacing one technical message is easier than replicating the full ecosystem, customer protections and commercial relationships.

Scale can support high incremental margins because processing another transaction does not require proportional physical investment. Nevertheless, technology, cybersecurity, incentives, sales relationships and regulatory compliance are ongoing costs. Client incentives are significant and affect reported net revenue.

Visa's FY2025 net revenue was $40.000 billion and operating income $23.994 billion. Mastercard's calendar-2025 revenue was $32.791 billion and operating income $18.897 billion. Different fiscal calendars, litigation provisions and service mix limit simple margin comparisons.

The growth base includes nominal consumer spending, digitization of cash payments, e-commerce, cross-border travel/commerce and services sold around the payment relationship. Cross-border activity often has different economics from domestic transactions, so total transaction growth alone does not settle revenue growth.

Ryan McInerney leads Visa and Michael Miebach leads Mastercard. Both companies are investing in tokenization, identity and AI-enabled commerce. The existence of those programs shows adaptation, not proof that new rails cannot threaten existing economics.

Stablecoins can offer alternative settlement, especially for some cross-border and business use cases. AI shopping agents may influence which payment method is selected. The underwriting issue is whether trusted credentials, acceptance, fraud protection, recourse and issuer relationships preserve network value as interfaces change.

Owning both businesses would diversify company-specific execution but retain common exposure to consumer spending, regulation, network pricing and technological substitution. They should therefore be treated as related holdings rather than independent sources of portfolio diversification.
