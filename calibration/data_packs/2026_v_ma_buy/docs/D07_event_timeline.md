Title: Technology initiatives and the first-quarter fear cycle

Date: 2026-03-31

April 30, 2025: Visa publicly announced AI-commerce initiatives opening its network to AI platforms, technology firms, banks, fintechs and merchants. This is pre-cutoff evidence that the company was developing agent-compatible capabilities, not a later announcement moved backward into March.

During 2025: Stablecoins and alternative payment rails attracted growing interest. Their potential use in settlement and cross-border transfers is distinct from proof of universal consumer-card replacement. This document does not assign an exact adoption rate without a dated source.

December 2025: Visa's annual report made its fiscal 2025 financials available, including $40 billion revenue and a large litigation provision. Mastercard's February 2026 10-K later supplied its calendar-year statements. Different reporting calendars are preserved in D02.

January 13, 2026: The Credit Card Competition Act of 2026 was introduced. Contemporary market reporting linked network-stock declines to Trump's endorsement. Its legal status at this point is a proposal, not completed network-fee reform.

January 29: Both networks reported growing transactions and cross-border activity. Visa fiscal Q1 revenue rose 15% reported; Mastercard Q4 services growth was 26% reported. These are the latest included company results.

February: A hypothetical AI-disruption scenario described agents avoiding card fees. Subsequent reporting says it contributed to share-price pressure. The scenario is not a report of already-observed mass displacement and should not be labeled a forecast certainty.

March 7: Bloomberg described stablecoin providers racing to build agent-payment systems, while emphasizing how early the use case remained. That captures the tension between a plausible new infrastructure market and limited current operating evidence.

March 31: Visa closed $302.24 and Mastercard $499.66. The snapshot ends before any unpinned second-quarter transaction window. No later earnings, regulatory conclusion or investor explanation is included.

The live question is whether technological change removes the networks' economic role, shifts it or enlarges it. A responsible model must make the affected revenue and margin assumptions explicit, not equate all digital payment innovation with either inevitable disruption or guaranteed incremental growth.
