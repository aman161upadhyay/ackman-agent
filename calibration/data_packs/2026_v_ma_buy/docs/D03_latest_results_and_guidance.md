Title: January results — spending, cross-border activity and services
Source: compiled from: https://s1.q4cdn.com/050606653/files/doc_financials/2026/q1/Q1-2026-Earnings-Release_vF.pdf; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1141391/000114139126000003/ma12312025-exx991xearnings.htm
Date: 2026-01-29

Both networks reported results January 29, but their periods differ. Visa reported fiscal Q1 2026; Mastercard reported Q4 and calendar 2025. The information is current before this March cutoff, not evidence about later spring spending.

Visa net revenue was approximately $10.9 billion, up 15% reported and 13% constant dollar. GAAP net income was approximately $5.9 billion, GAAP EPS $3.03 and non-GAAP EPS $3.17. Payments volume increased 8% constant dollar, total cross-border volume 12% and processed transactions 9%.

Cross-border totals can include intra-Europe activity; they should not automatically be treated as the higher-yielding ex-intra-Europe measure. The provided summary uses the total metric explicitly. Revenue growth above payments-volume growth reflects mix, pricing, services and timing, not necessarily a change in every transaction's fee.

Visa reports 1.93 billion diluted weighted-average Class A equivalent shares. That is a weighted-average conversion-based denominator rather than exact March outstanding shares. The company continues substantial shareholder distributions, but a complete current-quarter cash-flow bridge is not reproduced here.

Mastercard Q4 revenue was approximately $8.8 billion. GAAP net income was $4.1 billion, EPS $4.52 and adjusted EPS $4.76. Gross dollar volume rose 7% local currency, cross-border volume 14% and switched transactions 10%.

Mastercard value-added services and solutions revenue rose 26% reported and 22% currency-neutral. That is a disclosed growth rate, not an August portfolio manager's later estimate of services mix. The exact contribution to incremental operating profit requires additional segment reconciliation.

Full-year Mastercard revenue grew 16% reported and 15% currency-neutral. Both companies' latest data therefore show growing payments activity and monetization rather than an observed collapse in usage. These results precede any later AI-related disruption evidence.

Exact next-quarter/full-year guidance from the accompanying calls and a verified March consensus estimate were not recovered. The pack deliberately does not substitute later results or a later investor's forward multiple for missing guidance. An analyst must model spending, cross-border mix, pricing, incentives, services and capital return explicitly.

The key forward test is whether current cash generation remains durable as payment interfaces evolve. Strong January results support the starting earnings base but cannot alone disprove long-run fee compression or displacement.
