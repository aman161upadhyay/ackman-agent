Title: Marriott and Hyatt — modest industry RevPAR, differentiated room growth

Marriott and Hyatt are useful operating peers because their brand, management and franchise businesses also benefit from external owners' investment. However, geography, chain scale, owned-hotel mix, acquisitions and accounting definitions differ. Consolidated margins and enterprise multiples require adjustment before being treated as like-for-like measures.

| Q3 2025 | Hilton | Marriott | Hyatt |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Comparable system RevPAR change | (1.1%) | +0.5% | +0.3% |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $976m | $1,349m | $291m |
| Adjusted EPS | $2.11 | $2.47 | ($0.30) |
| Room-growth observation | 6.5% system net growth | Roughly 17,900 net rooms added in quarter | 12.1% including acquisitions; 7.0% excluding |

Marriott's November 4 release reports GAAP diluted EPS $2.67 and adjusted EPS $2.47. Adjusted EBITDA increased 10% and adjusted EPS 9%. Its call says global RevPAR's 0.5% growth reflected roughly 1% rate growth partly offset by lower occupancy. These observations suggest the modest-demand environment is not confined to Hilton, while Marriott's positive RevPAR contrasts with Hilton's negative quarter.

Hyatt's November 6 release reports $49 million GAAP net loss and $29 million adjusted net loss. Adjusted EBITDA rose 5.6%, or 10.1% after adjusting for assets sold in 2024. Gross fees were $283 million, up 5.9%. Its acquisition-inclusive room growth must not be labeled an organic rate.

The common industry mechanism is room additions plus fees on nominal lodging demand. Brand distribution, loyalty and owner profitability can create a reinforcing development loop. But owners can choose competing brands, conversions require investment, and larger pipelines do not automatically earn fees until hotels open.

Date-authenticated January 15 forward P/E, EV/EBITDA and reconciled FCF yield for these peers were not recovered. This table therefore benchmarks operations rather than claiming a complete relative valuation. It cannot establish that Hilton deserves its current premium merely because it has a high-quality fee model: competing franchisors also have capital-light attributes.

For an analyst evaluating an existing Hilton position, the useful questions are whether the RevPAR underperformance is mix/calendar-related, whether room growth remains superior after comparable definitions, and whether Hilton's expected per-share growth compensates for its earnings multiple. Strong industry structure helps business quality; it does not remove price discipline.
