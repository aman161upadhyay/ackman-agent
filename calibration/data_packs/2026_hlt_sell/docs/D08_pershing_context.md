PSH's December fact sheet, released January 15, lists Hilton among publicly disclosed holdings. Other names include Alphabet, Amazon, Brookfield, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, Hertz, Howard Hughes, Pershing Square SPARC, Restaurant Brands, Seaport, Uber and Universal Music. The list includes only publicly disclosed positions and is not a complete economic exposure report for that day.

The fact sheet reports $15.536 billion equity and $19.187 billion gross AUM, including debt principal. Its reported debt-to-total-capital ratio is 19.5%, and it lists thirteen issuer positions. These figures differ from manager-wide assets, current cash and US 13F value.

The most recent available US 13F at the cutoff is the September-quarter filing made November 14. Full line-item weights from that filing were not independently authenticated here. The older June-quarter secondary account reports about 21% Uber, 19% Brookfield, 11% Restaurant Brands and 9.3% Amazon in its reportable-book perimeter. These are stale contextual weights, not January PSH NAV weights. Exact current Hilton weight and current cash are unavailable in this snapshot.

H1 June net assets were $14.715 billion and cash/cash equivalents $1.404 billion. The H1 letter explains that permanent capital permits patient investment, but also discusses repositioning when market volatility presents better opportunities. It disclosed Canadian Pacific's April exit and maintained favorable Hilton business commentary. Later unpublished position information cannot be assumed from the passage of time.

The January fact sheet states the substantial majority of the portfolio is normally in 8–12 core holdings, usually liquid listed North American large caps. It seeks high-quality businesses with limited downside and predictable recurring cash flows, occasionally using managerial, operating or governance changes to create value. It also allows opportunistic hedging.

Hilton fits many of those qualitative preferences, but a portfolio decision must also compare prospective returns with other opportunities. Permanent capital removes redemption pressure; it does not eliminate the cost of owning a lower-return security. Neither a favorable business assessment nor a long ownership history is a stated instruction to ignore valuation.

No public pre-cutoff statement retrieved here commits Pershing to a minimum Hilton holding period, announces a new Hilton intervention or supplies a January intrinsic-value target. Those fields remain unknown rather than inferred from later disclosures.
