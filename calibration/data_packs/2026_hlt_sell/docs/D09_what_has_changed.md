Title: Re-underwriting an established franchise rather than replaying the original bargain
Source: compiled from: https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2022/10/07155526/2019-Semiannual-Financials-Letter-to-Shareholders.pdf; https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/20192925/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-June-2025-Interim.pdf; https://ir.hilton.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001585689-25-000158/q32025earningsrelease.htm; https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/HLT/history/?period1=1768176000&period2=1768608000
Date: 2026-01-15

The earliest recovered detailed thesis update, in 2019, emphasized a fee-based model, development pipeline, buybacks and management supporting mid-to-high-teens EPS compounding. The cited valuation was 23x consensus 2019 earnings. The question today is not whether those exact 2019 conditions can be recalled, but whether the remaining growth and the current price still offer a suitable return.

What appears intact: Hilton is still a large brand/distribution franchise, development pipeline is substantial, most hotel investment is external, and management continues to emphasize capital-light growth. PSH's August 2025 letter still describes the business favorably. Q3's 8% adjusted EBITDA growth despite negative comparable RevPAR supplies concrete evidence that the model is more resilient than a simple hotel-owner operating-leverage story.

What is different: the financial base is much larger. FY2024 adjusted EPS was $7.12, while the October outlook for FY2025 was $7.97–8.06. At $301.86, the stock is 37.7x that guidance midpoint, a calculated guidance-year multiple. The 2019 observation and current calculation are not perfectly like-for-like forward horizons, but the difference is large enough to require explicit valuation work rather than an assumption that the old purchase discount persists.

Growth expectations also need updating. The initial 2025 RevPAR forecast was 2–3%; the October forecast was flat to +1%. Net room additions can offset some weakness, but the development pipeline cannot remove all exposure to demand, owner financing or macroeconomic conditions. A higher multiple increases the cost of forecast errors.

Capital allocation is another change in the evidence set. Annual distributions remain substantial while net debt rose from $7.534 billion in 2022 to $10.600 billion in September 2025. Repurchases support per-share growth but require evaluating their cost and funding, not merely their existence.

The counterargument is that long-duration room growth and fee economics merit a premium and that weaker RevPAR is temporary. The valuation counterargument is that even an intact excellent franchise can offer insufficient future return at the current price. No resolved answer is embedded here; the analyst must forecast earnings, choose a defensible terminal valuation and compare holding with alternative uses of capital.
