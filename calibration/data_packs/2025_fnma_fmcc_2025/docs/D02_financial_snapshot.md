Title: Earnings rebuild net worth, but not unrestricted common capital

Both companies use December fiscal years. Full-year 2024 results have not been released. Financial-company net revenues and net worth are more relevant here than industrial EBITDA, capex or conventional net debt.

Fannie 2023 net revenues were $29.048 billion versus $29.735 billion in 2022, down 2%. Freddie Q3 2024 net revenues were $5.838 billion, up approximately 3% year over year, with net worth of $56.390 billion. A fully matched three-year revenue and expense table for both companies is not recovered.

Fannie's Q3 income declined from $4.484 billion in Q2, partly because fair-value gains and the benefit for credit losses declined. Nine-month income was $12.848 billion. Net worth increased by approximately $4.047 billion during Q3.

Freddie's Q3 net income increased $420 million year over year. The comparison benefits from an additional $313 million expense accrual in the prior-year quarter. Its earnings improvement is therefore not all recurring revenue growth.

GAAP common EPS does not equal total net income divided by existing legal common shares because senior preferred allocations and warrant-related share treatment matter. Freddie's release shows approximately 3.234 billion weighted-average common shares for EPS calculations; that is not a claim that every Treasury warrant has already been exercised.

There are no ordinary common dividends or buybacks to capitalize as current shareholder distributions. Capital retention builds accounting net worth while liquidation preferences can also increase. A conventional FCF yield, EV/EBITDA or net-debt ratio is not meaningful without a financial-institution balance-sheet framework.

The key financial sensitivity is normalized after-credit-loss earnings relative to required regulatory capital, followed by the allocation of that capital and earnings among Treasury, junior preferred and common holders.
