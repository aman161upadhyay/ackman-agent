Title: Mortgage guarantees, not ordinary unencumbered common equity
Source: compiled from: https://www.fanniemae.com/media/document/pdf/q32024release.pdf; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1026214/000102621424000098/a20243qerexhibit991.htm; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/310522/000031052224000632/fnm-20240930.htm
Date: 2024-10-31

Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac are separate government-sponsored mortgage-finance enterprises. They buy eligible mortgages, support securitization and guarantee mortgage-backed securities. Their function enables lenders to originate and distribute mortgage credit while investors receive securities with defined payment guarantees. Both have single-family and multifamily businesses.

Guarantee fees compensate for credit risk, capital, administration and the support arrangements surrounding the system. Scale, standardization, underwriting data, lender relationships and the depth of mortgage-security markets create valuable franchises. Those advantages coexist with statutory missions and political constraints; the companies are not free to maximize returns without regard to housing policy.

They remain in FHFA conservatorship, begun in 2008. The conservator exercises powers that ordinary shareholders and boards would normally hold. Treasury's senior preferred stock purchase agreements provide financial backing while also placing senior economic claims ahead of common equity.

Treasury holds warrants for 79.9% of each company's common stock on a fully diluted basis after exercise, under their terms. Existing common ownership percentages therefore overstate participation in any normalized earnings pool unless warrant dilution is incorporated. Junior preferred securities have different priority and capped contractual claims.

As of September 30, Fannie reported $90.530 billion net worth and Freddie $56.390 billion. These accounting capital balances are not freely distributable common equity. Treasury liquidation preferences, regulatory capital requirements and contractual restrictions must be analyzed separately.

Fannie CEO Priscilla Almodovar emphasizes supporting housing and increasing net worth. Management operates under government oversight; a good quarterly result cannot unilaterally end conservatorship, cancel Treasury claims or authorize common dividends.

A conventional company analysis asks whether earnings are durable and reinvestment attractive. Here the analyst must add a second question: who owns the economic benefit under current and potential future contracts? The businesses can earn billions while reported common EPS is near zero or negative because amounts accrue to senior preferred interests. Market capitalization divided by total enterprise net income is consequently not a valid ordinary common P/E.
