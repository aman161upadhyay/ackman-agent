Title: From election catalyst to a new consent requirement
Source: compiled from: https://www.cnn.com/2024/12/02/economy/fannie-mae-freddie-mac-mortgage-rates-housing/index.html; https://x.com/BillAckman/status/1873818034428694837; https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy2767; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/310522/000031052225000004/fnm-20250102.htm
Date: 2025-01-15

October 30–31, 2024: Freddie and Fannie released profitable Q3 results, with $56.4 billion and $90.5 billion net worth. The operating baseline entering the election was continued retained earnings, not financial distress comparable to 2008.

November 2024: Trump's election generated expectations that the incoming administration could resume the first term's unfinished recapitalization and release effort. This is a political catalyst, not an automatic contractual change. The companies remain in conservatorship.

December 2, 2024: CNN described the anticipated privatization effort and the potential for higher mortgage costs if investors lose confidence in the government relationship. A viable plan needs to address housing-market stability as well as taxpayer and shareholder economics.

December 30, 2024: Ackman publicly outlined a scenario crediting past Treasury payments toward repayment of senior preferred, setting a 2.5% capital requirement and pursuing hypothetical Q4 2026 IPOs. He estimated approximately $34 intrinsic value and $31 offering prices, assuming full Treasury warrant exercise and future government stock sales.

The post explicitly says these holdings have not been good investments to date and acknowledges substantial uncertainty. It announces an intention to share a detailed presentation in the new year, not an already public set of later slides.

January 2, 2025: Treasury and FHFA announced preferred-stock purchase agreement amendments restoring Treasury consent to release. FHFA agreed to a public-input process about market and housing impacts.

January 7, 2025: Fannie's SEC filing described those amendments. Neither the filing nor the official release cancels Treasury's senior preferred, exercises the warrants or lists common stock on an exchange.

January 15, 2025: historical closes are $6.21 FNMA and $5.66 FMCC following a large rally from early October. The outgoing administration remains in office; the incoming administration's detailed implementation choices are unresolved.

The timeline stops on January 15. A later presentation or statement cannot be used to add extra policy details. Current analysis should treat the December30 proposal as advocacy and a scenario, not a completed deal or an authenticated government commitment.
