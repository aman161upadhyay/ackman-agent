Title: The election rally and the difference between market price and scenario value

Date: 2025-01-15

The date-bounded historical series gives January 15 closes of $6.21 for FNMA and $5.66 for FMCC. Volumes were 46,566,200 and 19,818,800 shares, respectively. The instruments are OTC common stocks, with trading and ownership considerations different from a major-exchange constituent.

For context, October 1 closes were $1.17 and $1.14. Those starting observations precede the November election. By January 15 the calculated increases were approximately 431% and 396%, respectively. The observation window's intraday highs were $7.80 FNMA and $7.15 FMCC, putting the closes approximately 20.4% and 20.8% below those highs. These are October-to-January observations, not a verified 52-week range.

Average daily volume during that window was approximately 16.46 million FNMA and 7.47 million FMCC. Liquidity rose around policy events, but a highly liquid rally can still reverse when expectations change.

Reuters's December 30 report says the shares gained 18.4% and 18% after Ackman's post. His post estimates approximately $34 intrinsic value per share at hypothetical 2026 IPOs and assumes approximately $31 offering prices. These are investor scenarios, not consensus targets or government-approved transaction prices.

At $6.21 and $5.66, the gap to $34 is large, but depends on senior-preferred treatment, warrant exercise, regulatory capital and any new issuance. A probability-weighted valuation must include unfavorable outcomes, including heavy dilution or indefinite conservatorship. It is not enough to divide the target by the current price.

An ordinary trailing common P/E is not meaningful because total enterprise net income is not currently available to common shareholders. EV/EBITDA is similarly unsuitable for these mortgage-finance balance sheets. Point-in-time fully diluted market capitalization, consensus common EPS and a long-run normalized multiple history are not recovered.

The existing holder faces a different question from a new buyer: whether the rally sufficiently discounts a more credible policy path, or whether the remaining conditional upside still outweighs the unchanged contractual risks.

The answer requires explicit probabilities and security-level dilution assumptions.
