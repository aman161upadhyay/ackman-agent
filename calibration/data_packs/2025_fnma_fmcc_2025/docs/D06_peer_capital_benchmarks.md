Title: Capital rules and the limits of the mortgage-insurer comparison
Source: compiled from: https://www.fhfa.gov/document/Fact-Sheet-Final-Rule-Amend-Enterprise-Regulatory-Capital-Framework; https://x.com/BillAckman/status/1873818034428694837; https://www.fanniemae.com/media/document/pdf/q32024release.pdf; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1026214/000102621424000098/a20243qerexhibit991.htm
Date: 2024-12-30

Fannie and Freddie are each other's closest franchise comparables. Private mortgage insurers, banks and other guarantors can help test credit economics, but no listed company has an identical combination of government mission, mortgage-security scale, Treasury support and conservatorship restrictions.

The FHFA's February 2022 capital-rule fact sheet states a minimum tier-one leverage ratio of 2.5% of adjusted total assets, plus a leverage buffer. It replaces the earlier fixed 1.5% buffer with a dynamic buffer equal to half the stability capital buffer. Risk-based requirements also apply. Thus “the capital rule is 2.5%” is an incomplete description of the binding framework.

Ackman's December 30 proposal calls for 2.5% of guarantees outstanding. That is his preferred scenario, with a different simplified framing, not evidence that the current buffers and risk-based tests disappear. He argues that such capital could cover nearly seven times actual realized financial-crisis losses.

He compares this with private mortgage insurers that insure a first-loss portion of the mortgage, while GSE guarantees generally cover loans with borrower equity and, where required, mortgage insurance. This is his explanation for why the GSEs can support a lower capital ratio. The comparison should be stress-tested for correlated housing losses, model risk, operating needs and support fees.

At September 30, reported net worth was approximately $90.5 billion Fannie and $56.4 billion Freddie. These are accounting balances. Ackman's December post cites $168 billion combined capital accumulated since the sweep ended in 2019; his “capital” measure should not be silently equated with the two quarter-end GAAP net-worth figures.

A verified table of MGIC/Radian/Essent forward multiples, margins and matching regulatory ratios is not recovered. No present-day peer valuation data are substituted. Capital adequacy is more important here than a superficial cheap P/E comparison.

The analyst must distinguish three questions: whether the guarantee franchises are economically sound; whether a proposed capital requirement is prudent and politically acceptable; and what equity ownership remains after recapitalization. Favorable answers to the first do not automatically establish the second or third.
