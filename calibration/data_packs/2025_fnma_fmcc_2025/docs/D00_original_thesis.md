Title: Original investment rationale and the limits of the recovered record
Source: compiled from: https://www.reuters.com/article/markets/stocks/ackmans-pershing-square-takes-big-stakes-in-freddie-fannie-idUSBRE9AE0TO; https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2014/09/08113002/PSH-Annual-Report.pdf
Date: 2015-03-24

Pershing publicly disclosed the common-stock investment in November 2013. Reuters reported an approximately $500 million commitment and ownership of 9.98% of outstanding Fannie common and 9.77% of Freddie common. These are existing-share percentages, not ownership after Treasury exercises its warrants. The exact first execution date and a verified original 2013 purchase letter are not recovered.

The nearest authenticated detailed letter used here is the March 24, 2015 annual report, which describes the investment and its reasoning retrospectively. It is public well before this pack's January 2025 cutoff, but must not be mislabeled a contemporaneous 2013 quotation.

The letter says: “we believe that there is no credible alternative to replace them.” It connects the companies' role to the availability of the 30-year, prepayable, fixed-rate mortgage and argues that a renewed, recapitalized system is preferable to shutting them down.

It also states: “We view this net worth sweep as an unlawful taking of shareholders’ private property”. This is Pershing's legal position, not an adjudicated conclusion that Treasury's claim is invalid. Enterprise profitability and shareholder rights are different parts of the thesis.

The underlying economic proposition is that essential guarantee franchises can generate substantial recurring earnings if allowed to retain capital and operate under a viable regulatory arrangement. The security proposition is more contingent: common owners need a resolution of senior claims and government control that leaves meaningful value after dilution.

The letter records buying additional shares after the September 2014 adverse district-court ruling and concentrating legal resources on constitutional takings claims. Thus the investor's willingness to hold through adverse developments is documented; it does not establish that every legal theory succeeded.

For the current reassessment, the original thesis supplies four tests: the franchises remain necessary; underlying earnings can rebuild capital; government policy can evolve toward recapitalization; and common shareholders retain sufficient economic participation. A rising share price alone does not satisfy those tests. Nor should a decade of waiting automatically disprove franchise value if the capital structure and policy path are materially different today.
