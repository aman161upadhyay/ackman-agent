Title: What changed since purchase, and what must still happen
Source: compiled from: https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sm786; https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-422_new_l5gm.pdf; https://x.com/BillAckman/status/1873818034428694837; https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy2767
Date: 2025-01-15

The original position combined valuable operating franchises with a legal and policy overhang. Several things have changed since2013, but none converts current common into an unencumbered claim on all earnings.

In September2019 Treasury and FHFA allowed greater retention of earnings. Subsequent arrangements continued capital accumulation. The beneficial change is growing accounting resources inside the businesses rather than a perpetual cash sweep. The limiting fact is that Treasury's liquidation preference is not automatically extinguished by retaining earnings.

The Supreme Court's June23,2021 Collins decision rejected the broad statutory challenge to FHFA's authority to enter the relevant amendment and held the director-removal restriction unconstitutional. It did not order wholesale cancellation of the sweep, privatization or a windfall for every common shareholder. The current thesis cannot rest on pretending the litigation has already produced that result.

The2024 election changes the potential administrative decision-makers. Ackman's December30 post therefore shifts attention toward negotiation and recapitalization. He proposes crediting dividends against Treasury senior preferred at its stated10% rate, with an additional approximately$25billion benefit to government beyond that return.

He assumes a2.5%capital standard and hypothetical Q4 2026 IPOs requiring approximately$30billion combined new capital. He estimates approximately$34 per-share intrinsic values and$31 IPO prices. His taxpayer proceeds estimate exceeds$300billion, assuming full warrant exercise and a sell-down over five years.

These inputs form a conditional bridge, not adopted policy:

| Necessary step | Status at cutoff |
|---|---|
| Treatment of senior preferred | Proposed credit/repayment interpretation, unresolved |
| Treasury warrants | Outstanding; dilution must be included |
| Capital standard and new issuance | Investor assumptions, not approved replacement rule |
| Relisting/IPO and release sequence | Not completed or finally agreed |
| Continuing federal support | Contractual/policy terms require resolution |

The January2 amendments add Treasury consent and public-input requirements. Existing holders therefore have stronger operating capital and a plausible political catalyst, but still face seniority, dilution and timing risk. A HOLD or ADD argument must explain why this changed probability distribution outweighs the rally and opportunity cost; it cannot rely solely on remembering the original cheap purchase.
