Title: Competitor growth complicates a purely macro explanation

Adidas and On provide concrete benchmarks for athletic footwear and apparel demand. Their currencies, channel mix, product positioning and business maturity differ from Nike's. No unadjusted cross-company EPS or market-cap comparison is appropriate.

Adidas reported gross margin up 0.5 percentage points despite currency and tariff effects, and operating profit up 23%. Its operating margin improved 1.8 points. This demonstrates that the same broad industry environment does not produce uniform outcomes.

On reported gross profit of CHF460.8 million versus CHF340.2 million, with margin up from 59.9% to 61.5%. The company attributed strength partly to a greater direct-channel share, operational execution and favorable FX. Yet its net loss was CHF40.9 million versus CHF30.8 million net income, illustrating that gross margin does not settle bottom-line results.

Nike's current channel repair moves toward rebuilding wholesale while On's favorable mix includes a growing direct share. There is no universal rule that direct selling is always superior or inferior. Brand momentum, customer acquisition, fulfillment cost, inventory and retailer relationships determine the economics.

The competitive concern is not just that rivals exist. They are demonstrating growth and profitability that can attract consumers, shelf space and athletic communities while Nike resets. A recovery forecast should include realistic share and innovation assumptions.

Conversely, smaller premium brands do not have Nike's scale, breadth or global athlete ecosystem. Their success does not prove Nike cannot recover. The evidence argues for company-specific analysis rather than either automatic moat permanence or automatic obsolescence.

Cutoff peer forward valuations and harmonized ROIC/FCF yields were not verified. The table is an operating benchmark, not a fabricated precise investment ranking.
