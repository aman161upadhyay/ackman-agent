Title: The repair plan and evolving evidence through November 3

Date: 2025-11-03

Spring 2024: Pershing initiated the investment on a brand-repair thesis, according to its subsequently published March 2025 account. It expected either successful execution of a revised strategy or leadership change. Hill's later appointment was not already public.

Late June 2024: Results exposed a deeper near-term revenue and operating-deleverage problem than Pershing had expected. The March report explicitly acknowledges that underwriting error, making it legitimate information at this current cutoff.

September 19, 2024: Nike announced Hill would return as CEO, effective October 14. His operating history strengthened the repair case, but did not erase the need for inventory cleanup, marketing investment and rebuilt wholesale relationships.

Early 2025: Pershing converted common shares to deep-in-the-money calls, as publicly explained in March. The common-stock line's disappearance would therefore not, by itself, establish that economic exposure ended.

March 14: Pershing's annual report outlined a medium-term return to high-single-digit revenue growth and margins more than double then-current levels. It argued the call structure provided multi-year turnaround duration while freeing capital. These were forecasts and structure descriptions, not realized results.

June/July 2025: Full-year results and the 10-K documented fiscal revenue of $46.309 billion, EPS $2.16 and materially weaker EBIT, FCF and ROIC than fiscal 2024. Management's sourcing and pricing plans sought to mitigate new tariff costs.

September 30: Fiscal Q1 revenue stabilized, wholesale grew and North America improved. Gross margin still fell 320 basis points, EPS declined 30%, and China remained weak. Management forecast continuing Q2 margin pressure and no direct-channel growth during fiscal 2026.

October 29: Adidas reported strong brand growth, profit and margins. This supplies a fresh competitor benchmark before the cutoff and limits the persuasiveness of attributing every Nike difficulty solely to industry conditions.

November 3: Nike closed $62.65. The snapshot ends here. Later portfolio decisions, subsequent quarters and eventual turnaround outcomes are excluded. The present question is how much of the earnings decline is temporary repair expense versus a lower sustainable profitability level.
