Title: Global athletic brand with outsourced production and a channel reset
Source: compiled from: https://s1.q4cdn.com/806093406/files/doc_financials/2025/ar/Nike-Inc-2025_10K.pdf; https://investors.nike.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/555/nike-inc-reports-fiscal-2026-first-quarter-results
Date: 2025-09-30

Nike designs, markets and sells athletic footwear, apparel and equipment through wholesale partners and Nike Direct, including owned retail and digital. It also owns Jordan and Converse brands. Manufacturing is substantially outsourced, but brand building, inventory, distribution and retail still require meaningful ongoing investment.

The moat case rests on global recognition, athlete relationships, product innovation, scale, distribution and consumer aspiration. These assets can support premium pricing and retailer demand. They do not guarantee that every product cycle succeeds or that consumers cannot switch to newer brands.

Fiscal 2025 revenue was $46.309 billion, down from $51.362 billion. Q1 fiscal 2026 revenue was $11.720 billion, up 1% reported and down 1% currency-neutral. Nike Direct revenue was $4.5 billion, down 4%, while wholesale was $6.8 billion, up 7%. These are rounded channel figures and do not represent all consolidated revenue by themselves.

The channel shift is deliberate. Management is rebuilding wholesale partnerships and reducing reliance on discount-driven direct sales. Improved wholesale shipments can signal retailer confidence, but they must ultimately be supported by consumer sell-through rather than simply replenishing the channel.

North American Q1 revenue was $5.020 billion, up 4%. Greater China revenue was $1.512 billion, down 9% reported and 10% currency-neutral. EMEA was $3.331 billion and Asia-Pacific/Latin America $1.490 billion. Geographic dispersion provides diversification but also exposes the company to local competition, currencies and trade policy.

Elliott Hill is CEO. His “Win Now” program emphasizes sport, innovation, brand storytelling, marketplace execution and closer local/customer engagement. The approach assumes Nike's strengths can be reactivated, rather than requiring the brand to be rebuilt from nothing.

Q1 gross margin was 42.2%, down 320 basis points, and EBIT $904 million. Outsourcing production does not remove exposure to tariffs, supplier economics, markdowns or inventory cleanup. The capacity to restore margins depends partly on factors beyond Nike's direct control.

The relevant quality question is therefore not whether the swoosh remains recognizable. It is whether innovation, distribution and consumer preference can produce a sufficiently predictable growth and profit stream after the repair costs and any structural industry changes.
