Title: September results — a wholesale improvement with continuing profit pressure
Source: compiled from: https://investors.nike.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/555/nike-inc-reports-fiscal-2026-first-quarter-results; https://s1.q4cdn.com/806093406/files/doc_financials/2026/q1/NIKE-Inc-Q1FY26-OFFICIAL-Transcript_-FINAL.pdf
Date: 2025-09-30

Nike's fiscal Q1 revenue increased 1% reported to $11.720 billion, although currency-neutral revenue fell 1%. Wholesale grew 7% reported and 5% currency-neutral; Nike Direct declined 4% reported and 5% currency-neutral. Nike Brand Digital fell 12%.

North America increased 4%, while Greater China declined 9% reported. This is mixed regional evidence rather than an across-the-board recovery. Management expects China revenue and margins to remain headwinds throughout fiscal 2026 and says progress requires time and investment.

Gross margin declined 320 basis points to 42.2%. EBIT was $904 million and diluted EPS $0.49, down 30%. Revenue stabilization is not yet equivalent to earnings normalization. Inventory of approximately $8.1 billion was down 2%, indicating some cleanup progress without settling the long-term margin question.

For fiscal Q2, management expects reported revenue down low single digits, including a one-point FX benefit. It forecasts gross margin down approximately 300–375 basis points, SG&A up high single digits and a tax rate in the low twenties. These are dated forecasts, not later realized results.

The call estimates an annualized gross tariff cost of approximately $1.5 billion, with mitigation reducing the fiscal-year net gross-margin headwind to about 120 basis points. Gross cost, annualized run rate and net-year margin impact are different measures and should not be added together.

Mitigation involves sourcing, supplier/retailer coordination and selective pricing. Those levers can reduce costs but may affect demand or partner economics. The call does not establish that all tariff effects can be passed through without volume consequences.

Management does not expect Nike Direct to return to growth in fiscal 2026 while repositioning toward full-price selling. That is a longer repair horizon than a simple one-quarter inventory reset. Wholesale strength should be tested against sustained retailer sell-through and consumer demand.

Hill's program returns organizational attention to sport, innovation and integrated marketplace execution. This is an identifiable plan, but a credible downside case must allow for weaker China, slower direct-channel recovery, additional investment and margins below old peaks.

The current evidence is consequently better than an uninterrupted sales collapse, yet still inconsistent with treating historical profitability as an immediately attainable base case.
