Title: Economic exposure, released capital and the concentrated-fund hurdle

August gross core-fund AUM was $20.7111 billion, with PSH gross assets $18.5445 billion and firm-plus-Howard-Hughes assets $30.4653 billion. These figures include financing or different entity perimeters and must not be used as interchangeable NAV measures.

The August transparency report gives PSH NAV $15.3991 billion, cash 6.55% and debt -20.43%. These are dated scale/liquidity observations, not November balances. The June interim report lists a concentrated portfolio spanning technology platforms, brands, franchisors, music, real estate and financial-policy exposure.

Public names include Alphabet, Amazon, Brookfield, Chipotle, Fannie/Freddie, Hertz, Hilton, Howard Hughes, Nike exposure, Restaurant Brands, Uber and Universal Music. Exact current NAV weights are unavailable here; a 13F-only table would omit important foreign, OTC and derivative holdings.

At December 2024, PSH held 16,953,888 Nike common shares worth $1.2829 billion, or 9.86% of NAV. That is PSH alone, not the consolidated firm's roughly 18.8 million common-share peak, and not original cost. The denominators must remain distinct.

The March report publicly explains replacing common stock with deep-in-the-money calls in early 2025. Its stated purpose was to preserve upside exposure while releasing capital for new investments. An analyst cannot infer a final exit from a disappearing common-stock line without examining options.

The structure does not make risk disappear. Exact strikes, maturities, premiums, deltas and current exposure were not recovered. Underlying price returns and option returns can differ materially, and an option's multi-year tenor does not guarantee the business will recover within it.

The original fund thesis supports patient ownership through near-term volatility if the profit-recovery path remains credible. Concentration also raises the cost of a forecast whose range becomes too broad. The current evidence should be compared with other potential uses of capital rather than with original purchase price alone.

No post-cutoff decision or investor explanation is included. The live review must distinguish a financing choice that maintained exposure from a change in confidence about the economic turnaround itself. Past success in the separate earlier Nike investment is not evidence about today's forecast.
