Title: From fixable execution errors to a more demanding earnings recovery

Date: 2025-10-29

The initial case was a valuable global brand suffering from reversible strategic mistakes. Wholesale neglect, a less effective organization and overreliance on mature lifestyle products provided identifiable operational levers. A leadership change was one possible path, not an event known before purchase.

The leadership milestone has occurred. Hill returned in October 2024 and is refocusing on sport, innovation and marketplace execution. North American growth and wholesale improvement in fiscal Q1 are tangible evidence that some repairs are working.

The depth of the earnings decline has nevertheless exceeded the early expectation. FY2025 EPS was $2.16 versus $3.73 a year earlier, EBIT margin 8.2% versus 12.7%, and ROIC 20.2% versus 34.9%. Cash generation weakened substantially. These are not merely share-price changes.

The public March thesis anticipated medium-term margins more than double then-current levels. That expectation now requires explicit testing against ongoing direct-channel weakness, China pressure and new tariff costs. A projected recovery cannot rely solely on the fact that margins were higher before.

Tariffs introduce a potential structural cost change beyond the original product/channel repair. Management estimates $1.5 billion annualized gross exposure and about 120 basis points net fiscal-year margin pressure after mitigation. Pricing and sourcing can help, but their effectiveness and demand consequences are not certain.

Competitors are also demonstrating strength. Adidas's October release reports double-digit brand growth and improving profitability. On's earlier results show strong gross-profit growth and premium margins. The industry opportunity remains attractive, but Nike may need to work harder for share and cannot assume automatic recapture.

The balance sheet remains a strength, with substantial cash and investments relative to funded debt. The brand, athlete ecosystem and wholesale reach remain valuable. These facts argue against equating a difficult turnaround with total franchise destruction.

The key change is the range and timing of plausible normalized earnings. Continued holding would require confidence in a revised recovery path and sufficient prospective return at $62.65. A lower historical price comparison or a recognized logo is not a substitute for that forecast. The option structure adds a duration and exposure question that cannot be resolved without its actual terms.
