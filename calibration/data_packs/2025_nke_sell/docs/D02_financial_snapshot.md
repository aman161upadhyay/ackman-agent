Nike's ROIC was 20.2% in FY2025 versus 34.9% in FY2024. Its defined invested capital includes lease obligations and uses five-quarter average balances. That series is meaningful evidence of deterioration, but should not be compared to a peer ROIC definition without reconciliation.

FY2025 cash-flow distributions of $5.285 billion exceeded the calculated $3.268 billion CFO-minus-capex residual. Cash fell, and the company repaid $1.0 billion of borrowings. Lower physical capex partly cushions FCF, but marketing and product investment are expensed and remain economically necessary.

The latest quarter, ended August 31, 2025, produced revenue $11.720 billion, EBIT $904 million, net income approximately $0.7 billion and EPS $0.49. Diluted weighted-average shares were 1,479.0 million, basic 1,476.6 million. A complete latest-quarter CFO reconciliation is not included.

Quarter-end cash was $7.024 billion and short-term investments $1.551 billion, total $8.575 billion. Long-term debt was $7.996 billion, with no current long-term maturity in the extracted balance sheet. Calculated net cash on those definitions was $579 million, before leases and any other debt adjustments.

Inventory was $8.114 billion, down 2% year over year. Gross margin fell to 42.2% from 45.4%. A lower inventory balance is encouraging, but does not independently prove normalized full-price sell-through or restored product demand.
