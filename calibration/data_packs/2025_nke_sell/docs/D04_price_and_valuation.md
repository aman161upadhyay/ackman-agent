Nike's November 3 unadjusted close was $62.65, on 22,633,300 shares. The session opened $64.72 and ranged from $62.34 to $64.85. October 31 closed $64.59. These dated rows are separated from current quote widgets.

Reuters' September 5 market snapshot gave a 52-week range of $52.28–90.62. This is a stale but already-public range, not a verified November 3 rolling range. The cutoff close is 30.9% below that observed high, calculated. No later low or subsequent drawdown is imported.

Using Q1 basic weighted-average shares of 1.4766 billion gives a calculated equity-value proxy of $92.509 billion. The denominator is not exact cutoff shares. Deducting $579 million approximate net cash gives an EV-like proxy of $91.930 billion before leases and other adjustments.

FY2025 EPS of $2.16 implies a calculated 29.0x historical P/E. The same price divided by FY2024 EPS of $3.73 is 16.8x. The difference is the entire underwriting problem: whether older earnings are a credible future recovery benchmark, not a reason to label the stock automatically cheap.

FY2025 CFO minus physical capex was $3.268 billion, a calculated 3.5% yield against the equity proxy. FY2024's $6.617 billion residual would produce a much higher yield, but treating it as normalized requires evidence that revenue, margins and working capital can recover.

EV-like value to FY2025 EBIT of $3.778 billion is approximately 24.3x. This is EBIT, not EBITDA, and lease treatment limits comparability with data-vendor enterprise values.

FactSet's October 31 S&P 500 forward P/E was 22.9x, versus five- and ten-year averages of 19.9x and 18.6x. Nike's historical P/E cannot be ranked against that forward number without a consistent forward earnings estimate.
