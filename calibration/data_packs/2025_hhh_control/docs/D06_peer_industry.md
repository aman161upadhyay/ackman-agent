Title: A land developer comparison and the dangers of aggregate multiples
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/745308/000155837024013519/joe-20241023xex99d1.htm; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1981792/000162828024044983/hhhearningsreleaseq32024.htm; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1981792/000162828024007196/hhhearningsreleaseq42023.htm
Date: 2024-11-04

St.Joe is an imperfect but useful public comparison because it combines land, real-estate development, hospitality and recurring property income. Its geographic concentration in northwest Florida and different joint-venture structure make a direct consolidated-margin comparison hazardous.

The October23 Q3 release reports St.Joe revenue of$99.0 million, down2% from$101.4 million. Real-estate revenue declined32% to$28.0 million while hospitality revenue increased17% to$55.4 million. Operating income increased4% to$21.2 million. Different segments can move in opposite directions even when aggregate revenue looks stable.

HHH's Q3 continuing revenue was approximately$327.1 million and Operating Assets NOI$64.8 million, while MPC EBT reached$144.8 million. These measures have different definitions from St.Joe's operating income and must not be lined up as if they were identical consolidated EBIT margins.

HHH's2023 MPC EBT of$341.4 million and Operating Assets NOI of$244.4 million provide annual scale references. Its condo recognition depends on closings rather than smooth monthly revenue. St.Joe's hospitality mix has different seasonality and capital needs.

Homebuilders are another possible benchmark, but a builder generally turns lots and houses more quickly than a community owner controlling decades of development. A REIT benchmark may value stabilized rental cash flows while missing land and condominium development. No single peer multiple cleanly prices all three HHH segments.

Reliable cutoff-date peer forward P/Es, adjusted book values and cap-rate appraisals are not recovered. No invented peer NAV is supplied. A proper comparison would separate stabilized NOI, developable acreage, infrastructure obligations, entitled projects and corporate overhead before applying consistent assumptions.

The industry evidence supports analyzing recurring cash flow and development cash generation separately. It also warns that replacing a mixed company's headline P/E with an optimistic sum-of-parts does not automatically improve rigor. The value bridge must incorporate spending, timing, debt and taxes, then test how a different capital-allocation structure could improve realized per-share returns rather than merely rebrand the same assets.
