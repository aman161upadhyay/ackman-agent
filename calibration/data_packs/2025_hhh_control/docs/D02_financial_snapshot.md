Howard Hughes uses December fiscal years. The annual figures below come from releases before the Seaport separation and are not identical in perimeter to2024 continuing operations. Large condo closings and impairments make annual revenue and EPS unusually lumpy.

The2023 revenue decline is approximately36.3%, calculated. It should not be equated with a similar decline in recurring land/property economics: MPC EBT increased21%, while the timing of condominium deliveries and a large Seaport impairment materially affected the consolidated accounts.

The2023 third-quarter release recorded a $555.0 million after-tax Seaport impairment. Historical impairment is relevant to capital-allocation risk, but the asset has since been separated; it is not a forecast of the current real-estate portfolio's recurring annual loss.

Latest Q3 continuing revenue was approximately$327.1 million. Continuing net income was$96.5 million and diluted EPS$1.95, versus$0.64 a year earlier. The quarter included insurance-settlement and MUD-receivable effects, so earnings require a normalization bridge.

September30 cash was$400.7 million and reported debt approximately$5.299 billion. Management also cited$1.5 billion undrawn lender commitments for property development. Those commitments are not unrestricted corporate cash or net debt reduction.

A consistent three-year operating-cash-flow, development-capex and ROIC series is not recovered. Conventional FCF would need to distinguish maintenance, growth infrastructure and capitalized development. No claim that all capex is discretionary is made. The underwriting focus is asset value net of debt, future infrastructure/project spending, taxes and the time needed to realize cash.

Cash realization is the essential link between appraised assets and shareholder returns.
