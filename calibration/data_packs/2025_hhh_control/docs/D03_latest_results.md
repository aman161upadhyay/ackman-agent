Title: Strong land demand and operating NOI, with unusual quarterly items
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1981792/000162828024044983/hhhearningsreleaseq32024.htm
Date: 2024-11-04

The latest release reports record Q3 MPC EBT of$144.8 million, up71% from$84.8 million. Residential land revenue increased184%;191 residential acres were sold at an average approximately$1 million per acre. The average price was13% higher year over year.

Land sales are not equivalent to an indefinitely recurring quarterly annuity. Summerlin's sales mix, superpad timing and the particular acreage sold matter. The quarter included129 acres of Summerlin superpads at approximately$1.3 million per acre, versus39 acres a year earlier at a similar price.

Operating Assets NOI was$64.8 million, up8%, including unconsolidated ventures. Office NOI was$31.8 million, multifamily$15.9 million and retail$13.0 million. Stabilized office, multifamily and retail were88%,95% and94% leased, respectively. These disclosures allow a more granular view of recurring property economics than consolidated EPS alone.

CEO O'Reilly said the company had “a streamlined portfolio and refined strategic focus on the development of our world-class master planned communities”. The July Seaport spinoff supports that publicly stated focus. No broader financial-services expansion is announced in this release.

Management raised full-year MPC EBT guidance to a$330 million midpoint and Operating Assets NOI to approximately$257 million. Victoria Place had been completed and was100% presold; expected fourth-quarter closings would contribute approximately$760 million condo revenue at roughly27.5% gross margin. Those figures are guidance, not reported Q4 results.

Q3 included$90 million insurance proceeds related to the Waiea construction-defect settlement and$12.1 million additional project costs. A MUD receivable sale generated$176.7 million cash while recognizing a$51.5 million GAAP loss; proceeds helped pay down the Bridgeland borrowing.

The mix illustrates why accounting earnings, cash receipts and recurring earning power diverge. Receivable monetization accelerates cash that otherwise arrives later, while insurance proceeds resolve past losses rather than create a new recurring franchise. A valuation should reconcile these effects and retain adequate capital for unfinished projects before estimating surplus cash available to shareholders or new investments.
