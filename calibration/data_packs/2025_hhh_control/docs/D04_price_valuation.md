Applying September30 outstanding common shares of50,132,370 gives illustrative equity capitalization of$3.5985 billion. Adding approximately$5.2988 billion reported debt and subtracting$400.7 million cash yields illustrative enterprise value of$8.4966 billion. Minority interests, leases, project restrictions and later share changes require further adjustments; this is not an exact acquisition price.

At the guidance midpoint, Operating Assets NOI is approximately$257 million. An analyst sensitivity capitalizing that at5%–7% gives gross property values of approximately$5.14 billion–$3.67 billion. These are explicitly illustrative assumptions, not appraisals or company NAV estimates.

The sensitivity shows that valuing only the rental assets is insufficient. Land inventory, future development rights, condominium cash flows and other assets must supply substantial additional value, while debt, development obligations, overhead and taxes absorb value. The appropriate bridge must avoid counting future development profit twice through both land value and completed-property value.

MPC EBT guidance of$330 million supplies a current earning-power reference for land operations, but multiplying it by a perpetual multiple without considering finite acreage and reinvestment is risky. Long-duration land can appreciate while still requiring infrastructure spending and time to monetize.

A verified company-stated NAV, peer-adjusted forward P/E, EV/EBITDA and historical discount-to-NAV range were not recovered. Conventional P/E is also distorted by project timing, discontinued operations and unusual settlement items.

The valuation case is therefore an asset-and-cash-flow underwriting exercise, not a mechanical cheap-multiple purchase. Any control premium or new capital structure should be justified by a credible change in the rate, use or durability of future surplus cash, not simply by the possibility of a transaction.

Sensitivity to project delays and higher financing costs is especially important.
