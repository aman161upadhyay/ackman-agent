Title: A focused challenger demonstrates growth; an incumbent shows turnaround risk
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1858985/000185898524000014/ex991pressreleasedatedmarc.htm; https://www.adidas-group.com/en/media/press-releases/adidas-performs-better-than-expected-in-transition-year-2023; https://investors.nike.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/24/nike-inc-reports-fiscal-2024-third-quarter-results
Date: 2024-03-21

On and Adidas provide two different comparisons. On is a much smaller premium challenger with high growth; Adidas is a global incumbent undergoing its own transition. Neither has Nike's exact mix, accounting standards or fiscal calendar.

| Latest completed year available | Nike FY2023 | On CY2023 | Adidas CY2023 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | $51.217bn | CHF1.7921bn | EUR21.427bn |
| Reported revenue growth | 10% | 46.6% | (4.8)% |
| Gross margin | 43.5% | 59.6% | 47.5% |

On's March 12 release reports adjusted EBITDA margin of 15.5%, up from 13.5%, and net income CHF79.6 million. Wholesale sales increased 44.2% while direct sales increased 50.9%. That matters because a challenger can grow through both channels simultaneously; the industry is not simply shifting every consumer transaction away from wholesale.

On guided 2024 constant-currency sales growth of at least 30%, approximately 60% gross margin and 16.0%–16.5% adjusted EBITDA margin. Those are management targets, not reported 2024 results. The high growth starts from a small base and should not be mechanically applied to a company nearly thirty times larger in sales.

Adidas's March 13 release characterizes 2023 as a transition year, with currency-neutral sales flat and gross margin 47.5%. Its reported sales decline partly reflects currency and company-specific circumstances. A low growth rate at an incumbent does not prove that the whole sportswear market lacks attractive demand.

Comparable pre-cutoff forward P/Es and consensus EPS growth were not recovered. Gross margin comparisons must also account for channel mix, brand positioning, freight and product categories; a premium specialist can have higher gross margin without possessing a larger total competitive advantage.

The industry lesson is that scale creates sponsorship and distribution resources, but focused competitors can win consumer attention in narrow categories. Nike's investment case requires evidence that its innovation engine can respond, not merely that its marketing budget or sales base is larger.
