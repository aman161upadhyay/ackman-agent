Title: A large brand franchise whose direct-channel strategy is being tested
Source: compiled from: https://s1.q4cdn.com/806093406/files/doc_downloads/2023/414759-1-_5_Nike-NPS-Combo_Form-10-K_WR.pdf; https://investors.nike.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/24/nike-inc-reports-fiscal-2024-third-quarter-results
Date: 2024-03-21

Nike designs, markets and distributes athletic footwear, apparel and equipment. It owns Nike, Jordan and Converse, and principally uses outside manufacturers rather than owning the production system. Brand investment, product innovation, athlete relationships and retail distribution support the economic franchise.

The last completed fiscal year, ending May 2023, produced $51.217 billion revenue. Nike Brand generated $48.763 billion and Converse $2.427 billion. Nike Brand wholesale sales were $27.397 billion and Direct sales $21.308 billion. The Direct figure includes Nike-owned stores and digital commerce, not simply high-margin royalty income.

Direct selling captures the retail spread and consumer information but brings fulfillment, returns, stores, technology and customer-acquisition expenses. Wholesale partners provide reach and product discovery while taking a retail margin. Maximizing direct share is therefore not automatically the right objective if it weakens consumer availability or partner relationships.

Latest-quarter Nike Brand revenue was $11.948 billion, including footwear $8.162 billion, apparel $3.290 billion and equipment $487 million. North America revenue was $5.070 billion, EMEA $3.138 billion, Greater China $2.084 billion and Asia Pacific/Latin America $1.647 billion. The scale and geographic mix reduce reliance on any one product market without eliminating local competition.

Gross margin was 44.8%, but company-defined EBIT margin was only 10.9% after marketing, overhead and restructuring. The quarter included approximately $1.011 billion demand creation expense. The cost of sustaining desirability is material; brand value cannot be underwritten independently of product and marketing reinvestment.

John Donahoe is president and CEO and Matthew Friend CFO. The latest quarter had 1,513.2 million basic and 1,526.5 million diluted average common shares. Founder-associated governance and multiple share classes should be considered when evaluating outside shareholder influence; no specific public management replacement is assumed.

The business can be attractive if its cultural relevance and distribution scale survive a period of weaker execution. The harder analytical issue is whether slowing Direct demand reflects fixable channel/product mistakes or a durable loss of consumer preference. Outsourced manufacturing does not make that distinction irrelevant.
