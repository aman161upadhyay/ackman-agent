Title: Valuable intellectual property across businesses with different economics
Source: https://investors.thewaltdisneycompany.com/news/news-details/2022/The-Walt-Disney-Company-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-Earnings-for-Fiscal-2022-11-08-2022/default.aspx
Date: 2022-11-08

Disney combines entertainment brands and intellectual property with television networks, film/television production, streaming, theme parks, resorts, cruises and consumer products. Familiar characters and franchises can support several revenue streams rather than a single product launch.

The latest reporting separates Disney Media and Entertainment Distribution from Parks, Experiences and Products. Within media, linear networks, direct-to-consumer services and content sales/licensing have different competitive positions and cash requirements. A consolidated growth rate can conceal deterioration in one activity and recovery in another.

Parks benefit from distinctive destinations, large installed assets and customer affinity. Reopening and improved attendance/spending support a strong recovery. These are substantial barriers to entry, but parks need maintenance, expansion and staffing, and remain exposed to travel demand and household budgets.

Linear television has historically generated attractive cash, but changing distribution and consumer behavior complicate its long-term economics. Affiliate and advertising revenue do not necessarily remain stable while households migrate to streaming.

Disney+, Hulu and ESPN+ provide recurring subscriptions and broad distribution opportunities. Total subscriptions across services exceed235 million. That is not235 million unique paying households: consumers may subscribe to more than one service or use bundles, and service prices differ.

The quarter adds12.1 million Disney+ subscribers. Management celebrates scale and plans a US ad-supported Disney+ tier forDecember8. It expects Disney+ profitability in fiscal2024, assuming no meaningful economic shift. These are current plans and conditional forecasts, not achieved steady-state economics.

Streaming requires continuing content, marketing and technology investment. Subscriber growth is not sufficient to establish attractive lifetime cash returns; price, churn, acquisition expense, advertising and content costs all matter. Some valuable content also has alternative licensing or theatrical uses.

Bob Chapek is CEO at this snapshot. No future leadership transition is assumed. A shareholder can analyze incentives, structure and costs, but cannot simply presume a different leader will reverse customer behavior or restore an obsolete distribution model.

The investment question is whether the combined intellectual-property system will produce growing distributable cash after the transition. The assets are recognizable and differentiated; the future mix of profits among legacy networks, streaming and parks is less settled than the brand itself.
