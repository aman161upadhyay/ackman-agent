Title: A live dispute over price-led growth, reinvestment and reporting

Date: 2014-07-18

The most detailed public critique comes from hostile-bid target Allergan. This is an interested adversary, not a neutral regulator. On May 27 it published an SEC-filed analysis developed with Alvarez & Marsal and FTI Consulting. Investors should evaluate its evidence rather than assume either that its conclusions are proven or that the takeover conflict invalidates every concern.

Allergan questions Valeant's organic-growth definitions and product disclosure. Its presentation says pro-forma revenue growth including acquisitions was negative 0.5% in 2013 and negative 1.4% in Q1 2014, contrasting those figures with Valeant's communicated organic metrics. Definitions, discontinued products and portfolio composition must be reconciled before choosing a preferred growth series.

Using IMS prescription information, Allergan argues units declined for three of Bausch + Lomb's four largest prescription ophthalmic products and that list-price increases supported revenue. It makes similar claims about Medicis market share and pricing. Those are product-level allegations and should not be generalized into proof that all of Valeant is shrinking.

Allergan's June 23 rejection letter calls acquisition-based growth unsustainable, criticizes financial disclosure, questions proposed cost synergies and warns that reduced investment could damage its franchise under Valeant ownership. It highlights executive turnover and dependence on completing another large transaction. The letter also argues Allergan's own stock is undervalued by the offer; the incentives are explicit.

The Wall Street Journal's June 10 account by Jonathan Rockoff and Dana Mattioli examines cuts to Medicis research staff and projects after acquisition. The economic issue is whether purchasing later-stage assets can reliably replace internal research at attractive returns, not whether R&D spending is intrinsically good.

Valeant contested these claims. Reuters on July 18 reports the company publishing Bausch + Lomb sales-growth data to blunt Allergan's allegations. The latest February 2015 results provide further reported organic growth, including 11% annual Bausch + Lomb growth. That response deserves consideration without treating company-defined organic sales as independent verification.

The fundamental downside is linked: reimbursement resistance or weakening product volumes could reduce earnings, lower confidence in adjusted metrics and constrain the debt-funded acquisition model together. No later controversy is assumed in this information set. The criticism is already sufficiently specific to demand diligence at entry.
