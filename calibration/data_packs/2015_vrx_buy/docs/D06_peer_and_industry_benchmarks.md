Actavis is a useful acquisition-model comparator: large amortization and deal expenses also separate its GAAP earnings from adjusted earnings. Its February report raises standalone 2015 adjusted EPS guidance to US$16.30–US$17.30. The pending Allergan transaction is not yet reflected in that standalone outlook. Actavis's existence shows Valeant is not the only potential buyer able to consolidate specialty products.

Merck is much larger and research-intensive, with a different patent portfolio and innovation pipeline. Its headline GAAP earnings include the US$11.2 billion gain on divesting Consumer Care. This is a warning against comparing unadjusted P/Es without normalization on either side. Its 2015 adjusted EPS target of US$3.32–US$3.47 includes an expected US$0.27 FX headwind.

Allergan's February operating performance follows its resistance to Valeant and agreement to be bought by Actavis. BioPharma Dive reports R&D down 7% and SG&A down 2.9% while revenue grew. Cutting expenses need not destroy a business, but this evidence does not authenticate Valeant's much larger proposed reductions.

Valeant's annual R&D/revenue ratio is only approximately 3%. For an acquisitive specialty platform, purchased intellectual property and development obligations are part of the effective reinvestment burden. Neither low reported R&D nor high adjusted operating margin alone establishes superior ROIC.

Date-matched peer share prices and consistently defined forward multiples are not extracted. No fabricated sector ranking is supplied. The relevant comparative question is whether acquisition returns, growth durability, leverage and product investment justify a premium to slower but less transaction-dependent franchises.
