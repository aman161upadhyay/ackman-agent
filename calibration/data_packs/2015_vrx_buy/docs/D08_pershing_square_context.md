Date: 2015-02-20

Reuters on January 29 calls Pershing Square an approximately US$18 billion fund complex. A January 4 New York Post report had used US$19 billion. Neither is a company-level closed-end PSH NAV calculation. Firmwide estimates vary with performance, flows, date and scope.

The latest public 13F, for December 31 holdings, is summarized in February coverage as seven positions worth approximately US$16.04 billion. It excludes Herbalife short exposure, non-reportable securities, cash and many derivatives.

| Disclosed common-stock holding | Reported value | Approximate share of 13F value |
|---|---:|---:|
| Allergan | US$5.66bn | 35.3% |
| Air Products | US$2.96bn | 18.5% |
| Canadian Pacific | US$2.67bn | 16.6% |
| Zoetis | US$1.79bn | 11.2% |

The other reported names are Restaurant Brands International, Platform Specialty Products and Howard Hughes. A complete independently reconciled value table for those three is not recovered here. These weights are public long-book concentration, not actual February 27 NAV weights.

The Allergan exposure is already substantial. The Post describes it as roughly 30% of the then-US$19 billion fund complex and emphasizes the unusual collaboration with Valeant. Shares in an acquisition target and shares in its proposed acquirer are different securities with different risks. Gains in the former do not prove the latter is cheap.

Reuters reports approximately US$1.54 billion spent for an 8.5% Zoetis position, with discussions of board composition, cost reduction and a possible merger. That is additional healthcare and transaction exposure. CP, Air Products and Restaurant Brands provide operating-improvement and capital-allocation experience in different industries, not automatic expertise in drug reimbursement.

The January 7 CNBC interview provides a direct contemporary Ackman account of the Allergan legal/tender strategy: https://www.cnbc.com/2015/01/07/cnbc-excerpts-cnbc-exclusive-pershing-square-capital-management-founder-ceo-bill-ackman-speaks-with-cnbcs-squawk-box-today.html. It documents familiarity and adviser involvement, not an authenticated new long thesis in Valeant at this cutoff.

Portfolio capacity should consider concentration and correlated acquisition, credit and management risks, rather than only public-stock liquidity. No future disclosure of a Valeant stake is inserted here. The case asks what could be inferred before that public commitment, using contemporary information.
