Title: Platform economics compared with other hyperscalers
Source: compiled from: https://s206.q4cdn.com/479360582/files/doc_news/2025/Oct/29/attachments/2025q3-alphabet-earnings-release.pdf; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872425000121/amzn-20250930xex991.htm; https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2026-q2/press-release-webcast; https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q2
Date: 2026-01-28

Microsoft competes with Amazon and Alphabet in cloud services, with a broader set of vendors in productivity, security and AI. These businesses have overlapping infrastructure needs but different monetization routes. Microsoft's installed enterprise relationships connect cloud, productivity, identity and administration; Amazon's mix includes low-margin retail; Alphabet's includes search and video advertising. Consolidated P/E and margins are not standalone cloud comparisons.

The explicitly dated benchmark figures below come from Q3 2025 peer releases and Microsoft's December-quarter release. They are not synchronized calendar quarters or a claim that October releases remain every peer's latest available report.

| Business/period | Revenue | Reported growth | Relevant profitability observation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Microsoft, December quarter | $81.273bn | 17% | 47.1% calculated operating margin |
| Intelligent Cloud, December quarter | $32.907bn | 29% | Approximately 42% operating margin |
| Azure/other cloud, December quarter | Not separately disclosed | 39%, 38% constant currency | Standalone operating income not disclosed |
| Amazon, September quarter | $180.2bn | 13% | $17.4bn operating income including special charges |
| AWS, September quarter | $33.0bn | 20% | Do not infer Azure margin from AWS |
| Alphabet, September quarter | $102.346bn | 16% | 31% operating margin including EC fine |

Microsoft's Productivity and Business Processes margin of about 60% is evidence that the existing enterprise-software base has economics different from infrastructure rental. That base may fund the investment cycle and distribute new AI capabilities. However, a profitable installed base can also subsidize lower-return experiments; the source of cash does not determine the return on its use.

Compute availability, energy, networking and construction execution constrain hyperscaler supply. The January call says Microsoft is allocating capacity among Azure, its own applications and research. A peer's faster revenue growth may reflect different available capacity, business mix or starting size rather than superior underlying demand.

Contemporaneous cutoff forward multiples and harmonized FCF yields for the peers were not authenticated. No later market-data row is backfilled. The available comparison is operating growth and economic structure, not a complete relative-value ranking. A careful analyst should avoid using Microsoft's much higher consolidated margin as proof its cloud business is inherently superior to AWS or Google Cloud, or using a lower peer P/E without adjusting for cash, nonoperating gains and business mix.
