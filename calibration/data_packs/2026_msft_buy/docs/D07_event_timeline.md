Title: From OpenAI restructuring to the January earnings reset
Source: compiled from: https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2025/10/28/the-next-chapter-of-the-microsoft-openai-partnership/; https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2026-q2/press-release-webcast; https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/29/microsoft-market-cap-earnings.html; https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/MSFT/history/?period1=1769644800&period2=1770163200
Date: 2026-01-30

October 28, 2025: Microsoft and OpenAI announced a revised partnership following OpenAI's recapitalization. Microsoft's investment in OpenAI Group PBC was valued at about $135 billion, representing approximately 27% on an as-converted diluted basis. This is an equity holding, not cash in Microsoft's bank account and not annual operating profit. The same announcement said OpenAI contracted to buy an incremental $250 billion in Azure services.

The agreement preserves important IP and cloud rights while changing their boundaries. Microsoft retains model and product IP rights through 2032, including post-AGI models. Confidential research-method rights continue until verified AGI or 2030, whichever is earlier. An independent expert panel must verify an OpenAI declaration of AGI. Consumer hardware is excluded from Microsoft's IP rights.

Azure API exclusivity remains until AGI, but OpenAI can serve non-API products on other clouds and Microsoft no longer holds a right of first refusal to supply compute. Certain US national-security API uses can operate regardless of cloud provider. Microsoft may independently pursue AGI, including with third parties. These nuances argue against treating the partnership as either perpetual total exclusivity or a complete separation.

January 28, 2026: The December-quarter release reported $81.3 billion revenue, 39% reported Azure growth and $625 billion commercial RPO. The earnings call disclosed that about 45% of that RPO was OpenAI-related. The equity stake, recognized investment gains and service commitments are different exposures to the same partner; they should not be counted as three independent sources of diversification.

January 29: Shares fell about 10%; CNBC reported a $357 billion capitalization loss. Debate centered on Azure missing a narrow consensus expectation, capacity allocation, Copilot monetization and the $37.5 billion quarterly capital program.

January 30: Historical unadjusted close was $430.29, following the prior session's $433.50. This is the first trading day after the main earnings-related selloff, and the cutoff used here. Outcomes of the next results, subsequent partnership changes and future AI spending announcements are unknown.

There is no verified CEO replacement or activist campaign in this timeline. The public route to demonstrating value is operating execution: converting contracted demand into revenue, validating paid AI usage, controlling costs and earning acceptable returns on infrastructure. The dates establish what can be known, not which of those outcomes will occur.
