Title: Enterprise distribution, recurring software and Azure

Date: 2026-01-28

Microsoft sells enterprise productivity, cloud infrastructure, business applications, security, operating systems and consumer/gaming products. It reports three segments. Productivity and Business Processes includes Microsoft 365, LinkedIn and Dynamics. Intelligent Cloud includes Azure and server products. More Personal Computing contains Windows, devices, Xbox and search/news advertising. This mix matters: it is neither a pure AI laboratory nor a pure public-cloud supplier.

The first segment's margin is approximately 60%, Intelligent Cloud's 42%, per the January 28 call. Consolidated operating margin is 47.1%, calculated. More Personal Computing's operating income above is explicitly derived from consolidated income less the first two segments.

The recurring-software moat hypothesis rests on workflows, documents, identity, security and enterprise administration being integrated rather than purchased as independent utilities. Replacing one application is different from replacing an organization's collaboration, permissions, compliance and deployment infrastructure. These are analytical mechanisms suggested by the product architecture, not a guarantee against AI substitution.

Azure benefits from customers moving workloads into managed infrastructure and from AI training/inference demand. However, serving incremental cloud demand requires real capital. Microsoft says customer demand exceeds supply, and allocates some scarce compute internally to Copilot and research. That creates a trade-off between immediately visible Azure revenue and developing other franchises.

Commercial remaining performance obligations reached $625 billion, up 110%. Approximately 45% came from OpenAI. Backlog is not current revenue, cash on hand or riskless earnings; concentration and the time needed to deliver service both matter. Microsoft 365 Copilot had 15 million paid seats and GitHub Copilot more than 4.7 million paid subscribers, per the call.

Satya Nadella is chairman and CEO and Amy Hood CFO. December 31 common shares outstanding were 7,429 million; Q2 diluted weighted-average shares were 7,460 million. The annual report's segment comparison must be used with care because August 2024 reporting changes moved commercial Microsoft 365 activities into Productivity and Business Processes. Current segment growth should not be spliced onto older unrecast definitions.
