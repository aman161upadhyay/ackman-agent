Microsoft's fiscal year ends June 30. Annual figures were public with the July 2025 fiscal-year release; December-quarter results were released January 28. The cash-flow repurchase row can include equity-award settlement effects and should not be equated automatically to the company's discretionary repurchase-program figure. The earnings headline says $12.7 billion returned through dividends and repurchases in Q2; that differs from mechanically adding the two cash-flow rows. Definitions must remain consistent.

December 31 cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments were $89,462 million. Current long-term debt was $4,837 million and long-term debt $35,425 million. Calculated net cash excluding leases was therefore $49,200 million. This excludes operating leases, finance leases and other commitments. The balance sheet does not look like a debt-refinancing crisis, but that does not remove investment-return risk.

For the six months, CFO was $80,815 million and cash property additions $49,270 million, producing $31,545 million simple FCF. Q2's lower cash conversion is material even alongside a strong first half. Management reports $37.5 billion capex including finance leases for the quarter; that is a different investment measure from cash property additions.

GAAP Q2 EPS includes $1.02 from OpenAI investment gains. Company non-GAAP EPS excluding OpenAI impacts is $4.14, up 24%. Using the $5.16 headline to extrapolate recurring operating growth would overstate the quarter's underlying earnings progression. A consistent historical ROIC measure is not supplied here.
