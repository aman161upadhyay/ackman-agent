Title: January 28 results — growth, allocation constraints and spending
Source: compiled from: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2026-q2/press-release-webcast; https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q2
Date: 2026-01-28

December-quarter revenue rose 17% to $81.3 billion and operating income 21% to $38.3 billion. Microsoft Cloud revenue crossed $50 billion for the first time, reaching $51.5 billion, up 26%. Azure and other cloud services grew 39% reported, 38% constant currency. Productivity and Business Processes rose 16%, Intelligent Cloud 29%, while More Personal Computing fell 3%.

The GAAP/non-GAAP distinction is unusually important. Net income of $38.458 billion includes OpenAI investment gains; adjusted net income was $30.875 billion. GAAP diluted EPS rose 60% to $5.16, but EPS excluding OpenAI investment impacts rose 24% to $4.14. The comparable prior-year adjustment also removes an OpenAI loss. Neither adjustment is a direct measure of Azure's standalone profitability.

Quarterly capex was $37.5 billion, with about two-thirds on relatively short-lived GPUs/CPUs and the rest on longer-lived assets. Finance leases were $6.7 billion. Hardware is capitalized and depreciated over six years, per the call. A large current outlay can therefore create later depreciation even if the immediate operating margin appears healthy.

Amy Hood says, “Our customer demand continues to exceed our supply.” She characterizes Azure's outlook as an allocated-capacity guide. Management says using newly available GPUs exclusively for Azure could have put growth above 40%, but it has chosen to support other internal products. That is an allocation explanation, not proof that all those uses generate equal returns.

Hood also says: “The majority of the capital that we’re spending today, and a lot of the GPUs that we’re buying are already contracted for most of their useful life.” Contracted demand supports visibility, while counterparty risk, service costs and realization timing remain relevant.

Nadella explains the goal is not to maximize one business but to allocate capacity to the best lifetime-value portfolio. Investors must distinguish that long-run strategy from a promise to beat every near-term Azure growth expectation. A complete verified calendar-year capital budget and any unannounced future partnership terms remain unavailable for this January review.
