Title: AI competition and restructuring timeline
Source: compiled from: https://www.nytimes.com/2023/01/20/business/google-alphabet-layoffs.html; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204423000013/googexhibit991q42022.htm; https://blog.google/intl/en-africa/products/explore-get-answers/an-important-next-step-on-our-ai-journey; https://www.bbc.com/news/business-64576225
Date: 2023-02-10

**2022:** Alphabet's hiring and capital investment expanded. The February earnings release reports 190,234 employees at December 31, versus 156,500 a year earlier. FY2022 capex was $31.485 billion, versus $24.640 billion. The operating cost base has therefore grown while revenue growth has slowed.

**November 30, 2022:** ChatGPT's public launch introduces a prominent conversational alternative to conventional web search. The subsequent February coverage places the Bard announcement in the context of this competitive response. The commercial effect on Google's revenue is not yet disclosed.

**January 20, 2023:** Alphabet announced plans to cut about 12,000 jobs, approximately 6% of its global workforce. The New York Times reports Pichai's admission that the company had expanded too quickly during the pandemic. These actions signal a change in cost priorities, but employment reductions take time and incur charges.

**February 2:** The company released Q4/FY2022 results. Revenue growth was 1% in the quarter and operating margin declined. Management discussed durable cost restructuring, AI investment, severance and office charges, a server-life accounting estimate change, and reclassification of DeepMind costs.

**February 6:** Pichai's Google blog introduced Bard as an experimental conversational AI service powered by LaMDA. The company is opening it to trusted testers before broader public availability in coming weeks. It is not described as a fully deployed replacement for search. The blog emphasizes combining research with product development and testing for quality and groundedness.

**February 7-8:** Contemporary BBC coverage describes Microsoft's AI search push and Google's response. Google's promotional example misstates the James Webb Space Telescope's role in photographing exoplanets. The error becomes a focal point for concerns that Google is rushing its response.

**February 8-10:** The share price declines sharply amid the AI narrative. Reuters reports a 7.7% February 8 Class A decline; historical Class C data show a $94.86 February 10 close. These are market observations, not evidence of a measured customer migration.

No subsequent earnings, AI-launch usage, change in market share, or investor position disclosure is included. The evidence available at this point is a profitable incumbent facing a credible technological challenge while reorganizing its costs.
