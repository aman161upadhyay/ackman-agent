Title: Digital advertising and Cloud benchmarks

Date: 2023-02-02

Microsoft and Meta provide two distinct benchmarks: a Cloud/software competitor and a major advertising platform. The comparison uses quarters ending December 31, 2022, released January 24, February 1 and February 2 respectively. All values are $millions.

| Company/segment | Revenue | Reported growth | Operating income |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Alphabet consolidated | 76,048 | 1% | 18,160 |
| Google Services | 67,838 | decline from 69,400 | 21,101 |
| Google Cloud | 7,315 | approximately 32%, calculated | -480 |
| Microsoft consolidated | 52,747 | 2% | 20,399 |
| Microsoft Intelligent Cloud | 21,508 | 18% | 8,904 |
| Meta consolidated | 32,165 | -4% | 6,399 |

Calculated consolidated operating margins are approximately 23.9% for Alphabet and 38.7% for Microsoft; Meta reports 20%. These figures reflect different mixes and restructuring costs. They do not show that Alphabet can automatically reach Microsoft's margin.

Microsoft reports Azure/other cloud-service growth of 31%, or 38% at constant currency. Intelligent Cloud includes businesses beyond Azure, so its 41.4% calculated segment operating margin is not an Azure standalone margin. Google Cloud includes Workspace as well as infrastructure/platform services. Direct comparisons require caution.

Meta's Q4 revenue decline and operating-profit decline show that advertising-platform pressure is broader than Google search. Meta's reported quarterly margin is affected by expenses and investment choices; its social advertising has different intent and targeting economics from search.

The strategic structures also differ. Microsoft can introduce AI through productivity software, Azure and a smaller search business. Alphabet must balance defending a large existing search profit pool with investing in the next interface. Meta primarily competes for advertising budgets and user time rather than conventional search queries.

Timestamped peer forward multiples are not recovered and are omitted rather than inferred from current quote pages. The useful point-in-time comparison is economic: Google Services remains a substantial profit engine, Google Cloud is still loss-making, and other large platforms are simultaneously funding AI and restructuring. Whether these investments create moat reinforcement or an industry cost escalation is unresolved at the cutoff.
