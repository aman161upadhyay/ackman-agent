The following financial comparisons were public in the February 2021 and February 2023 company releases. Figures are $millions, except EPS. The 2020 EPS figure is omitted rather than mix a pre-split number with the subsequent split-adjusted presentation.

| Metric | FY2020 | FY2021 | FY2022 | Q4 2022 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | 182,527 | 257,637 | 282,836 | 76,048 |
| Revenue growth | 13% | 41% | 10% | 1% |
| Operating income | 41,224 | 78,714 | 74,842 | 18,160 |
| Operating margin | 23% | 31% | 26% | 24% |
| Diluted EPS, split-adjusted | not supplied | 5.61 | 4.56 | 1.05 |

Revenue grew in 2022, but operating income and EPS fell. EPS also reflects investment-market volatility: other income/expense moved from positive $12,020 million in 2021 to negative $3,514 million in 2022. Operating profit is therefore a useful separate check on the underlying franchise.

FY2022 operating cash flow was $91,495 million and physical capex $31,485 million. Calculated FCF is $60,010 million. Corresponding FY2021 figures were $91,652 million, $24,640 million and calculated $67,012 million. Q4 FCF was $16,019 million. The cash-flow statement adds back $19,362 million of 2022 stock compensation, an economic dilution cost that should not simply be ignored.

Cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities totaled $113,762 million at year-end, down from $139,649 million. Long-term debt was $14,701 million. Cash/securities less long-term debt is $99,061 million; this is an explicit simplified net-cash calculation, not a comprehensive enterprise adjustment for leases or every other liability.

Repurchases consumed $59,296 million in 2022, versus $50,274 million in 2021. Outstanding shares fell approximately 3.0% year-on-year, calculated from 13,242 million to 12,849 million. No dividend is declared in the release.

Alphabet is financially capable of investing in AI and Cloud while returning substantial capital. Nonetheless, cash fell and capex rose while operating margins contracted. A complete ROIC history is not available in the retrieved sources. Cash generation and a large net-cash reserve support resilience; they do not remove the need to estimate reinvestment returns and future search profitability.
