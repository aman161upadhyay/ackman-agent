Revenue increased approximately 12.3% in 2005 and 13.1% in 2006. The latter benefits from the extra week. Rising EBIT margins suggest substantial operating strength, not an obvious large peer-efficiency deficit. Repurchases contribute to EPS growth exceeding aggregate earnings growth.

At February 3, cash was US$813 million, long-term debt including current maturities US$10,037 million, and shares outstanding approximately 859.8 million. The cover reports 858,580,232 shares on March 14. Total assets were US$37.349 billion. Net debt using only reported cash and this debt measure is US$9.224 billion, calculated.

Card contribution to pretax earnings rose from US$255 million in 2004 to US$452 million in 2005 and US$693 million in 2006. Its economics include finance charges, fees, allocated interest expense, operating/marketing expenses and credit losses. Gross receivables rose to US$6.711 billion from US$6.117 billion a year earlier.

CFO less capex is not a maintenance-only cash-flow measure. Store openings and remodeling absorb substantial investment, while changing receivables also affect financing needs. A consistent acquisition-inclusive ROIC is not extracted; profitability from owned assets must be evaluated against capital employed rather than assuming that low explicit rent represents costless operating advantage.
