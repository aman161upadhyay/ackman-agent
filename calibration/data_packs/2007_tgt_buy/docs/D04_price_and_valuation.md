The forward denominator is dated February, not a verified April20 live consensus snapshot. Current consensus, historical multiple distributions and a matched S&P forward multiple are not independently recovered.

There are at least three valuation pools: retail operations, credit-card economics and property. A correct sum-of-parts must account for interactions. Selling US$6.711 billion gross receivables at par does not add US$6.711 billion incremental shareholder value: the earning asset disappears, allocated financing must be repaid or reassigned, and lost card earnings offset some interest/buyback benefit. Gross receivables also differ from the US$6.194 billion net carrying balance.

Likewise, property value cannot simply be added to earnings already generated by occupying it rent-free. A sale-leaseback or other separation would create rental obligations, transaction costs, possible tax effects and altered growth flexibility. An operating-business multiple should then be applied to properly rent-adjusted earnings.

The 1,260 owned stores and US$4.934 billion historical-cost land balance establish an asset base worth investigating, not an authenticated market appraisal. A speculative cap rate or future activist target is not inserted. The thesis requires demonstrable excess value after separation costs and a feasible capital-return mechanism, particularly when current earnings already receive a respectable multiple.
