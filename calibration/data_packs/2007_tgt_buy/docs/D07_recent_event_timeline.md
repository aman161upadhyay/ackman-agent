Title: Completed fiscal-year disclosure and the spring sales calendar
Source: compiled from: https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna17364894; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/27419/000110465907014086/a07-6530_18k.htm; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/27419/000104746907001800/a2176656z10-k.htm; https://www.marketwatch.com/story/targets-march-same-store-sales-rise-12
Date: 2007-04-11

**February 3 — fiscal2006 year closes.** The 53-week year ends with 1,488 stores and substantial earnings growth. The calendar includes a 14-week fourth quarter rather than the usual 13. Comparable growth and reported annual growth should not be treated interchangeably.

**February 27 — Q4/full-year earnings.** Reported Q4 US$1.29 EPS exceeds Reuters Estimates' US$1.27. Management discusses US$3.60 2007 EPS consensus as within likely outcomes and expects ongoing growth from stores, comparable sales and card revenue. Market commentary is mixed on earnings quality.

**February 27 — comparable-sales definition change.** The 8-K states that Target.com will enter the comparable-store measure starting with February2007. Had it been included during the previous two years, monthly growth would have been approximately 30–50 basis points higher. The new definition has no effect on total sales or profit.

**March 15 — annual report filed.** Investors receive the detailed property-ownership table, card receivables and contribution data, cash flows and risk disclosures. Historical-cost property is visible; an independently appraised liquidation or separation value is not.

**April 11 — March sales.** March comparable sales rise 12% and retail sales 16.8% to US$5.592 billion, consistent with management's previously communicated 11–13% comparable expectation. The company reiterates a combined March/April 4–6% increase because the early Easter boosts March at April's expense.

**April 12 — industry comparison.** Reuters reports other retailers also benefiting from the early holiday while warning of softer April comparisons and weak home merchandise demand. Source: https://www.reuters.com/article/business/retailers-get-easter-lift-but-grim-april-looms-idUSN12298249/. The Reuters archive contains a later migration timestamp; the text explicitly identifies the original April12 report.

The next operating tests are the combined holiday-period result, quarterly margins, expense growth and card-credit normalization. There is no authenticated pre-cutoff announcement of a property separation, card sale or new outside shareholder campaign in this record. Such possibilities can be analyzed as scenarios, not recorded as scheduled transactions. The timeline deliberately stops before any later April sales revision or activist disclosure and does not import subsequent credit-market events.
