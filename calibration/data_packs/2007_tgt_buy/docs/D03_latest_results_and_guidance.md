Title: A strong holiday quarter and an Easter-distorted March
Source: compiled from: https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna17364894; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/27419/000110465907014086/a07-6530_18k.htm; https://www.marketwatch.com/story/targets-march-same-store-sales-rise-12; https://dev.sgbonline.com/target-march-comps-jump-12-percent/
Date: 2007-04-12

Reuters on February 27 reports Q4 profit of US$1.119 billion and EPS of US$1.29, versus US$939 million and US$1.06 a year earlier. Reuters Estimates consensus was US$1.27. The quarter had 14 weeks versus 13 in the prior year, limiting a simple extrapolation of the growth rate.

CFO Doug Scovanner said US$3.60 annual analyst EPS expectations were within the range of likely 2007 outcomes. This is a reported management comment about consensus, not a formal precise new range. Revenue growth was expected around the retailer's customary low-double-digit rate, translating into high-single-digit sales growth after calendar differences.

Latest monthly sales provide more current demand evidence. For the five weeks ended April 7, March retail sales increased 16.8% to US$5.592 billion and comparable-store sales rose 12.0%. Year-to-date retail sales were US$9.720 billion, up 14.1%, with comparable growth 9.2%.

The very high March comparison reflects Easter occurring April 8 rather than April 16. Target reiterates 4–6% combined March/April comparable growth. The holiday timing makes a negative April result compatible with a positive underlying two-month trend. A prospective investor should not annualize 12% March comps or treat a calendar-driven April decline as a sudden structural break.

The February 27 8-K announces that Target.com sales will be included in comparable-store results beginning with February 2007. Historically that change would have added approximately 30–50 basis points to monthly comparable growth. It does not change total sales or profitability. Comparisons against prior disclosures should recognize the definition change.

Management plans continued expansion of food and design/product innovation. The 2006 annual report records 91 net store openings. New locations can support durable growth but require property, inventory and systems capital; headline EPS growth does not measure the cash return of each new store.

There is no authenticated post-April20 sales revision in this pack. The operating information available at cutoff is relatively favorable, with some uncertainty about expense growth, the quality of the quarterly beat and consumer spending. A value thesis must explain why a successful retailer's current capitalization is inadequate rather than rely on an assumed imminent turnaround.
