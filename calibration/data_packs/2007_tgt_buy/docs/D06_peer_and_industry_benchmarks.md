Wal-Mart has far greater scale and substantial food exposure. Its annual continuing earnings increased 6.7% to US$12.178 billion; its quarterly result included a US$0.02-per-share tax benefit. Target competes through design, merchandising and guest experience rather than replicating Wal-Mart's entire assortment and cost structure.

Costco's membership economics, limited assortment and inventory turnover make a direct retail-margin comparison misleading. Its reported quarter includes a sales-return-reserve charge, stock-option-related tax charge and an excise-tax refund. Excluding the sales-reserve adjustment, quarterly sales were US$15.03 billion, up 9%. Adjusted and reported figures must remain separated.

Kohl's is more concentrated in apparel/home merchandise and therefore a useful discretionary-demand comparator. Its fiscal 2006 EPS increased 36%, while net income increased 31.7%; the extra week contributed about US$200 million sales. Growth and share-count effects matter at peers as well as Target.

These releases show that Target is not uniquely enjoying retail growth. Nor does its earnings record establish a large fixable operating deficit like an obviously inefficient competitor. An investment thesis may instead concern asset intensity, credit allocation and the value of the franchise relative to capitalization.

Owned-versus-leased stores, card earnings, international scope, membership fees and fiscal calendars all complicate simple multiple or margin rankings. No date-matched peer price/forward-EPS set is independently extracted. A full relative valuation should normalize property costs and card profits before concluding that Target is either cheap or expensive.

The relevant catalyst is not generic industry growth. It would need to be a specific feasible action or sustained operating improvement that changes recognized per-share value without undermining the competitive model.
