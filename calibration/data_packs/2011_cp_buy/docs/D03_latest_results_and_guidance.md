Title: Flood disruption, higher fuel costs and management's recovery claim
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/16875/000095012311068899/o72585e6vk.htm; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/16875/000095012311037682/o70312e6vk.htm
Date: 2011-07-27

The latest available quarterly release is Q2 2011, published July 27. Revenue was C$1,264.5 million, up C$30.3 million; operating expenses were C$1,034.0 million, up C$73.9 million. Operating income declined to C$230.5 million from C$274.1 million. Net income was C$128.0 million and diluted EPS C$0.75, versus C$166.6 million and C$0.98. The operating ratio deteriorated to 81.8% from 77.8%; the first-half ratio was 86.0%, versus 80.0%.

Fred Green said: “We had almost 90 separate outages during the quarter and our engineering team worked as swiftly as possible to bring the track back.” Management rerouted and detoured traffic over other railways and incurred higher operating costs to deliver customers' freight. Green's assessment was: “Repairs are now complete and service levels are returning to normal.” This is a management statement about conditions in July, not independently verified evidence of a sustained earnings recovery.

Average fuel price increased 37% to US$3.50 per gallon. Fuel expense rose from C$177.9 million to C$237.4 million, while compensation and benefits fell from C$349.7 million to C$336.1 million. Purchased services and other expenses increased from C$203.3 million to C$227.3 million. These line items suggest that the problem cannot be reduced to excessive headcount alone.

The April 21 first-quarter release provides the immediately preceding guidance context. Q1 EPS was C$0.20 and operating income C$109.2 million, against C$0.60 and C$206.6 million a year earlier. Green said: “We remain committed to delivering our two- to four-year target of a low 70’s operating ratio and providing a quality service offering.” The target is management's, with a multi-year timeframe, not a promise for the next quarter.

The practical underwriting question is how much of the gap reflects temporary outages and how much reflects persistent operating design. A low-70s ratio implies a much stronger margin than the latest 81.8%, but reaching it requires service recovery, adequate capacity and cost discipline simultaneously. Neither a more aggressive external target nor a committed replacement executive is established by these releases. No third-quarter results are in the information set.
