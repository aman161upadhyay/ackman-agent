Title: Operating recovery promises and a rail-activism precedent
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1166893/000095012311005522/o68062ae6vk.htm; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/16875/000095012311037682/o70312e6vk.htm; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/16875/000095012311068899/o72585e6vk.htm; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/277948/000095015708000543/form8-k.htm
Date: 2011-07-27

**January 26, 2011 — prior-year results.** CP announced 2010 results. Fourth-quarter revenue increased 13% across all business lines. The annual operating ratio of 77.6% represented a recovery from the recession year. The existence of that recovery matters: a turnaround case must explain why the gap remains attractive after some improvement has already occurred.

**April 21, 2011 — winter-related setback.** First-quarter EPS fell to C$0.20 from C$0.60, with almost flat revenue and C$94.0 million more operating expense. Green described severe winter outages constraining both capacity and customer service. He reaffirmed a low-70s operating-ratio target over two to four years. That is a publicly stated performance yardstick against which shareholders can evaluate execution.

**July 25, 2011 — CN's comparison.** Canadian National reported 61.3% operating ratio, 8% revenue growth and 12% adjusted EPS growth for Q2. CN retained its annual outlook despite uncertainty about global growth. Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/16868/000001686811000027/form6k.htm. The release supplies current peer evidence rather than requiring an analyst to extrapolate an old cycle's peak.

**July 27, 2011 — flood impact.** CP's Q2 release reported C$0.75 EPS, an 81.8% ratio and almost 90 outages. Management said repairs were complete. Whether this is an inflection or another temporary explanation is still unresolved. No realized third-quarter recovery is assumed.

**Governance precedent, July 2008 — CSX.** This is older contextual evidence, not a new CP event. CSX's July 29, 2008 filing documents board entry for TCI/3G nominees Alexandre Behring and Gilbert Lamphere and director John McPherson following incumbent resignations. The filing explicitly preserves TCI/3G nominees' rights concerning the contested election. It demonstrates that shareholder intervention in a large railroad can reach board composition; it does not prove intervention improves operations or that identical tactics fit Canadian law.

No announcement in this source set establishes a CP CEO change, agreed succession candidate or committed activist transaction by the cutoff. The investable question is whether a shareholder can identify specific operational changes, retain a qualified operator and obtain board support. Public operating targets are a starting point, not an executable governance plan.
