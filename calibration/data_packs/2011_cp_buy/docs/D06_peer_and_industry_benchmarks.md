The table compares fiscal 2010 ratios available by January 2011. Margin is calculated as 100% less operating ratio. SMART's January 28 rail-industry roundup supplies the Canadian National figure; the other US figures are corroborated by the cited issuer releases.

| Railway | FY2010 operating ratio | Implied operating margin |
|---|---:|---:|
| Canadian Pacific | 77.6% | 22.4% |
| Canadian National | 63.6% | 36.4% |
| Union Pacific | 70.6% | 29.4% |
| CSX | 71.1% | 28.9% |
| Norfolk Southern | 71.9% | 28.1% |

The latest same-quarter Canadian comparison is starker: CP's Q2 2011 ratio was 81.8%, while CN reported 61.3% on July 25. CN revenue increased 8% to C$2,260 million; operating income increased 8% to C$874 million. CN said its own network encountered floods, forest fires and mudslides. Differences in where disruptions occur matter, but adverse weather was not unique to CP.

CN reiterated expected adjusted EPS growth of up to 15% for 2011 from its C$4.20 base and approximately C$1.2 billion free cash flow, as defined by CN. It planned C$1.7 billion capex, including more than C$1 billion for track infrastructure. Superior operating ratios do not eliminate rail reinvestment.

Union Pacific's January 20 release reported 2010 revenue carload growth across all six business groups, 10.8% ROIC, C$-independent US$5.53 EPS, and nearly US$1.25 billion share repurchases. Norfolk Southern reported revenue growth of 19% and EPS growth of 45%; CSX reported operating income growth of 35%. Peer growth underscores the opportunity cost of persistent operational underperformance.

Hunter Harrison has a public operating background, independently of any proposed role at CP. CN's December 13, 2002 release named him CEO effective January 1, 2003; he had been CEO of Illinois Central from 1993 to 1998 and CN COO from March 1998. CN's November 15, 2005 SEC-filed presentation describes an operating-ratio progression into the mid-60s: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/16868/000095010305002331/nov0405_6k.htm. This establishes relevant experience, not his willingness or contractual ability to take another job. Comparable dated peer market multiples are unavailable here; operating ratios are the reliable benchmark.
