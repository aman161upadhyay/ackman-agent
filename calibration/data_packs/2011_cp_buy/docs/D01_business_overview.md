Title: A scarce rail franchise with a measurable operating problem
Source: compiled from: https://s21.q4cdn.com/736796105/files/doc_financials/Annual-Report/2010/cp-ar-2010.pdf; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/16875/000095012311068899/o72585e6vk.htm
Date: 2011-07-27

Canadian Pacific operates a transcontinental freight railway in Canada and the United States. It sells transportation rather than the commodities transported. Its physical right-of-way, terminals and connections form the competitive asset; replicating a network requires land, construction, regulatory approvals and sufficient traffic density. These barriers do not make traffic or profits immune to a recession. Grain, fertilizer, coal, industrial production and consumer imports all affect demand. The 2010 annual report is the source for the following full-year figures, in Canadian dollars.

There was no customer exceeding 10% of revenue in 2010 or 2009. Intermodal serves domestic container movements and international port traffic; bulk commodities provide different demand exposures. Fuel recoveries help offset diesel costs but introduce timing and mix effects. Higher reported revenue can therefore reflect fuel rather than better underlying pricing.

In 2010 CP moved 2,660.6 thousand carloads and 126,428 million revenue ton-miles. Freight revenue per carload was C$1,824; intermodal yielded C$1,260 per carload and sulphur/fertilizers C$2,679. These are revenue measures, not contribution margins: distance, weight, train configuration and equipment needs differ. Segment-level operating profits are not supplied here.

Fred Green is president and CEO. The operating ratio, expenses divided by revenue, is the primary efficiency yardstick: 77.6% in 2010. Its complement, 22.4%, is operating margin, not return on capital. An improvement can come from pricing, train productivity, utilization or traffic recovery; cutting essential maintenance would not create equivalent sustainable value.

At June 30, 2011, 169.4 million common shares were outstanding; Q2 diluted weighted-average shares were 170.7 million. The company has public shareholders rather than a disclosed controlling owner in this source set. Net property of C$11,981.2 million emphasizes that this is an asset-intensive network, not a capital-light royalty. Management's explanations and operating targets should be tested against peers rather than treated as proof of an inevitable turnaround.
