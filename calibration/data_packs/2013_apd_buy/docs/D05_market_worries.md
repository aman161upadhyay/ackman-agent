Title: Cyclicality, project spending and the risk of misreading a peer gap
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2969/000119312513166085/d526189dex991.htm; https://www.fool.com/investing/general/2013/04/24/air-products--chemicals-increases-sales-but-misse.aspx; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2969/000119312512476878/d409668d10k.htm
Date: 2013-04-24

The latest report does not describe a robust synchronized industrial recovery. McGlade cites slower US growth, European contraction, softness in China and an electronics market weaker than expected. Positive acquisition contributions obscure weak existing-business volumes.

The April24 S&P Capital IQ-based earnings recap reports sales below analyst estimates and Q3 guidance below its cited EPS consensus. Those observations provide a plausible market worry: investments and acquisitions may not generate near-term growth sufficient to justify the capital committed.

Pension expense, acquisition integration, energy/distribution costs and customer outages affect margins. An analyst must distinguish costs management can sustainably remove from cyclical weakness or unavoidable supply/service obligations. Cutting capacity or maintenance aggressively could impair the very reliability that makes industrial gases attractive.

The latest company release points to a project backlog aboveUS$3 billion. Backlog supports future revenue opportunities, but also requires capital, execution and customer credit. Long-term contracts help defend returns but do not make construction overruns or a bad contract price harmless.

Fiscal2012 non-GAAP capital expenditures of US$2.778 billion far exceed simple cash plant spending. Indura and other acquisition activity add exposure and integration work. A purchase funded with debt may lift sales while depressing returns if the price or capital requirements are too high.

Portfolio complexity is another concern. Merchant distribution, large tonnage plants, electronics materials and equipment projects need different performance metrics. A centralized structure can dilute accountability, but decentralization is only a potential analytical remedy, not a documented completed change.

The countercase is durable customer relationships, essential products and visible opportunities for productivity. The existing company already reports cost savings and positive pricing in parts of Merchant. An investment need not assume a macro boom if specific operating and capital improvements can be established.

No authenticated public pre-July15 demand for a named CEO or promised margin target is available. A good thesis must identify what a different operator would actually do, the limits imposed by mix, and the returns on retained capital. “Praxair earns more” is an important clue, not a full executable plan.
