Title: Long-lived industrial-gas infrastructure with uneven business mix
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2969/000119312512476878/d409668d10k.htm; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2969/000119312513166085/d526189dex991.htm
Date: 2013-04-23

Air Products supplies industrial gases, performance materials, equipment and related technology. Its customers include refineries, chemical plants, steelmakers, electronics manufacturers, food processors and healthcare users. Fiscal2012 sales were approximately US$9.612 billion across more than50 countries.

The four reported segments are Merchant Gases, Tonnage Gases, Electronics and Performance Materials, and Equipment and Energy. They have different capital intensity and cyclicality; a single company-wide margin does not identify which activities explain a peer gap.

Merchant gases reach customers through bulk liquid deliveries, cylinders and smaller on-site systems. Local plant density, distribution efficiency, reliable service and pricing discipline matter. A customer's gas bill may be small relative to the cost of interrupted production, supporting durable supplier relationships.

Tonnage gases involve large on-site plants supplying oxygen, hydrogen and other gases under long-term arrangements. The installed plant and integration with customer operations create switching barriers. Contracts and energy pass-through can reduce certain risks, but utilization, customer outages, project execution and capital cost still matter.

Electronics and materials expose the group to semiconductor cycles and product-specific competition. Equipment and Energy includes LNG-related technology and equipment, with project timing rather than a smooth recurring delivery pattern.

Fiscal2012 segment sales were approximately US$3.662 billion Merchant, US$3.207 billion Tonnage, US$2.323 billion Electronics/Materials and US$420 million Equipment/Energy. Segment operating income was approximately US$644 million, US$512 million, US$426 million and US$45 million, before unallocated company items. Segment sums are not interchangeable with consolidated adjusted EBIT.

Chairman/CEO John McGlade emphasizes productivity, emerging-market projects and recovery leverage. The company has announced withdrawal from polyurethane intermediates, demonstrating that portfolio pruning is already possible without assuming a new activist's control.

An attractive franchise thesis rests on essential products, technical competence, installed assets and long-duration customer relationships. It does not imply every investment earns a good return. High project capex and acquisitions can absorb cash for years.

The main analytical question is whether the weaker group economics reflect business mix and geography or remediable operating/capital discipline. Leadership change or separation could be tools to investigate, but neither is an established post-cutoff outcome to assume in the valuation.
