August figures come from WWD's September2 industry report. Same-store definitions and customer mix differ, but the direction is clear: Penney's recovery is materially weaker than these peers'. This supports investigating execution and assortment rather than treating every problem as macroeconomic.

Macy's attributes improvement to My Macy's local assortment, centralized functions, customer focus, exclusive brands, selling skills and store/online integration. These are concrete operational levers rather than a generic claim that a new CEO creates value. Its prior-year reported EPS included restructuring; the cleaner adjusted comparison was US$0.20.

Kohl's management emphasizes market-share gains, inventory discipline and store payroll/marketing leverage. Its updated annual EPS guidance is US$3.57–US$3.70. J.C. Penney's US$1.40–US$1.50 outlook should not be compared dollar-for-dollar as a margin measure, but the stronger same-store sales and net income direction matter.

Nordstrom serves a more affluent customer. Its multichannel and direct sales significantly outperform, showing that technology and inventory integration can improve execution. It is not proof that Penney can instantly import Nordstrom's customer economics. Nordstrom Rack comparable sales declined 0.9%, reminding investors that formats within one company may diverge.

The investable question is how much of this peer gap is fixable within Penney's existing brand/customer franchise. Assortment speed, exclusive merchandise, labor productivity and inventory turns are plausible levers; changing the customer's basic shopping behavior is a more speculative undertaking.

Date-matched peer multiples, sales per square foot and normalized rent/pension-adjusted margin tables are not extracted. No fabricated precise upside benchmark is supplied. The observed sales gap is enough to identify a problem; it is not itself a validated turnaround plan or evidence that peak historical margins will return.
