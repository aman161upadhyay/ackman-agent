Title: A modest profit recovery accompanied by disappointing guidance
Source: compiled from: https://sgbonline.com/jc-penney-posts-2q-profit-but-outlook-disappoints/; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1166126/000116612610000040/jcpenney10q2nd2010.htm; https://www.cnbc.com/2010/09/02/retailers-post-surprisingly-strong-august-sales.html
Date: 2010-09-10

Q2 2010 sales were US$3.938 billion versus US$3.943 billion a year earlier. Comparable sales increased 0.9%, while internet sales increased approximately 4% according to the August earnings coverage. Discontinuing the Big Book catalog contributed to the total-sales comparison.

Net income of US$14 million, US$0.06 diluted EPS, improved from a US$1 million loss. Gross margin rose to 39.4% from 38.5%, but SG&A increased and pension expense remained substantial. The quarter included approximately US$0.05 per share of bond-premium expense related to a debt tender.

The company had already indicated earnings near the low end of the US$0.05–US$0.08 range. The more important disappointment was forward guidance: full-year EPS US$1.40–US$1.50 versus the prior US$1.64 target and approximately US$1.54 analyst expectations in the contemporary Reuters report. Q3 EPS guidance is US$0.16–US$0.20 versus US$0.24 consensus.

Management forecasts Q3 comparable sales up 2–3% and total sales up 1–2%. New exclusive offerings are expected to drive much of the growth. These targets require better customer response, not just cost reduction.

Inventory at July31 rose approximately 7.1% year over year, ahead of sales growth. Management partly attributes this to protecting supply against container shortages from Asia. The alternative interpretation is increased markdown risk if consumer demand disappoints. Either interpretation has consequences for holiday gross margin and working capital.

The latest September2 monthly report provides a positive but incomplete update: August comparable sales rose 2.3%, exceeding the 1.6% analyst estimate reported by CNBC. The company says sales strengthened as school openings approached. Tax-free holidays and promotions helped the broader industry.

The quarterly filing anticipates approximately US$500 million full-year capex, down from US$600 million the prior year. That conserves cash but must be assessed against required systems, store maintenance and growth projects. A turnaround cannot assume permanently minimal investment.

The September improvement does not erase weak second-quarter relative performance. It does show that customers still respond to merchandise and promotions. The central uncertainty is whether new offerings can restore sustainable sales and returns without an expensive, untested reset of the customer proposition.
