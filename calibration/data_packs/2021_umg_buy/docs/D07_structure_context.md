Title: Separation timeline and vehicle-specific capital
Source: compiled from: https://www.vivendi.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/20210518_VIV_PR_Update-on-the-proposed-distribution-of-UMG-shares.pdf; https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2021/02/18084718/PSH-2021-Annual-Investor-Presentation.pdf
Date: 2021-06-03

Vivendi's May 18 announcement describes a planned distribution of 60% of UMG and an Amsterdam listing by September 27 at the latest. It also discusses retaining 10% for at least two years and possibly selling an additional stake before the distribution.

The Tencent-led group already owns 20% according to the earlier results announcement. The proposed additional 10% sale is distinct from that existing investment and from the planned in-kind distribution.

The prospective public-company governance would observe one share/one vote, no poison pill, two-year director terms and a majority-independent non-executive board. Neither Vivendi nor Bolloré intends board representation at that stage. These are proposed arrangements subject to the process/consents described, not a final operating history.

The manager's February 18 presentation reports February 16 core funds total AUM of $13.562 billion and equity AUM $11.462 billion, with $2.1 billion PSH debt included in the first figure. PSH alone is $12.027 billion total/$9.927 billion equity.

PSTH's trust and commitments are separate from those core-fund assets. SPAC capital is not interchangeable with PSH cash, and a proposal by one vehicle does not establish ownership by another or the eventual economic outcome for its shareholders.

Precise June vehicle cash, current NAV weights and binding transaction terms are not recovered from qualifying pre-cutoff disclosures. A large negotiated stake would require explicit analysis of funding, approvals, custody, listing distribution and potential co-investment.

June 3 press items later updated on June 4 cannot safely be treated as evidence that Friday's official confirmation was already public. The pack therefore excludes the manager's confirmed proposed terms and identity as buyer, rather than use a misleading initial publication date.

This leaves an independent decision setup: a valuable music-rights platform approaching separation, an existing private valuation anchor and possible new stake availability. The investor must assess the business and structure without assuming the transaction closes, the listing trades at a particular price or the same investor vehicle ultimately owns the shares.
