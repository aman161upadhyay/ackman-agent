Title: Music intellectual property and a global monetization platform
Source: compiled from: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210303005864/en/Vivendi-The-Good-Results-for-2020-Confirm-the-Resilience-of-the-Groups-Main-Businesses; https://www.vivendi.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/20210518_VIV_PR_Update-on-the-proposed-distribution-of-UMG-shares.pdf; https://www.ifpi.org/ifpi-issues-annual-global-music-report-2021
Date: 2021-05-18

Universal Music Group is a leading global recorded-music and music-publishing business within Vivendi. Its economics derive from developing artists, owning/licensing recordings and compositions, and monetizing consumption across physical, digital, performance and other channels.

Recorded music and publishing are related but distinct rights. Streaming services distribute music to users; labels and publishers license rights and compensate creators under their contracts. UMG is not the same business as a streaming application, and subscriber fees are not all label revenue.

Existing catalogs can be licensed repeatedly without manufacturing a new physical product for every listen. That provides attractive incremental economics. It does not eliminate artist royalties, advances, marketing, acquisition costs or the need to develop new repertoire.

The 2020 results show resilience during a difficult year. Recorded-music subscription/streaming revenue grew 16.2% at constant currency/perimeter, while merchandising suffered from limited touring. The exposure is therefore broader than a single recurring subscription line.

Lucian Grainge leads UMG. The recorded-music platform includes prominent artists and a deep catalog, while publishing extends exposure to composition rights. Competitive advantage depends on talent relationships, marketing/distribution scale, rights administration and a repertoire valuable to consumers and platforms.

Industry growth is supported by paid streaming. IFPI reports 443 million users of paid subscription accounts at the end of 2020, with subscription streaming revenue up 18.5%. Penetration and improved monetization can expand the industry, though lower-price markets and foreign exchange affect realized revenue per user.

UMG remains unlisted at this cutoff. Vivendi proposes distributing 60% of the equity and listing the company in Amsterdam by September 27 at the latest, subject to the process described in its May announcement.

The proposed governance includes one share/one vote, no poison pill and a largely non-executive board with a majority independent. These are proposals, not proof of an already independent completed public company.

The ownership opportunity combines a valuable rights franchise with a structural separation. The business merits analysis independently of transaction excitement: the investor needs a defensible price, normalized cash economics and confidence that artists, platforms and shareholders can all participate sustainably in industry growth.
