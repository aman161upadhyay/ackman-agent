Title: Industry growth and Warner's contemporaneous operating evidence
Source: compiled from: https://www.ifpi.org/ifpi-issues-annual-global-music-report-2021; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1319161/000131916121000017/q22021ex991.htm; https://www.vivendi.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/20210518_VIV_PR_Update-on-the-proposed-distribution-of-UMG-shares.pdf
Date: 2021-05-18

IFPI's March 23 Global Music Report says global recorded-music revenue rose 7.4% in 2020 to $21.6 billion, the sixth consecutive year of growth. Paid subscription streaming revenue rose 18.5% and paid-account users reached 443 million.

The growth is not a uniform rise across every format. The industry release identifies weaker physical/performance-rights categories alongside streaming expansion. Labels need to manage both the transition and the economics of new monetization.

Warner Music's May 4 release for the quarter ended March 31 provides a relevant public-company comparison. Revenue grew 17%, or 13% at constant currency, while digital revenue grew 23%, or 20% constant currency.

Warner's adjusted OIBDA rose 21% to $255 million from $210 million. Reported OIBDA was $228 million versus $12 million; net income was $117 million versus a prior-year $74 million loss. The large reported-versus-adjusted differences caution against naive headline-profit comparisons.

UMG's corresponding calendar-quarter revenue grew 9.4% at constant currency/perimeter and EBIT rose 35.8% on that basis to €322 million. Subscription/streaming revenue grew 19.6%. Both companies' evidence supports ongoing digital growth, but their reported profit definitions differ.

OIBDA, EBIT, EBITA and EBITDA are not interchangeable. Currency, acquisitions, release timing, rights mix and adjustments also affect comparability. No claim that one company has a superior margin is made by dividing incompatible measures.

Sony's music business is another relevant rights participant, but a comparable timestamped standalone table and valuation were not recovered. Nor was a verified June 3 Warner forward multiple. The pack does not invent a two-to-four-peer relative-valuation screen.

Industry growth helps validate the addressable trend, while the investor still must determine UMG's capture of value. Market growth can coexist with artist-cost inflation, catalog-acquisition competition and platform bargaining.

The useful benchmark is a rights business participating in a growing, increasingly digital market, not a software subscription company with no content obligations. The sector's recovery from prior disruption supports a growth hypothesis, but expected returns still depend on the private entry terms and normalized cash conversion.
