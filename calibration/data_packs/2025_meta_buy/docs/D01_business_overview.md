Title: Meta's advertising franchise, segment economics and control
Source: compiled from: https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2025/Meta-Reports-Third-Quarter-2025-Results/default.aspx; https://s21.q4cdn.com/399680738/files/doc_financials/2025/q3/META-Q3-2025-Prepared-Remarks.pdf
Date: 2025-10-29

Meta operates Facebook, Instagram, Messenger and WhatsApp within Family of Apps (FoA). Its second segment, Reality Labs (RL), develops virtual, augmented and mixed-reality hardware, software and content. The financial engine is advertising against attention on the apps; hardware and other activities account for a much smaller revenue stream. The following figures are from the October 29 results, in millions of dollars.

| Q3 2025 | Revenue | Operating income |
|---|---:|---:|
| Advertising | 50,082 | Not separately disclosed |
| FoA other revenue | 690 | Not separately disclosed |
| FoA total | 50,772 | 24,967 |
| Reality Labs | 470 | (4,432) |
| Consolidated | 51,242 | 20,535 |

FoA's operating margin is 49.2%, calculated from these reported figures; consolidated margin is 40%. Removing RL from a valuation is an analytical sensitivity, not a claim that those losses are eliminated or available to shareholders. In the first nine months, RL lost $13,171 million against revenue of $1,252 million. Founder Mark Zuckerberg remains CEO; Susan Li is CFO. The Q3 diluted weighted-average share count was 2,572 million, versus 2,600 million a year earlier. That is an EPS denominator, not the exact November 3 common shares outstanding. Headcount was 78,450, up 8%.

Scale is visible in family daily active people of 3.54 billion in September, up 8%. Zuckerberg said Instagram reached 3 billion monthly actives and Threads exceeded 150 million daily actives. These are different metrics and cannot be added together. Advertising growth reflects both more impressions and better pricing: Q3 impressions rose 14%, while average price per ad rose 10%. More audience engagement creates additional inventory; relevance and measured conversion can make each impression more valuable to advertisers.

The moat hypothesis is a reinforcing combination of audience, creators, advertiser access and recommendation quality. It is not immunity from competition or changes in mobile operating-system policies. Meta itself identifies dependency on third-party data signals, privacy restrictions and regulation as risks. Nor does audience scale prove that frontier-model investment will earn an attractive return.

Management says its AI investments are already improving recommendations and ads. The same management is committing substantial resources to less proven products and frontier research. Governance therefore matters: investors are underwriting capital allocation as well as current app profitability. Exact voting-control percentages, individual insider ownership and independent-director evaluations are not supplied by these earnings materials and are not assumed here.
