Title: October 29 earnings — ad strength versus infrastructure commitments
Source: compiled from: https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2025/Meta-Reports-Third-Quarter-2025-Results/default.aspx; https://s21.q4cdn.com/399680738/files/doc_financials/2025/q3/META-Q3-2025-Prepared-Remarks.pdf
Date: 2025-10-29

Q3 revenue of $51.24 billion rose 26%, or 25% in constant currency. Advertising revenue was $50.082 billion, also up 26%. Operating income rose 18% to $20.535 billion, slower than sales as expenses rose 32% to $30.707 billion. Margin fell from 43% to 40%. These operating results are separate from the tax item that reduced net earnings.

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act produced an unusual distinction between tax accounting and cash taxes. Meta expects a significant reduction in US federal cash tax payments, but recognition of a valuation allowance against deferred tax assets generated a one-time, noncash $15.93 billion charge. Reported effective tax rate was 87%, net income $2.709 billion and diluted EPS $1.05. Excluding the charge, management reports a 14% rate, $18.64 billion net income and $7.25 EPS. The charge is not an operating cash outflow of $15.93 billion.

| October 29 outlook | Management forecast |
|---|---|
| Q4 revenue | $56–59 billion |
| Q4 currency effect | Approximately 1% revenue-growth tailwind |
| FY2025 total expenses | $116–118 billion, previously $114–118 billion |
| FY2025 capex including leases | $70–72 billion, previously $66–72 billion |
| Q4 tax rate absent landscape changes | 12–15% |

The pivotal statement is about 2026, for which a specific capex dollar range is not provided: “capital expenditures dollar growth will be notably larger in 2026 than 2025.” Management also expects expenses to grow at a significantly faster percentage rate, driven primarily by infrastructure costs, cloud expenses and depreciation, then employee compensation. This is not merely a higher cash-investment budget: it creates future income-statement costs.

Zuckerberg describes the strategy as aggressively front-loading capacity for optimistic AI timelines. His fallback is also explicit: “in the worst case, we would just slow building new infrastructure for some period while we grow into what we've built.” That option may reduce future incremental spending; it does not recover sunk capital.

Near-term evidence for monetization includes AI recommendation improvements generating 5% more time on Facebook and 10% on Threads; Instagram video time rose more than 30% year over year. Reels' annual revenue run rate exceeded $50 billion, while annualized spending through end-to-end AI ad tools exceeded $60 billion. These are management run-rate metrics, not additive new revenue categories. No 2026 operating-income floor or peak-Reality-Labs-loss commitment is in this October outlook.
