Title: Pershing Square's publicly available portfolio and capital context

Date: 2025-09-15

The latest authenticated portfolio materials in this snapshot are the August 20 H1 report, June 30 holdings disclosed in August and August performance/transparency materials. They are stale relative to November 3, but are public and do not depend on an unpublished September-quarter filing. Current portfolio weights and trades after those observations are unknown.

PSH reported June 30 net assets of $14.715 billion and cash/cash equivalents of $1.404 billion. Its August performance report gives total core-strategy AUM of $20.7111 billion and firm AUM including Howard Hughes of $30.4653 billion. The gross PSH AUM figure was $18.5445 billion and included bond proceeds. These measures are not interchangeable with PSH shareholder NAV or uncommitted cash.

August transparency data show cash/cash equivalents at 6.55% of market value and bonds payable at negative 20.43%, with NAV $15.399 billion in that report. Net short-term liquidity at PSH is therefore not equivalent to an unlevered 6.55% allocation. The manager operates with permanent capital but still faces opportunity costs and contractual debt.

June portfolio names include Alphabet, Amazon, Brookfield, Chipotle, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, Hertz, Hilton, Howard Hughes, Nike exposure, Restaurant Brands, Uber and Universal Music. The August 14 Benzinga account of the June filing reports roughly 21% Uber, 19% Brookfield, 11% Restaurant Brands and 9.3% Amazon in its reported portfolio perimeter. The article incorrectly labels some dollar values “million”; only its percentages are used here, as secondary and approximate. Exact weights for the other names are not authenticated. US 13F assets omit some foreign securities, preferreds, cash and derivatives; those percentages must not be presented as full PSH NAV weights.

The H1 letter publicly says Canadian Pacific was exited in April because it was more exposed to tariffs and macroeconomic factors than the rest of the portfolio. It still calls CP a “fantastic business.” That disclosed exit may enter the context. Chipotle and Nike exposure remain in the publicly available holdings set; no complete exits of either are established by these cutoff materials.

The stated approach emphasizes high-quality businesses, limited downside and predictable recurring cash flows, with constructive engagement and selective hedging. The H1 report calls Hilton an “incredible business that offers a unique combination of predictability, resiliency and capital light growth.” No authenticated pre-cutoff Meta-specific Ackman quotation was recovered. Prior ownership of Alphabet and the newly disclosed Amazon investment supply sector context, not a public instruction about Meta.
