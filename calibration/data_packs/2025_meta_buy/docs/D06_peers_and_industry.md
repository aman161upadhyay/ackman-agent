Title: Alphabet and Amazon — competing platforms and investment intensity

Date: 2025-10-30

Alphabet and Amazon provide useful benchmarks, but neither is a pure comparable. Alphabet combines search, YouTube, cloud and other activities. Amazon combines retail, seller services, advertising and AWS. Meta is much more concentrated in advertising and funds RL from app profits. Comparing consolidated margins without this mix distinction can produce a false conclusion about competitive strength.

Alphabet's operating income includes a $3.457 billion European Commission fine. Its earnings release reports diluted EPS of $2.87 and net income $34.979 billion, with $12.759 billion other income. Those items are reasons not to equate reported EPS growth with recurring operating growth. Amazon's operating income includes $2.5 billion for an FTC legal settlement and $1.8 billion estimated severance costs. Management says operating income without those charges would be $21.7 billion. AWS revenue increased 20% to $33.0 billion.

CNBC's October 30 account says Alphabet raised its 2025 capex forecast to $91–93 billion, while Microsoft expected higher fiscal-year spending growth. Infrastructure escalation is industrywide. Meta lacks a directly comparable external hyperscale-cloud revenue line; its investment case rests more heavily on internal advertising/product returns. That can be attractive if returns are high, but means the monetization evidence must be different.

As a historical valuation reference, Macrotrends' September 30, 2025 Alphabet row gives $242.47 price, $10.13 trailing EPS and 23.94x P/E. This is an explicitly dated quarter-end trailing multiple, not Alphabet's November 3 forward multiple. Authentic cutoff forward multiples for Alphabet and Amazon have not been recovered and are not estimated. Amazon's retail working-capital model and cloud investment make simple FCF yield comparisons particularly sensitive to timing and lease treatment.

The structure question is whether global platforms' audience, distribution, data and infrastructure reinforce one another as AI improves. Conversely, if AI shifts discovery or user behavior away from existing surfaces, today's dominant advertising economics need not persist. Strong industry growth is not sufficient if competition forces reinvestment to absorb all incremental returns.
