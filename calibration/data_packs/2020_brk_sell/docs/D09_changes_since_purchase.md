Title: What has changed relative to the 2019 purchase assumptions?
Source: compiled from: https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2019/08/20165951/PSH-2019-Interim-Financial-Statements1.pdf; https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2020/04/15125723/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-2019-Annual-Report-1.pdf; https://berkshirehathaway.com/qtrly/1stqtr20.pdf; https://www.cnbc.com/2020/05/02/warren-buffett-berkshire-hathaway-annual-meeting-live-updates.html
Date: 2020-05-04

The original investment combined a discount to estimated economic value, resilient subsidiaries, operational improvement and productive deployment of excess cash. The pandemic creates different effects on each assumption. Treating the thesis as a single undifferentiated proposition would obscure the actual decision.

**Franchise quality:** Insurance, BNSF and regulated utilities remain large businesses with substantial barriers and resources. Q1 operating earnings were positive and increased in aggregate. The evidence does not show a conglomerate-wide solvency problem or a disappearance of the assets.

**Near-term earnings:** Shutdowns affect transport, manufacturing, service and retail demand. The Q1 period contains only the beginning of widespread US disruption, making its reported earnings an incomplete measure of current conditions. The original growth path may be delayed even where long-term competitive positions survive.

**Operational improvement:** The Jain/Abel structure remains, and the 2019 letter's rail and insurance improvement opportunities do not automatically disappear. However, economic disruption may complicate the timing of benefits. A peer margin gap is not itself a scheduled catalyst.

**Cash-deployment option:** Berkshire has more cash after the rout, yet limited immediate buying and the airline exit demonstrate caution. That may preserve value under a wide outcome distribution. It also means an investor should not simply assume the cash is already earning acquisition-like returns.

**Outside alternatives:** Pershing has separately monetized a large hedge and directly bought several portfolio businesses during the dislocation. Its smaller capital base can establish meaningful positions without requiring Berkshire-sized deals. The original capital-allocation option can now be compared against public-market opportunities that were less attractive in 2019.

**Portfolio constraint:** The April letter reports continuing free cash. Owning Berkshire also indirectly owns another large cash reserve. That combination may be desirable protection or unnecessary duplication, depending on expected opportunities and the intended portfolio risk level.

The decision is therefore broader than whether Berkshire is below intrinsic value. It includes expected return per unit of capital, the timing of deployment and relative recovery sensitivity. An investor can find Berkshire undervalued and still prefer a different use of limited capital. No manager decision or later price is supplied; this document maps the updated underwriting questions only.
