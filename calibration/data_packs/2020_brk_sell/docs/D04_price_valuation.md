Title: Valuation reference and the pitfalls of pandemic GAAP earnings
Source: compiled from: https://fiscal.ai/company/NYSE_BRK.A/; https://berkshirehathaway.com/qtrly/1stqtr20.pdf; https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2019/08/20165951/PSH-2019-Interim-Financial-Statements1.pdf
Date: 2020-05-26

Fiscal.ai's historical daily data show Berkshire Class A closing May 26 at $270,330, with 600 shares traded. That is a Class A quote and volume, not the Class B market record. The exact Class B close/volume was not independently recovered. Dividing the A quote by the 1,500 economic conversion ratio gives $180.22 of B-equivalent value, not an observed Class B execution.

The March 31 share counts imply 1,620,023 A-equivalent shares, calculated as 696,338 plus 1,385,527,870/1,500. At the Class A reference, illustrative capitalization is approximately $437.95 billion. Using shareholder equity of $371.565 billion gives a price/book proxy of approximately 1.18x. The calculation uses stale shares and ignores class-price differences.

Book value is not a complete intrinsic-value estimate. Public equities are carried at market, but controlled subsidiaries are generally not marked up to their market values. Conversely, goodwill and intangible values may not be fully realizable. Insurance liabilities and regulated-utility debt also prevent treating the cash balance as a simple sum-of-parts subtraction without further analysis.

Dividing illustrative capitalization by FY2019 operating earnings of $23.972 billion gives approximately 18.3x. This is a trailing operating-earnings ratio, not a forward economic P/E. The initial 2019 manager thesis used a different measure: 14x next-year economic EPS, excluding acquired-intangible amortization and assuming a normalized 7% investment return, or under 12x after excess cash.

Q1's very large securities loss makes a GAAP trailing multiple unstable and potentially misleading. It should not be read as proof that recurring economic earnings have collapsed by the same amount.

A fully verified 52-week high/low, marketwide forward P/E, consensus EPS growth and comparable five-to-ten-year economic multiple series were not recovered. The Class A volume understates total company equity liquidity because B shares trade separately.

The relevant valuation question is whether a roughly book-based discount and durable operating earnings compensate for waiting on capital deployment compared with available direct investments. The price/book proxy alone neither proves a bargain nor identifies an appropriate sale price.
