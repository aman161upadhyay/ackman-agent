Title: Segment benchmarks and the conglomerate comparison problem
Source: compiled from: https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2019/08/20165951/PSH-2019-Interim-Financial-Statements1.pdf; https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/2019ar/2019ar.pdf; https://berkshirehathaway.com/qtrly/1stqtr20.pdf
Date: 2020-05-02

Berkshire does not have a single clean listed peer. A railroad, auto insurer and regulated utility have different capital structures, economic cycles and valuation conventions. A parent-level headline P/E comparison obscures both insurance investment returns and the capital-intensive operating businesses.

The initial public 2019 Pershing analysis supplies two explicit operating benchmarks, which remain historical comparisons rather than updated May 2020 estimates:

| Business | Comparator | 2019 manager's stated gap |
|---|---|---|
| BNSF | North American rail peers | Operating margin nearly 500bp below peer average |
| BNSF | Best-in-class North American railroad | Operating margin nearly 800bp lower |
| GEICO | Progressive | Loss ratio over 800bp higher |
| GEICO | Progressive | Underwriting margin about 400bp lower |

The manager argues these gaps offer improvement opportunities under Jain and Abel's expanded oversight. A gap is not by itself proof of recoverability: mix, pricing, service standards, network geography and risk selection can explain part of peer differences.

Railroads benefit from hard-to-replicate routes and long-distance transportation economics, but volume is cyclical and maintenance requires investment. Comparing operating ratios can illuminate execution, although it should not erase differences in freight mix and route structure.

Auto insurance rewards low distribution and underwriting costs, disciplined pricing and claims management. GEICO's direct model provides a structural cost advantage versus agent-dependent channels, while Progressive is a relevant underwriting benchmark. Loss ratios measure claims relative to premiums; they are not equivalent to expense ratios.

Regulated utilities provide essential services and authorized-return frameworks, but require capital, debt financing and regulatory cooperation. Their cash cannot simply be treated as a parent cash balance available for opportunistic stock purchases.

No synchronized current forward multiples for two to four pure-play peers were independently recovered. Rather than fabricate a blended peer target, this pack supplies the concrete original benchmark gaps and identifies how the economic structures differ.

The existing thesis does not require every subsidiary to become best in class. Durable base earnings, some operational progress and reasonable cash deployment may suffice. The more ambitious mid-teens growth case, however, relies on improvements and capital productivity beyond passive ownership of an index-like group of assets.
