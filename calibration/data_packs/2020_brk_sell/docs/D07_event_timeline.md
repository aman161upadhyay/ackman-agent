Title: Purchase, pandemic and capital allocation through May 26
Source: compiled from: https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2019/08/20165951/PSH-2019-Interim-Financial-Statements1.pdf; https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2020/04/15125723/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-2019-Annual-Report-1.pdf; https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/2019ar/2019ar.pdf; https://www.cnbc.com/2020/05/02/warren-buffett-berkshire-hathaway-annual-meeting-live-updates.html
Date: 2020-05-26

**Q2 2019:** Pershing initiated Berkshire. Its August interim report describes an approximately 11% NAV holding and a discount-to-value thesis with insurance, operating improvement and cash-deployment components. Exact first-day execution details are not public in that report.

**August 2019:** The initial public analysis estimates 14x next-year economic earnings and under 12x net of excess cash, on its own normalization assumptions. It highlights GEICO/Progressive and BNSF/rail-peer margin gaps, not merely Buffett's public-stock record.

**2019 year-end:** Berkshire's operating earnings were $23.972 billion. Its cash/Treasury reserves were very large, supporting the optionality argument. The annual report emphasizes retaining financial strength while seeking rational opportunities.

**Late February-March 2020:** COVID disruption spreads and equity markets decline sharply. Pershing establishes credit-index protection and subsequently unwinds it. Its April annual letter reports $2.6 billion gross all-fund proceeds versus $27 million in premiums/commissions, including approximately $2.1 billion proceeds attributable to PSH.

**March 2020:** The same pre-cutoff letter reports additions to Berkshire and other holdings. Berkshire shares held increased 39%; additions also included Agilent, Hilton, Lowe's, Howard Hughes and Restaurant Brands, with Starbucks re-established. This shows the existing position had recently been reaffirmed.

**April 6:** Pershing's annual report is published. It says a substantial majority of hedge proceeds have been redeployed and approximately 18% remains in free cash. That is a letter-date snapshot, not a verified May 26 cash balance.

**April 2020:** Berkshire exits the US airline positions, as disclosed at the May 2 meeting. The disposal is a response to changed industry economics and uncertain demand.

**May 2:** Q1 results and the virtual annual meeting reveal large GAAP investment losses, positive operating earnings, a record cash reserve and limited stock buying during the rout. Buffett does not commit to a near-term large acquisition.

**May 26 cutoff:** The analyst has the original thesis, current pandemic results and public capital-allocation behavior. No subsequent investor call or decision disclosure is included. The open issue is whether the same capital now has a better prospective use elsewhere, despite the continuing quality of Berkshire's businesses.
