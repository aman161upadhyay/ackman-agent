Title: Hedge proceeds, available liquidity and competing portfolio opportunities
Source: compiled from: https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2020/04/15125723/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-2019-Annual-Report-1.pdf; https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2020/05/01172019/Performance-Report-April-2020-PSH.pdf
Date: 2020-05-01

Pershing's April annual report explains the credit-index hedge and reinvestment already public before the cutoff. Across funds, $27 million of premiums/commissions produced $2.6 billion gross proceeds, including about $2.1 billion for PSH. The proceeds are not $2.6 billion net profit; costs must be deducted.

The manager describes adding to Agilent, Berkshire, Hilton, Lowe's, Howard Hughes and Restaurant Brands, and re-establishing Starbucks. Reported increases in shares included Agilent 16%, Berkshire 39%, Hilton 34%, Howard Hughes 158%, Lowe's 46% and Restaurant Brands 26%. These are changes in shares, not percentage-point NAV weights.

The letter says approximately 18% of the portfolio is in free cash after substantial redeployment. It explains retaining flexibility under continuing uncertainty. This observation is dated April 6 and should not be presented as a current May 26 cash balance.

The latest April 30 update, published May 1, reports PSH AUM of $6,627.2 million and total strategy AUM of $8,110.6 million under its definitions. The report gives 109% net equity/debt exposure and zero remaining CDS notional. AUM, NAV and net equity exposure use different denominators and must not be mixed when determining position size.

The holdings described in the annual report include Chipotle, Hilton, Lowe's, Restaurant Brands, Berkshire, Agilent, Howard Hughes, Starbucks and Fannie/Freddie securities. Precise contemporaneous name-by-name NAV weights were not recovered. The non-US/OTC and preferred positions make a US 13F an incomplete economic portfolio.

The opportunity set includes direct ownership of businesses with operating recovery or secular-growth drivers. Hilton is exposed to travel recovery; Lowe's to home improvement; Restaurant Brands and Starbucks to restaurant demand; Agilent to analytical/life-science activity. These are different risk exposures, not guaranteed beneficiaries of reopening.

The manager's public actions show a willingness to monetize protection into equity capital while retaining liquidity. That creates a meaningful capital-allocation comparison for an existing Berkshire holding. A decision can depend on relative expected return and deployment flexibility without implying a loss of respect for Buffett or a conclusion that Berkshire's franchise has permanently deteriorated.
