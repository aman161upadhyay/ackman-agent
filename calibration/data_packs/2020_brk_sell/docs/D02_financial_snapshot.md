Berkshire's operating earnings exclude investment/derivative gains and certain other items. The distinction is critical because public-equity marks now flow through GAAP income. The table uses $millions and the company's releases for after-tax operating earnings.

The annual operating-earnings series is more useful than a headline GAAP P/E for evaluating recurring profitability, though it too requires adjustments for insurance cycles, taxes and economic conditions. The 2017 tax regime and 2018 accounting changes limit naive year-to-year comparisons.

Q1 revenue was $61,265 million versus $60,678 million a year earlier, approximately 1.0% growth. Operating earnings increased from $5,555 million to $5,871 million. GAAP losses reflected $70,275 million of pretax investment/derivative losses, not a comparable amount of cash operating losses.

March 31 Berkshire shareholder equity was $371,565 million. Consolidated cash and Treasury bills were $137,263 million, calculated from insurance/other cash of $38,717 million, short-term bills of $94,623 million and railroad/utility cash of $3,923 million. The total includes subsidiary liquidity and should not all be treated as freely deployable parent excess cash.

Notes/borrowings were $37,486 million in insurance/other and $66,747 million in railroad/utilities/energy. A simple consolidated net-debt subtraction obscures regulated-utility financing and insurance obligations; no such leverage shorthand is imposed here.

Q1 operating cash flow was $6,798 million and purchases of property/equipment and leasing equipment $2,968 million. Their difference is $3,830 million, a simple cash-flow proxy rather than distributable parent FCF. Treasury-stock purchases used $1,741 million in Q1. No dividend is proposed.

A complete consistent three-year capex/FCF/ROIC series and contemporaneous consensus EPS growth were not recovered. They are omitted rather than filled from a later financial website. The available evidence supports strong liquidity but also highlights that large reported earnings swings and cash availability require careful definitions.
