Title: Original 2019 Berkshire thesis and valuation framework
Source: https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2019/08/20165951/PSH-2019-Interim-Financial-Statements1.pdf
Date: 2019-08-15

The initial public thesis reports an investment representing approximately 11% of PSH NAV. It explicitly rejects the idea that Berkshire is merely a bet on Buffett picking publicly traded stocks: roughly half the estimated value is insurance, with the rest in controlled operating businesses. The manager expects the organization to remain effective beyond Buffett's personal tenure.

Short verbatim extracts preserve the purchase assumptions:

“the company is currently trading at one of the widest discounts to its intrinsic value in many years”

“we believe that Berkshire currently trades at only 14 times our estimate of next 12 months’ economic earnings per share”

“Net of its excess cash, Berkshire currently trades at less than 12 times our estimate of earnings per share over the next year.”

These are manager estimates, not GAAP or sell-side consensus multiples. The framework assumes a normalized 7% return on the insurance investment portfolio and excludes acquired-intangible amortization. Approximately $100 billion of cash is characterized as excess. A low interest return on that cash depresses current earnings.

The insurance moat comprises unusual underwriting capacity, a large capital base, reputation, long-duration float and GEICO's direct-distribution cost advantage. The industrial portfolio includes BNSF, Precision Castparts and regulated utilities. The latter contribute essential-service earnings, while rail benefits from barriers to entry and long-distance cost advantages.

The thesis also expects operational improvements under the structure elevating Ajit Jain and Greg Abel. It identifies BNSF margins nearly 500 basis points below average North American peers and nearly 800 below the best performer. GEICO's loss ratio is described as more than 800 basis points above Progressive's, with underwriting margin about 400 basis points below it. Those are 2019 manager comparisons, not current forecasts.

Without operating improvements or excess-capital deployment, the manager projects high-single-digit earnings growth. With both, it estimates mid-teens EPS growth over an intermediate horizon. Acquisitions and larger buybacks are potential value levers.

The purchase therefore combines existing durable earnings, a discount, fixable operating gaps and a capital-deployment option. The cash reserve is not the entire thesis, but its productive use is important to the higher-growth scenario. This distinction matters when evaluating the current capital-allocation evidence.
