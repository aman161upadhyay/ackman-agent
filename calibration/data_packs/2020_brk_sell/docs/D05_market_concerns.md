Title: Contemporary concerns — inactivity, cyclicality and uncertain recovery
Source: compiled from: https://www.cnbc.com/2020/05/02/warren-buffett-built-up-cash-bought-only-small-amounts-of-stock-during-the-stock-market-rout.html; https://www.cnbc.com/2020/05/02/warren-buffett-berkshire-hathaway-annual-meeting-live-updates.html; https://www.reuters.com/article/business/berkshire-sells-entire-stakes-in-us-airlines-buffett-idUSKBN22E0VM; https://berkshirehathaway.com/qtrly/1stqtr20.pdf
Date: 2020-05-04

CNBC's May 2 coverage emphasizes that Buffett built cash and bought relatively little stock during the market rout. The issue is not merely whether cash is safe. Shareholders who expected crisis deployment must consider the return drag and the time required to find sufficiently large opportunities.

Reuters' May 3 report describes the complete disposal of the four major US airline holdings. Buffett's statement that the world has changed for aviation underscores uncertainty about capital-intensive travel businesses, not just quarterly trading volatility. It also signals a willingness to recognize that a prior industry thesis no longer applies.

Berkshire is exposed to a wide economic shutdown through manufacturing, transport, industrial parts, services and retail. Q1 financial statements capture only the start of those effects. The absence of a full shutdown quarter makes extrapolating current operating earnings hazardous.

Insurance adds another layer. Lower driving can reduce auto claims, but investment yields are falling and pandemic-related claims/coverage questions can affect other lines. Large float and a strong capital base provide flexibility, yet neither guarantees a fixed future underwriting return.

The securities portfolio creates visible volatility in GAAP net income and book value. A bear case that focuses solely on the $49.7 billion Q1 net loss overstates operating damage. A bull case that dismisses all of it as accounting noise may understate the consequences of weaker holdings or persistently reduced earnings power.

The original investment case also included margin improvements at BNSF and insurance subsidiaries. Economic disruption may delay those improvements, while the longer-term competitive positions remain intact. Delay matters to expected annualized returns even if ultimate intrinsic value is not permanently impaired.

The counterweight is exceptional liquidity, diversification and decentralized operating expertise. Berkshire can wait without being forced to sell under stress. For an outside investor, the challenge is opportunity cost: a smaller capital pool can buy individual public businesses without requiring Berkshire-sized deals.

No post-May-26 assessment is included. The contemporary decision is whether to accept the combination of safety, patience and discounted value or redeploy toward more direct recovery exposure. That comparison is distinct from alleging management incompetence or imminent balance-sheet distress.
