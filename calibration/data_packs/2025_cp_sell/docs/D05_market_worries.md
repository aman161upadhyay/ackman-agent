Title: Trade-policy uncertainty versus network resilience
Source: compiled from: https://financialpost.com/transportation/rail/tariff-threat-cpkc-forcasts-trade-growth; https://abcnews.com/Business/stocks-tumble-tariff-fallout-roils-markets/story?id=119508907; https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2025/03/03/trump-tariffs-canada-mexico-tuesday/81172627007; https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2025/03/14183709/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-2024-Annual-Report-1.pdf
Date: 2025-03-14

The central bear mechanism is that a network built to benefit from North American economic integration is unusually exposed to policy that disrupts that integration. Higher tariffs can reduce trade, delay customer investment, change sourcing and alter the mix of rail shipments. Track scarcity does not protect against every reduction in freight demand.

Bart Jansen's March 3 USA Today account reports President Trump's statement that 25% tariffs on imports from Canada and Mexico would begin the next day. ABC News' Max Zahn, March 6, reports a temporary pause for goods compliant with the USMCA, expanding an automaker carve-out. The exemption is a current policy fact, not proof that the dispute is permanently resolved.

Repeated announcements and temporary relief can create uncertainty even when some immediate shipments are exempt. Customers may postpone orders, build inventory ahead of changes or reroute supply chains. Timing effects could initially make traffic look stronger before weakening, so a single week's carloads would be insufficient evidence of long-term resilience.

The January Canadian Press report captures the bullish counterargument. Creel expects pragmatism because integrated supply chains remain valuable. Pershing's March 14 report similarly emphasizes nearshoring, predecessor earnings growth during the first Trump administration and the necessity of continued regional trade for US growth.

Those statements are explicitly forward-looking. Prior tariffs were not necessarily identical in scope, duration or retaliation. The key analytical question is whether earnings can remain predictable enough under several plausible policy paths, not whether the network has suddenly become unnecessary.

Other risks include fixed-cost operating leverage, fuel and labor costs, service disruptions, integration complexity and substantial capital requirements. FY2024 debt net of cash was approximately C$21.9 billion. Strong free cash generation helps, but debt and required infrastructure spending reduce flexibility if volumes deteriorate sharply.

Counterevidence includes a strong Q4 ratio, adjusted EPS above expectations and already-realized merger revenue synergies. A skeptical case must explain why those gains are insufficient rather than pretend they do not exist.

No dated pre-cutoff source here proves a permanent loss of competitive position. The strongest evidence concerns a wider range of macro outcomes. That uncertainty matters particularly for a concentrated investor whose standard includes limited exposure to uncontrollable extrinsic factors.
