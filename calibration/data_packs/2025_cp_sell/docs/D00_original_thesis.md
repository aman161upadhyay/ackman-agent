Title: Late-2021 underwriting — the KCS network combination
Source: https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2022/03/29140526/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-2021-Annual-Report.pdf
Date: 2022-03-29

Pershing's March 29, 2022 report states: “Beginning in late 2021, we reinitiated an investment in Canadian Pacific.” This is a distinct investment from the 2011 activist campaign, which ended in 2016. Historical management change supplies background, but the new thesis centers on acquiring and integrating Kansas City Southern.

The letter describes the KCS purchase as “a transformational and value-creating transaction.” The combined network would be the only North American railroad with a direct Canada-to-Mexico route, connect six of the seven largest North American metropolitan regions, and create revenue and cost synergies. At the letter date, CP owned KCS economically through a voting trust but lacked operating control pending regulatory approval.

The business-quality argument was explicit: “We believe CP is an attractive business as it operates in an oligopolistic industry where the barriers to entry are high due to considerable capital requirements, regulation, and network effects.” Rail is often the cheapest or only practical way to move heavy freight over long distances. That mission-critical role supports pricing, but does not make volume immune to economic conditions.

Management execution mattered. The letter says Keith Creel and predecessor Hunter Harrison had more than doubled operating margins from 19% in 2011 to 42% in 2021. It expected CP's leadership to improve KCS operations, while rail's lower long-distance cost and lower emissions could support share gains from trucking.

Valuation was also part of the original case. Despite stronger prospective growth from the combination, shares were described as trading below their historical and peer valuation levels. Successful consummation and integration were expected to produce robust medium-term FCF-per-share growth and a higher valuation more consistent with history and peers.

The essential underwriting tests are therefore merger approval and integration, incremental traffic and synergies, operating discipline, cash generation after necessary rail investment, and the price paid for that growth. Nearshoring and greater North American economic integration were favorable demand assumptions, not guarantees.

This letter does not give a verified original average cost, exact initial PSH cost weight or a precise target price. Those missing figures should not be reconstructed using the much earlier activist position. A current review should compare today's network and policy environment with this specific 2021–22 thesis.
