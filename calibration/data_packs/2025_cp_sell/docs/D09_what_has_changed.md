Title: Testing the 2021 KCS thesis at the March 2025 checkpoint

Date: 2025-03-14

The initial investment anticipated obtaining operating control, realizing synergies, improving KCS operations and compounding FCF per share. Several risks have diminished. Regulatory uncertainty over obtaining control is no longer the central live issue: the combination has operated under common control since April 2023.

Synergies are no longer only a presentation forecast. The March 14 Pershing report cites more than $800 million run-rate revenue synergies and ongoing cost savings. FY2024 CFO rose to C$5.269 billion from C$4.137 billion, while adjusted combined EPS grew 11%. These observations support important parts of the original underwriting.

The quality of the network and management has not visibly disappeared. The same published report praises the trilateral route, management execution and long-term cash generation. An assessment claiming established competitive deterioration would need evidence beyond the material included here.

What has changed is the policy backdrop. Nearshoring and increasing North American integration were favorable original assumptions. Broad tariffs, retaliation and repeated temporary exemptions create a wider range of possible traffic and customer-investment paths. The unique network's greatest strategic differentiator is consequently also a source of direct policy sensitivity.

There is an important counterargument: integrated supply chains are costly to rebuild, USMCA-compliant shipments have temporary relief, and management still guides to double-digit EPS growth. The prior administration's tariff experience offers some resilience evidence. None of that makes current policy decisions controllable by the railway.

Financial flexibility remains a separate test. The combined company has C$22.623 billion debt before cash and plans C$2.9 billion 2025 capex. Its required infrastructure spending means EBITDA cannot be treated as fully distributable cash. Volume shocks would affect a fixed-cost business while maintenance needs continue.

The stock trades at about 25.3x FY2024 adjusted combined EPS and 21.4–22.6x the company's 2025 guided range. Exact rerating since entry is not established because original average cost and consistent historical multiples were not recovered.

The current decision therefore requires distinguishing successful integration from changing forward risk. It is possible for the operational thesis to be progressing while prospective risk-adjusted attractiveness changes. It is equally possible for policy noise to leave long-term economics largely intact. The evidence does not justify replacing either possibility with hindsight.
