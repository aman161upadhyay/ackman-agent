Title: January results and the public 2025 growth case
Source: compiled from: https://www.cpkcr.com/en/media/Q4-2024-Results; https://financialpost.com/transportation/rail/tariff-threat-cpkc-forcasts-trade-growth; https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2025/03/14183709/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-2024-Annual-Report-1.pdf
Date: 2025-03-14

CPKC's January 29 release reports Q4 revenue of approximately C$3.9 billion, up 3% from C$3.8 billion. Reported EPS was C$1.28 and core adjusted combined EPS C$1.29. The reported operating ratio was 59.7%; the core adjusted combined ratio was 57.1%. These are different bases, not interchangeable precision alternatives.

Christopher Reynolds of Canadian Press, writing January 29 and updated January 30 in Financial Post, reports adjusted EPS above the C$1.24 analyst expectation. Management's constructive tone therefore followed actual operational outperformance rather than only a distant merger promise.

The January guidance calls for core adjusted diluted EPS growth of 12–18% from 2024's C$4.25. Calculated, that implies C$4.76–5.015 per share. Management expects mid-single-digit revenue-ton-mile growth and C$2.9 billion capital spending. The release notes a higher USD/CAD rate contributes to the capex increase.

Creel says, “I think a pragmatic approach will carry the day,” in response to the tariff threat, per the Canadian Press report. His view is that integrated North American supply chains will continue to require rail service. This is a management judgment about policy and customer behavior, not a binding assurance from the US administration.

Pershing's March 14 annual report supplies an independently public thesis update. It reports more than $800 million of run-rate revenue synergies, exceeding management expectations, plus operational/procurement savings. The paragraph's dollar denomination is not repeated explicitly, so this pack does not relabel that figure as USD or CAD.

The report says these synergies supported 11% EPS growth in 2024 despite labor disruptions and a difficult freight market. It argues that both predecessor businesses grew EPS at mid-teens annual rates during the first Trump administration. That historical analogy supports resilience but does not prove the current tariff regime will have identical effects.

The last recovered company guidance remains the January range. No April results, later revision or eventual realized 2025 effect belongs in this March snapshot. The immediate tension is between demonstrated synergy execution and a policy environment that may change cross-border volumes more quickly than the railway can adjust fixed costs.
