Title: Public year-end ownership and portfolio opportunity cost

Date: 2025-03-14

The March 14 report gives December 31 PSH net assets of approximately $13.01 billion. The latest recovered wider firm AUM is September 30, 2024, $18.4988 billion including PSVII and bond proceeds. This is stale gross AUM, not March NAV or free buying power.

The audited PSH schedule provides actual fund-NAV weights, unlike a US 13F-only table. CPKC was 13,267,340 shares worth $960.157 million, or 7.38% of PSH net assets, at December 31. That is a market-value allocation, not original cost weight and not current March exposure.

Other disclosed year-end weights included Universal Music 23.11%, Alphabet A/C together 15.12%, Chipotle 10.19%, Nike 9.86%, Howard Hughes 9.77% and Hilton 9.21%. The listed common-stock total exceeded 100% of NAV because gross exposure is financed partly by fund-level debt. These weights should not be forced to sum to 100% by omitting financing or other assets.

Cash and equivalents were approximately $436.5 million at year-end. Liquidity, other instruments and financing mean this is not all deployable firm capital. The March report also publicly discusses Uber as a new investment; no future transaction is inferred from that fact.

Pershing's published CPKC commentary remains constructive. It describes a “high-quality, inflation-protected North American railroad” and a “best-in-class management team.” It expects double-digit earnings and FCF-per-share growth supported by synergies, pricing and repurchases. Tariff uncertainty is acknowledged but countered with regional economic integration and predecessor resilience.

This context matters because the decision is not between owning a railroad and holding nothing forever. A concentrated fund compares each position's prospective return and forecastability with available alternatives. Strong historical management execution and a scarce network must be weighed against current policy sensitivity and the capital required to support that network.

The report's preferences for durable competitive advantages, predictable cash generation, sound balance sheets and capable management are relevant. They do not predetermine a current decision. No later portfolio action or post-March public macro commentary is part of this document.

Exact March weights, a full recent-exit register and the complete firm cash position remain unavailable. The dated year-end PSH schedule is the honest ownership baseline.
