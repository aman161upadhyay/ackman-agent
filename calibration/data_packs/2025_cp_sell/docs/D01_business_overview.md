Title: A scarce trilateral rail network with real capital requirements
Source: compiled from: https://s21.q4cdn.com/736796105/files/doc_financials/2024/ar/CPKC-2024-Annual-Report-Web_Final.pdf; https://www.cpkcr.com/en/media/Q4-2024-Results
Date: 2025-02-25

Canadian Pacific Kansas City provides freight rail transportation across Canada, the United States and Mexico. Its network combines legacy Canadian Pacific with Kansas City Southern, operating under common control since April 14, 2023. The single-line service proposition reduces interchange complexity for some cross-border routes and creates new customer options.

Rail has physical network advantages. Building competing rights of way, track, terminals and operating capacity is expensive, time-consuming and heavily regulated. Existing route density and connections matter. These barriers support an oligopolistic industry structure, but competition remains from other railroads, trucking, water transport and alternative routing.

Customers move commodities and industrial products as well as intermodal freight. Demand depends on agricultural production, energy and industrial activity, trade patterns and consumer supply chains. Some traffic is essential, yet annual tonnage and mix remain cyclical. Price increases can partly offset inflation without fully offsetting a major volume shock.

FY2024 freight revenue was C$14.223 billion and non-freight revenue C$323 million, for total revenue C$14.546 billion. Operating income was C$5.179 billion. The reported 64.4% operating ratio means operating expense divided by revenue; lower is better. It is not EBITDA margin, and depreciation is included in operating expense.

Keith Creel is president and CEO. The thesis relies on operating execution, safe and reliable service, network capacity and realization of merger synergies. Procurement efficiencies and better asset utilization can improve margins; revenue synergies require customers to adopt new services and depend partly on trade flows.

This is not a capital-light platform. FY2024 additions to properties were C$2.825 billion, plus C$38 million to Meridian Speedway properties. Long-lived infrastructure requires continuing maintenance and investment. Accounting earnings, EBITDA and spendable FCF are consequently different economic measures.

Year-end debt was C$22.623 billion including current maturities, against C$739 million cash. The company had 933.5 million common shares outstanding. Debt was part of financing the combined network, making deleveraging and cash discipline relevant even when the operating franchise remains strong.

The key business-quality distinction is between scarcity of the network and variability of the freight moving over it. Tariffs can affect traffic, customer investment and routing without eliminating rights-of-way advantages. A durable network is necessary but not sufficient for a predictable short-to-medium-term earnings path.
