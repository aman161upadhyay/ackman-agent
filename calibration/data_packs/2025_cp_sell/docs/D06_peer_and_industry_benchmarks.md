Canadian National and Union Pacific provide relevant operating comparisons. Both have extensive established networks, significant capital requirements and cyclical freight exposure. Neither exactly replicates CPKC's single-line Canada–US–Mexico service, so the investment case cannot be reduced to choosing the lowest operating ratio.

Canadian National's report says revenue grew 1%, while operating income fell 5% and reported operating ratio worsened 2.6 percentage points. The adjusted ratio worsened 2.1 points. Industry-wide infrastructure advantages therefore do not guarantee annual margin expansion.

Union Pacific's January 23 release reports FY2024 EPS up 6%, a 59.9% operating ratio and 15.8% return on invested capital. Q4 EPS was $2.91, up 7%, and operating ratio 58.7%. These figures demonstrate a high-quality peer benchmark, but US-dollar EPS and a different business mix prevent direct share-price comparisons.

CPKC's acquisition accounting includes larger depreciation and amortization from combining businesses. Adjusted combined metrics attempt to improve economic comparability, but use of them requires explicit reconciliation rather than selectively pairing adjusted CPKC results with unadjusted peers.

The industry's competitive moat is common: rail corridors are hard to reproduce and efficient for long-distance bulk freight. Differentiation comes from route geography, service reliability, customer mix, pricing, network fluidity and capital allocation. Trucking is also an important alternative, particularly over shorter distances and where customers value flexibility.

Trade-policy exposure differs with geography and freight composition. CPKC's unique trilateral route is both a strategic advantage and a channel through which a cross-border volume shock could matter. A peer with lower direct exposure may still suffer indirectly through industrial demand.
