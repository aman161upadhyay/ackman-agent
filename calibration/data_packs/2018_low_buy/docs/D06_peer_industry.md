Title: The Home Depot benchmark — what the gap does and does not prove
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/60667/000006066718000015/exhibit991_02022018.htm; https://ir.homedepot.com/~/media/Files/H/HomeDepot-IR/reports-and-presentations/quarterly-earnings/q4-2017-press-release.pdf
Date: 2018-02-28

Home Depot is the most directly relevant scale benchmark. The companies share broad home-improvement demand, store/distribution infrastructure and both household and professional customers. The comparison is still imperfect because their customer, geographic and operational mixes differ.

| FY2017 measure | Lowe's | Home Depot |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue | $68.619bn | $100.904bn |
| Operating income | $6.586bn | $14.681bn |
| Calculated operating margin | 9.6% | 14.5% |
| Diluted EPS | $4.09 | $7.29 |
| Comparable sales growth | 4.0% | 6.8% |
| CFO | $5.065bn | $12.031bn |
| Capex | $1.123bn | $1.897bn |

Home Depot reported sales per square foot of $417.02. Lowe's year-end selling-area figure is not a matched weighted-average denominator, so a directly comparable productivity ratio is not manufactured by dividing annual sales by year-end area.

The approximately 490bp reported operating-margin gap indicates meaningful potential economic value if even part is fixable. Each 100bp on Lowe's sales base is about $686 million EBIT before taxes, reinvestment and any growth.

However, peer-level margin is not an automatic entitlement. Home Depot's larger scale, mix and operating systems may support durable advantages. Some catch-up spending can depress near-term earnings before benefits appear.

The balance-sheet/cash-flow comparison is also important: Home Depot's FY2017 CFO-minus-capex was approximately $10.134 billion, versus Lowe's $3.942 billion. Different scale accounts for part, but the data show why current profit conversion deserves attention.

Home Depot's 2018 outlook includes approximately 14.5% operating margin, while Lowe's projects a 30bp decline from its lower base. The public forward evidence does not yet show the gap narrowing.

Complete timestamped valuation and margin tables for additional peers were not recovered. Smaller specialty retailers or general merchandisers would be less direct benchmarks, and no two-to-four-peer valuation ranking is invented.

The investable thesis must identify which parts of the gap are operationally addressable, by whom and at what cost. A lower P/E without a credible bridge from current execution to improved cash earnings is not enough.
