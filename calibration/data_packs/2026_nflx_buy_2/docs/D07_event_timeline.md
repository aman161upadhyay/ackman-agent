Date: 2026-04-20

November 2025: Netflix effected a ten-for-one stock split. January and April 2026 shareholder-letter per-share comparisons are retroactively adjusted. Old 2022 share counts and prices need the same conversion before comparison.

December 5, 2025: Netflix and Warner announced a transaction for Warner's studios and streaming operations following the contemplated separation of Discovery Global. The announcement valued the target equity at approximately $72.0 billion and enterprise value at $82.7 billion. That headline value is not the value of all Warner activities remaining in every scenario.

January 20, 2026: Netflix reported FY2025 revenue of approximately $45.2 billion, 29.5% operating margin, more than $1.5 billion ad revenue and more than 325 million paid memberships. It forecast $50.7–51.7 billion revenue and 31.5% operating margin for 2026. The letter also disclosed modest 2% H2 viewing-hours growth.

February 26: CNBC reported Netflix was walking away after Warner's board deemed Paramount Skydance's revised offer superior. Paramount agreed to pay the $2.8 billion fee Warner owed Netflix. The abandoned transaction removes prospective financing/integration demands, while leaving any hoped-for combined-company strategic benefits unrealized. This is already public before the cutoff.

April 16: Netflix reported Q1 growth and unchanged annual revenue/margin targets. It recognized the termination fee outside operating income and in cash flow. It announced that Hastings would not seek board reelection at the June meeting, while the co-CEO structure remained in place.

April 17: Reuters reported shares down more than 10% early in the session on the forecast and governance news. Historical close was $97.31, on 125.96 million shares.

April 20: The next trading day closed at $94.83 on 63.30 million shares. This is the snapshot date. It is selected because the April earnings dislocation is independently dated, not because any particular investor's execution day is known.

Events beyond this date, later viewing data, later earnings and subsequent prices are not part of the information set. The appropriate live questions are standalone growth, advertising economics, content cash conversion and whether the reset price offers sufficient compensation for remaining uncertainty.
