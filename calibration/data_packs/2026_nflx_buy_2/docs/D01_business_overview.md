Title: Global subscription scale with a growing advertising layer
Source: compiled from: https://s22.q4cdn.com/959853165/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/FINAL-Q4-25-Shareholder-Letter.pdf; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528026000137/ex991_q126.htm
Date: 2026-04-16

Netflix sells access to entertainment through recurring memberships, with advertising on lower-priced plans and programming available across a global distribution system. Content includes licensed and internally commissioned productions. It is fundamentally a portfolio business: the economics depend on acquiring and retaining an audience over many titles rather than predicting one film's success.

The January 20 letter reports more than 325 million paid memberships in Q4 2025. It reports 96 billion viewing hours in H2 2025, up 2%, with branded-original viewing up 9%. Memberships and hours measure different things: more customers can coexist with modest aggregate-hours growth, and revenue can grow through pricing and plan mix without an equivalent increase in hours.

Scale potentially supports a reinforcing loop. A large audience spreads programming, technology and marketing costs; attractive programming supports retention and acquisition; distribution and recommendation capabilities help audiences discover titles. However, content investment is real and recurrent. It should not be described as a zero-maintenance royalty simply because delivery is digital.

Advertising revenue exceeded $1.5 billion in 2025, more than 2.5 times 2024, per the January letter. The April letter says the ad-supported plan represented more than 60% of Q1 sign-ups in countries with advertising. Those countries are not the entire world, and the figure is sign-ups, not the existing global subscriber mix. Netflix still expects roughly $3 billion advertising revenue in 2026.

The company reports consolidated financials rather than separate public subscription and advertising profit segments. Q1 revenue was about $12.25 billion, operating income $3.96 billion and margin 32.3%. The reported margin is evidence of scale economics, not a disclosed advertising margin.

Ted Sarandos and Greg Peters are co-CEOs. Reed Hastings intends not to stand for reelection at the June annual meeting. That is an announced future board transition, not a completed management departure as of April 20. Q1 fully diluted shares were 4.298 billion, and recent financial comparisons reflect the November 2025 ten-for-one split.

Moat durability depends on consumer relevance, pricing net of churn, content-spend efficiency and the ability to monetize attention. YouTube, social video, other streaming services, games and traditional entertainment compete for time and money. Scale is valuable but does not make those alternatives irrelevant.
