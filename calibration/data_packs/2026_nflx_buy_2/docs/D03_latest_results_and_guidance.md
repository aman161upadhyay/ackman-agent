Title: April 16 results — strong operating growth, unchanged annual outlook
Source: compiled from: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528026000137/ex991_q126.htm; https://s22.q4cdn.com/959853165/files/doc_financials/2026/q1/Netflix-Inc-_Earnings-Call_2026-04-16T00_00_00_English-1.pdf
Date: 2026-04-16

Netflix reports Q1 revenue growth of 16%, or 14% currency-neutral, and operating-income growth of 18%. Revenue was approximately $12.25 billion and operating income $3.96 billion, with 32.3% operating margin. Management says revenue and operating income exceeded its guidance because subscription revenue was slightly stronger than planned.

Diluted EPS of $1.23 is not a clean recurring operating indicator. A $2.8 billion payment following termination of the Warner transaction was recognized outside operating income. It also materially lifted operating cash flow and FCF. Any forecast that simply annualizes Q1 EPS or FCF would capitalize a one-time receipt as if it repeated.

The call's estimates table shows a $12.627 billion Q2 revenue consensus and $0.83 EPS consensus against $12.574 billion and $0.78 guidance. These are analyst estimates at the call date, not management promises. The full-year revenue and margin guidance is unchanged.

Netflix expects Q2 to have the year's highest year-over-year content-amortization growth. It expects cash content spending around 1.1 times annual amortization. That helps explain why the quarterly profit trajectory need not match revenue growth exactly.

Management says recent price changes “have gone well” and its internal primary quality-engagement metric reached a Q1 record. Those are management assessments rather than a disclosed universally comparable viewing-hours series. The January letter's 2% H2-hours increase remains a separate factual observation.

The April letter describes Warner as a potential accelerant only at the right price. With that transaction no longer pursued, current public guidance is an organic-growth case rather than a forecast of combined-company synergies. Hastings' announced decision not to seek board reelection adds a governance event, but the letter does not announce a co-CEO change.
