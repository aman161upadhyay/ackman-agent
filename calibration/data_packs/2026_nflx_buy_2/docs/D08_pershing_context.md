The February 11 presentation reports February 9 gross core-fund AUM of $19.748 billion and core equity of $16.077 billion. PSH comprised $17.872 billion gross AUM and $14.202 billion equity. Including Howard Hughes, reported gross assets were $30.040 billion. These are different measures; gross AUM includes debt, and Howard Hughes assets are not uncommitted fund cash.

The presentation gives Amazon at 13% and Meta at 10% of capital as of December 31, and Hertz at 2%. These are published portfolio-context weights, not prospective allocations for a new investment. Other public core names include Alphabet, Brookfield, Uber, Restaurant Brands, Universal Music, Howard Hughes and Fannie/Freddie exposure. Complete exact April weights and cash are unavailable.

December-quarter 13F information is public by this cutoff, but a complete independently reconciled line-item table has not been assembled here. US reportable holdings omit some foreign shares, preferreds, cash and derivatives. A 13F percentage would not automatically be a full PSH NAV percentage.

The February presentation restates core principles: “Simple, predictable, free-cash-flow-generative business,” formidable barriers to entry, limited extrinsic-factor exposure, strong finances, minimal capital-markets dependence, and exceptional management/governance. It emphasizes valuation without reliance on future acquisitions or platform value. The stated discipline is particularly relevant after an abandoned large transaction.

Netflix-specific public history is the 2022 correspondence in D00. The April 2022 letter explains that concentrated ownership requires a high degree of predictability and that model changes widened the forecast range too much. It did not deny the possibility of subsequent business success. This standard should be applied to the currently visible advertising business, content spending and cash conversion, rather than inferred from any unpublished current ownership.

Permanent capital can support a long investment horizon, but it does not make uncertainty costless. Adding a consumer subscription/media business would diversify some industry exposure while adding sensitivity to household demand, entertainment competition and content investment. Those correlations should be evaluated alongside existing technology and consumer franchises.

No authenticated 2026 Netflix-specific public Ackman thesis statement before April 20 is supplied. The current decision must come from the pack's company evidence and the previously stated investment standard, not later disclosures.
