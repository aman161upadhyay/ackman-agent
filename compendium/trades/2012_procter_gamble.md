## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
On July 12, 2012, public reporting revealed approximately $2 billion of P&G stock. The company was lagging peer growth/returns; Ackman's case sought better performance from existing consumer brands, not a new category invention. HBS's case abstract dates the disclosure and subsequent engagement; a full entry-date financial pack and original presentation remain unavailable. [1]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
**Original quote gap:** no authenticated original P&G deck or full interview transcript recovered. Contemporary reports attribute to Ackman dissatisfaction with peer-relative performance, pressure for leadership change and portfolio simplification. These are paraphrases, not invented quotations. [1–2,4]

## What Pershing Square did
Disclosed in July 2012; engaged directors and publicly pressed CEO Bob McDonald's performance. In May 2013 McDonald departed and former CEO A.G. Lafley returned. By November 2013 Pershing had sold much of the ordinary-stock stake but replaced it with call options: a falling 13F common-share count was **not by itself an economic exit**. Final 2014 derivatives closure remains unverified. [1–3]

## What happened after
Leadership changed; P&G subsequently moved to streamline the portfolio, including an April 2014 agreement to sell most pet-food brands to Mars for $2.9 billion. This is a corporate outcome, not verified Pershing realized P&L. `win` records direction of activist objectives, with exact return unknown. [2,4]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Peer gap:** improving an existing franchise rather than relying on category growth alone. [1–2]
- **CEO change:** pressure culminated in Lafley's return, though Ackman reportedly initially favored an outsider. [2]
- **Portfolio simplification:** non-core asset disposal could improve focus. [4]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: high-quality franchise, underperformance and governance intervention resemble the CP template, but brand/category management differs from rail operating-ratio optimization. Options make ownership and economic exposure distinct. [1–3]

## Public discussion
Fortune's May 2013 report documented the leadership reversal and Ackman's reaction. The Motley Fool in November cautions against interpreting stock sales without the replacement calls; NYT's April 2014 story covered the portfolio slimming. Contemporary social and forum links remain unverified. [2–4]
