## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

Primary March 2009 holdings establish a common-stock investment, 1,554,315 shares worth $42.712M. Exact initial day and financial snapshot unknown. The original letter later places this opportunity among late-Q1/early-Q2 purchases; the March holding proves Yum itself was already owned in Q1. [Primary 13F, 2009-05-15](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336528/000117266109000911/0001172661-09-000911.txt); [original Q2 letter, page 7](https://www.scribd.com/document/20150688/Pershing-Square-Q2-2009-Investor-Letter).

## Ackman's thesis in his own words

In discussing missed buying opportunities: “The principal misses this year have been positions that we initiated in late Q1 and early Q2 at prices we believed to be extraordinarily attractive – YUM Brands in the low to mid-$20s, various REITs, Saks Inc. at less than $3 per share…” He says they failed to buy aggressively as prices rose. This verifies perceived cheapness and a sizing regret, **not a detailed 2009 franchise or China-separation argument**. [Original Q2 2009 letter, page 7, public by 2009-09-24, above.]

## What Pershing Square did

March shares comprised 984,219 and 570,096 across accounts. Q2 holdings coverage records full sale; June is the quarter-end bound, not a precise sale month. Approximately 2.14% weight describes March **13F assets**, not total NAV. [Primary March filing, above; Market Folly, 2009-08-17](https://www.marketfolly.com/2009/08/pershing-square-new-positions-in.html).

## What happened after

Exact realized return unknown. Ackman's later regret was a mistake of omission/underbuying during a fast rally, not a claim that the underlying shares lost money. Do not conflate this episode with his much later Yum/China discussions. [Original Q2 2009 letter, page 7, above.]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

Illustrates the trade-off between time-consuming diligence and speed in a rebound: Ackman admits missing the desired position size. That is different from a failed proxy battle. [Original Q2 2009 letter, pages 7–8, above.]

Contemporary tracker identifies Yum among positions exited in Q2 and notes the rotation into McDonald's; rotation motive is the observer's inference. [Market Folly, 2009-08-17, above.]
