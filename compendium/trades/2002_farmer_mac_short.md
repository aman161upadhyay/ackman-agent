## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Public-data cutoff March 27, 2002 / campaign disclosure May 23, 2002.** Farmer Mac was a congressionally chartered buyer/guarantor of agricultural loans. Its FY2001 10-K reported $1.3B purchased or guaranteed under Farmer Mac I during 2001 and $3.5B post-1996-Act Farmer Mac I securities outstanding at year-end. Its GSE identity did not make its obligations those of the other Farm Credit System institutions. Exact first-purchase date is unknown, so these are a pre-report information base rather than a guaranteed pre-entry snapshot. [3]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
Gotham, **May 23, 2002**, *Buying the Farm*: “We believe Farmer Mac’s earnings are overstated and may, in net economic effect, be illusory as a result of the company’s inadequate levels of loan-loss reserves.” [1, introduction]

The report argued reserves of 19.5% of delinquent loans were about one-tenth comparable agricultural lenders' and about one-eighth money-center banks'; $2.3B/over 72% of debt was commercial-paper funding with approximately one-week average maturity. **These are Gotham's allegations and calculations**, not independently adopted findings. [1]

## What Pershing Square did
Gotham met management April 8 and listened to the April 19, 2002 call, according to its report. The report discloses bearish investments but not an adequately detailed position ledger. Observer reported the short was already in place at the April meeting. Gotham published May 23, and subsequent research followed management rebuttals. Precise notional, borrow, put/CDS mix and covers are unknown. [1,2,5]

## What happened after
Observer reported in July 2002 that the stock had approximately halved since April coverage. Bloomberg's later biography assigns $80M earnings to the Farmer Mac short; treat that as a **secondary reported figure**, not an audited Gotham-specific realized P&L. No claim is made that a much later company event was a payoff held continuously from 2002. [2,4]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Under-reserving:** changing reserve assumptions could eliminate retained earnings/book capital, Gotham argued. [1]
- **Disclosure:** report separately analyzes denominator growth and numerator reductions in delinquency measures. [1]
- **Funding mismatch:** weekly refinancing dependence could create a liquidity event before ultimate loan losses. [1]
- **Government association/capital:** report questions the market's rating assumptions and whether capital survives realistic credit provisioning. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Original disclaimer says Gotham did not generally make shorts but considered this an exception. This is a credit/accounting dislocation with public persuasion, not an operational activist long. [1]

## Public discussion
Observer's July 29 article documents management's accusation of an overly close journalist/short-seller relationship and the NYT's defense. It also records UBS Warburg's bullish $60 target and view that credit/interest-rate risk was low. This is useful contemporaneous opposition, not proof of misconduct. No authenticated VIC discussion was recovered. [2]
