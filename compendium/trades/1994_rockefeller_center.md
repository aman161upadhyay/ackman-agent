**Entry snapshot unavailable.** The requested 1994 start has not been established from a purchase record; the documented public contest is in 1995. RCP was a mortgage-investment vehicle, not simply direct fee ownership of the buildings; the successor's filing describes participating convertible mortgages on the original landmark buildings. ## What Pershing Square did
Gotham, not Pershing Square, pursued the Rockefeller Center investment with Leucadia in 1995. The issuer's November 1995 filing establishes termination of the Zell-related combination agreement and execution of the Whitehall/Rockefeller/Exor merger agreement. The Leucadia/Gotham offer and first-purchase details require the original proxy and Gotham correspondence. [1,6]

## What happened after
The Goldman/Whitehall-led group prevailed. Goldman's retrospective records a $1.15B offer including $306.1M equity and approximately $845M assumed debt and liabilities; these final totals should not be confused with the earlier $7.75-per-share/$296.5M bid reported October 2, 1995. **Failure to win control does not establish an investment loss.** Gotham's cost, exit proceeds and return remain unknown. [2,3]
