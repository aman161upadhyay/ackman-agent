## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2005 decision snapshot, figures subsequently laid out in November presentation.** The company's 2004 results were already public: Pershing's presentation reports $19.065B revenue, $5.183B adjusted EBITDA and $3.5B pretax unlevered FCF. Its thesis was **not that management had failed at the operating turnaround**: it credited product innovation, capital discipline and improved execution after weak 2001–02 results. First-purchase average price is unknown. The November $42B market cap and Pershing's $46B owned/controlled property estimate are not represented as entry facts. The 2009 letter calls the original purchase “mid-2005”; September is the documented engagement period, not safely the first purchase. [1,7]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
**November 15, 2005**, Ackman at Value Investing Congress: “This is not a case at all where we think McDonald's is doing a bad job.” [3]

**January 18, 2006 deck:** “McOpCo, as a wholly owned subsidiary, is not achieving its full business and financial potential.” It argues uncharged rent/franchise fees subsidized corporate restaurants. [2]

## What Pershing Square did
- **2005:** accumulated 4.9% economic exposure; September 22 presented to management, October 31 received management's response. Exact initial purchase month unknown; retrospective letter says mid-2005. [1–3,7]
- **November 15:** public *A Value Menu for McDonald's* proposed an IPO of 65% of roughly 8,000 McOpCo restaurants, leaving McDonald's with franchise/real-estate economics, and borrowing $14.7B against real estate for repurchases. Do not misdescribe this simply as a REIT spin. [1,3]
- **January 18, 2006:** *A Plan to Win / Win* revised proposal after discussions with management, franchisees and shareholders. Management's concerns included frictional costs, credit effects and system issues. Press describes a proposal to refranchise 1,000 restaurants in mature markets and redeploy capital to developing markets. [2,5]
- **January 25–26:** after management changes/disclosure commitments, Ackman dropped the IPO demand and supported management rather than running a proxy battle. [4]

## What happened after
The initial McOpCo transaction was not adopted. That is **not equivalent to a failed investment**: the investor adapted to refranchising, greater transparency and capital return. The recovered original Q2 2009 letter states: “We sold our previously held stake in McDonald’s in the fourth quarter of 2007 at approximately $56 per share.” December in front matter is a quarter-end bound, not an exact sale month. Exact realized P&L remains unknown. Its 2009 repurchase is a [distinct passive re-entry](2009_mcdonalds_reentry.md), not evidence the 2005 derivatives persisted. [4,7]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Separate economics:** low-margin owned operations obscured high-margin rent/royalty value. [1]
- **Normalize unit economics:** compare McOpCo and franchisees only after market rent and franchise fee. [2]
- **Capital return:** financing/repurchase proposals sought to monetize excess asset value. [1,3]
- **Adaptation:** revised deck and later withdrawal of IPO demand demonstrate means could change while value-creation objectives persisted. [2,4]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Analytical comparison: constructive engagement with a good management team, not automatic CEO replacement or “activism must win its original demand.” Options made the investment's payoff and timing different from an unlevered stock holding. [1–4]

## Public discussion
AP's November 15 report records management's objection that the plan was financial engineering, costly and reduced operating control, alongside supportive institutional reactions. January coverage documents the negotiated pivot. No verified era forum thread. [3–5]
