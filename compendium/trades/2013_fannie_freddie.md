## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**November 2013 disclosure decision set:** Fannie and Freddie were government-conserved mortgage-finance companies, not ordinary unencumbered common equities. Treasury's senior preferred interest, the net-worth sweep and warrants created a capital-structure/policy problem distinct from business profitability. Pershing disclosed approximately $500 million invested, 9.98% of Fannie's outstanding common and 9.77% of Freddie's. These percentages must not be read as fully diluted ownership **after** Treasury exercises its warrants. Exact first purchase date and entry weighted-average prices remain unknown. [1–2]

The pre-entry sweep arrangement existed, but its later legal fate and the 2019/2021 amendments were not knowable outcomes. The business could earn money while the existing common received no distributions; this is central to the point-in-time distinction between enterprise economics and security value. [2,5–6]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
- **2014 Sohn presentation, May 5:** commonly called **“It's Time to Get Off Our Fannie.”** Contemporary reporting describes a 110-slide case, low liquidity risk and a potential Fannie share-value range of approximately $23–47 versus a price just above $4. These are **2014 scenarios**, not 2013 entry prices or realized returns. The full original deck was not authenticated in this pass; the contemporary slide reproduction is linked. [3–4]
- “there is no credible alternative to replace them” — March 24, 2015 annual report, defending the role of the 30-year prepayable fixed-rate mortgage. [2]
- “We view this net worth sweep as an unlawful taking of shareholders’ private property” — same report. This is the investor's legal position, **not a final court holding**. [2]
- **December 30, 2024 X post:** “they have not been great investments to date.” Ackman said common shares then traded at or around Pershing's average cost after more than a decade. He outlined approximately $34 intrinsic value/$31 IPO pricing in a **hypothetical Q4 2026 IPO** and a 2.5% capital rule; neither is an actual completed offering. [8]
- **March 30, 2026 UTC X post:** “And Fannie and Freddie are stupidly cheap. Asymmetry at its best. They could be a 10X and it could happen soon.” This is later promotional/investment opinion, not a realized tenfold return. [12]

## What happened after
### Legal and capital-building arc
The 2014 adverse district-court outcome did not settle every cause of action; Pershing distinguished its takings case. The September 2019 Treasury announcement allowed retention of additional earnings but also must be read with the PSPA terms: **retained accounting capital is not equivalent to extinguishing Treasury's liquidation preference**. [2,5]

In *Collins v. Yellen* (June 23, 2021), the Supreme Court rejected the statutory challenge to FHFA's authority to enter the third amendment, held the director-removal restriction unconstitutional, and remanded aspects of relief. It did **not** invalidate the sweep wholesale or grant common holders a clean release. The 2023 jury verdict concerned contractual implied-covenant claims and specified classes; the Fannie preferred, Freddie preferred and Freddie common awards were approximately $299.4m/$281.8m/$31.2m. Do not assign the entire $612.4m to Pershing or assume Fannie common was in the same class. [6,14]

### 2025–2026 thesis evolution
January 2025's **The Art of the Deal** argued that release could generate over $300 billion for taxpayers and discussed a 2.5% capital standard. The December 2024 post's assumed Q4 2026 IPO was a **scenario**. By November 2025 Pershing explicitly advocated a **relisting-first, conservatorship-still-in-place** approach rather than a near-term primary/secondary sale. That is an important evolution, not one unchanged IPO forecast. [8–11]

The November 18 deck proposes: (1) account for repayment of Treasury's senior preferred, (2) exercise its 79.9% warrants at $0.00001/share, and (3) relist on NYSE. Its illustrative 2026E valuations use approximately $2.65 Fannie EPS at 16 times and $3.39 Freddie EPS at 13 times, implying approximately $42.47/$44.13 per share. It opposes conversion of senior preferred into common, warning that dilution/litigation would destroy value. **All are Pershing assumptions**, not adopted Treasury policy. [11]

The February 18, 2026 annual report restates the plan and says an eventual exit would require capital-rule revision, an ongoing paid government backstop, clear FHFA powers and strengthened boards/management. The August interim says there is still time for the administration to deliver and that shares could be worth multiples. Its **−39% FNMA/−41% FMCC first-half 2026 share performance** and **−2.8/−2.7-point PSH contributions** are period measures, not lifetime P&L. Full realized-plus-unrealized dollar outcome remains unknown and the position remains open in the latest primary report. [13,17]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Essential franchises:** the system's fixed-rate prepayable mortgage function is the core business-quality argument. [2]
- **Retain/recapitalize:** earnings power matters only if capital can build and shareholder claims survive. [2,5,9]
- **Sweep challenge:** distinguish Ackman's property-rights belief from actual legal results. [2,6,14]
- **Warrant alignment:** Treasury's potential 79.9% common ownership creates taxpayer upside but substantial dilution for existing common; do not omit either side. [2,11]
- **Administrative catalyst:** January/November 2025 plans seek policy action, not solely judicial recovery; timing and terms remain political risks. [9–11]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: dominant, cash-generative franchises fit quality investing, but the **security** is encumbered by discretionary sovereign policy, senior claims and litigation. A decade of waiting illustrates that low business liquidity risk is not low common-equity outcome risk. The December 2024 post explicitly warns investors to limit exposure to what they can afford to lose; do not teach the model that the upside scenario is guaranteed. [2,8]
