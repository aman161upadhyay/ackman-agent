## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Summer 2008 accumulation, exact first date unknown.** Longs was a West Coast drugstore chain with owned properties, long below-market leases and RxAmerica PBM. The original August 13D was not retrieved; the September letter explicitly says buying began during summer. Do not put the subsequent CVS bid into the pre-purchase narrative. [1]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
**September 11, 2008 letter:** “In effect, CVS is buying Longs’ real estate and is getting its PBM business and retail operations for free.” [1]

The letter's valuation identifies four components: owned property, leased property, PBM, and retail operating improvement. It estimated $1.3B owned property at $30/sq ft rent and 7% cap rate, plus $1.6B leasehold value using a 9% rate; these were **Pershing estimates subject to lease restrictions/tax leakage**, not appraisals. [1]

## What Pershing Square did
Built summer position; August 13D; CVS announced $71.50 cash deal within a week. Pershing hired Blackstone to seek a competitive outcome and, by September 11, opposed tendering 3.1M shares, with additional synthetic exposure. The letter viewed the one-year offer obligation as a $71.50 “put,” conditional on the actual transaction terms—not unconditional government-like protection. [1,2]

## What happened after
Walgreens offered $75; Longs rejected the bid on regulatory/timing concerns, and Walgreens withdrew amid market conditions. Ackman's Q3 letter said Pershing expected to be fully cashed out that day at CVS terms, approximately 44% above average cost. Exact letter signature/settlement day and net derivative-inclusive P&L not recovered; exit date remains unknown rather than equating repost date with settlement. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Real estate downside:** owned/below-market lease value was said to cover cost. [1,2]
- **PBM:** roughly 20% of LTM EBITDA, with strategic synergies and higher comparable multiples. [1]
- **Retail margin:** 2007 EBITDAR 6.0% versus Walgreens 10.1% in the letter; normalize without awarding full turnaround value. [1]
- **Strategic scarcity:** zoning/site barriers in California/Hawaii create acquisition value. [1]
- **Process:** one informed bidder was unlikely to maximize proceeds; seek alternatives, not merely reject a sale. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Ackman explicitly called it unusual to buy expecting a strategic sale; he emphasized both buyers' financing ability during the credit crisis and value if a sale failed. This is a specified exception, not evidence all cheap companies should be sold. [2]

## Public discussion
The filed shareholder letter lays out Pershing's process criticism, while the Q3 letter acknowledges Walgreens' regulatory disadvantage and eventual withdrawal. No independent era forum discussion authenticated. [1,2]
