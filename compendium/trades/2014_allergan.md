## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**February 2014 partnership date:** Pershing and Valeant formed a joint investment structure before publicly launching a bid for Allergan. The subsequent Actavis white-knight bid and lawsuit were not known outcomes. Original entry operating financials and a verified standalone valuation of Allergan are missing; do not treat acquisition-premium appreciation as proof of original operating undervaluation. [1]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
“A merger between Valeant and Allergan had the potential to create enormous shareholder value.” — 2014 annual report, March 24, 2015, describing the completed campaign retrospectively. Original April 2014 joint presentation remains an archival gap. [1]

## What Pershing Square did
February 2014: established the Valeant joint structure and accumulated 9.7% at approximately $128/share. April 2014: publicly supported Valeant's bid and pursued shareholder pressure. November 17, 2014: Allergan announced an Actavis transaction instead. March 17, 2015: transaction closed; Pershing hedged much of the Actavis stock consideration, initially retained 1.35 million Actavis shares across funds, and subsequently sold the residual holding during 2015. Thus merger close and complete successor-share exit are distinct. [1–2]

## What happened after
The alternative Actavis transaction created the profitable exit rather than Valeant winning Allergan. Approximately **$2.6 billion** was the reported **projected gross partnership gain at the November 2014 announcement**, not a fully litigation-adjusted final net investment return. Contemporary reporting also describes approximately $2.2 billion for Pershing after Valeant's share; final economics changed with the stock consideration. The 2014 report valued merger consideration around $240/share at closing, versus approximately $219 at announcement. These dates/scopes must not be silently conflated. [1,5–6]

In December 2017, Pershing and Valeant agreed to a **$290 million** settlement of shareholder insider-trading claims; reported allocation was **$193.75 million Pershing / $96.25 million Valeant**. Settlement is not a trial finding of liability; the litigation alleged trading with advance knowledge of the planned bid. Exact costs borne by PSH versus other funds/manager require the report's allocation detail. [3–4]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Merger value:** explicit Pershing rationale for partnering with Valeant. [1]
- **Efficiencies:** proposed acquisition economics, not later proof Valeant itself was a good equity investment. [1]
- **Shareholder catalyst:** campaign pressure created a strategic contest, ultimately monetized via Actavis. [1–2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: using a large ownership stake to catalyze value fits activism, but pre-bid partnership information and tender-offer rules introduced unusual legal risk. The **winning Allergan trade and losing Valeant stock trade must remain separate training examples**. [1,3]

## Public discussion
Forbes and Business Insider's December 2017 coverage emphasized the reputational and settlement cost, contrasting the large earlier gain with litigation exposure. This is contemporary settlement discussion, not February 2014 sentiment. Original contemporaneous shareholder/press debate and forum threads remain incomplete. [3–4]
