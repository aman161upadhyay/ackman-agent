## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

Initial purchase day unknown. **Public-disclosure snapshot, November 2009:** controller/CEO Tilman Fertitta proposed cashing minority shareholders out at $14.75. Contemporary coverage reports his 55.1% ownership and a majority-of-minority approval condition. That condition, unlike an ordinary powerless minority stake, created potential negotiating leverage. [Original 13D, 2009-11-13](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336528/000095012309062431/y02586sc13d.htm); [Market Folly, 2009-11-23](https://www.marketfolly.com/2009/11/pershing-square-enters-landrys.html).

## Ackman's thesis in his own words

Signed filing: “The Reporting Persons do not intend to support the Transaction.” It identifies the proposed $14.75 cash-out immediately above. No standalone operating valuation or original detailed Ackman interview recovered. This is documented deal opposition, not proof of every restaurant-economic driver an analyst could imagine. [13D, Item 4, 2009-11-13, above.]

## What Pershing Square did

Initial 13D: Pershing funds bought 1,554,255 common shares for $21,668,747 including commissions; **McGuire personally**, separately, held 50,000 shares costing $566,705. Joint group's 9.9% voting ownership and 23.7% economic exposure at filing included 2,223,876 swap reference shares. November 20 amendment coverage reports 24.8% aggregate economic exposure after more swaps. **Do not call 24.8% Pershing voting ownership or treat swap notional as cash invested.** [Primary 13D, Items 1, 3, 6, above; contemporary amendment coverage, 2009-11-23, above.]

## What happened after

June 20, 2010 voting agreement records $24.50 amended cash consideration and Pershing's support obligations. This supports a successful price-improvement classification, not a precise realized fund profit. Final merger settlement, swap-financing costs and complete realized P&L remain unknown here. [Original voting agreement, 2010-06-20](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/908652/000129993310002443/exhibit2.htm).

## Reasoning drivers checklist

- **Offer inadequate:** explicit refusal to support $14.75.
- **Minority leverage:** contemporaneously reported minority-vote condition made opposition meaningful.
- **Standalone valuation:** unknown; swap reference prices are not a complete valuation model. [Original 13D, 2009-11-13; coverage, 2009-11-23, above.]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

Comparable to Sears Canada minority protection, not generic activism against a controller without voting leverage. It qualifies the 2008 controlled-company avoidance lesson: transaction protections can change minority bargaining power. [Original 13D and voting agreement, above.]

## Public discussion

Contemporary coverage explicitly asks whether Fertitta would raise his bid and explains the minority-vote condition; those were live contingencies, not guaranteed at purchase. [Market Folly, 2009-11-23, above.]
