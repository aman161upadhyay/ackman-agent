## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

**Conservative cutoff: 2026-01-31; first purchase day within February unknown.** Microsoft's January 28 release reported Azure/other cloud revenue +39% reported and +38% constant currency; Intelligent Cloud revenue $32.9B in the accompanying call. [1] The company had already disclosed its fiscal Q2 results before Ackman's first purchases. Risks were future cloud growth, the economics of compute investment and competitive substitution for software; do not assume that later OpenAI partnership amendments, later capex budgets or later Copilot changes were all public in January. Exact pre-purchase stock decline, analyst estimates and execution range: **unknown**. [1,2]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words

Firecrawl verified Ackman's **2026-05-15** disclosure:

> “We began building our position in MSFT in February following a meaningful share price decline after the company reported its fiscal Q2 2026 results.”

> “We were able to establish our position at a valuation of 21 times forward earnings”

> “Unlike point software solutions, which may be vulnerable to disintermediation by better-performing AI alternatives, M365 is tightly integrated into the daily workflow of nearly every large enterprise” [2]

**Complete stated reasoning, disclosure-time rather than entry-time:** M365 and Azure represented about 70% of profits; enterprise distribution, identity, security, compliance and governance are difficult to replicate; approximately $20 monthly M365 ARPU is less than half a collection of standalone substitutes; Copilot product investment and seat-plus-consumption pricing create upside. Azure benefits from cloud migration and AI inference, while multi-model architecture makes Microsoft less dependent on one model provider. OpenAI's approximately 27% economic stake had value outside headline P/E. AI compute spending was growth capacity, not inherently wasted capex. The post contrasts investors' Claude Cowork/M365 fears and OpenAI/Azure concerns with this underwriting. Later post figures, including a roughly $190B CY2026 capex budget, **must not leak into February's point-in-time pack**. [2]

## What happened after

PSH interim reports Microsoft down 8% from average cost through June 30, then a +3.3 percentage-point gross contribution through August 11. These measure different dates and different objects; they are not contradictory realized returns. Its June estimate was 19x NTM EPS and approximately 19% medium-term EPS CAGR including yield. [6] Still open at latest verified disclosure.

## Reasoning drivers checklist

- **M365 moat:** embedded bundle and governance are harder to replace than a point application. [2]
- **Azure:** supply-constrained, mission-critical cloud workloads grow with AI inference. [2,6]
- **Monetization:** agents plus consumption can add revenue beyond seat counts. [2]
- **Variant view:** AI is an enabler of the platform, not necessarily its substitute. [2]
- **Valuation:** 21x entry multiple and ancillary OpenAI value made Microsoft more attractive than a fully valued alternative holding. [2,4]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

Fits long-followed dominant franchises bought during fear. It sharpens the distinction between technology-provider risk and enterprise distribution moats, while adding exposure to capital-intensive AI construction. [2,6]

## Public discussion

The scraped post contains replies arguing that enterprise IT adoption makes replacing Office much harder than building a competing app. These are May 15 reactions, not February inputs. [2] Principal countercase: AI reduces paid seats, Copilot fails to monetize, model providers bypass Azure and huge capital deployment earns low returns. Ackman's response relies on bundle economics, multi-model distribution and capacity demand, not denial that AI changes software. [2]
