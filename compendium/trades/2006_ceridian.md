## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**October 6, 2006 entry.** The purchase start date comes from Pershing's July 2007 proxy. The company combined human-resources/payroll services with Comdata payment/fleet services. Initial financial snapshot and consensus at purchase have not been reconstructed from pre-October filings; later buyout terms and 2007 board conflict must not be injected into this snapshot. [1]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
**July 24, 2007 proxy:** “We initiated this proxy contest because we were concerned about Ceridian’s future direction.” The proxy values its 14.9% stake at approximately $750M and discusses borrowing/return of capital, separating Comdata/HRS and accelerating efficiencies. These are the **campaign-stage** reasons, not demonstrated first-day intentions. [1]

## What Pershing Square did
Bought starting October 6, 2006; initially described its stake as passive, subsequently pushed separation and governance changes. The July 24 proxy opposed the $36 sale as inadequate and sought board change. [1,4]

September 7 agreement resolved the contest; four Pershing nominees were to join an expanded 11-person board at the September 12 meeting, and Pershing supported the transaction. [2,3]

## What happened after
Settlement cleared the way for the approximately $5.2B/$36-per-share cash acquisition by Thomas H. Lee Partners and Fidelity National Financial. Closing date and Pershing's exact proceeds/cost basis were not independently recovered here; “win” denotes the higher-value/transaction resolution reported, not a precise calculated gain. Comdata's proposed standalone spin was not the ultimate exit route. [2,3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Comdata obscured:** separately valuing payment economics rather than averaging them with HRS. [1]
- **Separation/efficiency:** spinoff and operating/capital improvements were alternatives to immediate sale. [1]
- **Price/process:** proxy contended $36 did not maximize value. [1]
- **Governance:** four-board-seat settlement shows willingness to compromise rather than demand every original nominee. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Analytical comparison: activist long with a specific sum-of-parts thesis, changing from passive ownership to governance intervention; not a 2007 first purchase as sometimes summarized. [1,4]

## Public discussion
The shareholder lawsuit provides accusations about the sale and management response, while the settlement is the authoritative record of the agreement. CNBC documents the practical compromise. No era forum post authenticated. [2–4]
