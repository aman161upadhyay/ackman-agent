## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Not a portfolio-company purchase:** PSH existed before the 2014 IPO; operations began December 31, 2012. October 2014 converted/listed a closed-end vehicle, raising $2.9 billion. The decision concerned capital structure and investor liquidity, not buying a new operating business. Later Valeant losses and the 2018 refocus must not enter the 2014 information set. [1]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
The 2014 report calls growing capital stability a “competitive advantage” and says it allows the firm to be “nearly 100% invested in our core activist strategy.” This is the primary permanent-capital rationale recovered. [1]

“reinforcing the implementation of our core investment principles” — 2017 report published in 2018, alongside restructuring into a smaller investment-centric organization. [2]

“simple, predictable, free-cash-flow-generative businesses” — 2018 annual letter, dated March 19, 2019. **Dating correction:** the supplied label “Pershing Square 3.0 letter (2018)” was not authenticated as an exact document title. Separate actions taken in 2018 from the 2018 annual report **published in 2019**. [2–3]

## What Pershing Square did
October 2014 public listing and capital raise supplied stable capital. In 2017/early 2018 it reduced legacy risks, converted then exited Herbalife and restructured the manager. The March 2019 letter described refocusing over the preceding 18 months and returning to an investment-centric operation. A distinct signed letter bearing the exact “3.0” title/date remains unverified. [1–3]

## What happened after
PSH continued as a closed-end listed structure. Its reported NAV return, listed-shareholder return and discount/premium to NAV are different outcomes and should never be merged into one trade return. The March 2018 letter discusses leverage, NAV discount and tender proposals explicitly. No single realized P&L is meaningful for this structural case. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Duration:** stable capital avoids forced selling to fund redemptions. [1]
- **Liquidity drag:** less need to park capital in liquid passive holdings/cash. [1]
- **Core principles:** refocus followed failures, rather than proof the earlier portfolio had always met them. [2–3]
- **Organization:** fewer investor/fundraising demands sought to free research capacity. [2–3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
This is a **process/vehicle case**, included because requested, not an independent operating trade. It explains the financing environment in which concentrated activism was attempted and how post-loss organizational constraints changed. [1–3]

## Public discussion
The annual letters are public primary explanations, but an independent contemporary IPO debate and original “3.0” commentary archive were not recovered. Do not treat the manager's assessment of its discount as consensus fair value. [1–3]
