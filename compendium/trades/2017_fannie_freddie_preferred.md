## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Entry-year boundary uncertain:** the 2017 annual report published in March 2018 says preferreds were purchased “in recent months,” which verifies ownership but does **not conclusively prove first purchase before December 31, 2017**. Retain this as the requested 2010–2017 boundary candidate, not a fabricated exact date. Preferred securities were perpetual/noncumulative, so time-to-resolution materially affected prospective returns. [1]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
“it hedges our risk of a restructuring that disproportionately benefits the preferred versus the common shares” — March 2018 report. It gave three explicit reasons: more-proximate favorable outcome, a restructuring hedge and attractive current prices. [1]

“We still prefer our investment in the common shares” — same report, citing alignment with Treasury's 79.9% warrants. This establishes the preferred position was a **complement**, not proof Pershing abandoned the common thesis. [1]

## What Pershing Square did
Added preferreds by the March 2018 report; approximately 21% of its combined Fannie/Freddie investment and approximately 1% of net assets were preferred. No authenticated series-by-series purchase list, first-date evidence or subsequent full exit reconciliation was recovered. `status: open` is **provisional**, not proof of continuous preferred ownership through October 2026. ## What happened after
See the common-stock file for legal/policy chronology. Total realized/unrealized preferred P&L unknown. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Timing:** noncumulative perpetual claims earn no missed dividend accrual while investors wait. [1]
- **Security hedge:** preserves exposure to a restructuring that advantages a senior class. [1]
- **Price:** Pershing said prices were attractive; exact series discounts remain unknown. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: separates business conviction from capital-structure risk. Preferreds have a different payoff cap and timing sensitivity; the model should not use common-share upside targets to value them. [1–2]
