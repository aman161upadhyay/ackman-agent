## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**April 26, 2005 disclosure snapshot.** Wendy's combined a hamburger business with Canadian coffee/doughnut franchisor Tim Hortons. The later July letter identifies April 26's unaffected close as $41.73 and an initial disclosed 9.3% stake. First purchase is not established; a retrospective article dates involvement to 2004, so the filename uses the verified campaign year rather than asserting an exact 2004 entry. Do not treat July's valuation as already public in April. [1,5]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
**July 11, 2005 letter:** “the Company should commence a strategic refranchising initiative whereby it would convert the substantial majority of its Wendy’s operated restaurants to franchised restaurants.” [1]

On allocation: “management should refrain from pursuing a significant acquisition prior to a full evaluation of the strategic opportunities outlined herein.” [1]

The letter/Blackstone analysis put value at $60–70 against approximately $48 market, with $11–13 per share incremental refranchising value. Tim Hortons was expected to supply over half of 2005 operating income; rental income was estimated at over half its EBITDA. These are **July 2005 estimates**. [1]

## What Pershing Square did
April 26 13D disclosed the initial 9.3%; July 11 letter reported 9.9% and proposed full Tim Hortons spin, refranchising, buybacks, and no major acquisition before review. Proposed refranchising was 230–300 restaurants annually during 2006–10, reaching roughly 99% franchised, with 50 owned stores for testing. These are proposals, not achievements. [1]

By August 2005 press reported 1.366M shares and calls on another 10.2M, over 10% combined exposure. July 29 Wendy's announced a partial sale/IPO plan following shareholder pressure. [2,4]

## What happened after
Tim Hortons was spun off in 2006. November 23 reporting says Pershing reduced Wendy's to 5,000 shares from 6.42M at September-end, substantially exiting the first campaign. Exact combined Wendy's/Tim Hortons/call-option P&L is unknown. A **separate later Wendy's/Arby's exposure** appears in the September 2008 13F and should not be treated as an uninterrupted holding; see the separate re-entry file. [3,5,6]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Royalty/rental cash flows:** stable, low-maintenance-capital Tim Hortons economics should command a distinct valuation. [1]
- **Separation/incentives:** separate stock and financials focus managers and allow performance-linked compensation. [1]
- **Refranchising:** franchisees can operate units more efficiently while the parent retains brand/real-estate economics. [1]
- **Buybacks:** convert proceeds from equipment/franchise rights into share reduction. [1]
- **Acquisition discipline:** Allied Domecq-related speculation prompted the timing of the letter. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Analytical comparison: franchise cash flows and a specific corporate catalyst, not merely “sell real estate.” The letter explicitly favors selling franchise rights/equipment while retaining real estate, a distinction important for faithful reasoning. [1]

## Public discussion
The original letter quotes supportive sell-side spin-off analysis; AP July 29 reports management's partial-sale response. The November 2006 Bloomberg story tracks the exit. No authenticated era forum post recovered. [1–3]
