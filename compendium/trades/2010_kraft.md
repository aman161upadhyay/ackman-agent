## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
At the February 5, 2010 public discussion, Kraft was buying Cadbury. The decision to own Kraft was therefore a bet on an existing packaged-food business and a pending change in its portfolio, not a prediction of the later Kraft breakup. Exact first purchase date, entry financial snapshot and cost basis are unknown in the retrieved record. [1]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
“We think (the acquisition of Cadbury) is a transformational transaction for the company,” and “We think that Kraft is interesting on a standalone basis.” — Ackman, CNBC interview, February 5, 2010. These are interview quotations transcribed by CNBC, not a reconstructed deck. [1]

## What Pershing Square did
- February 2010: publicly discussed approximately 33 million shares, approximately 2% of Kraft. [1]
- At December 31, 2010: approximately 19 million shares remained, worth approximately $594 million; this is a quarter-end mark, not acquisition cost. [4]
- Q2 2012: eliminated the position according to the subsequent 13F report. Exit day unknown; the August filing date is not the trade date. [3]
