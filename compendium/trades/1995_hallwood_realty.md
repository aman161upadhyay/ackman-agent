## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Entry date unverified; do not treat this as a complete 1995 snapshot.** Hallwood owned commercial office buildings and industrial parks, with exchange-listed partnership units. The 2002 court opinion describes low 1994 unit prices in the real-estate recession and a 1995 reverse split, option plan and odd-lot transactions. These judicial findings supply historical context, not proof of what Gotham knew on its first purchase date. [1]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
No original purchase letter recovered. **Hindsight, January 24, 2013:** Ackman stated: “He paid my investors $80 per share and agreed to what he called ‘schmuck insurance.’” His contemporaneous exit economics can be checked against the March 1, 2003 contract rather than inferred from the later feud. [2,3]

## What Pershing Square did
Gotham challenged the general partner's transactions. Delaware's Supreme Court on August 29, 2002 affirmed liability under the partnership agreement's fairness provisions but reversed/remanded the damages remedy because it failed to capture the control premium. Gotham owned approximately 14.8% in that record. [1]

On March 1, 2003 Gotham contracted to sell 235,000 units to Icahn's High River for $80 each/$18.8M, with closing transfer initiated March 3. The contract required additional consideration on qualifying transfers within 36 months. Ackman's later description specifies half of profit above Icahn's 10% annual return hurdle. [2,3]

## What happened after
Ackman's 2013 statement dates HRPT's acquisition to April 14, 2004 and consideration to $136.16 per unit. He says the resulting approximately $4.5M earnout was disputed, won through litigation and ultimately collected in 2011 with interest. Icahn's own release disputes the narrative of reneging and explains his legal position. Do not confuse $136.16 paid to Icahn with Gotham's initial $80 exit or use the earnout alone as total trade profit. [2,4]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Asset value:** the commercial real-estate discount thesis is reconstructed; an original valuation document remains missing. [1,2]
- **Controller blocking stake:** court record establishes the challenged control acquisition and remedy issue. [1]
- **Contingent upside:** directly documented by sale contract; this is an exit decision, not the initial buy thesis. [3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Analytical comparison: minority-rights litigation can recover value but can take years after the security is sold. This example should test legal/capital-structure reasoning, not be mislabeled a simple cash-flow compounder. [1–3]
