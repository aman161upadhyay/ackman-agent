## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

**Cutoff: 2025-04-20.** Amazon's February 6, 2025 release reported Q4 2024 revenue $187.8B (+10%), AWS sales $28.8B (+19%), operating income $21.2B versus $13.2B a year earlier. [1] The April 2 tariff announcement preceded entry and created uncertainty for imported goods, consumer demand and retail profitability; this is distinct from the later May earnings report. [2] First purchase was April 21, not simply “sometime during the May disclosure.” [2] Exact pre-entry price history, consensus tariff exposure and execution range have not been recovered; **unknown**. Do not use H2 2025 AWS acceleration to justify this April purchase in a blind replay.

## Ackman's thesis in his own words

The 2025 annual report, a **retrospective account**, calls AWS and retail “two of the world’s great, category-defining franchises” and says entry was at “only 25 times forward earnings.” [3] Main reasons: dominant hyperscaler with secular migration of IT workloads; AI extends demand; world-leading e-commerce scale and logistics; broader assortment and faster delivery support volume; automation and network productivity expand retail margins; advertising improves business mix. [3]

On the 2026 capex controversy, he writes: “When a business you own, managed by a management team you trust, announces a large increase in capital spending due to increased demand for its products or services, you should be applauding rather than booing.” [3] This is a **February 2026 holding thesis**, not an April 2025 quote. His May 15, 2026 Microsoft X post also explicitly recalls purchasing Amazon “in the weeks following Liberation Day.” [4]

**H1 2025 call gap:** the requested original call quotation has not been independently retrieved. Do not relabel the February 2026 retrospective as the H1 2025 transcript. Exact first public disclosure day remains unverified.

## What happened after

PSH reports 2025 gross NAV contribution +2.4 percentage points. The February 2026 report says AWS growth accelerated from 17% Q2 to 24% Q4 2025, with constrained capacity and planned expansion; shipping cost per unit had declined for eight quarters. Those are post-entry observations. [3] H1 2026 letter says Amazon shares rose 3% through June, AWS growth exceeded 30% during 2026, and forecast long-term EPS growth remains 20%+. These are manager estimates/dated observations, not guaranteed returns. [8]

## Reasoning drivers checklist

- **Dislocation:** tariff fear, rather than abandonment of the franchises, provided the price. [2,3]
- **AWS:** cloud migration plus AI compute expands the addressable opportunity. [3]
- **Retail moat:** unmatched assortment/logistics reinforce customer value. [3]
- **Margin:** network productivity, automation and advertising improve unit economics. [3]
- **Capex:** underwrite returns on incremental capacity, not merely headline free-cash-flow compression. [3,8]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

Fits a long-followed franchise purchased during short-term panic. More capital-intensive cloud growth complicates the “capital-light” screen; the distinction is return-generating growth investment versus recurring maintenance capital. [3,8]

## Public discussion

The bear case is tariff-driven consumer/import pressure, cloud competition and AI capex earning inadequate returns. The later August 13, 2026 call discusses two-to-three-year construction/revenue lags and contracted compute demand; it is helpful rebuttal evidence only as of that later date. [9] No AMZN-specific original X thesis post was recovered beyond the verified Microsoft comparison. [4]
