## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

**First purchase mid-2018; exact date unknown.** No clean dated pre-entry financial pack collected; later China growth and US recovery cannot be treated as entry evidence. October public disclosure is not the initiation date. [1,2]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words

The 2018 annual letter describes a high-quality consumer franchise with temporary sales/operating challenges, digital/rewards levers, improving store execution, long-run Chinese growth and significant capital returns following licensing transactions. [2] It presents a passive/supportive investment, not a proxy contest. Direct entry-day quotation and X post unknown.

## What Pershing Square did

Built approximately $900M / 1.1% position during mid-2018; announced at an October 9 conference. Average cost $51.30 in the subsequent letter. [1,2] Exited January 2020 as price approached estimated value. **Separate** March 2020 re-entry appears in `2020_sbux_reentry.md`. [3,4]

## What happened after

Reporting/manager update describes approximately 73% total return over 19 months. [3] January sale preceded the pandemic market collapse; it is not evidence that the original trade was a COVID macro short. The manager soon repurchased the same franchise at a lower valuation. [4]

## Reasoning drivers checklist

- **Brand/habit:** frequent affordable premium purchase with network convenience. [2]
- **US fix:** improve throughput, product/digital execution and comps. [2]
- **China:** secular unit/consumption growth, with real country/competition risk. [2]
- **Cash return:** licenses and repurchases improve per-share economics. [2]
- **Exit price:** prospective return had compressed after appreciation. [3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

Familiar brand bought on fixable disappointment; sell near value and later repurchase rather than demand permanent ownership. [2-4]

## Public discussion

October coverage highlighted the unexpected passive position. The bear concerns were weak same-store sales, China competition and premium consumer demand; subsequent recovery is an outcome, not proof those risks were irrational at entry. [1,2]
