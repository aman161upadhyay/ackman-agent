## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

**First purchase bounded to Q2 2026 by reported acquisition/disclosure; exact day unknown. Conservative data-pack cutoff: 2026-03-31.** Netflix's January 20 shareholder letter reported 2025 revenue $45.2B (+16%), operating margin 29.5%, advertising revenue over $1.5B and more than 325M paid memberships. It also reported H2 2025 viewing hours +2%. These were public before Q2 and provide both profitability evidence and a measurable engagement concern. [1] Later H1 portfolio-letter claims about current margins, costs and FCF cannot automatically be used as March facts. April/Q2 releases and subsequent stock recovery are excluded. Pre-entry purchase range is **unknown**.

## Ackman's thesis in his own words

The August 2026 interim says Netflix has “effectively won the streaming wars” and explains that scale allows more content investment amortized across more users. It reports a different financial model from 2022: modest cash-content-spend growth, higher margins, approximately 90% earnings-to-FCF conversion and buybacks. [2]

**Reconciliation with the loss in `2022_nflx.md`:** in 2022 competition, large cash spending relative to amortization and the surprise advertising/business-model pivot widened the forecast range. By 2026 he considered competitive position, advertising economics and cash conversion sufficiently demonstrated to restore predictability. This is a **new evidence set**, not a claim that the April 2022 exit was made because Netflix could never succeed. [2,3]

Additional reasons: advertising monetization and a cheaper tier expand the market; live viewing helps retention beyond raw hours; short-form video is not the same product as scripted television; AI video costs and Netflix's distribution scale limit displacement while AI improves recommendation and advertising. Share price had fallen about 50% from its June 2025 high and valuation from above 40x to about 21x earnings. Expected double-digit revenue growth, costs below revenue growth and buybacks support approximately 20% EPS compounding. These are August-dated manager assertions. ## What Pershing Square did

Q2 2026 repurchase; August 13 disclosure of a new investment; August 14 SEC filing for June 30 reports **$934.0M and 4.80%**, not an exact $950M/4.9%. Share count **13.081M is split-adjusted** and cannot be compared directly to the 3.1M pre-split shares bought in 2022. [2,4] Exact purchase dates, cost dollars and executions remain unknown.

## What happened after

PSH reports -11% versus average cost at June 30. It remains invested and estimates about 19% three-to-five-year EPS CAGR including yield. Those are not realized returns. [2] Q3 2026 13F had not been filed by the October 25 archive cutoff.

## Reasoning drivers checklist

- **Scale:** dominant platform spreads content costs over a larger audience. [2]
- **FCF:** proof of disciplined spending changes the predictability assessment. [2]
- **Advertising:** tested monetization option, no longer just an unmodeled pivot. [2]
- **Disruption/engagement:** distinguish geographic/content mix from fundamental competitive decline. [2]
- **Price:** a material de-rating creates prospective returns rather than simply buying a former winner at any price. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

An unusually explicit re-entry after a rapid loss, consistent with changing decisions when evidence changes. The answer key should reward acknowledging both the old forecasting failure and the new observable economics. [2,3]

## Public discussion

Reuters highlighted the return to a company sold at a loss in 2022. September coverage framed the question as “why this time is different,” rather than pretending the first investment succeeded.
