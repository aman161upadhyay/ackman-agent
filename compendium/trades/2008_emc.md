## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

**Entry established by September 2008, not first initiated in 2009.** Primary holdings show 58,615,731 shares worth $701.044M at September 30. Exact first purchase month and entry-date financial multiple are unknown. The detailed business description recovered is the later 2009 letter and therefore belongs below, not disguised as an original 2008 presentation. [13F, filed 2008-11-13](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336528/000117266108000803/pers3q08.txt).

## Ackman's thesis in his own words

“We invested in EMC because of the high quality nature of the company’s two principal business lines – the Information Infrastructure and Virtual Infrastructure businesses – and our ability to acquire a position at a substantial discount to our estimate of fair value.”

“We believe that EMC is undervalued on a sum-of-the-parts basis, and that the value of its two core operating segments will continue to increase at an attractive rate.” The letter describes switching costs, recurring maintenance/warranty/consumables, scale and scope, and EMC's 84%-owned VMware. **It does not make a demanded VMware spin-off the sole investment thesis.** [Original Q2 2009 letter, pages 2–3, public by 2009-09-24](https://www.scribd.com/document/20150688/Pershing-Square-Q2-2009-Investor-Letter).

## What Pershing Square did

Common-stock investment; no verified proxy fight or board campaign in the recovered record. June 2009 primary filing reports 58,678,780 shares and $768.692M market value. The frequently quoted approximately 33.5% Q1 2009 weight refers to **reported 13F assets**, not whole-fund NAV; overseas shares, cash, shorts and derivatives require separate treatment. [13F, 2009-08-14](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336528/000117266109001387/0001172661-09-001387.txt); [Q1 digest, 2009-07-08](https://www.marketfolly.com/2009/07/bill-ackmans-pershing-square-has-large.html).

## What happened after

Q4 2009 digest records full sale; December is the quarter-end bound. CRN's 2014 account calls the 2008–09 investment profitable. Exact profit, final sale price and duration unknown. Do not attribute Elliott's later breakup campaign or Dell's later acquisition to Pershing's already exited position. [Market Folly, 2010-02-17](https://www.marketfolly.com/2010/02/bill-ackmans-pershing-square-dumps.html); [CRN, 2014-07-22](https://www.crn.com/news/storage/300073478/activist-investor-pushes-for-emc-breakup-partners-analysts-weigh-in).

## Reasoning drivers checklist

- **Leadership/switching costs:** risk-averse customers concentrate infrastructure spending.
- **Recurring FCF:** data growth plus recurring maintenance and consumables.
- **Capital-light economics:** assembly from competitive suppliers avoids traditional hardware burdens.
- **VMware:** cloud/virtualization ecosystem could earn attractive economics.
- **Valuation:** explicit sum-of-parts discount. [Original Q2 2009 letter, pages 2–3, above.]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

The original rationale places a technology issuer inside a durable-cash-flow framework, rather than treating it as a speculative gadget forecast. Sum-of-parts analysis did not require a public activist campaign. [Original Q2 letter, above.]

## Public discussion

Contemporary 13F coverage stressed the position's reported-book concentration. Later breakup coverage is useful only as retrospective exit corroboration, not an original Ackman quote. [Market Folly, 2009-07-08; CRN, 2014-07-22, above.]
