## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

**Cutoff 2018-10-07.** First purchase date comes from the 2026 chart; dated pre-entry operating/valuation pack not assembled. Calibration incomplete; do not use the eventual pandemic resilience or 2026 multiple as evidence available in 2018. This is a re-entry after [the smaller earlier Hilton trade](2016_hilton_initial.md). [1,2]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words

The 2018 letter calls Hilton high-quality, asset-light and high-margin, with significant growth potential and a strong team. [2] The underlying economics: hotel owners supply capital, while Hilton supplies brands, loyalty and distribution in exchange for fees; unit growth adds recurring fees with limited capital needs; cash can fund buybacks. The later exit rationale was valuation: prospective returns no longer met its threshold despite continued admiration for the business. [1]

## What Pershing Square did

First purchased October 8, 2018; $73 average announcement cost in presentation. [1] Added approximately 34% more shares around $70 during March 2020 using hedge proceeds, rather than selling Hilton itself as a pandemic short. [3] Reduced over time and **fully exited February 2, 2026**, publicly announced February 11. [1,4]

## What happened after

Pandemic travel shock severely disrupted fees but did not permanently destroy the franchise model; the holding recovered substantially. Presentation reports **492% gross / 341% net PSH investment return**, which must not be called underlying share-price return. [1] Exit multiple about 32x forward EPS versus about 20x at entry; valuation, not newly bad management, reduced expected return. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist

- **Fees:** asset-light ownership model separates fee economics from hotel real-estate capex. [2]
- **Network:** brands/loyalty attract owners and guests. [2]
- **Growth:** unit pipeline supports fee compounding. [2]
- **Management/cash:** Nassetta plus capital return. [2]
- **Exit:** a great company need not be attractive at every multiple. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

Strong example of long-duration fee compounding, supported through a macro collapse, then sold on valuation. [1-3]

## Public discussion

Reuters paired the February 2026 exit with the Meta initiation. [4] Bear case includes travel cyclicality, owner distress and expensive growth expectations; “asset-light” does not mean demand-insensitive. [2,3]
