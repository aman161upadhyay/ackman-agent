## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

**Cutoff January 20, 2022 after earnings; before Friday January 21 first purchase.** Netflix had just announced Q4 results and weaker forward subscriber guidance; the earnings selloff was an observable catalyst. [1,2] No full independently extracted release/price/consensus pack is stored here, so precise subscriber/EPS figures remain unknown rather than reconstructed from April's miss. Advertising pivot and Q1 subscriber loss were **not** public January purchase facts. [3]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words

January disclosure praised management and the durable subscription model, global scale, content advantage and long-term growth opportunity made cheaper by the selloff. [1,2] April letter then says the outcomes were not “sufficiently predictable” for its concentrated strategy and describes the dispersion as “sufficiently large.” [3] This is the essential error/update: the team no longer could reliably bound earnings, notwithstanding admiration for management.

## What Pershing Square did

Accumulated starting January 21, announced January 26 **3.1M shares**, approximately $1.1B. [1,2] Sold entire holding April 20 after Q1 results and required shift toward advertising; no attempt to average down to avoid recognizing error. [3] Separate 2026 purchase appears in `2026_nflx_2.md`; its 13M shares are split-adjusted, not directly comparable to 2022 share count.

## What happened after

Reporting estimates approximately $400M loss; letter says about four percentage points of fund performance impact. [3,4] April share collapse alone is not a correctly calculated percentage return on Ackman's cost basis. The subsequent successful Netflix recovery does not make the April decision information-free hindsight; 2026 primary letter explicitly cites newly demonstrated competitive/cash economics for re-entry. [5]

## Reasoning drivers checklist

- **Subscriptions:** attractive recurring business rather than individual hit guessing. [1,2]
- **Scale/runway:** global penetration and content amortization. [1,2]
- **Management:** confidence persisted even at exit. [3]
- **Price:** initial de-rating believed excessive. [2]
- **Predictability:** adverse evidence invalidated the permissible forecast range. [3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

The initial purchase underestimated forecasting uncertainty; the rapid sale fits his stated discipline of responding promptly to errors rather than defending sunk cost. [3] Do not collapse “good company” and “suitable concentrated holding” into the same judgment.

## Public discussion

April coverage emphasized both the short holding and loss. The primary letter supplies the actual reason, not generic “he sold because price fell.” [3,4] A known bear countercase was competitive saturation and rising acquisition/content costs; exact pre-January quantified bear thesis not independently archived.
