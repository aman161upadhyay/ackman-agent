## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

**Q2 2019 first purchase day unknown; conservative cutoff March 31.** Complete dated financial/price snapshot not assembled; the later cash balance during COVID and manager's April 2020 valuation cannot be inserted into the original pack. [1]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words

The 2019 report argues Berkshire offered quality operating businesses, insurance float and cash allocation at a large discount, rather than a bet that the S&P 500 simply would rise. Buffett and the substantial cash reserve provided optionality for attractive acquisitions/buybacks. [1] Direct entry-day quotation/X unknown.

## What Pershing Square did

Bought in Q2 2019, disclosed August 14 approximately $1B. Added approximately 39% more shares during March 2020 using hedge proceeds. [1,2] Sold entirely May 2020 after concluding Pershing could deploy cash more nimbly in direct holdings with better prospective returns. [2]

## What happened after

2020 annual report records Berkshire as a -3.1 percentage-point gross NAV detractor; this is a **calendar-year contribution**, not lifetime trade return. [3] Later reporting says Ackman had expected Buffett to be more aggressive in the crisis. No verified precise lifetime dollar loss; do not repeat an unsourced amount. [4]

## Reasoning drivers checklist

- **Discount:** value underlying businesses/portfolio rather than headline book multiple alone. [1]
- **Quality:** durable operating subsidiaries support long-term value. [1]
- **Allocator/float:** tax-efficient cash deployment and insurance funding. [1]
- **Cash option:** capacity for crisis acquisitions. [1]
- **Opportunity cost:** smaller fund could act directly rather than await Berkshire deployment. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

Fits admiration for trusted allocators; breaks reliance on operational control because he could not influence Buffett's pace. Better comparative opportunities prompted a loss-realizing sale. [1,2]

## Public discussion

May reporting emphasized selling despite admiration for Buffett, while November coverage highlighted disappointed crisis deployment expectations. Both are post-entry accounts, not original evidence. [2,4]
