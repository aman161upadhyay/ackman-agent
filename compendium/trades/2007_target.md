## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**April 2007 first purchases; exact day unknown.** Target was a discount retailer with owned real estate and a credit-card portfolio. The Reuters chronology places first purchases in April and July 16 disclosure at 9.6%. Ackman's later Q3 2008 letter says purchases were in the mid/high $50s; this is a retrospective cost statement, not an April price forecast. The dedicated derivatives vehicle and 2008 land plan should not be treated as identical to the first-day operating thesis. [1,4,5]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
**Q3 2008 letter:** “there has been no meaningful diminution in the per-share value of Target since our initial purchase 18 months ago.” This was his assessment despite falling shares, not an established fact. [5]

**February 8, 2009 investor letter**, quoted by Reuters February 9: “I am deeply disappointed by PSIV's dreadful performance and I apologize profusely for the fund's results to date.” [2]

The same letter said: “We do not believe that a Target real estate transaction is necessary in order for an investor to earn an attractive return from current values through an investment in PSIV.” This is a **revised, post-loss** position rather than the original 2007 claim. [2]

## What Pershing Square did
- **2007:** accumulated stock/derivatives and raised roughly $2B for Pershing Square IV, including roughly $1.3B from other hedge funds. PS IV's concentrated option exposure must be distinguished from diversified funds' common shares. [2,4]
- **2007–08:** advocated credit-card monetization and buybacks. Target ultimately sold 47% of credit-card receivables; Pershing claimed credit but the issuer contested its contribution and proposal details. [1,6]
- **October 2008:** proposed putting land under stores in a REIT/ground-lease structure and monetizing a minority interest; Target rejected the proposal. [3,4]
- **February 2009:** apologized; offered full cash redemptions in March and fee waivers in other funds until affected investors recovered PS IV losses. Ackman committed $25M more personal capital. Economic exposure was reduced from 12.9% to 10.5%; direct stake reported at 9.7%. [2,7]
- **May 28, 2009:** lost the contested election seeking Ackman and four others on the board. Proxy materials contain the detailed nominees' backgrounds and rationale. [1,3]
- **Q1 2011:** sold final 7.4M shares; exit month is a quarter-end bound, not an exact trade date. [3]

## What happened after
PS IV's reported approximately 90% loss describes a **dated drawdown of a dedicated derivatives fund**, not a 90% permanent loss on all Target common shares across all Pershing funds. Final PS IV lifetime result and full manager-wide Target P&L remain unverified. The REIT transaction did not occur and the board campaign lost. [2,3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Franchise quality:** Ackman remained bullish on business value while market prices declined; quality alone did not protect option capital. [5]
- **Receivables/capital return:** a separate value driver, partially implemented; avoid conflating it with the land REIT. [1,3,6]
- **Land:** separate ownership/ground leases would surface real-estate value; management disputed feasibility and distraction. [1,3,6]
- **Board expertise:** the definitive proxy explains the slate; it was not a bid to own or operate Target outright. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Analytical comparison: operating-quality confidence and asset-value activism were combined with a highly concentrated, time-sensitive payoff vehicle. A correct assessment of a retailer's long-run survival would not make derivatives immune to large interim/permanent loss. The apology and redemption terms demonstrate accountability; they do not erase the loss. [1–3,5]

## Public discussion
The issuer's May 2009 filings provide the necessary contrary view, including the argument that activism distracted an already strong retailer during recession. Reuters's February report quotes the actual apology. The 2011 Star Tribune account includes analysts' reservations about the REIT but greater support for card monetization. No contemporaneous forum thread authenticated. [2,3,6,8]
