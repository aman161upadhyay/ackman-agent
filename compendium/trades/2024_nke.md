## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

**Cutoff: 2024-05-14.** Nike's 2024-03-21 earnings release reported quarterly revenue $12.4B, approximately flat; Direct revenue $5.4B, approximately flat; digital sales down 3% reported and 4% currency neutral; wholesale revenue $6.6B, up 3%. The dated data support a slowing-growth/product-channel problem, not a completed turnaround. [1] John Donahoe was still CEO. **Elliott Hill's appointment was not public until September 19 and cannot enter this May calibration pack.** [2] The June 27 results/guidance shock, tariffs announced in April 2025 and subsequent estimate cuts likewise belong only below. Pre-entry daily price range and consensus margin estimates: **unknown**.

## Ackman's thesis in his own words

The 2024 annual report explains the brand/innovation/channel repair thesis and welcomes Hill's return; it admits underestimating near-term revenue declines and operating deleverage. This is a **post-purchase account**, not proof that Hill was the original May catalyst. It also explains the early-2025 replacement of common shares with deep-in-the-money calls to release capital while retaining upside. [3]

The 2026 presentation's explicit reversal: “We underestimated the problems created by prior management” and “we grew uncomfortable with underwriting a return to pre-COVID levels in the medium-term.” [4] More fragmented competition and structurally lower gross margins from tariffs made the old earnings target less reliable. [4] **X gap:** no verified thesis post/status URL recovered; no fabricated quotation.

## What happened after

The 2026 presentation describes NTM EPS estimates falling 25% between spring entry and Hill's October 2024 start; further turnaround costs and tariffs worsened the outlook. [4] PSH reports gross NAV contribution -2.2 percentage points in 2024 and -2.5 in 2025. [3,8] Institutional Investor reports a cumulative approximately 30% investment loss. [7]

**Source conflict, retained deliberately:** the 2026 NKE slide visually states underlying total return **(30)%**, yet its footnote prints PSH gross **34%** / net **26%** without minus signs. The PDF page was inspected visually, not merely OCR. These cannot responsibly be turned into a verified positive investment return, nor silently corrected to negative percentages. Exact dollar loss and reliable fund-level gross/net return remain **unknown**. [4,7,8] Do not confuse this loss with the profitable 2017-18 Nike trade.

## Reasoning drivers checklist

- **Brand/scale:** the franchise was expected to survive execution mistakes. [3]
- **Product/channel repair:** re-emphasize innovation, sport and wholesale distribution rather than extrapolating the failed direct-channel strategy. [3]
- **Management:** Hill strengthened the thesis after entry, not before it. [2,3]
- **Profit recovery:** revised downward when structural industry and tariff changes undermined old margin benchmarks. [4]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

Fits brand moat plus fixable operational underperformance; breaks the desired earnings predictability when the depth of repair is underestimated. Exit is a calibration example of selling because outcome dispersion widened, not because the company ceased to have a valuable brand. [3,4]

## Public discussion

August disclosure generated “new position” coverage; February 2025 coverage noted adding Nike while trimming Chipotle. [5,6] November reporting correctly discussed Nike as a loss and distinguished the remaining options from common shares. [7] The bear case is durable brand-share loss, weakening wholesale execution, more innovative competitors, and an earnings rebound that takes longer or stops below prior margins; those later confirmed concerns must not all be assigned to the May information set.
