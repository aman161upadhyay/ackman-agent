## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**September–October 2011 information set:** CP was a large, established North American rail network with an underperformance problem relative to peers; Pershing began accumulating in September and disclosed on October 28. The later full-year 2011 operating ratio of **81.3%** was not yet a reported full-year number at initial purchase and must not leak into an entry-date financial snapshot. Exact then-public trailing financials, share-price history and initial valuation model remain gaps. [1]

By the February 6, 2012 campaign presentation, the full-year comparison was central: the reported 2011 OR was 81.3%, and Pershing/Hunter Harrison advocated approximately 65%. This is the **campaign-date** decision set, not September's. OR means operating expenses/revenue; lower is better. [1,4]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
**Original February 2012 deck/transcript gap:** the operating-ratio target and Harrison proposal are corroborated by both the later case and CP's contemporaneous SEC-filed rebuttals, but a verified original deck was not recovered. Do not label the paraphrased target as a verbatim Ackman quotation. [1,5–7]

“The remarkable transformation of Canadian Pacific continues under the leadership of Hunter Harrison and the reconstituted CP Board” — Pershing's 2014 annual report, released March 24, 2015. This is an **interim evaluation after intervention**, not the original pitch. [2]

“At the inception of our investment in 2011, CP earned $3.15 per share.” — same letter, contrasting an eventual company target of approximately $20/share in 2018. The target was forward-looking, not an achieved outcome. [2]

## What Pershing Square did
| Date | Action / public evidence |
|---|---|
| September 2011 | Began investment, according to case chronology. [1] |
| October 28, 2011 | Public stake disclosure; later accumulated approximately 14.2%. [1] |

## What happened after
Pershing's 2014 letter reports OR of **64.7% in 2014**, better than its 65% four-year objective after only two operating-plan years, and 32% full-year EPS growth despite severe winter weather. This is a directly reported post-intervention operating result, not proof every subsequent rail improvement was uniquely caused by Pershing. [2]

The 2016 annual letter reports **318.9% total shareholder return during ownership** and the August 4 sale. The CAIA case reports approximately **$2.6 billion profit** across Pershing's investment. Distinguish issuer TSR, aggregate dollar gain, vehicle-specific P&L and investor net return. [1,3]

The same case raises governance and distributional questions about calling the intervention an unqualified success. It is a useful contrary assessment, not an original-entry source. A later CP re-entry is a **separate trade**, not a continuation of this 2011–2016 position. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Operating-ratio gap:** a measurable peer-performance gap rather than a vague “good company cheap” case. [1,4]
- **Fixable operations:** Pershing argued existing assets could perform substantially better; CP disputed comparability of network constraints. [1,6]
- **Named operator:** Harrison's CN record made the management replacement specific and falsifiable. [1]
- **Governance catalyst:** winning board support was necessary to execute the leadership change. [1,5–7]
- **Asset utilization/service:** operating changes aimed to release capacity and improve costs, service and earnings. [1–2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: an unusually complete underperformer-versus-peers activist template—durable physical franchise, explicit efficiency target, named executive and a governance route to implementation. Do not train the model to assume every cost gap is fixable: the incumbent's terrain/network and execution objections were genuine information available before the vote. [1,5–7]

## Public discussion
CP's contemporaneous SEC filings are essential opposing evidence: on February 6 it said Pershing offered no clear plan/timetable; in March it defended its own 70–72 OR goal for 2014; on April 26 it argued Ackman had moved the 65 target's timeframe. The Harvard governance article and CAIA paper are **retrospectives**, not public sentiment at entry. Authenticated contemporaneous VIC/COBF/Reddit/Twitter threads and the original Pershing presentation remain missing. [4–7]
