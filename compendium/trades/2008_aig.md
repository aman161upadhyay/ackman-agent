## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

**Post-bailout September 2008:** AIG common equity sat behind government financing with high interest/fees and approximately 80% zero-strike-warrant participation, as Ackman characterized it. Pershing thought conservative derivative-loss estimates still left book value well above stock price. That was a risky valuation judgment, **not proof stated book value was realizable**. [Q3 letter, AIG section, reproduced 2008-11-27](https://advisoranalyst.com/2008/11/27/bill-ackman-pershing-square-q3-2008-letter.html/).

## Ackman's thesis in his own words

The original letter describes asset disposals, CDS-portfolio termination and new capital as routes to a better arrangement for existing equity. His exit explanation is unusually candid: “We ultimately concluded that the return on invested brain damage from this investment exceeded the probability-weighted opportunity for profit, and we decided to fold the tent.” [Q3 letter, reproduced 2008-11-27, above.]

## What Pershing Square did

Bought stock after the bailout, limiting exposure to 2.5% of fund capital because of leverage and total-loss risk. Ackman approached other shareholders, policymakers and Berkshire; organizing holders proved difficult because some preferred buying AIG's assets to rescuing its common. The September 13F aggregates 33,088,568 common shares worth $110.185M and call positions referencing 407,000 shares. The **13F call value is not option premium or amount risked**. [Q3 letter, above; 13F, 2008-11-13](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336528/000117266108000803/pers3q08.txt).

## What happened after

Sold for what Ackman called a modest loss to fund capital. Exact trade return, cost, last sale and duration were not recovered. No later AIG stock-price path is used to manufacture Pershing's P&L. [Q3 letter, reproduced 2008-11-27, above.]

## Reasoning drivers checklist

- **Adjusted book-value discount:** investor's estimate, not audited liquidation proceeds.
- **Terms could change:** renegotiation was central; accepting current terms was not the whole thesis.
- **Corporate actions:** asset sales/capital could reduce government financing burden.
- **Sizing:** explicit recognition of embedded leverage and wipeout risk. [Q3 letter, above.]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

Government bargaining and shareholder coordination made this less predictable than a durable-FCF franchise. Exiting when effort-adjusted expected return became unattractive is documented process discipline, not evidence the bailout thesis succeeded. [Q3 letter, above.]
