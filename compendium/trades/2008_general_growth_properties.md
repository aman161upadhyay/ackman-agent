## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**November 13, 2008 entry.** GGP was a major shopping-mall landlord with a severe refinancing problem during a frozen credit market. Ackman's later letter gives initial purchases that day at $0.35 average. Contemporary December reporting describes approximately $27B debt and urgent refinancing pressure. The entry-time public underwriting pack is incomplete: **April 2009 bankruptcy, Q1 2009 occupancy, May cap rates and a future Brookfield sponsor must not be assumed known in November**. [2,5]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
**May 27, 2009 original Sohn deck, after filing:** “GGP’s bankruptcy is the result of the unprecedented disruption in the credit markets coinciding with large near-term debt maturities.” [1, problem slides]

On residual rights: “When a debtor’s asset value exceeds the amount of its liabilities, equity holders are entitled to the residual value.” [1, bankruptcy section]

These are **2009 elaborations**, not fabricated November 2008 quotations.

## What Pershing Square did
- **November 13, 2008:** initial stock purchases; later used equity, total return swaps and unsecured debt. [1,2]
- **April–May 2009:** argued equity-preserving Chapter 11 rather than forced liquidation. The deck cites 150 studied bankruptcies and the special combination of positive cash flow before debt maturities and asset value exceeding liabilities. Proposed seven-year maturity extension, cash-flow deleveraging, and alternatively debt/equity conversion; discussed cramdown and *Till* interest-rate precedent. [1]
- **May valuation:** Q1 occupancy 90.9%; trailing cash NOI $2.524B. At 7.5–8.5% caps, the mall REIT component was estimated at $9.11–21.50 per share, before separately valued management/MPC assets. These are dated advocacy estimates, not court findings. [1]
- **2010 reorganization:** supported Brookfield-led recapitalization, contributed $1.061B, helped raise $2.6B Fairholme and $500M Blackstone capital according to the later letter. HHC spin occurred November 9, 2010; operating malls and development/MPC assets became separate securities. [2,4]
- **January 2013:** sold Brookfield warrants on 18,432,855 GGP shares for $271,884,611 and agreed to limits on activism/ownership. That was not an immediate disposal of all common stock. [3]

## What happened after
Ackman's **August 23, 2012** letter reports all GGP creditors received par plus accrued interest and calculates **77-fold** return for a shareholder buying on the initial purchase date, including HHC and Rouse spinoffs/dividends. This directly resolves the often-misdated “77x in 2013” anecdote. It does **not** mean the complete Pershing exposure, including over $1B later recapitalization capital, returned 77x. [2]

2013 warrant sale and significant common-share reductions are documented, but exact final common-stock exit was not established here. Thus the requested “2008–2013” span is a campaign/reduction interval, not a certified final-exit date. HHC remained a distinct investment; do not call continuing HHC ownership continuing ownership of GGP. [2–4,6]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Liquidity vs solvency:** maturity failure is different from cash operating losses; reorganization can fix timing. [1]
- **Cash-flow quality:** diversified tenants and long leases buffer landlord economics relative to retailer sales. [1]
- **Asset coverage:** cap-rate sensitivity and separate management/MPC valuation anchor residual recovery. [1]
- **Legal mechanism:** creditors recover valid claims, not automatically the entire enterprise; extensions and cramdown can avoid fire sales. [1]
- **Advocacy/incentives:** creditors/management can favor low reorganized valuations; common equity needs representation. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Analytical comparison: highly leveraged distressed equity is an exception to balance-sheet conservatism, justified by claimed asset coverage and specific legal control of timing. It must not be simplified to “buy bankrupt stocks” or “real estate always recovers.” The 77x headline is not an underwriting assumption. [1,2]

## Public discussion
Market Folly's June 1, 2009 deck commentary acknowledges the asset/liability argument but warns about the moribund commercial-real-estate market. The 2012 letter contests Simon's attempted takeover valuation and Brookfield's conduct; it is an interested party's account. No original VIC/COBF thread was authenticated. [2,7]
