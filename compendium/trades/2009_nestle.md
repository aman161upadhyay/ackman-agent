## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

**June 2009 first purchase**, established by the original December letter. Nestlé's pre-existing April 2008 agreement included a future right to sell its Alcon stake to Novartis. Exact entry-day cost, multiple, financial statements and position size unknown. December estimates below are not relabeled June observations. [Original letter, 2009-12-03, Nestlé section](https://pdfcoffee.com/pershing-square-third-quarter-investor-letter-pdf-free.html).

## Ackman's thesis in his own words

“Unlike many of its food and beverage competitors, Nestle has strong pricing power and limited private label competition across a substantial proportion of its product categories, including confectionary, baby foods, soluble coffee and pet foods.” The letter describes “strong global franchises, predictable free cash flow and unlevered capital structure.” These are original Ackman words, unlike attributing another investor's later Nestlé activist demands to Pershing. [Letter, page 5, 2009-12-03, above.]

## What Pershing Square did

Initiated June 2009; December 3 letter publicly explained the investment. No verified proxy slate, board seat or controlling stake in the recovered record. The letter expected roughly $1B annual cost savings over several years and approximately $28B cash if the Alcon put were exercised at then-current prices, much potentially used for buybacks. **Forecasts, not already-realized cash proceeds.** [Original letter, 2009-12-03, above.]

## What happened after

Final sale date, allocation and realized P&L unknown. This foreign ordinary-share investment's absence from a US 13F is not evidence of a sale. The source establishes the 2009 investment and reasoning, not eventual monetization.

## Reasoning drivers checklist

- **Brands/pricing:** categories with limited private-label pressure.
- **FCF/balance sheet:** predictable cash generation and unlevered structure in the letter's description.
- **Growth:** emerging markets estimated at 35% of sales/40% of profits and growing high-single to double digits; developed-market consolidation.
- **Margins:** roughly $1B annual cost-saving opportunity.
- **Capital release:** 52% Alcon stake plus previously negotiated put, alongside 31% L'Oréal stake. All percentages and forecasts are **December letter-date** claims. [Original letter, page 5, 2009-12-03, above.]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

Combines branded durable FCF with an identifiable capital-return catalyst, without evidence of a public activist campaign. A 2009 Nestlé dossier should not be omitted just because it is absent from domestic holdings screens. [Original letter, above.]
