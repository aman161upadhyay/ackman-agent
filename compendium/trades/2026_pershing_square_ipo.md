## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

**Cutoff 2026-04-28 for public launch.** March 10 S-1 identifies Pershing Square Holdco, L.P. as converting into Nevada corporation Pershing Square Inc., the parent of the investment manager. PSUS was the separate US registered closed-end investment fund, not the same issuer as the manager or London PSH. [1] The 2024 attempt had been withdrawn; its demand problem and potential closed-end-fund discounts were observable risks. [2] Full final allocation, issue-price bridge and pre-IPO valuation model remain gaps; no invented stock entry range.

## Ackman's thesis in his own words

The August 13 call characterizes the manager as a royalty-like participation in the compounding of underlying holdings, with fee/performance revenue growing as assets compound even absent new fundraising. [4] **This is post-IPO explanation.** Manager stock and fund shares have different economics: PSUS owns a portfolio; PSI owns the management platform, fee streams and direct balance-sheet investments. The bundle's manager shares create an incentive unavailable in the failed standalone 2024 fund offer. [1,3]

## What happened after

August call states PSUS approximately 95% invested and plans conservative investment-grade bond leverage around 15%-20% debt/assets. PSH retains distinct offshore/PFIC tax characteristics and incentive fees; PSUS no incentive fee, and future PSUS management fees partially offset PSH incentive fees under the arrangement. These are dated structure explanations, not proof discounts disappear. [4,8] No comprehensive realized PSI/PSUS investor return calculation verified.

## Reasoning drivers checklist

- **Capital permanence:** removes redemption timing risk. [1,4]
- **Subscription incentive:** manager-stock bundle addresses why buy at IPO. [3,6]
- **Economic separation:** distinguish portfolio NAV from fee-business value. [1,4]
- **Scale:** incremental capital can use an existing investment library and small team. [4]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

Builds an asset-management enterprise rather than buying an unrelated stock. Potential conflicts, related-party holdings, incentives and discounts are part of the underwriting, not incidental legal detail. [1]
