## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
On June 1, 2015, Pershing subscribed to Nomad alongside its Iglo acquisition. Nomad originated as Martin Franklin/Noam Gottesman's SPAC. The Iglo transaction was €2.6 billion, approximately 8.5 times trailing EBITDA according to Pershing's later primary account. The subsequent August Findus transaction was not yet an accomplished event at entry. [1]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
“Long-term, Nomad intends to create value as a consolidator in a fragmented packaged foods sector.” — March 24, 2016 annual report, describing the investment's strategy. The same report describes strong cash generation, low capex and modest cash taxes; an original June subscription pitch was not recovered. [1]

## What Pershing Square did
June 1, 2015: invested $350 million at $10.50/share; Brian Welch joined the board. September 2017: announced sale of the entire approximately 33.3 million-share stake; company participated with a repurchase, and the annual report documents a September 11 agreement. [1–3]

## What happened after
Reuters valued the stake at approximately **$497 million using the previous closing price of $14.92**, not the final placement price. Do not subtract $350 million from that mark and call the result audited realized profit. The 2017 Pershing letter confirms Nomad was a profitable exited position. Its 1.3-point PSH 2017 contribution is distinct from investment return. [2,4]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Brands/scale:** Iglo supplied leading European category positions. [1]
- **Cash economics:** low capex and taxes supported deployment capacity. [1]
- **Consolidation:** explicitly stated long-run plan. [1]
- **Sponsors/structure:** Franklin approached Pershing; territorial domicile was described as an acquisition advantage. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: predictable food franchises fit quality preferences, while a roll-up adds financing and execution risk beyond a static branded-food investment. No sourced new process rule or explicit reason for the September sale was recovered. Reuters says Pershing declined to supply one. [1–2]

## Public discussion
Reuters' September 6, 2017 report noted the bright spot in a difficult period for Pershing and no stated sale rationale. Company documents clarify the buyback/secondary mechanics. Original contemporaneous forum/Twitter threads remain missing. [2–3]
