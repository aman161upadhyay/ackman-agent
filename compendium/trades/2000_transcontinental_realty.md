## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

TCI owned commercial real estate but depended on an affiliated manager, Basic Capital Management. The subsequent court record dates the public indictment news to June 15, 2000 and describes shares falling from $10.75 to as low as $2.875 on June 16, followed by a trading halt June 20–26. Affiliates' margin collateral was sold. These facts explain the contemporaneous forced-sale opportunity; an indictment was an allegation, not proof of criminal guilt. [Court, 2002-08-15](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp2/218/415/2360196/).

## Ackman's thesis in his own words

“We purchased shares of TRI because we believe that its share price at the time of purchase and at today's trading price of $12 substantially understates the true value of the company.” He attributed the discount to BCM's “excessive fees” and the controller's reputation, and proposed “an outstanding third party real estate manager on competitively bid terms.” This is unusually strong original evidence of Gotham's actual reasoning, not a later inferred asset-value story. [Declaration, executed 2000-08-02, paragraphs 4–6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/733590/000089998300000018/0000899983-00-000018-0002.txt).

## What Pershing Square did

Gotham, not Pershing, bought approximately 1.835M shares for $10.5M during June 2000, roughly 21% by the declaration. Ackman supported court termination of the affiliated advisory contracts and attacked a $12M related-party loan secured by shares with approximately $3.6M public value. [Declaration, paragraphs 1–5, 2000-08-02](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/733590/000089998300000018/0000899983-00-000018-0002.txt).

An October 3 agreement covered 1,858,900 shares and provided $8,365,050 of nonrefundable option consideration, initially $3/share plus $1.50/share later. **That option payment alone is not total sale proceeds or profit**; further exercise provisions and the later litigation matter. [Agreement, 2000-10-03](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/827165/000095013400008660/d80997aex99-1.txt).

## What happened after

The issuer sued over short-swing-profit liability; the August 2002 decision analyzes which acquisitions occurred before Gotham became a statutory greater-than-10% holder. This legal result is not a complete investment P&L. Final exit, net proceeds and costs remain unknown here. [Court, 2002-08-15](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp2/218/415/2360196/).

## Reasoning drivers checklist

- **Asset discount:** explicitly stated in declaration paragraph 5.
- **Forced-sale dislocation:** paragraph 2 links purchase to indictment-related price collapse; paragraph 4 separates business value from share price.
- **Governance discount:** paragraph 5 identifies fees and reputation.
- **Concrete remedy:** replacement manager and competitive bidding, paragraphs 5–6. [All: declaration, 2000-08-02](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/733590/000089998300000018/0000899983-00-000018-0002.txt).

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

The documented combination is tangible assets plus a legal/governance catalyst. Control entrenchment and litigation complicate realization; this is a reason not to describe early Gotham as exclusively simple operating-company investing. [Declaration, 2000-08-02; court, 2002-08-15, above.]

## Public discussion

The court record supplies the issuer's adversarial position, not independent validation of Ackman's asset appraisal. No contemporaneous retail-forum discussion was verified.
