## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Corporate-action entry:** Beam was the spirits continuation of Fortune Brands, not an unrelated 2011 initial idea. The parent breakup was publicly discussed in December 2010 and the golf sale in May 2011. A standalone opening valuation and Pershing's allocated cost basis remain unknown. [3–4]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
Original standalone Beam quotation **unverified**. Ackman's documented favorable response to the parent breakup was “great news for the company and great news for the shareholders” on December 7, 2010. Do not portray Suntory's 2014 bid as known at the 2011 continuation date. [3]

## What Pershing Square did
Continued to own spirits exposure after the Fortune Brands restructuring. On January 13, 2014, Suntory and Beam announced an $83.50/share cash acquisition; shareholders approved the merger in March. April 2014 reporting identified Beam as a positive contributor for Ackman. The exact Pershing exit execution versus cash-merger settlement is unknown. [1–2,5]

## What happened after
Strategic acquisition provided a monetization event. The takeover price is not Pershing's net return; allocated parent basis, dividends and timing of any trims are needed to calculate it. [1–2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Focused spirits company:** continuation of the documented breakup, not a second full allocation of parent cost. [3–4]
- **Brands:** Jim Beam, Maker's Mark and other brands are identified in the sale/breakup reporting. [4]
- **Strategic value:** realized through Suntory; whether it was an explicit original Ackman catalyst remains **unverified**. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: brand durability and corporate simplification fit a franchise-plus-catalyst pattern. No authenticated independent standalone Beam deck was recovered. [1,3–4]

## Public discussion
CNBC's April 22, 2014 scorecard described a strong Ackman year and Beam's positive stock performance. The company's SEC-filed January announcement is the primary takeover document. Specific contemporary forums and Twitter threads not verified. [1–2]
