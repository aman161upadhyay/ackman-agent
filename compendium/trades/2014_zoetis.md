## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
At July 22, 2014 entry, Zoetis was an independent animal-health business following Pfizer's 2013 IPO/split-off. Do not import its subsequently announced 2015 restructuring targets into the entry information set. First-purchase date is explicitly disclosed retrospectively by Pershing; exact entry-date market cap and trailing financials remain unknown. [1–2]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
“Zoetis’ business model passes our high bar for business quality.” — March 24, 2015 report. It explains that products' durability came from brand, customer relationships and service rather than patents, and that small individual markets discouraged generic entry. This is the earliest recovered detailed primary explanation, after initial buying. [1]

“Despite the high quality nature of the business and its strong management team, we sold to redeploy the capital in certain new investments.” — March 28, 2017 exit explanation. [3]

## What Pershing Square did
Began buying July 22, 2014; publicly announced approximately 8.5% in November. In February 2015 Zoetis agreed to board additions including Bill Doyle; annual reports use different dates for agreement/joining versus effective board appointment, so those should not be silently conflated. May 2016 sold an approximately $800 million block but retained 25 million shares. Sold the final shares November 9, 2016. [1–4]

## What happened after
The company pursued supply-chain restructuring, SKU reduction, smaller-market distribution changes and corporate savings. Pershing described a goal to move operating margins from approximately 25% in 2014 to approximately 34% by 2017 and said Zoetis outperformed its objectives during ownership. The reported **57.9% TSR** includes dividends and is an issuer return, not a transaction-weighted or investor-net fund return. [2–3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Product durability:** approximately 80% of 2014 revenue was from non-patent-protected products, supporting a different risk model from human pharma. [1]
- **Secular demand:** rising protein consumption, pet ownership and medicine use were explicit drivers. [1]
- **Independent efficiency:** post-Pfizer simplification created a controllable operating lever. [1–2]
- **Margins/allocation:** restructuring targeted measurable improvement; board involvement supported execution. [2–3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a high-quality business plus margin gap and board influence. Exit was explicitly opportunity-cost driven, not a statement that the franchise had deteriorated. It is a useful contrast with Valeant rather than a generic “all healthcare is risky” lesson. [1,3]

## Public discussion
NYT's May 9, 2016 block-sale story discussed possible redemption/new-investment motivations, but these were not authenticated Ackman explanations at that moment. His later signed letter supplies the explicit redeployment reason. Forum/social threads and original entry deck remain gaps. [3–4]
