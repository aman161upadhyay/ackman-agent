## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**December 20, 2012 public thesis date, not first short-sale date:** Herbalife sold nutritional products through multi-level distribution. Ackman publicly alleged the company's economics were driven by recruiting distributors rather than genuine external retail demand. Those were **his allegations**, not an adjudicated finding. No FTC 2016 settlement, Icahn accumulation or 2018 exit belongs in this information set. Entry balance-sheet snapshot and borrow economics remain unknown. [1–2]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
- The original special Sohn presentation was titled **“Who Wants to Be a Millionaire?”**, December 20, 2012; NYT describes a roughly three-hour presentation questioning retail sales. An authenticated downloadable original deck was not recovered in this pass. [1]
- “HLF is an illegal pyramid scheme that will collapse or otherwise be shut down by regulators.” — Pershing's 2014 annual report, released March 24, 2015. This is Pershing's stated belief, **not a court finding**. [2]
- **Hindsight, 2017 annual letter published in 2018:** “we underestimated Herbalife’s ability to access debt capital and use financial engineering”; “technical factors are a critically important additional consideration.” Both explain why being right about selected business metrics did not ensure a profitable short. [3]

## What Pershing Square did
| Date | Action |
|---|---|
| December 2012 | Publicly presented the approximately $1 billion short and regulatory thesis. [1,5] |
| January 2013 onward | Public confrontation with Carl Icahn; Icahn later accumulated stock, opposing the short. [3,5] |
| 2014–2016 | Continued public campaign and regulatory arguments; Pershing reiterated shutdown/reform expectations in annual reporting. [2,4] |
| July 15, 2016 | FTC announced settlement requiring business restructuring and $200 million consumer redress. [4] |
| 2017 | Replaced the open-ended stock short with put options to cap downside; reduced free float and buybacks were explicitly acknowledged risks. [3] |
| February 28, 2018 | Public reporting confirmed exit from the losing bet; subsequent annual letter confirms the investment was exited. [3,5] |

## What happened after
The FTC settlement required restructuring and $200 million consumer redress; the company was **not shut down**. The agency alleged deceptive income claims and compensation incentives poorly linked to retail demand. Do not collapse “settled FTC charges,” “proved illegal pyramid scheme,” and “successful short” into the same claim. Reuters described the outcome as a blow to Ackman. [4,6]

Pershing's 2017 letter explained that debt-funded repurchases and Icahn's purchases reduced available float, making the short increasingly hazardous despite its continued belief in its fundamental analysis. Its **−4.0 percentage-point contribution to PSH's 2017 gross performance** is not total life-of-trade return. The precise audited aggregate dollar loss was not recovered; approximately $1 billion is a reported estimate, not derived from original notional. [3,5]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Pyramid allegation:** the central regulatory hypothesis, carefully attributed to Pershing. [1–2]
- **Distributor economics:** the pitch questioned genuine outside retail demand and rewards for recruitment. [1,4]
- **Enforcement catalyst:** shutdown or severe reform was supposed to impair value; the actual settlement did not provide the predicted stock-price outcome. [2,4,6]
- **Earnings:** Pershing claimed deteriorating metrics supported its analysis, while conceding capital-market mechanics defeated the position. [3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: intensive primary research and catalyst identification fit the activist approach, but a concentrated, public short exposed the fund to theoretically unbounded losses, financing/borrow risks and adversarial float reduction. The 2017 shift to puts was a concrete risk-control change; a later promise never to short publicly should be separately sourced, not inferred here. [3]

## Public discussion
Herbalife's December 19 and 20, 2012 statements called the claims bogus and the presentation a malicious attack; these are contemporary company rebuttals. NYT covered the original pitch; Reuters covered the FTC outcome; CNBC revisited the Icahn confrontation when reporting the February 2018 exit. Original VIC, COBF, Reddit and authenticated Twitter threads remain gaps. [1,5–8]
