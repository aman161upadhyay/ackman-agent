## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Two decision dates:** Justice's February 2011 IPO was a blank-check investment; Burger King's April 2012 transaction was the operating-company selection. Do not present a fully specified Burger King thesis as known at the SPAC IPO. Justice raised about $1.5 billion, including approximately $458 million from Pershing. In April 2012 it agreed to acquire a 29% Burger King interest for $1.4 billion; 3G would retain control. [1–2]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
“QSR’s franchised business model is best described as a capital-light, high-growth annuity.” — 2015 annual report, March 24, 2016. This is a later articulation of the franchise thesis, not a verified 2011 IPO quote. [4]

“Controlling shareholder 3G is an ideal operating partner and sponsor.” — same report. The original 2012 Burger King deck/transcript remains a gap. [4]

“QSR remains a highly attractive investment.” — February 18, 2026 report, after describing approximately 16 times estimated EPS and a near-30% peer discount. This is the **2026 hold rationale**, not the initial valuation. [5]

## What Pershing Square did
- February 2011: co-sponsored and invested in Justice. [1]
- April–June 2012: Justice/Burger King combination announced and completed; Burger King returned to public markets. [2–3]
- December 2014: Burger King/Tim Hortons combination created Restaurant Brands International. [7]
- 2017: QSR added Popeyes; Pershing continued describing the acquisition playbook as replicable. [8]
- 2025–2026: QSR remained in the portfolio; the 2025 annual report and June 2026 interim report contradict a claimed complete 2024 exit. [5–6]

## What happened after
The model evolved from Burger King into a multi-brand franchisor. The 2025 report discusses Tim Hortons, Burger King, Popeyes and Firehouse Subs and reports 3% comparable-sales and 8% operating-profit growth for 2025. Those are issuer operating metrics, not Pershing's investment return. Full realized-plus-unrealized P&L across Justice/BKW/QSR is unknown. [5]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Royalty annuity:** Pershing described 4–5% unit-sales royalty fees and minimal required parent capital for growth. [4]
- **Units:** international expansion makes growth less dependent on mature domestic same-store sales. [4–5]
- **Operator:** 3G's cost and incentive discipline was explicitly praised. [4,7]
- **Repeatability:** the Tim Hortons/Popeyes acquisitions expanded the operating template. [7–8]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: operating-company ownership fits predictable cash flows and brand durability; the initial blank-check vehicle required trust before a specific target was identified. Counterparty/operator confidence should not be confused with a majority Pershing operating-control stake. [1–4]

## Public discussion
NYT's April 4, 2012 article examined Ackman's role in accelerating Burger King's return to public markets. The SEC-filed announcement documents 3G's continuing control. CNBC's 2020 retrospective is useful for Justice economics but is not contemporaneous entry sentiment. Original 2012 forum/Twitter discussion remains unverified. [1–3]
