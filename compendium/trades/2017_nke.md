## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

**First date unknown within late 2017.** No clean dated pre-entry earnings/price pack retrieved. Therefore not calibration-ready; the January disclosure and March letter are post-entry evidence only. [1,2]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words

The March 2018 letter identifies Nike among profitable ideas where prices rose during research/accumulation before a meaningful stake could be built. Its Nike discussion emphasizes business quality and improvement potential. [1] No longer original Ackman quote or verified X post recovered; avoid importing the 2024 Elliott Hill thesis into this earlier position.

The primary letter calls Nike “a classic Pershing Square-style investment” and identifies health/wellness and casualization, emerging-market growth, pricing power, and manufacturing/distribution-channel margin improvement. These are dated 2018 explanations of the 2017 investment, not the 2024 turnaround thesis. [1]

## What Pershing Square did

Passive late-2017 purchase, publicly reported January 26, 2018; approximately 0.71% at year end. Sold March 2018 after rapid appreciation, rather than an activist campaign. [2,3]

## What happened after

CNBC/Reuters report approximately $100M gain, roughly 32% over about six months. Treat as sourced reporting, not independently reconstructed audited realized P&L. [3] Separate 2024 re-entry was a loss; see `2024_nke.md`.

**Conflicting duration/return measures:** the primary annual letter says “four-month ownership” and stock-price appreciation of 34%, versus the press's approximately six months/32% investment-profit characterization. The front-matter six months follows the press; do not silently treat those measures as identical. [1,3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist

- **Brand:** quality franchise justified research and ownership. [1]
- **Improvement:** operational potential, not a publicly launched proxy plan. [1,2]
- **Price/size:** fast appreciation prevented a meaningful intended position and reduced prospective returns. [1,3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

Fits price discipline; unusually small/short-lived compared with a typical multi-year concentrated holding. [1,3]

## Public discussion

January press emphasized that normally vocal Ackman had taken a passive position; March coverage emphasized the quick profit. Neither justifies claiming he installed management or executed a turnaround. [2,3]
