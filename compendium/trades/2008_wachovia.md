## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

**September 29, 2008, before buying:** the announced Citigroup transaction proposed $2.1B of Citi shares to Wachovia's parent and assumption of approximately $53B of holding-company debt. Wachovia stock opened around $1.80, down 82% from Friday. Unlike Washington Mutual, the parent would retain cash, Wachovia Securities/AG Edwards, Evergreen's approximately $245B managed assets, insurance and other businesses. These are Ackman's later account of the **already announced** terms he read that morning, not knowledge of Wells Fargo's future offer. [Original Q3 letter, Wachovia section, reproduced 2008-11-27](https://advisoranalyst.com/2008/11/27/bill-ackman-pershing-square-q3-2008-letter.html/).

## Ackman's thesis in his own words

“Our conservative estimate of value of New Wachovia was in excess of $8 per share even assuming that the preferred stock was redeemed or valued at par.” He considered the $9.8B noncumulative perpetual preferred economically worth less than half par, and discussed potential tax-loss/carryback value as additional upside, **not cash guaranteed in hand**. [Original Q3 letter, reproduced 2008-11-27, above.]

## What Pershing Square did

- Began buying September 29 after reviewing public documents; accumulated 178M shares, approximately 8.3%, through October 2 at $3.15 average, roughly $560M.
- Wells Fargo announced its 0.1991-WFC-share exchange agreement October 3, then worth over $7/WB share; Pershing began selling that Friday.
- A short-sale ban initially blocked a hedge. Subsequently sold additional shares and shorted WFC to hedge 100% of the remaining merger consideration. Do not call the original September 29 purchase a pre-existing Wells merger arbitrage. [Original Q3 letter, reproduced 2008-11-27, above.]

## What happened after

At the letter date Ackman reported approximately 67% profit **assuming transaction completion**, after prior sales and the hedge. This is a specific trade-level report, not a 67% fund return or an unhedged purchase-to-final-market-price calculation. The recovered letter does not supply final settlement date, fees or realized dollar P&L, so final exit remains unknown here. [Original Q3 letter, reproduced 2008-11-27, above.]

## Reasoning drivers checklist

- **Misread parent survival:** WaMu analogy was misleading under the proposed Citi terms.
- **Retained assets/debt assumption:** parent balance sheet, not a generic bullish bank call.
- **Preferred/tax optionality:** distinguish conservative par-preferred base case from additional upside.
- **Documents before narrative:** buying began after transaction-document review.
- **Hedge:** subsequent WFC agreement changed the position into exchange-ratio arbitrage. [Original Q3 letter, reproduced 2008-11-27, above.]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

Fast, opportunistic document-based investing rather than a multi-year operating turnaround. Initial asset-dislocation underwriting and later merger hedging were different stages of the same position. [Original Q3 letter, above.]

## Public discussion

The original letter discusses Citi's attempted legal opposition and later consent as transaction risks. No contemporaneous social-thread evidence recovered; do not replace the conditional 67% statement with a precise realized figure.
