## Ackman's thesis in his own words

The 2021 report underwrites a familiar high-quality railroad led by Keith Creel, combined with KCS to form a unique Canada-US-Mexico single-line network. Specific gains: better service, operating efficiencies, previously unavailable through-routes, nearshoring and conversion from less efficient/more emissions-intensive trucking. Network scarcity and operating management support durable value. [1]

The 2026 exit presentation explains growth arrived more slowly than expected; operating execution did not offset weak freight demand and later tariffs. This is a revised underwriting result, not pre-entry knowledge. [2]

## What Pershing Square did

Bought October 11, 2021; $73 average announcement cost per chart; about 8% of equity portfolio March 2022. [1,2] Held through 2023 regulatory approval/integration. Final date **May 14, 2025** in the presentation. **Conflict:** annual report's high-level text says April exit; use the exact dated performance interval and preserve the discrepancy rather than silently harmonize. ## What happened after

KCS close/control and integration took longer than anticipated. Weak consumer/industrial/grain volumes plus 2025 trade uncertainty reduced earnings growth; presentation cites about 6% USD EPS CAGR 2022-25. PSH reports 68% gross/49% net investment return through May 14, 2025; include currency-hedge effects where applicable rather than calling this pure share return. [2] Proceeds funded better prospective returns including Amazon. [3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist

- **Network moat:** rights-of-way cannot economically be replicated. [1]
- **Management:** prior experience with Creel supports conviction. [1]
- **Combination:** unique route and operational synergies. [1]
- **Secular demand:** nearshoring and truck conversion, subject to cyclical volumes. [1]
- **Exit:** slow realization and tariff uncertainty versus alternatives. [2,3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

Familiar company re-entry under new economics; no claim the original turnaround was repeated. Great operating management did not eliminate macro/geopolitical risks or opportunity cost. [1-3]
