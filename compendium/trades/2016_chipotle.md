## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**September 6, 2016 disclosure date:** Chipotle was a fast-casual restaurant brand recovering from food-safety incidents; contemporary NYT reporting said the stock had fallen more than 40% over the preceding year. Pershing's 9.9% stake was accumulated at an average $405/share, according to its later primary letter. First execution date remains unknown. **Brian Niccol was not yet CEO**; do not smuggle his later recruitment into the original-entry facts. [1–2]

The 2016 annual letter's Q4-2016 unit-volume data are later evidence, not available on the September disclosure date. The entry case was that the franchise's customer proposition and unit economics could survive the crisis, not that food-safety risk had disappeared. [1]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
“We have always believed that a good time to buy a great business is when it is in temporary trouble.” — March 28, 2017 annual letter. [1]

“all of the key drivers of Chipotle’s powerful economic moat and long-term success remain intact.” — same letter. It lists brand, differentiated value proposition, scale/real estate, strong unit economics and new-unit/operating enhancements. This is the first detailed primary thesis recovered, approximately six months after public disclosure. [1]

“the right leader to reinvigorate the company and help it achieve its enormous potential” — 2017 annual report published in 2018, discussing Niccol. This is the **management-change checkpoint**, not the original September thesis. [3]

## What happened after
Niccol's leadership, operating/digital execution and recovery helped realize the franchise thesis. Pershing's November 2025 call coverage describes approximately 9% average comparable-sales growth during 2018–2024. Do not use that future trajectory in a 2016 simulation. [6]

**Return accounting:** Institutional Investor's November 21, 2025 report says Pershing had sold approximately **85% of its original stake**, generating about **$2.4 billion profit and just-under-22% realized IRR** before disposing of the remainder. A call-summary source separately gives just-under-16% inception-to-exit investment IRR. These are **different cash-flow scopes**; no single “22% total completed-trade IRR” is asserted here. The stock was a multi-bagger but later purchases/trims and exit price matter to capital-weighted performance. [6,8]

The final sale followed disappointing 2025 operating trends, according to contemporary call coverage. The 2026 annual report verifies the **2025 exit**; therefore “2016–2026” denotes follow-up coverage, not ownership continuing throughout 2026. [6–8]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Temporary trouble:** Pershing believed reputational damage was reparable; this was an assumption to test, not certainty. [1]
- **Moat/value:** fresh customizable meals, attractive price/quality and throughput underpinned customer relevance. [1]
- **Volumes/economics:** recovering sales at existing units would improve restaurant earnings without reinventing the concept. [1]
- **Growth levers:** ordering technology, catering and additional stores supplemented recovery. [1]
- **Governance/management:** board refresh and later Niccol recruitment supplied execution capability. [3–4]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: temporary crisis in a durable franchise contrasts with JCP's need to change the customer proposition. It also tests the Valeant lesson that reputation damage can destroy intrinsic value: Pershing here explicitly argued the moat remained intact and implemented governance changes. The final 2025 exit shows willingness to stop holding when prospective fundamentals change. [1,3,6]

## Public discussion
NYT's September 2016 story contextualized the crisis entry. Reuters tracked subsequent trims; Institutional Investor reported the final sale and deterioration narrative in November 2025.
