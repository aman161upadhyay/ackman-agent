## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

**Update only, 2024-2026.** Cross-reference [original HHH/GGP-derived holding](2010_howard_hughes.md), rather than duplicate its history. This is a distinct primary-capital/control-strategy checkpoint within that long holding.

**Cutoff 2025-05-04 for the incremental primary purchase.** February 18 revised proposal publicly contemplated 10M newly issued shares at $90 and a diversified-holding-company strategy; it replaced the January majority-merger proposal. August 6, 2024 13D amendment had already announced evaluation of a transaction; unaffected August 5 price was $61.46 in the February proposal. [1] The eventual $100/9M-share agreement and Vantage acquisition were not yet completed facts. A complete pre-control HHH financial snapshot and independent fairness valuation remain gaps.

## Ackman's thesis in his own words

The February 2026 retrospective states the objective as a “modern-day Berkshire Hathaway.” [3] The economic argument is that land, condo development and MPC earnings suffer from a high equity cost of capital; diversifying into high-quality cash-generating businesses and adding equity can reduce financing costs and improve returns on capital. Pershing would supply its team rather than only recruit outsiders. [2,3] Insurance investment came later and must not be represented as an already completed May acquisition.

## What Pershing Square did

2024-08-06 transaction exploration; January 13, 2025 initial proposal; February 18 revision; **May 5** $900M investment by **Holdco**, not a $900M new common purchase by PSH: 9M shares at $100, combined ownership 46.9%, Ackman executive chairman, Ryan Israel CIO. [1,2] PSH fund interests and GP/Holdco interests must remain separate.

The transaction retains an independent-majority board and generally limits voting power to 40% despite 46.9% economic ownership. Management fees are $3.75M quarterly base plus quarterly 0.375% of market-cap increase above the defined reference. They are real related-party costs, not free capital allocation. [2] December 2025 announced $2.1B Vantage acquisition, with PSH supporting up to $1B non-voting exchangeable perpetual preferred and HHH cash. PSH management-fee offset applies to HHH fees attributable to PSH's common holdings. [3] June 2026 acquisition closed; the consolidated June 30 13F shows **two HHH rows**, 18,852,064 fund shares and 9M Holdco-related shares, not 9M new Q2 purchases. [4,5]

## What happened after

August 2026 letter reports Vantage leadership additions Marc Grandisson and David Gansberg, intended redeployment of $2.5-$3B real-estate excess cash over three-to-five years and targeted 20%+ insurer ROE. These are plans, not realized results. It says Vantage's longer bonds were replaced by short Treasuries and common stocks after control. [4] The August call expands the asset-light real-estate/insurance transformation rationale but is not a May entry source. [6]

## Reasoning drivers checklist

- **Capital cost:** address the persistent discount structurally, not just demand re-rating. [3]
- **Reinvestment:** harvest existing real-estate cash and allocate to higher-return businesses. [3,4]
- **Team/control:** direct executive roles allow implementation. [2]
- **Insurance:** combine experienced underwriting with Pershing asset management; losses and regulatory capital remain risks. [3,4]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

Extends activism into an external-manager/control model and creates fee/conflict considerations alongside investment returns. It is a different economic exposure from the original passive receipt of HHH in a spin. [2,3]

## Public discussion

The $100 primary price versus public price prompted attention; the premium finances the company rather than paying selling shareholders. [2] Bear cases include related-party fees/conflicts, conglomerate complexity and insurance underwriting/investment risk. The preferred was reviewed independently by PSH's board according to the interim report; that does not eliminate conflicts. [4]
