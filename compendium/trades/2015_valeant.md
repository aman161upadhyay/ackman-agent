## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2015 entry, exact month unverified here:** Valeant was an acquisition-driven pharmaceuticals business. Pershing already knew its management through the **2014 Allergan partnership**, which was a different security and transaction. At entry the investment case relied on existing business value and continued capital allocation; the later Philidor controversy, price-collapse trajectory and final loss must not contaminate the point-in-time case. Original entry presentation, precise drug-portfolio financials, leverage and valuation reconciliation remain gaps. [1–2]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
“We viewed our purchase price as representing a modest discount to the value of the company’s existing assets, but a large discount to intrinsic value in light of our expectation that management would continue to be able to invest capital in new transactions on terms that would create significant long-term value.” — **2017 hindsight description of the entry thesis**, not an independently recovered 2015 quotation. [2]

“Business is fundamentally about trust and confidence. Without trust and confidence, business value can vaporize quickly.” — 2015 annual report, released March 2016, **after the business controversy**, not the original entry rationale. [1]

“Clearly, our investment in Valeant was a huge mistake.” — March 28, 2017 letter. This is a direct admission, not the researcher's characterization. [2,4]

## What Pershing Square did
- 2014: partnered with Valeant to seek Allergan; do not book the Allergan gain as a Valeant equity return. [3]
- 2015: established the large Valeant equity investment; maintained exposure through falling prices and deteriorating confidence. Exact additions/cost basis require original 13D amendments. [1–2]
- 2016: became actively involved in the attempted recovery and leadership transition. The annual letter describes the effort to help turn the business around; exact board-seat chronology remains a filing gap. [2]
- March 2017: exited. The March 28 letter explained the failure and apologized. [2,4]

## What happened after
The reported aggregate loss was approximately **$4 billion**. This is not PSH-only P&L, nor the loss on Allergan. The annual letter judged that prior management overpaid for Salix, that the acquisition-heavy model demanded flawless capital allocation/execution, and that its business was insufficiently durable to survive reputational damage. [2,4]

**Post-mortem and process corrections, dated March 2017:** Pershing said it had assigned too much value to management's historical acquisition record; underestimated political/regulatory risk around pricing and distribution; sized the investment too large relative to those risks; and overestimated the relevance of the successful GGP rescue to Valeant's damaged franchise. These are lessons **learned after loss**, not risks Ackman demonstrably priced correctly at entry. [2]

The 2017 letter reinforces the implementation of core investment principles and resolves legacy risks. The 2018 report then describes a return to simple, predictable, free-cash-flow-generative businesses and a smaller, investment-centric organization. No numerical new position limit or blanket ban on all pharmaceutical investments is established by the sources here. [5–6]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Existing assets/valuation:** his retrospective account said the purchase discount to existing assets was only modest. [2]
- **Acquisition skill:** a large part of perceived upside depended on future transactions; the letter explicitly rejects treating management's record as a durable asset. [2]
- **Cash generation:** the business was underwritten as cash-generative; the later trust/reputation discussion exposed fragility in that assumption. [1–2]
- **Recovery:** GGP success encouraged a turnaround analogy, which Ackman later acknowledged failed because Valeant's underlying business was less durable. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation grounded in the post-mortem: concentration and management influence fit his approach, while reliance on acquisitions, hard-to-model political outcomes and fragile reputation broke the predictability requirement. The answer key should reward recognition of the **reasoning failure**, not simply memorization of a catastrophic stock chart. [2,6]

## Public discussion
CNBC's January 27, 2016 report discussed Ackman's acknowledgment that he should have cut large positions, including Valeant. A contemporary January 29, 2016 investment blog challenged the ephemeral nature of acquisition-platform value; Fortune covered the apology in March 2017. This supplies actual contemporaneous disagreement rather than a backfilled consensus. Original VIC/COBF/Reddit/X threads and the original 2015 deck remain missing. [4,7–8]
