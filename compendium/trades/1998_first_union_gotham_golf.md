## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**March 27, 1998 public-campaign snapshot, not a verified first-purchase date.** First Union was a real-estate investment trust facing Gotham's contested trustee slate. The Ohio court had rejected attempts to prevent Gotham voting and running its slate before the scheduled April 14 meeting. Original entry valuation, leverage, portfolio composition and Gotham cost are unknown. Do not project the later golf merger into this initial situation. [1]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
Original 1998 investment letter not recovered. For the **later merger phase**, Ackman said on December 11, 2002: “We stand by our deal with First Union and its shareholders as we believe that it is fair and the product of a rigorous and lengthy arm's-length negotiation process.” This is his defense of the proposal, not independent evidence of fairness. [3]

## What Pershing Square did
- **1998:** Gotham pursued the proxy contest; issuer's 2001 10-K/A places Ackman as a trustee from June 1998. [1,5]
- **September 21, 2001:** merger letter of intent contemplated $2.70 per First Union share. [2]
- **February 14, 2002:** definitive deal reduced consideration after the HQ Global investment was written to zero. Shareholders could receive $2.55 cash, less intervening dividends, or $2.20 plus an interest in an asset-backed note, as well as rights. The operator had 25 courses, 21 clustered within 250 miles of Hershey. The merger included preferred shareholders receiving replacement Gotham Golf preferred. [2]
- **December 6, 2002:** New York court preliminarily barred the merger. **December 11:** Gotham proposed alternatives including cash to preferred holders, dilution of its interest, a $20M standby equity commitment, and First Union loans of up to $12M to Gotham Golf for expenses/acquisitions. Ackman announced resignation as First Union chairman. [3]

## What happened after
The blocked transaction coincided with redemption requests and an inability to turn illiquid investments into cash. The January 19, 2003 NYT account describes the resulting run on Gotham; liquidation was announced December 27, 2002 according to the retrospective biography. “Big_loss” denotes the failed control/illiquidity episode, **not an independently calculated return on FUR stock**. Exact distributions from the wind-down remain unknown. [4,6]

The Spitzer/SEC inquiries must be kept separate from the merger injunction. Contemporary reporting discusses scrutiny of research/trading in Pre-Paid Legal, MBIA and Farmer Mac; an inquiry is not a finding of manipulation. A later Bloomberg biography states no charges were filed. [7,8]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Trustee replacement:** directly documented by the 1998 proxy solicitation. [1]
- **Combination with golf:** explicitly proposed in the 2002 joint release; not established as the 1998 purchase thesis. [2]
- **Structured consideration:** cash, collateral-backed note and rights are original transaction terms, not analyst conjecture. [2,3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Analytical comparison: the episode illustrates a liquidity mismatch between redeemable fund capital and controlled/private assets, and conflict risk when the same investor sits on both sides of a transaction. Do not describe the contemporaneous claim of arm's-length negotiation as an adjudicated conclusion. [2–4]

## Public discussion
NYT's January 2003 rescue-transaction account is critical contemporaneous reporting; the December SEC release gives Gotham's response and revised terms. January 2003 NY Post reporting supplies the regulatory allegations, explicitly not findings. No era forum archive was verified. [3,4,7]
