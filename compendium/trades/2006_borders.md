## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2006 entry year reported retrospectively; exact first purchase unknown.** Borders was a physical bookstore chain exposed to online book retailing. An original 2006 financial/valuation pack has not been recovered. Later collapse cannot be written as inevitable at entry; the relevant historical disagreement was whether Amazon fears overstated the threat to a valuable store franchise. [1,5]

## Ackman's thesis in his own words
Reuters's 2011 chronology attributes the original claim that Amazon fears were “exaggerated” and a $36 value versus $23.92 share price. This is **a later reconstruction of the original pitch**, not an authenticated 2006 letter. [1]

**Q2 2010 letter**, quoted September 1, 2010: “We have long believed in the strategic logic of a business combination between the two largest superstore companies.” This supports the later consolidation rationale, not an entry-date fact. [4]

## What Pershing Square did
Bought an activist stake, later supplied a $42.5M loan and received warrants. The March 31, 2009 issuer filing documents amendments, the UK-business purchase-offer arrangement and warrant exercise tranches; separate loan recovery from equity economics. [1,2]

September 2008 13F reports 10,597,880 common shares/$69.522M. In December 2010 Pershing offered to finance Borders' potential $16-per-share bid for Barnes & Noble, approximately $963M. This was a financing proposal, not a consummated acquisition or proof of a new $963M cash investment. [3,6]

## What happened after
The strategic combination did not occur. Borders filed bankruptcy in 2011 and ultimately liquidated; retrospective accounts classify this as a major Ackman loss. **The requested “2006–10” holding range is not accepted as a verified final exit.** Loan principal, interest, warrants and common shares require separate cash-flow accounting before assigning a precise loss. [1,5]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Undervaluation/threat assessment:** later chronology captures the dismissed online-competition concern, a central potential reasoning error. [1]
- **Consolidation:** supported by the 2010 letter excerpt; should be timestamped as a later route to salvage value. [4]
- **Financing:** issuer filings document willingness to bridge liquidity, which increased complexity and commitment. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Analytical comparison: a physical retailer's apparently cheap earnings can prove non-durable. Repeated financing/merger efforts differ from a predictable royalty stream; do not label every retail asset discount a defensible moat. [1,2,4]

## Public discussion
The 2010 Ann Arbor account follows losses, non-book merchandise and digital strategy; contemporary December merger coverage questioned whether combining two threatened chains would help. These are more informative opposing views than hindsight bankruptcy alone. No authenticated VIC thread recovered. [4,6]
