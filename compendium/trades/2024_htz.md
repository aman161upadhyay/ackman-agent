## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

**Cutoff: 2024-11-10, not April 2025.** Hertz's 2024-08-01 release reported Q2 revenue $2.4B, GAAP loss $865M, adjusted loss $440M and corporate liquidity $1.8B at June 30. Elevated fleet depreciation and the ongoing fleet refresh made this a financially fragile turnaround, not a normalized stable compounder. [1] **Q3 results released November 12 were not public before the November 11 first purchase.** April 2025 tariffs, Ackman's residual-value calculation and the AV thought experiment also are not pre-purchase inputs. [2,3] Pre-entry execution prices/borrow-market consensus: **unknown**.

## Ackman's thesis in his own words

Firecrawl verified the **2025-04-17** post:

> “We began accumulating shares in @Hertz late last year, and as of today, we have a 19.8% stake in the company comprised of outright share ownership and total return swaps.”

> “Hertz can be thought of as an operating company combined with a highly leveraged portfolio of automobiles.”

> “A 10% increase in used car prices would equate to a $1.2 billion gain on its auto assets”

> “What if UBER partnered with Hertz on an AV fleet roll out over time?” [2]

**Full stated argument, paraphrased:** Enterprise/Avis/Hertz oligopoly; improved post-pandemic pricing discipline; disposal of costly Teslas and correction of residual-value losses; Gil West's operating experience, higher unit revenues and lower costs; fleet rotation lowering depreciation; liquidity and debt predominantly maturing in 2028-29; auto tariffs raising used-car prices while 2025 replacement purchases had already been negotiated. His scenario combined $1,500 RPU, low-$30/day operating expense, about $300 DPU and 85% utilization by 2029 into approximately $2B adjusted EBITDA and approximately $30/share at 7.5x. These are **April 2025 scenario assumptions**, not outcomes or November 2024 facts. AV servicing was explicitly a thought experiment, not a signed autonomous-fleet contract. [2]

## What happened after

February 2026 letter cited younger fleet, 84% utilization, first profitable quarter in two years, used-car retail partnerships and AV servicing opportunities. Those positive interim operating observations did **not** prevent an exit. [4] August reporting quotes CIO Ryan Israel saying confidence in management was lost after the June financing, which the team considered unnecessary; Ackman called it “bungled.” Attribute those explanations to the August discussion, not to the April entry thesis. [7] PSH records -1.1 percentage points gross contribution through August 11 and the July exit. Dollar realized loss and exact exit executions remain unknown. [6]

## Reasoning drivers checklist

- **Oligopoly:** pricing and competitor economics suggested rental profitability could improve. [2]
- **Fleet/management:** Gil West and fleet rotation were supposed to repair unit economics. [2]
- **Asset values:** used-car increases have large equity sensitivity; scenario is not cash earnings realized immediately. [2]
- **Liquidity/leverage:** leverage magnified upside but made financing decisions central to the eventual reversal. [2,7]
- **Ancillary optionality:** retail dispositions and AV servicing were additional possibilities, not necessary proven earnings. [2,4]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

Breaks the usual low-leverage, predictable-FCF template; explicitly a smaller asymmetric turnaround. It illustrates why “cheap assets” do not substitute for confidence in the people allocating capital. [2,7]
