## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

Pershing's June 2007 US filing reports Cadbury Schweppes ADR holdings of 19,718 and 15,665, $1.922M combined market value. This verifies ownership but not first purchase, foreign ordinary-share exposure or a full position size. No original entry financial snapshot recovered. [13F, 2007-08-14](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336528/000117266107000402/pers2q07.txt).

## Ackman's thesis in his own words

No verified entry-date pitch recovered. The original Q2 2009 letter retrospectively identifies “our substantial position in Cadbury PLC that we purchased originally in early 2007 and sold in the third and fourth quarters of last year.” It explains that deteriorating credit would, they thought, delay a sale and concludes: “We underestimated the rapidity of the recovery of the credit markets and missed out on the opportunity for an attractive sale to the strategic buyer whom we had previously identified as the likely acquirer. We were wrong.” This is an authentic Ackman catalyst explanation, not Nelson Peltz's campaign or Einhorn's Dr Pepper thesis. [Letter, page 8, public by 2009-09-24](https://www.scribd.com/document/20150688/Pershing-Square-Q2-2009-Investor-Letter).

## What Pershing Square did

The ADRs are present in June 2007 and March 2008 filings. Ackman's Q3 2008 letter confirms the complete sale of “Cadbury PLC” during that quarter. The successor naming is retained here; the separately verified Dr Pepper Snapple holding has its own dossier. [13Fs filed 2007-08-14, above; 2008-05-14](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336528/000117266108000313/pscm1q08.txt); [Q3 letter, reproduced 2008-11-27](https://advisoranalyst.com/2008/11/27/bill-ackman-pershing-square-q3-2008-letter.html/).

## What happened after

**Source discrepancy:** the Q3 2008 letter lists Cadbury among positions exited that quarter; the later original Q2 2009 letter says Q3 **and Q4**, with initial purchase in early 2007. Accordingly December 2008 is the later account's quarter-end bound, not an exact sale month. Full realized return is unknown. Ackman explicitly calls selling too early a mistake; later takeover gains to other holders cannot be credited to Pershing. [Both original letters, linked above.]

## Reasoning drivers checklist

- **Strategic buyer:** retrospective original testimony identifies an anticipated acquirer.
- **Valuation:** unknown.
- **Financing/timing:** original 2009 explanation says credit deterioration motivated selling, then the rebound was faster than expected.

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

Ownership and a strategic-sale catalyst are established; detailed entry underwriting is not. The original 2009 lesson is explicitly about selling too early and misjudging catalyst timing, not a claim that Cadbury's business quality deteriorated.

## Public discussion

The contemporary Market Folly digest records Cadbury among Q3 exits, corroborating the letter but supplying no original company thesis. [2008-11-18](https://www.marketfolly.com/2008/11/hedge-fund-tracking-bill-ackmans.html).
