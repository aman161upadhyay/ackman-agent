# H1 2026: sustainable growth, AI disruption analysis and the broader portfolio

**Information date:** August 12, 2026; performance period ended June 30; manager discussion includes developments through August 11. **Source:** [official interim report](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/13002306/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-June-2026-Interim.pdf). Manager letter is signed **William A. Ackman and Ryan Israel**. All descriptions below refer to this source.

## Record and attribution

H1 PSH NAV return **-12.6%**, S&P 500 **+10.2%**; by August 11 PSH **-4.3%**, S&P **+13.7%**. These are period fund returns, not batting averages.

The report acknowledges disparity between underlying business growth and quoted performance and argues multiples/market flows caused part of the divergence. This is management's explanation; future recovery is not guaranteed.

## Valuation framework

The portfolio table compares estimated current earnings multiples with prospective three-to-five-year EPS growth. Weighted estimate approximately **19x earnings and 20% annual growth**, versus S&P **20x and 12%**. These are **Pershing's estimates**, not realized outcomes. The growth column **includes dividend yield**. Weighting uses delta market values as of August 11 and excludes HHH/Fannie/Freddie; some entry multiples use purchase cost, not June 30 prices.

**Exact excerpt:**

> “By investing in businesses that have higher rates of sustainable earnings growth, we can achieve high levels of investment returns without significant turnover in our portfolio”

A higher quality growth portfolio can be held while value compounds rather than sold immediately after a rerating. This does not repeal the Hilton return-hurdle exit discipline.

## Portfolio count and dates

Valuation table has fifteen issuer rows: Uber, Brookfield, Microsoft, Meta, QSR, Amazon, HHH, Fannie, Freddie, Visa, Mastercard, Netflix, S&P Global, ICE and Alcon. **ICE and Alcon were initiated after June 30**, as the table footnote states. Thus this is not a clean June 30 holdings snapshot or proof all fifteen are equal-sized core positions.

UMG and Hertz appear among year-to-date detractors even though absent from this forward valuation table. A performance table can include sold positions; absence from a forecast table alone is not sufficient to infer an exact exit date.

## S&P Global: decompose the AI bear case

The report identifies Ratings, Indices and Platts as dominant benchmark franchises producing over 80% of profit, while some exposed desktop products are much smaller. It says Capital IQ is less than 7% of total revenue and still less of profits. The stock's earlier-2026 repricing from roughly 25x to 19x EPS is treated as disproportionate to actual susceptible earnings.

**Pattern:** identify the part of the profit pool truly threatened; don't apply one narrative to all segments. These figures are report-date manager estimates/claims and should be verified independently for a decision pack.

## Netflix: reversal of a prior exit, not denial of it

The new investment follows evidence of a successful business-model transition and attractive price. The report discusses cash generation, content-cost leverage, advertising, buybacks and AI recommendation/ad-targeting benefits.
