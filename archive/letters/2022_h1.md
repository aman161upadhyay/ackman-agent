# H1 2022: predictability, Netflix exit and the asymmetry of updating

## Sources and separate information dates

- **April 20, 2022:** [original Netflix sale letter](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2022/04/20184527/Letter-to-Shareholders-4.20.2022.pdf), independently fetched in full.
- **August 19, 2022:** [H1 report letter](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2022/08/19191407/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-June-2022-Interim-Letter.pdf); performance period June 30, additional data through August 16.
- **Later contrast, not part of 2022 information:** [H1 2026 report, August 12, 2026](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/13002306/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-June-2026-Interim.pdf).

## Netflix: what changed by April 20

The original letter says the entire investment was sold that day. It reports the loss reduced **Pershing Square Funds' YTD returns by four percentage points**; this is not the percentage loss on the security itself.

Subscriber disappointments caused management to propose tougher action on unpaid sharing and an advertising offering, with implementation estimated at one to two years. Ackman considered those sensible responses but found their effects on subscriber growth, revenues, operating margins and capital intensity difficult to predict.

His prior favorable interpretation of operating leverage reversed: changes in growth could have an outsized effect on intrinsic value. The issue was not that a streaming business suddenly became impossible to explain, but that simple business mechanics no longer produced a narrow enough forecast distribution.

**Exact short excerpt:**

> “we have lost confidence in our ability to predict the company’s future prospects with a sufficient degree of certainty.”

He explicitly allowed that Netflix might continue to be highly successful and an excellent investment at its current market value. The dispersion of outcomes nevertheless made it unsuitable as a **core concentrated holding**.

## The updating rule is point-in-time, not a post hoc maxim

The April letter connects prompt action to learning from past mistakes when information contradicts the original thesis. This same-day source is stronger for exit reasoning than the annual report published nearly a year later.

The letter does not specify which subsequent investment received these proceeds; it says an opportunity-rich environment should provide a good use. **Do not infer a direct April 2022 purchase of Alphabet from a loose later podcast recollection; Alphabet entry occurred in 2023.**

## August H1 framework

The later interim letter restates the preference for durable, well-capitalized growth businesses capable of surviving severe environments. It distinguishes macroeconomic/multiple pressure and market-flow changes from the underlying cash performance of the companies held.

Discussed holdings include UMG, QSR, Chipotle, Hilton, Lowe's, CP and HHC; Netflix and PSTH appear in attribution/exit context. The macro-hedging narrative cannot be used to erase the Netflix mistake. A profitable source of capital and a successful use of that capital are separate judgments.

## Contrast with 2026: no permanent blacklist

The 2026 report describes a new Netflix position after evidence on advertising, cash generation, content-cost leverage and a more favorable entry valuation. That is a later re-underwriting, **not information proving the April 2022 decision obviously right or wrong**. Agent evaluation should score the ability to explain forecast uncertainty and update appropriately at each cutoff, rather than reward hindsight certainty.
