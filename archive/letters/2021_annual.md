# 2021 annual report: organizational reset and asymmetric hedging doctrine

**Source:** [official report, March 28, 2022](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2022/03/29140526/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-2021-Annual-Report.pdf). Chairman Anne Farlow's statement and Ackman's manager letter are distinct. All findings below come from this source; the reset is **hindsight about 2017–2018**.

## Four pillars of the turnaround

1. Exit problematic positions.
2. Shrink to an investment-centric organization; let future asset growth come from returns.
3. Stabilize capital through a larger insider PSH stake and stopping marketing/private-fund capital raising.
4. Recommit to core quality, simplicity, predictability and cash-generation principles, physically recorded on desk tablets.

The account says about $20bn equity by 2015, roughly 60% in quarterly-redeemable private funds. Ackman had become a de facto chief marketer; investor meetings fragmented investment-research time. In January 2018 the investment team stopped routine investor meetings, with Ben Hakim handling that function. This is a causal theory about **attention and funding**, not a newly invented stock-selection metric.

## Exit does not mean no future upside

The letter acknowledges that retaining Valeant and the Herbalife short would subsequently have been profitable from the final exit prices, but says replacement investments did better. It includes a March 22, 2022 counterfactual comparison. Those numbers **cannot** be used to justify an earlier entry or held-out exit decision.

**Short exact excerpt:**

> “don’t need to make it back the way you lost it”

## Permanent capital and UMG

By the report date nearly 90% of assets were in PSH; employee/affiliate PSH ownership exceeded 25%, with more insider capital in private funds. The letter says Vivendi valued a long-term UMG owner with alignment and no required exit strategy, unlike investors requiring a planned realization.

**Exact excerpt:**

> “We do not need an exit strategy.”

This is **preference for suitable permanent holdings**, not a promise never to sell UMG or any other quality business.

## Hedge doctrine

**Exact excerpt:**

> “We do not use hedges to protect our mark-to-market performance from short-term downward volatility.”

The manager prefers small-capital asymmetric instruments such as options/CDS against major disruption, not costly continuous insurance against normal fluctuations.

The chairman's account gives an initial rate-swaption cost of $157m, approximately 1.4% of PSH assets at the time; sale of most exposure in January 2022 produced $1.25bn **across funds**. Proceeds funded Netflix. This links a successful hedge to an ultimately unsuccessful equity purchase; hedging skill is not proof the redeployment will succeed.

## Portfolio / structure

UMG, Domino's and CP new investments; profitable Starbucks/Agilent exits; early-2022 Netflix; continuing Chipotle, QSR, Hilton, HHC and Fannie/Freddie. PSTH/SPARC structure and issues are separate from ordinary portfolio stock selection.

## Replay cautions

- January 2022 Netflix confidence is present; April 2022 Netflix failure is not yet known.
- “No required exit” must not be transformed into “no sell discipline.”
- Insider ownership and permanent capital do not prevent valuation losses.
- Later 2024–26 platform fundraising reverses part of the 2018 **asset-management business strategy**, not necessarily the preference for durable businesses.
