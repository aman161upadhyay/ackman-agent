# 2015 letters: contemporaneous defense versus eventual lessons

## Documents and information dates

1. **January 26, 2016 original annual letter:** [official PDF](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2014/09/Pershing-Square-2015-Annual-Letter-PSH-January-26-2016.pdf).
2. **March 2016 annual-report update:** [letter excerpt](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/media/2014/09/09115507/2015-Annual-Report-Letter-Only.pdf).

These are different documents. The January letter's assertions were made before the subsequent Valeant stabilization, board changes and final exit. Descriptions below identify the relevant document.

## January letter: actual admitted errors

Ackman identified failure to trim positions nearing intrinsic value, overvalued acquisition/platform potential, and loss of trading flexibility while restricted. These are direct contemporaneous admissions—not merely a commentator's diagnosis.

**Verbatim excerpts, January 26:**

> Our biggest valuation error was assigning too much value to the so-called “platform value” in certain of our holdings.

> “we should have avoided becoming restricted to preserve trading flexibility”

He argued platform value depended on cheap capital, talented people and transaction prices and was more ephemeral than existing operating assets. Valeant was purchased around $196 average cost: he considered it only a modest discount to existing-asset value but a major discount including future acquisitions. Failure to trim in the mid-$200s was, in his account, costly.

Restrictions arose after receiving information about a potential transaction. He says that, given leverage and regulatory/political sensitivity, avoiding restriction or starting smaller would have been preferable. This reveals a distinction between **publicly tradable in theory** and **actually free to trade**.

CP also should have been trimmed around C$240 given portfolio concentration and macro concerns, despite still appearing below intrinsic value. Buying more Platform Specialty Products at $25 to support an acquisition is identified as another error: financing, integration, currency and operating execution did not support assumed value creation.

## Preserve the contemporaneous disagreement with the later postmortem

The January letter nevertheless argued much of 2015's loss reflected repricing without commensurate intrinsic-value deterioration and did not anticipate permanent impairment from Valeant's then-known events. **The archive must retain that defense.** By March 2017 the final-exit letter would admit that the Valeant investment was a huge mistake. Do not rewrite January 2016 as if the later explanation had already been accepted. [2016 annual letter, March 2017](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/media/2014/09/09120722/2016-Annual-Report-Letter-Only.pdf)

## March update: engagement as defense

The report describes intervention at Valeant after severe challenges: board involvement and management stabilization. It also explicitly explains that leverage magnifies downside as well as upside. The portfolio update covers APD, CP, Fannie/Freddie, Herbalife, HHC, Mondelez, Nomad, Platform, QSR and Zoetis, plus exited Allergan/Actavis.

**Process implication:** an initially passive investment can become a defensive activist engagement. That transition creates new attention demands and restrictions; it should not be retrospectively described as the original entry catalyst.

## Training implications

- A January 2016 agent can know the platform-value and sizing admissions; it cannot know the final 2017 sale.
- Acquisition track record is not equivalent to an inexhaustible asset.
- Managing concentration may require selling a still-admired holding.
- “No margin loans” does not mean the fund or portfolio companies have no leverage.
- Contrasting January 2016 and March 2017 reasoning is an explicit test of updating, not an inconsistency to erase.
