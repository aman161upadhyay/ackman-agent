# 2014 annual letter: foundational process anthology

**Information date:** March 2015. **Performance period:** 2014. **Primary source:** [official letter/principles PDF](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/media/2014/09/09114633/2014-Annual-Report-Letter-only.pdf). All descriptions below refer to this document unless otherwise noted.

## Why this source matters

This letter intentionally collects older writing on strategy, risk, sizing, governance, trading, capital stability, scale, transparency and humility. It is not merely a portfolio update. Ackman says he re-read prior letters and preserved excerpts largely as written, with updates in brackets. **An anthology quote is verified as publicly reprinted in March 2015; its original date may remain unknown.**

## Process evolution in Ackman's account

- **1.0:** meaningful stakes, structural/corporate changes, shorter holdings and continuous recycling of ideas; Wendy's/Tim Hortons and Ceridian are examples.
- **2.0:** GGP board participation developed into deeper intervention; creation of HHC required a board, management recruitment and incentives. Operational/managerial engagement extended to JCP, Burger King/QSR, CP, APD, Platform and Zoetis.
- He acknowledges JCP as the conspicuous disappointing board-engagement case. This is not a universal success claim.
- Existing high-quality governance can make his own seat unnecessary; Restaurant Brands/3G is the explicit example.

**Exact short excerpts:**

> “Our holding periods were shorter.”

> “We often seek investments where we can effectuate positive change to catalyze the realization of value.”

These distinguish a historical description and a recurring preference from an absolute requirement to run a proxy fight.

## Core economic logic

The businesses sought have understandable, predictable cash flows; sustainable advantages from brands, unique assets or contractual structures; adequate financing; and cash beyond reinvestment needs. Discounts to intrinsic value provide protection. Management, governance and capital allocation can improve both intrinsic value and its recognition. A short-term stock decline does not necessarily reduce business value, particularly when the company can buy discounted shares.

The letter **allows exceptions** for more leveraged or lower-quality assets when price compensates for the risk. Separate higher-risk/probabilistic investments can be a few percent or less, often under 1%; they should not be assigned the same risk budget as the core portfolio.

## Funding, concentration and risk

Risk is framed as permanent loss rather than short-run standard deviation. Avoiding margin calls prevents sturdy assets from being liquidated at the wrong time; careful counterparty selection, collateral/ISDA terms and counterparty hedges are additional considerations. No claim is made that counterparty risk can be eliminated perfectly.

Concentration implies eccentric monthly/annual results and large contributions from a few ideas. The method requires deep understanding and monitoring rather than merely the confidence to hold few names. The letter explicitly anticipates underperformance periods.

PSH's IPO improves capital permanence and reduces dilution from historic cash/passive buffers. That is a funding advantage, not a guarantee PSH shares trade at NAV.

## Trading and the option to abandon

The anthology discusses building researched positions rather than token research stakes, often retaining capacity to add. It also stresses willingness to reverse a conclusion when new evidence contradicts the thesis. A public position can produce confirmation bias; the JCP loss is cited as evidence the firm can still exit.

**Exact short excerpt:**

> “Confidence and conviction without humility can be dangerous in the investment business.”

This is not an instruction to abandon every volatile holding. Contradictory business evidence must be distinguished from a changing quotation.

## Portfolio context

Allergan/Valeant partnership and Actavis transaction; Herbalife short; APD/Ghasemi; CP/Harrison; QSR/Tim Hortons/3G; Platform Specialty Products; Zoetis; HHC; Fannie/Freddie; exits Beam, GGP and P&G. **Do not infer that the later direct Valeant investment or its final outcome was already fully knowable from this earlier portfolio context.**

## Replay boundary

The 2.0 narrative is hindsight through March 2015. Original 2005 Wendy's, 2008 GGP or 2011 CP entry simulations should use their contemporaneous filings, not this later account of what worked. The principle anthology can inform a March 2015 agent, but undated reprints should not be silently assigned to 2004.
