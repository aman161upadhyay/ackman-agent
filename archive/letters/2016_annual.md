# 2016 annual letter, issued March 2017: Valeant final exit

**Primary source:** [official letter PDF](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/media/2014/09/09120722/2016-Annual-Report-Letter-Only.pdf). **Information date:** March 2017, after the reported Valeant sale; exact signature day not visible in the fetched excerpt. **Not** information available at the 2015 entry.

## Final-exit explanation

After joining the Valeant board in March 2016, Ackman describes replacing management/directors, refinancing, covenant negotiations, asset disposals and improved disclosure. He did not argue at exit that no recovery was possible.

At sale the position represented about **3% of PSH**. Even a substantial rebound would have only a modest portfolio effect, while continued ownership would consume large human resources and attention. The lesson is an explicit **return-on-attention/opportunity-cost** calculation, not a mechanical stop loss.

**Verbatim clean-text excerpt:**

> “Clearly, our investment in Valeant was a huge mistake.”

## Lessons, paraphrased to avoid damaged OCR

Several bullet passages in the PDF scrape double characters. The statements below are faithful **paraphrases**, not fabricated clean “verbatim” quotes:

1. Prior acquisition success is not a sufficiently durable asset to assign substantial intrinsic value to.
2. Uncontrollable regulatory/political changes can materially impair intrinsic value; size must reflect that exposure.
3. Even a manager with a strong investment record can make very large mistakes; Salix is discussed.
4. A price collapse can impair *business* value through morale, recruitment, reputation and retention. Price and value need not remain independent under stress.

The letter distinguishes Valeant from the prior GGP success: the underlying businesses were insufficiently durable against reputational damage. This is the failure of analogical transfer—“we helped rescue another collapsing stock” was not enough.

## Chipotle as the positive contrast

**Verbatim process excerpt:**

> “We have always believed that a good time to buy a great business is when it is in temporary trouble.”

The Chipotle section treats its brand/economics as recoverable after food-safety problems. For training, the key distinction is **proof of durable franchise and a repairable problem**, not simply the magnitude of the price fall.

## Process implications and limits

The letter mentions human-resource/process changes but does not provide a complete internal operating manual. Do not invent committee veto rights, new maximum weights, or a universal prohibition on turnarounds. The later 2021 annual report supplies a more systematic **retrospective** account of the four reset pillars.
