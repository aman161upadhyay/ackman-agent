# 2025 annual report: watchlists, capex economics and real exits

**Source/information date:** [official report, February 18, 2026](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/18175039/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-2025-Annual-Report.pdf). Covers 2025 plus events into February 2026. All findings below are from this source unless separately linked.

## Portfolio chronology: avoid common date errors

CP exited **April 2025**; Chipotle and Nike **November 2025**; Hilton **February 2026**. Amazon began April 2025; Meta late 2025. The report describes $17bn year-end core-strategy assets; do not confuse this with the February presentation's gross AUM or HHH-inclusive platform total.

## Prepared watchlist, then price

**Exact excerpt:**

> “Our investment in Meta highlights our approach of closely following a watchlist of high-quality businesses and waiting patiently for an attractive entry price.”

Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Brookfield and Uber illustrate a shift toward durable growth economics and perceived AI/automation beneficiaries, rather than a prohibition on technology. This requires an actual disruption thesis and an attractive entry price; “AI” is not a substitute for valuation.

## Growth capex versus maintenance capex

The letter distinguishes potentially valuable growth investment from unexpectedly larger maintenance needs. A capex increase can rationally reduce current FCF while increasing intrinsic value if returns on investment exceed cost. Conversely, labeling maintenance as growth would overstate recurring earning power.

**Training implication:** never infer that every fall in near-term free cash flow makes an investment ineligible; reconcile why cash is being spent and whose estimates support the expected returns.

## Hilton: a valuation exit, not a thesis collapse

The position initiated in 2018 at less than 23x estimated following-year EPS; the February 2026 exit was around 32x. The report praises the asset-light model, Nassetta's management, buybacks and results through COVID, and reports more than seven years of ownership. It says the valuation made further share appreciation unlikely to meet the firm's high return thresholds.

This is the clearest answer to “why sell a great business?”: future return from today's price, not a falling opinion of business quality or a need to realize gains for its own sake.

## HHH / Vantage: distinct securities and mandates

The manager's May 2025 $900m HHH investment is **not** an identical transaction to PSH buying ordinary shares. The December 2025 Vantage agreement is described as expected to close Q2 2026, subject to approvals. Price: $2.1bn. PSH could provide up to $1bn preferred financing; the report outlines redemption/exchange economics tied to Vantage book value.

Do not call the acquisition completed as of February 18. Later completion must be sourced to a later report. Do not treat HHH's insurance liabilities as ordinary fund margin debt or manager fee-paying AUM.

## Uber / autonomous vehicles

The report argues a broader field of AV developers can benefit Uber's demand/network position rather than simply disintermediate it. This is a contestable underwriting judgment, not a fact established by share-price appreciation. The cited operating evidence and partnership progress must be kept separate from prospective supplier economics.

## Replay boundary

No Microsoft/Netflix-2026 entry is inferred merely from this annual report's existence. The August 2026 report supplies later holdings. Portfolio exits known by February 18 belong to this date's information set; their later performance does not.
