# Professional forums, independent analysis and press

Cutoff/retrieval: **2026-10-25**. Organized by trade, separating independent arguments, contemporaneous discussion, and later hindsight. These sources are **not Ackman's own reasoning** unless an item explicitly identifies a primary statement. Search-only/gated items are discovery leads, not fully read reports. No claim is made that every requested outlet covered every trade.

## Source/access conventions

- **Fetched**: article/page content retrieved with webFetch or Firecrawl. A successful fetch can still expose only a summary or page shell; this is specified.
- **Exa text**: indexed page text returned with a semantic search; may be incomplete.
- **Search extract**: argument bounded by the visible title/snippet; full article not verified.
- **Gated**: only metadata/valuation table or limited comments exposed. A linked VIC write-up is not treated as read simply because its title and date are known.
- **Date unknown** means the retrieved material did not establish a publication day. Relative ages and inferred chronology are not invented.

### Seeking Alpha

[Joseph Parrish, “Fannie Mae Common And Preferreds: The Home Stretch”](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4829905-fannie-mae-common-and-preferreds-the-home-stretch), **2025-10-14** (**Exa summary**). Bullish on both common and preferreds, argues a discount to book and potential release unlock value, while preferring downside protection in discounted preferreds. Recognizes warrant dilution and political timing. **Contrast to Ackman:** Preferred upside is capped while common has greater residual upside but greater sensitivity to capital structure and dilution.

**Reasoning caution:** Administrative willingness to monetize government holdings is not proof that senior preferreds must be extinguished on Ackman's preferred terms. Archive his assumptions and contemporaneous opposing capital-structure possibilities separately; do not use later price moves to settle this debate.

**Trade-off:** Permanent capital supports patient investment during drawdowns, but cannot by itself eliminate persistent discounts, fees, governance risk or the distinction between gross portfolio results and investor returns.
